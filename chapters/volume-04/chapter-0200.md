# Chapter 200 — Who Is Keeping Them

The fourth day of the thirty-ninth week is a Thursday, the corridor is shut, and the school did not run and lost nothing. It ran yesterday and it will run on the fifth day, and the nine go to the room at the kitchen-line end and nowhere else. Twelve mornings have come into this anteroom since a foreman went up the middle passage on a sixth day with two pieces of paper in an inside coat, and on the fourth of those twelve a man came back down it, and he is at the anvil end now with a coat over the back of a chair, and on the trestle in this anteroom are the two pieces of paper, and on each of them the number in the corner has a line through it.

Nobody in that anteroom has said a word about either of those two lines for a whole day, and Dalla Sen entered that this morning at the first bell, and entered the reason, which is that a room which says a word about a thing it has not decided what to do with is a room making a plan.

Bel Ordry put the plate and the coat and the rule where they go. The plate on the shelf above the bench is wrapped in work cloth and has been unopened for thirty-nine weeks, and the brass survey rule in the pocket of the coat on the hook has said nothing for thirty.

"**I have written that pair of figures on the inside of my own cover twice in a month and I have not looked at either of them since and I am telling you they are wrong and I am not going to say by how much,**" he said. "**A number about a coat is a number about a coat. A person in a year who finds one in a book of mine has to decide for himself whether a number about a coat is a number about anything, and I have stopped being the person who decides that for him.**"

Dalla Sen entered both in the state they were found and entered that neither came into a room.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twentieth reading, and the same as nineteen, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and the line along the join is still in two places, and she gave no figure for either of them and put the rule away.

Renn Vale came down the four steps at the second bell, which is her bell on a shut morning, and was there about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times. The clerk entered a figure of nothing against it, and entered that she was not giving the run a number this morning either, and entered that the figure had not gone up or down since the beginning of the thirty-seventh week, and that a figure that has not moved is a figure and not a reading.

The anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, all six, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and the pump was attended and stopped on the rising water line that has still not moved.

The two networks were in the room and were not in each other. Renn Vale had a slate in her apron and Nella Bray had one in her belt, and neither of them took hers out, and the clerk entered that two conditions were in the building and entered neither of them and entered that she had entered that twice now and would go on entering it.

Then the room got to the trestle, and it took until the third bell, and nobody had called it and nobody had arranged it.

Emrys Callow did not stand up. He had a cup in his hand that had been empty for an hour.

"**I will give it in the order it happened and I will not do it twice and I will not be asked to do it twice, because the first time is a thing that happened and the second time is a thing somebody is making happen, and I have watched four of the second kind in nine years and one of them was mine.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in a foreman's words, and entered that he said it before anybody asked and not after.**"

"**Four days up and a morning at a counter. Two sheets of paper, and this boundary's own copy of a form that came off a board eight weeks ago with a seal number on the corner of it that was not the number it had had before, and six lines in a clerk's hand with one name in both of them.**"

"**On the first morning a woman I have never spoken to said that the second line of that form asks for the name of a person responsible for a load, and that there is no load on that ground, and that a form which cannot be answered correctly is a form which cannot be used, and that the sheet stays on the board. She said it kindly. I want that entered, because everybody in this building thinks a counter is a wall and it was not a wall, it was a woman who agreed with me.**"

"**I said it again on the fifth day. I said it a third way on the sixth day and I got the order wrong both times and she corrected me twice, patiently, and on the second of those mornings she stopped writing.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything.

"**On the third morning a different woman was on the counter and she had not read anything I said, and I said it from the beginning, and when I came to the part about the second line she put two fingers on the number in the corner and said *I cannot produce him*, and struck it through.**"

He turned the cup round on the table a quarter of a turn.

"**Four words. That is the whole of what was said to me across three days by anybody in that office and none of it was a concession and all of it was correct. I have not been refused. I want that said, because I said on Saturday in this room that a person gets refused, and I was wrong about that, and the clerk is going to want the reason for it and here it is: I was wrong about what an office does and I was right about how a person behaves in one, and those are two separate things and I have had twelve days to get them the right way round.**"

"**The other number is the insurance office's and it is not that counter's to strike, and the woman at the counter told me so in one sentence on the first morning and wrote nothing down about it. It was struck through on the third morning by a man who has worked at that counter for twenty-two years and who is not a hero and who does not want to be thanked and who did it in about four seconds, and he did it because a head on a sheet is a piece of wax with a number on it and the counter was holding the sheet.**"

Nella Bray had been at the kitchen-line end of the room with her back to the pump and had not turned round.

"**That is the correct use of the word and it is not how an office works, and I want it entered that I have been nineteen years at a counter and I have never once seen it done and I could not have told you it was possible,**" she said, to the pump. "**And I have been in this building eight weeks and I did not ask him to do it and he was not asked by me and he had read that sheet, and the reason he did it is that he is a man who reads things, and I am not going to sit in a room and pretend that is a virtue, because a man who reads things is a man who can be found, and a man who can be found has a name and a description and a reason to write both down, and neither of those is anybody in this room.**"

"**And in a month it will be in a letter, and the letter will be about him and not about the form, and this room should know that is coming rather than find out in a month.**"

While he was giving it, the building went on being a building, and the room let it, which it does. Six people who are not this building's were in two rooms off the far end of the anteroom, past the pump, with both doors on and both latched, and one of the two rooms is in a page nobody in this room can read and none of the six of them knows that a sentence about a counter was said eleven feet above their heads on a Thursday. The school corner stood open at the top of nine steps with nothing in it and nothing put in it, the way it has stood since a door came off its head in a fortnight of the thirty-second week. The five men of a shoring crew were on the common landing with their own work and their own check, and the check had not crossed since the Wednesday of the thirty-seventh week, and nobody in this anteroom had asked them anything about it and nobody was going to.

The corner of the anteroom floor where a spreader came out of it was still open, and a hole a person puts a foot down into, and the ninth school morning in a month had gone round it eight days ago and would go round it tomorrow, and the dust of small feet was in the seam of it where the boards do not meet.

Bel Ordry came down the step, all the way, which he does perhaps twice in a week, and stood at the end of the trestle with his hands behind his back.

"**And the half against all of it, and it is the only half against there is going to be, so nobody should get comfortable. A head struck through a number is not a rule that has gone. The rule is standing. The office is right about the rule and always was. What has happened is that one number on one sheet has a line through it, and a number is printed, and the next quarter somebody prints that number again with no line on it, and the next form comes down that passage with a fresh second line in it, and this room will be standing exactly where it is standing this morning with the same two reasons and no way to use either of them.**"

"**And the reason it worked this morning was a person, and it was a person of twenty-two years' standing who had never been asked to do it and did it in four seconds, and that is a person and not a rule, and a person can be moved, and a person can be put on a rota, and a person can be told by somebody above him that heads on sheets are not his to strike.**"

He put one hand flat on the trestle, an inch from the near edge of the sheet.

"**So write down what it cost and not what it won. It cost a man at a counter who did not agree to anything, and it is going to cost him, and this room is not going to be able to put that in a column either, and it is the same blank page.**"

Dalla Sen asked for the book and wrote for most of a bell.

"**Entered as a finding with a reason beside it, which is the first one of those in my book since I came to it, and I am entering the reason in the space where a reason goes because the reason belongs to this one and does not belong to the other three.**" She turned the slate over and put it face down. "**And I am entering that neither of those two forms has a word written on it by anybody in that office, and that the whole of the reason this boundary has for what it did is sitting in this room in a foreman's mouth, and that a sentence said out loud in a room is not a sentence, and that it became one because a man stood at a counter and said it again and was not ashamed enough to stop.**"

"**And the half against that, which is mine, and it is short.**" Bel Ordry had not moved. "**That is a sentence in a room that has now travelled, and I have spent thirty-eight weeks in this building explaining why sentences in rooms do not travel, and one did, and I would like somebody to write down that I said that and said it kindly.**"

Nobody joined the two forms to the wall in the party wall. Dalla Sen entered that it had been possible, and entered the time of day at which it had been possible, and gave no reason.

Then the cook was in the kitchen-line doorway with the cloth over her arm, and she stood in the doorway and not on the step, and she stayed there for the rest of it.

"**One day,**" said Mikka Hale. "**It was two on Friday and it is one this morning and I did not decide that and I want that entered first. The seam gave nothing on fourteen mornings running and it is at four threads, and I have been asked more times than I can be bothered to count to be careful with that seam, and the answer is the same every time, which is that I do not know. And seventeen people eat out of this pot and six of them are not this building's, and I have said the arithmetic out loud in that room so many times that a number said out loud in a room has stopped meaning anything, and I would like it entered that I noticed that this week and that I am not stopping.**"

"**Entered, and entered that she was not asked,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then Tams Quill, from the end of the bench, with the piece of chalk he has not used.

"**A nineteenth entry,**" he said, unprompted, and then, before the clerk had the chalk up, "**No. And the seventh reason is not for this morning, and she does not get to ask me for it in the same breath as two lines through two numbers. A room that has got what it wanted this morning is not going to get a column out of me before the sixth bell.**"

Dalla Sen put the chalk down.

"**Entered, and entered that I had it up,**" she said, "**and entered that I put it down, and entered that a man who has refused six things on six grounds said no to a seventh before I was able to ask and gave no reason and that nothing was manufactured here in front of me, and entered that the two of those facts came within about four seconds of each other on a shut morning and that a person in a year would like to know it.**"

Then the room got to the thing the day was for, and nobody called it and nobody arranged it, and it happened at the fourth bell with the pump between strokes.

Iven Tallo set the chisel down on the anvil and left it there, and kept his left hand open and flat on the iron, and looked at the wall and not at anybody in the room.

"**On the fourth day of last month I counted four steps in a wall,**" he said. "**I have been in front of that join every working morning for a month with my hands on the stone at about the height of my chest, and I found four places along the length of it where the face on the kitchen-line side steps back about a hand's width and then comes out again. Four. And a wall that steps back four times is not a wall. It is four rooms with about nine feet of stone between them, and I have counted them twice since and I got four both times, and how I count them is mine, and if somebody asks me I will say that it is my trade and that it is a carpenter's answer and not a survey.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And I am not asking what is in them,**" he said. "**I have not asked this room what is in that gap once in a month and I am not starting this morning, and I have watched four people in this building come close to asking it and go somewhere else, and the fifth of those four people will not be me, and I would like it entered that I have said so before I asked what I did ask.**"

Then he gave the reason he was asking, in his own mouth, which nobody had asked him for and which he had plainly been carrying for a fortnight.

"**I was handed a reason on a Thursday for turning down a wage on the first and the fifteenth and a boy off a roll,**" he said. "**And I have had a fortnight with it and I have not worked out what it is. That is the truth and it is a fortnight old. And the part of it I have not worked out is whether a thing can be kept by somebody who is not the person it belongs to, and that is not a question about a wage and it is not a question about a boy, and it is a question about a wall, and I am the only man in this room who has spent his life on walls and has never once owned one.**"

He put his hand flat on the anvil.

"**So here is the question, and I am asking it out loud in a room with people in it, and it is said once. Who is keeping them.**"

The pump started and got four strokes and stopped, and the room did not use one of them.

Nobody answered it.

Bel Ordry did not come down the step again.

"**I am going to say the half against the question and not an answer to it,**" he said, "**and I want that distinction entered before I say anything else, because in this building the two get confused about once a week and this is the week it would have been expensive.**"

"**A man asked this room a question in the thirty-first week,**" he said. "**It was four words, and it was *who owns a wall*, and it came up nine steps in a survey camp foreman's own mouth because he was the only person in this district whose whole trade is keeping a thing that is not his, and nobody answered him, and he said that was all right, and he went up the passage on the Monday of the week after with three of his own and the fir from his store.**"

"**That was eight weeks ago and nobody has answered it, and nobody in this room should pretend that the question a carpenter asked this morning is a different question because it has four rooms in it. It is the same question with a carpenter in it. I will not answer it either, and I am the man in this building most likely to be able to, and I have been asked twice this month by people in offices whether this boundary owns its own walls and I have said no both times and both times it was true and neither time it was the question.**"

Then Mero Senn said the sentence, once, out loud, and then again in a different shape, which is what he does.

"**A plan with a named injured person in it is not a plan,**" he said, from the doorway with his bag against the wall. "**And a question with a named room in it is not a question, it is the first line of a plan, and the first line of that plan is written by the man who found out how many rooms are behind a wall, and every person in this room knows how his voice sounds when he has found out something, and it is not the voice he is using now.**"

Then Renn Vale came down all four steps and stood in the middle of the floor with the hole in the corner at her feet, and she did not go near the wall.

"**I am the person in this room whose whole job is a question about whether a place is a room,**" she said, "**and I have been asked about that place four times in four weeks and I have said nothing four times, and this morning I am going to say one thing and then stop.**"

"**If there are four rooms on the other side of that join then this building has five ways out of itself and not two, and I said the number out loud in this room seventeen days ago and put it on nothing, and a number of ways out of a room that a room has heard and not objected to is a drawing. So the first cost of that carpenter's question is that somebody in this room is going to have to draw something, and I have spent thirty-eight weeks making sure that nobody in this room is anybody's draughtsman.**"

Then Ilsa Norr came up the nine steps from the kitchen-line end with her slate in both hands, and she stopped at the fourth one and did not come further.

"**The condition stands,**" she said, from the fourth step. "**The first of us to stop agreeing takes it out, and neither of us has to give a reason first, and it has been unexamined since the fourth day of the thirty-sixth week, and the man who put the brace there is in this room and he is the man who asked the question, and I am not asking him anything and nobody in this room is going to ask him anything, and I would like the clerk to enter that the two of those facts are next to each other on purpose.**"

Nobody asked him anything.

Then Sefu Aran came down two of the nine steps, which is as far as he has come in a month, and stood there.

"**I am the elected keeper of this boundary and my instrument is admission and a crossing,**" he said, "**and a question about who keeps four rooms on the other side of a wall is neither of those, and the answer is not coming out of me, and I will not pretend I could not have given it, because I could. I have been the second person to hear that sound and I did not find out what it was and I entered that I did not.**"

"**And the half against me not answering is this,**" and he did not come down. "**A keeper who declines to spend his instrument on a question asked in a room is a keeper who is waiting for a better room, and there is no better room coming, and I have been on a road long enough to know exactly what it is to be away when a thing is decided, and I am not going to pretend that is a reason for anything except that I know it.**"

Then Nera Oduya came down all three steps, which she does for air and for nothing else, and stood at the bottom of them with the dyed cloth folded in her hand and not out.

"**I do not have half of it this time,**" she said, and she put the folded cloth down on the bottom step where it has lain all morning.

Nobody filled in the rest of the sentence for her and nobody asked her to.

"**I have said *I have half of it* in this building three times across two of its years and I gave half of it once and kept the other half twice, and this morning I have none of it and not a part of it, and that is the first time it has happened and I am telling the room because somebody in it will otherwise assume I am about to, and I would rather be known to have nothing than be left holding something in front of seventeen people.**"

"**I have four numbers and not a fifth this morning,**" she said, "**and the fifth stays in my mouth, and my first condition is not met and my second condition is not met and nobody has asked me about either of them and nobody is going to this morning, and I am not going anywhere, and I have not said any of those five things to make anybody feel better about the sixth.**"

Nobody asked her.

Then the man with the shut book under his forearm spoke, and he had not spoken all morning, and what he said was the shortest thing anybody said in that anteroom and it took the room about four strokes of nothing to get used to it.

"**I know it for two of them,**" Marek Venn said.

The anteroom did not fill in after it.

"**Which two of them is mine and not this room's, what I know is mine, and how I know it is mine, and all three of those refusals are the same refusal and I am going to give the reason for it in one sentence and then that reason is not given again this month.**"

He put the hand that does not close flat on the shut book.

"**Saying it in a room is making a claim. A claim in this building is a drawing, and a drawing is a plan, and a plan has a person in it, and I have spent thirty-eight weeks refusing plans and the first one will not be mine.**"

He did not put it down then. He stood there with his hand on it a while longer, and what he was thinking about was not the wall.

It was the fourth morning at that counter, and a man of twenty-two years' standing who had read a piece of paper, and four seconds, and a line through a number. This boundary has spent thirty-eight weeks refusing to have a figure of what is inside one of its own rooms put into a piece of paper, and it paid for that refusal in one morning in the twenty-ninth week, in a cook's arithmetic about a pot, out loud in a room, and it went on refusing and the price is permanent and the only figure anybody can find is a ten on a stranger's sheet. **And this morning an office took a figure off a piece of paper.** A figure of a man, with a name on it and a description and a reason to write both down, and it was correct, and the man never agreed to be a figure.

For three weeks he had stood in rooms in this building refusing an arrangement that would have put his hours in a line with a heading over it, and he had been proud of the refusing in front of people, and the refusing and this morning are the same instrument, and this morning it went through a number on a corner in four seconds without asking him.

And there was no blank page in that building at the counter. There was a clerk, and a rule, and a second line, and the second line was full, and the whole of what this building has spent thirty-eight weeks protecting is the emptiness of a second line, and it has been emptied out somewhere else by a person who never met him and does not know he exists.

"**The half against that,**" he said, before anybody could, "**and it is the only part of this morning that is mine. I have wanted to be the man who found out since the second week of this building, and the whole of what I have wanted since the second week is to be a man in a room who says a true thing and is believed, and this is the first morning in thirty-eight weeks where saying it would have worked, and it would have worked because a carpenter asked the question and not me, and a man's question is not the place for my answer, and I would like a clerk to enter that I noticed that the reason I was not going to answer was the best possible reason and that I am not sure it was the true one.**"

"**And the other half, which is worse, and which nobody is going to like either.**" He had not moved his hand off the book. "**This morning this room got what it wanted and somebody four days up a passage is going to be named in a letter, and I have been in this building five weeks telling a room that a person who can be found is not a keeper, and I am the man in this room who cannot be found, and I have never once asked myself what that costs the people who can.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he did not soften the second half,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then the clerk was asked, and she had been waiting for it since the fourth bell, and she said so before she answered it.

"**Somebody asked me at the fourth bell to enter the question and the three things in my book from Sunday in the same place on the same page,**" she said, "**and I said no, and I am going to say why and then I am going to stop.**"

"**A question is not a finding and a finding is not a question, and putting them on one page makes a third thing out of them that is neither, and a third thing is the only kind of thing anybody has ever sent up a passage to an office. A week ago I was asked to put two questions next to each other and I would not, and they are next to each other in my book by accident and not by hand, and this morning I have been asked a third time in a fortnight and I have said no, and I am entering that I have been asked twice in a month to put two things next to each other and have refused twice, and that the two refusals are mine, and that they are not the same refusal, and that the second one was easier, and I do not know why, and that is as far as it goes.**"

"**And entered,**" she said, "**that a carpenter asked a question in this room at the fourth bell on a shut morning and that seventeen people were in it, and that nobody answered it, and that I am entering it in the shape of a question and not in the shape of a finding, and that two seals came off two numbers this month and a foreman asked a question out loud in the same week, and that neither of those two things is in the same book as the other one, and that a person in a year is welcome to read all four of those lines and work out for themselves whether anything was answered.**"

Nobody said a word about the man who had asked it.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a man at the end of the bench had refused a seventh thing an hour ago and unprompted and a clerk does not walk a column in the same morning as that.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and she read them by themselves, with nothing else in the sentence and nothing else in the room.

"**Two today and three hundred since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and seventeen and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

She gave the reason out loud, once. A room had a question in it that nobody had answered, and a total read over an unanswered question is a clerk telling the room that the morning is over, and this morning is not over, and there is a rule about not putting a day on a morning and the rule applies to this one too.

And then she read the count, and she read it by itself, after a pause, out loud, and nobody in that room had asked her to.

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and forty-five days and one hundred and forty-five nights. It is not going to be one hundred and forty-six. I have been promising this room for forty-four weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in forty-four weeks, and I have said out loud in most of those weeks that it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and this morning she left it out, because a carpenter has asked a question in this room and I would rather my last sentence of that kind belonged to somebody who had said four words and got nothing.**"

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book. The reason is that a room asked a question at the fourth bell and got no answer, and a crew that is owed nothing is not the instrument for answering a question. Asking five men to go and look at something nobody can name would be putting the question somewhere it can be found, and this boundary has spent thirty-eight weeks refusing to do that with anything.

The pot is one day, and the seam is at four threads and gave nothing on fourteen mornings running, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for fifteen weeks. The four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and there is no figure on this building's anteroom either and there is not going to be one.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor, and a trestle with a book on it that had three pages turned forward in it and three empty spaces in it, and two sheets of paper on it with a line through a number in the corner of each. There was a row of packs along the bench belonging to five men, and at the end of the row a gap of about a foot where a sixth pack had stood a fortnight ago. The corridor is shut and it opens tomorrow, and nine children will cross it and go round the corner of the floor without being told to.

Behind the party wall there were five and a half shores, a spreader under the head of the sill, a sixth place with nothing in it, a brace of a school's own beech, a bad repair on one face only, and a keeper's nine. On the other side of that join, if a carpenter was right, there were four rooms, and nothing else anybody in this building could say about them. On the far side of the two rooms off the far end of this anteroom past the pump there were six people who are not this building's and a door that latches. In the pocket of a woman on the fourth step there was a piece of slate with a condition on it and no number and no day, and in the belt of a woman from a counter there was another one.

A man at the anvil end was putting his chisel away and would not be thanked, and nobody was going to try. A man with a shut book under his forearm knew the answer for two of the four and had given a reason in one sentence and was not going to give it again this month. Four days up a middle passage, a man of twenty-two years' standing who had read a piece of paper was doing the ordinary work of an ordinary morning, and in a month a letter would come down that passage with his name at the top of it, and this room had known that on the first morning and had put it in a book and had no way to put it anywhere else.

And the room went on being the room it is: a hole in the corner of its floor, a corridor that opens tomorrow, two lines through two numbers on a trestle, and a question somebody asked out loud in it in the fourth bell of a shut Thursday that nobody in it has answered.
