# Chapter 194 — The Fact That Cannot Be Chased

The second day of the thirty-seventh week is a Tuesday, and a shut Tuesday is nine steps, one pump, and two hundred and eighty-three hours of work in a clerk's book since the day a keeper signed one. The school did not run and lost nothing. The packs of the six who are not this building's are still in a row along the anteroom bench where a crew that is not working puts them, and nobody has moved them, and a room notices that and does not comment on it.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell and it was a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the sixteenth reading, the same as fifteen, and the string was where the string was. She kept the string in her hand for the rest of the morning and used it on nothing, which two people noticed and neither of them mentioned.

Renn Vale came down the four steps at the second bell, which is her bell on a shut day, and put her ear against the party wall on this side of it and was there about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and did not improve on it.

Then Bel Ordry came down the step and looked at the shelf above the anteroom bench, and at the coat on the hook by the door, and said the thing he says about those two objects once every so often, and this time a clerk wrote it down.

"**Twenty-eight weeks,**" he said, "**for the brass, and thirty-seven for the plate, and I have been counting them out of a calendar that this building does not keep and that I keep myself, and I am the only person in this room with that number and it is a number about a coat.**"

"**Then the two of them were looked at,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and found in the same state, and neither of them came into a room this morning.**"

"**They come into a room every morning,**" said Bel Ordry. "**They are in it. They just do not come into it with a sound.**"

Then the arrangement of last week was used, because there was a thing in the anteroom for it to be used on, and the thing was a length of the school's own beech that had been left on the trestle since the flag stone went in and had got damp on one end.

"**That is my timber,**" said Iven Tallo from the anvil end, with the chisel in his right hand and his left open and flat on the anvil, and he did not put the chisel down, which is what he does when he is going to say a thing he means.

"**And my five read it,**" said Emrys Callow, "**because the check crosses. And I am going to read it out loud in this room, the way we agreed, and I am going to say what I can tell and what I cannot, and the part I cannot is going to be said in the same breath and not after.**"

One of his five put both hands flat on the beech and rocked it once and got a sound out of it that everybody in the anteroom could hear, and named the two ends of it and the length of it out loud in his own mouth, and then said that the damp end was soft to about the width of a thumbnail and that he was not a man who read timber and that he was telling them what his thumb knew and nothing else.

"**Which is a different thing from what I know,**" from the bench where his packs were, "**and the difference is the whole reason we are both standing in this room. My five checked your timber and I cannot check your timber, and the second of those is not a smaller version of the first, and I am not letting this room hear it as one.**"

"**Somebody who reads timber has to,**" from the fourth step, without moving off it.

"**Nobody is going to be asked,**" from the anvil end, "**because the woman who reads walls is the one who could, and I am not going to ask her twice in a fortnight by two people on the same morning, and the last time I said no to a term somebody wrote it in a book and it is still in there.**"

"**Entered, in his words,**" said Dalla Sen, and she wrote the two hours on one line and did not look for a second line, and then she stopped with the chalk a half inch above the page and put it down again.

"**That,**" from the step.

"**Nothing went wrong,**" and she turned the slate round so that the two lines were facing up and nothing was on them. "**I was going to write the second line. It is there, it is under no heading, it is where it has been since the fifth day of last week, and my hand went to it, and I have been not adding two numbers up for eight days and I have just felt my own arm reach for it, and I would like that entered as a thing that happened to my hand and not as a thing I did.**"

"**Entered, in the clerk's words,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Entered, in mine,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the two lines are still under no heading and that nobody has added them up and that this morning the clerk nearly did it herself.**"

Nella Bray came in at the second bell from the kitchen-line doorway with her slate under her arm, and she came all the way into the room, which she has done four times in a fortnight, and she stopped at the trestle and put her slate on it and did not sit down.

"**Then I am going to say the thing I came in to say and I am going to say it before the morning gets any further,**" she said. "**Emrys. You have now not taken the middle gate's work for eleven days. Not four. Eleven. Six weeks of paid shoring on a face that has let go, at a district rate, more than those five have had in nine months, and it does not run out, and you have been turning it down in this room rather than turning it down anywhere it can be recorded.**"

"**I know what I have been doing,**" said Callow.

"**Then you know there is a difference between a man who has not decided and a man who is declining to decide in a room with a clerk in it,**" said Nella Bray. "**And I want to tell you what the difference costs, because I have spent nineteen years on the other side of it and nobody has ever told me. A man who has not decided gets asked again. A man who is visibly not deciding gets a visit. And a visit is a man in a doorway with a form, and the form has a column, and the column has a heading, and the heading has a person under it, and that person is you.**"

"**Then I have been putting my own name in a column for eleven days,**" said Callow.

"**You have been refusing to give an office a place to put your name,**" said Nella Bray, "**which is the same instrument played backwards and it is not the same act, and in my experience the office cannot tell the difference either.**"

"**Then it is a bad instrument,**" from the step, "**and the half against both of you is mine, and I am going to say it before the room decides anything. This boundary has spent thirty-seven weeks refusing to put a number of what is in it into a book, and a man has now spent eleven days refusing to put a yes or a no into a book, and those two refusals are the same refusal, and a room that has learned one of them is a room that will learn the other, and the second one is going to be harder to unlearn than the first.**"

"**And the other half,**" and she stayed on the step she was on, "**which is that I have watched him not decide and I have watched her ask him and I have not said a word, and I am saying it now. I did not say it because a foreman who is owed work and a woman who works at a counter do not need this building to referee it. And that was true, and it was also convenient, and I do not know which of those two things I was doing.**"

"**I have no answer to that, in this room or anywhere else,**" said Callow, "**and the reason is that I do not have one. I stopped looking for one on the fourth day and I have not started again.**"

"**Then the clerk enters that,**" said Nella Bray. "**She enters a man who has said no in a room without a reason in it. I want you to understand that this is the hardest kind of fact to argue with at a counter. A no with a reason is a no anybody can weigh. A no without a reason is a shape, and a shape is what an office fills in.**"

"**And it is the only shape in this building that has ever been worth keeping,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Then somebody had better keep it,**" and she did not look at her slate while she said it, "**and I would rather it was kept by a person than by me with a slate.**"

Nera Oduya was on the second of the three steps and stayed on it, and she came down that far at the start of every working morning for the air and for nothing else that anybody has ever been able to name.

"**Two facts and neither of them is a request,**" she said. "**The first is that the air at the top of nine steps on an open morning and the air at the foot of them on a shut morning are not the same air, and they have not been the same air since the fourth day of the twenty-ninth week, and I have two rates for them and I have never put either rate in a column. The second is that this building has two ways out of it, and it has had two since before I came into it, and nobody has ever asked me to certify the air in either of them, and a room with two ways out of it is a room with two airs in it, and two airs is a thing a person looks after rather than a thing a person keeps.**"

"**I am not asking for anything,**" before anybody in the room had opened its mouth, "**and nobody is going to ask me about either of those on this morning, and if a room makes a decision about ways out of this building I would like it entered that the air at the bottom of nine steps was not part of the decision.**"

Nobody asked her. The clerk entered that she had said two facts and had said in front of the room that nobody was going to ask her about either, and entered that the clerk had not asked, and entered that she had noticed herself wanting to and had not. She was not giving the noticing a number, because she gave it one once and found the number had become the thing she was keeping instead of the thing she was keeping a number of.

Then the clerk read the six terms out loud, because a room had a stranger's business in it on a Monday and a clerk does not carry that into a Tuesday unsaid, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and the pump was attended.

And then Nella Bray sat down, which she has not done in this room, and put her slate on her knee, and gave the morning the thing she had been carrying since the first bell and had not put down.

"**The message is still up the passage.**"

Nobody said anything, because everybody in the room knew that and had known it since Sunday.

"**I want to say what happens to it,**" she said, "**and I want to say it as a fact about a counter and not as an opinion about this boundary, because I have no opinion about this boundary. At the counter a message that has not come back does not wait. It waits until somebody chases it, and the chasing is done by a person, and a person who chases is a person whose hours go in a book with a heading over them, because chasing is work and work is entered. And there is no page in this building where that goes, and the page at the low end is blank, and it is not blank for want of a heading over it, it is blank because it carries three conditions, and the third of those three is that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make a thing true, and chasing a message is making a thing true by a person with hours on a page.**"

"**So it is not coming back,**" from behind the trestle.

"**It is going to come back when somebody from here goes and stands in front of the person it is addressed to,**" said Nella Bray, "**and that is the only mechanism any counter in this district has, and I have watched it work on a form about a bakehouse and it worked in about nine days.**"

"**And nobody is going to be sent,**" said Renn Vale.

"**So the true fact about five and a half where a district's book says six is in nobody's book,**" from the step, and slowly, in the voice of a man reading a line off a counter himself.

"**It is in a book,**" said Nella Bray. "**Four days up that passage, in the district, in a hand that belongs to a man who does not work for this boundary, and the day of the week is on it because a day of the week is what a foreman can stand behind and a date is not. Whether anybody ever reads it is a different question, and I have not seen a room in nineteen years confuse the two, and this one has confused them since the third week, and that is the only criticism I have of it.**"

"**And the sheet with the line for the date it was observed,**" said Dalla Sen, and her hand went towards the chalk and stopped there.

"**That is a different fact, and your hand is already going towards the chalk, and the two of them do not sit next to each other,**" said Nella Bray.

"**They stay apart,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and which way that day was chosen is a question I am not asking this week either. Last week I decided not to find out and I entered that a decision of that kind is the first one in thirty-seven weeks I cannot take back. And it is a second week now and I have not asked, and I have noticed that the reason I have not asked is no longer a decision. It is a thing I do now. And I do not know which of those two is worse, and I have entered that I do not know, and the space where a reason goes is where that goes and not a column.**"

Then Renn Vale came down all four steps, and stood in the middle of the anteroom floor, and put her hands flat on the trestle on either side of the hole in the corner of it.

"**Then I am going to make a decision this morning and I am going to put the cost of it in the room before I make it,**" she said, "**and the cost is the whole of what this boundary has got, which is nothing. We cannot ask anybody for anything, because asking is a page. We cannot pay anybody, because there is no money and the only wages in this building are food and a bed and hours entered as work. We cannot date anything, because a date is a day somebody chose. And we cannot put a name in a line, on any paper, in any book, in any room, which is the thing this boundary has refused three times and once this week with a good reason.**"

"**And having said all of that,**" and she put her thumbs in the belt of her apron, "**here is what I am deciding. This building is going to stop waiting. We are not going to wait for the timber, we are not going to wait for the iron, we are not going to wait for a man from a district to come and read a true fact about five and a half. And we are not going to do that because the waiting has stopped being free, which is the half of the reason Bel Ordry has been standing on that step for a week, and I am going to say his half better than he would have said it.**"

"**The rest of it,**" from the step, and he did not come down for it this time.

"**A boundary that stops waiting for an answer is a boundary that has decided the answer does not matter, and the answer matters, and the true fact about five and a half shores is either in a district's book or it is nowhere, and it is in nobody's.**" He came down one step, which is twice in two days and is not a thing he does. "**And I would like a clerk to enter that I have said that about a decision I was not asked about, before the decision was made, because if I wait until after it I will have lost the nerve and because a man who only finds the objection afterwards is a man who is managing the room.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he gave it before the decision rather than after it,**" and she wrote it down while he was still standing on the step.

"**And the decision has a shape and the shape is this,**" said Renn Vale. "**Not a plan. A plan has sixteen rows and a date in the top corner and no name anywhere and it does not leave this building, and I have kept it for thirty-seven weeks, and it is not being used for this. What is going to be made is two ways to be out of a room, and each of them held by one person, and each condition written in the hand of the person who is going to hold it, and no order on either of them.**"

"**Two,**" from the step, and he did not come down for it.

"**Two,**" she said, and she did not go up her steps again. "**And not one word more about either of them until somebody has said the cost of two, out loud, in a room. That is not today and it is not from me.**"

She went back up her four steps and stopped on the second one, which is where she stops, and looked down at the anteroom for a while.

"**And the reason it is two and not one,**" and she stayed on the step she was on, "**is that this building has two ways out of it, and I found that out yesterday morning in a room with a hole in the corner of the floor, and I have been thinking about it since, and I have not slept, and I am not going to be the only one awake on it.**"

Mikka Hale came to the doorway with the cloth over her arm.

"**Three days,**" from the doorway, "**and the seam gave me nothing yesterday and nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and I have been asked four times in a fortnight by four people to be careful with that seam and four times the answer has been the same, which is that I do not know. And there is a second jar in the frame this morning, entered separately, and it does not go into the pot, and it has been entered separately for the same reason every jar has been entered separately since the nineteenth week, and nobody has ever asked me for the reason, and nobody is asking today.**"

"**Nobody is going to ask you for the reason today,**" said Renn Vale, "**and I want that entered as a kindness and not as a practice, because it is a kindness, and because in a fortnight somebody is going to ask and I would like it on the page that the first time was a kindness and not a habit.**"

Tams Quill read the board at the sixth bell, and it is at eighteen entries, and he gave the eleven that are nothing first and then the seven that are not, which are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and there are nine in the ordinary column and nine in the one with nothing over the top of it. He gave no reason for the column, and she did not ask him for one, and she entered that he had given none and that she had not asked, and that this was the second morning running on that entry, and that a clerk who kept a figure of how often a man says nothing would end up keeping the figure instead of the nothing.

At the sixth bell she entered the day's figure and the total and read neither of them, and entered the reason, and the reason was that a room had a decision coming and a man had gone eleven days without answering a question, and a clerk who reads a total out loud over both of those is handing her morning to a person who is not in it.

Two today and two hundred and eighty-three since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath a foreman's two hours and above a clerk's own two lines, and the clerk wrote it in without being asked to, which she has not done on a day when a room was arguing since the twenty-ninth week. The reason she gave out loud, once, to the anvil end, was that a room which has spent the morning refusing to put a number of itself in a book ought at least to have the one number it keeps written down in the ordinary way, in the ordinary place, by the ordinary person, in case the ordinary person is not there tomorrow.

Wen Pask is one hundred and twenty-nine days and one hundred and twenty-nine nights. It is not going to be one hundred and thirty. That promise is thirty-eight weeks old as a thing said out loud, kept every morning, and not one thank for any of them, and she wrote it and turned the page, and that is the whole of what the thirty-eighth week did for a man nobody has ever thanked.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book. A foreman has spent eleven days without answering a question in public and a room has spent a morning refusing a plan, and neither of those is a condition under which five people can be handed a piece of work with the terms out loud, and a clerk who cannot say which of the two reasons is the real one has two reasons and therefore no reason.

The pot is three days, the seam gave nothing on two mornings running, the four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and six packs along a bench and a length of the school's own beech on the trestle with a soft end and a hard end. A woman who keeps a corridor was two steps up a stair with her hand on the rail, not asleep, not going to bed. A man who keeps a page was on the step with his hands behind his back, looking at a chalk line on the anteroom table that has been under two documents since the fifth day of the thirty-fourth week with nothing written under it. Neither of them said anything, and the clerk entered that neither of them had said anything, and it was nearly the first true thing she had entered all day.
