# Chapter 112 — The Head Of The Frame

**The second day of the twenty-second week was the day Marek Venn did the thing he had said out loud in a room on the fourth day of last week, in the anteroom, with nine people in it and the corridor shut behind them, and there was no bell for it.**

**The anteroom was emptied at the second bell for the second time in two days,** and Jo Verrick said what she thought of that before anybody asked her.

"**That is a habit now. I do not like habits on a fill, and that is not the fill, it is a floor, and I do not like habits on a floor either. I have emptied this room twice in two days and both times it was a good reason, and one good reason is how a bad habit starts.**"

They went out in the strokes, four at a time and the fifth on Isel Ferrow's shoulder, and Dalla Sen counted at both ends and said the number out loud at each end, and the room was empty, and the pump went on.

Then Marek Venn said the thing.

"**I said in this room on the fourth day of last week that there was one thing left that was not a reading and that I was not going to do it, and the clerk wrote down that she did not understand the second half of it and was not going to pretend she did, and she was right not to, and I am not going to explain it now either.**

"**I have been not doing it since the fourth day of last week. I have decided on and off, mostly off, and I want the room to know that, because a man who says he has been thinking about a thing sounds braver than a man who says he has been arguing with himself, and it was the second one.**"

"**Say what the thing is,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps, in the flat voice she keeps for things that are not about air.

"**Not yet.**"

"**Then say it in the order it has to be said in,**" said Renn Vale, "**because I keep this room's terms and I am not going to be handed a thing at the fifth bell.**"

---

**So they went and looked at the marks first, which is the oldest piece of business in this building and the reason the new one was possible.**

**The shut school corner's door is at the school-door end of the corridor,** and above its head on the lintel there are four marks and a groove, read two ways for three weeks by two people who will not say what they are.

Marek Venn and Renn Vale stood in front of them with a lamp before the anteroom was emptied, because he was not going to put a mark on a stone in this building without having looked at the marks already on one, and Dalla Sen wrote that down as the reason.

"**Four,**" said Renn Vale. "**And the broken one and the groove, and that is what I have always said and I am not going to say what they are.**"

"**I read the broken one as two lines and a gap,**" said Nera Oduya, who had come down four steps and a bit more, "**and she reads it as a mark and a number, and I am not going to change my reading because we are about to make one.**"

"**Nobody is going to change anything,**" said Renn Vale. "**But I am going to say the one thing about them I have not said, and I am saying it because there is a man about to make a mark on the other side of this corridor and he should have to sit with it.**

"**Three weeks I have been unable to tell you what those are, and not because I cannot read them. A mark a hundred years old tells you what somebody needed once, and a mark ten minutes old tells you what somebody needs now, and I cannot hold both of those in my head at once, and I did not think I would ever have to.**"

Marek Venn did not say anything to that for a while, and then he said: "**What is the groove.**"

"**About two inches long, and something has been put in it and taken out of it more than once, and the edges of it are not chipped and stone does not do that without a tool.**"

"**Then somebody held something in that groove,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and took it away, and came back and held something in it again. And it told them nothing. That is all I will say about it.**"

Jo Verrick put her rule against the jamb beside the marks, looked, and put it away, and Dalla Sen entered in the clerk's book the one sentence that had to go in that day, which was not about the frame at all.

"**A mark will hold on anchorstone, and there is none in this Hearthhold that we can reach, and what we have is dressed stone, which is not the same thing and is not nothing either.**"

---

**Then the anteroom was emptied and Marek Venn said the rest of it, standing in a room with a lamp in it, and the room did not argue, and the thing it argued about was not whether.**

"**I have said in this building that anybody who comes and asks me to read a boundary is going to be told no. That has not changed and it is not what I am doing, and if anybody in this room cannot tell the difference between those two sentences then I would rather be asked a question now than in a month.**

"**A mark is a different act from a reading. A reading tells me what a room is doing. A mark tells a seam which way to carry a small amount of what it is already carrying, and it has one direction and it has a limit, and I have to say the limit out loud in a room afterwards, and if I cannot say it then I am not allowed to do it at all.**"

"**Who said that,**" said Mikka Hale.

"**Nobody. I am saying it, because it is the only rule I have and it is mine and it is two months old, and I am telling you the whole of it because that is what this room does and because I would rather be stopped here.**"

"**Then I am going to stop you for about a minute,**" said Renn Vale, "**and you are going to let me, and then you are going to have the floor again, and both of those are going in the book.**"

She had the slate out and took her time.

"**A mark is a claim. I said that in a room about the ninth room and I refused to have one cut in that stone and I have not changed my mind about the ninth room and I am not going to be asked about the ninth room today.**

"**So here is my whole objection, and it is one sentence and then I am out of the way. A mark cut in a stone is a claim that outlasts the person who agreed to it. The stone does not know whose hand it was, and the next person along reads it and does not know there was a woman in this room with a slate in the fourteenth week saying no.**"

"**Agreed,**" said Marek Venn. "**Then it is not cut.**"

"**No.**"

"**Chalk and grease, on the dressed face of the jamb, at the height of a hand, in a place where anybody who comes through that door can see it. And when it comes off it comes off, and nobody has to read a groove to find out there was one.**"

Renn Vale stood with her hand on the slate for a while.

"**That is a smaller thing than you have just described and I want everybody to hear me say that I know it is.**" She took her hand off it. "**I agree to it. I keep this room's terms and I am the person who agreed, and I am going to write the condition in my own hand, with no number on it and no day on it.**"

---

**And then the air engineer did the thing that the rest of the twenty-second week is going to be about, and she did it in front of nine people and then entered it herself, which is not how she usually works.**

She came down the four steps and stood beside him at the jamb, and she did not touch the stone, and she said:

"**Say what the limit is.**"

"**It carries the head of this frame and the clay packed in it, and nothing else. And it carries it while the head is out of true. And if anybody puts a weight on that head that is not the clay, it comes off at once and there is no argument.**"

"**That is the shape of it and it is not the limit.**" She turned round to the room. "**He has just said *and nothing else*, and *nothing else* is not a number and not a weight, and in eleven days somebody in this room will need that frame to carry something and will hear a man say *nothing else* in a hurry and take it for permission. I have watched it happen to a pump.**

"**The limit is this. The head of that frame is carrying nothing, and that is the whole reason it is moving — it is a hole the clay has left and the head is the only soft thing in it. So the mark is not being asked to carry a load. It is being asked to carry the **absence** of one. And if the head ever takes a real weight — a stone, a beam, a man, a bar — then it is not holding, and everything I have said is out of date.**"

"**Write that,**" said Marek Venn, "**in your hand, and I will say it.**"

Nera Oduya did not move.

"**No. I am going to give you the sentence and you are going to say it, and I am going to tell the room that I have done that on purpose, and Dalla Sen is going to write down that I did it on purpose, and I want all three of those things to happen in that order.**"

Not one person in that room had asked her for anything, and in twenty-two weeks she had never once offered to hand a person a sentence to say.

"**Say why,**" said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up.

"**Because he put his hand on a wet wall in a schoolroom three weeks ago and got nothing, twice, and wrote it down both times, and the room has watched him be wrong in public and corrected in public, and that is why a room will believe a limit out of his mouth and not out of mine.**" She said it without any weight at all, the way she says a reading. "**And because a mark is only a mark as long as one person can say no to it. If that one person is the man who made it, the mark is a claim and Renn Vale is right and we have not got a mark. So the limit has to come out of my mouth, and out of a mouth that can stop the air gate, and be said by the man, because the room has to see the two of us in the same sentence.**"

Dalla Sen wrote: *the air engineer gave the sentence of the limit to the man to say, and gave the reason out loud, and the reason is that the room will take a limit from him and not from her, and that a mark whose only keeper is the man who made it is a claim and not a mark. Entered because she asked for it.*

"**I am not doing you a kindness,**" said Nera Oduya. "**I am doing the mark. If you say it, the mark has a keeper who is not you. If you do not say it, I have not said it either, and the room is holding a thing with one name on it.**"

"**Say the condition,**" Marek Venn said, "**and then I will say the limit.**"

---

**Renn Vale wrote it on a separate page, in her own hand, with the chalk, and read it out twice, and the clerk copied it and said her copy out against the original, which takes four minutes and is the way this room checks anything.**

"**The mark is on while the head of this frame is out of true. It is off when the head has not been further out of true than the line on this jamb, through a night and a morning, and I take it off and nobody argues with me about it.**

"**And there is no number on that and no day on that, and somebody told me three weeks ago what is wrong with that, when a man in this room said a condition you can satisfy is a date with a number on it. This is not that. A man can wake up on a Tuesday having done nothing and come back on Wednesday having done a great deal, and the line will be the line. It is a state and not a day, and it took me three weeks to be able to write one and have it be a condition.**"

"**Who can stop it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I can stop it. I agreed to it. And there is a second person who can stop it, standing four steps up with a strip of dyed cloth in her hand, and she can stop it because the air in that room comes in at the service door and leaves at the service door, and I am not having that frame argued about with grease while somebody carries a lamp and a bar through that door in a bad month. And the two of us can be stopped by each other.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I asked whether the second person had been asked, and she had not been, and she answered anyway, and that is entered.**"

"I did not answer *anyway*," said Nera Oduya. "**I answered because a frame is a wall and I keep the air. Say that properly, Dalla.**"

"Then it is entered that you answered because a frame is a wall and you keep the air," said Dalla Sen, and wrote it.

---

**He did it at the fourth bell, with his left hand, and it took him about nine minutes, and nobody helped him and nobody was asked to.**

Chalk first, for the line and the length of it, and then grease, which is the grease off the pump's gland and not a pencil and not a mark of a man, because there is nothing in this Hearthhold that would take the mark.

Marek Venn had the jamb, the lamp and his left hand, and he did not close his eyes, and the anteroom was full, and the sixth minute of the pump had gone some time before.

**And he did not see anything.** Dalla Sen entered that twice, in her own hand, because she wrote it, said it out, and made him say it again.

"**Enter it twice if you want,**" he said.

"**I am going to enter it twice, because in a month somebody is going to ask me what a man saw when he put a mark on a stone, and the true answer is nothing, and I want the nothing on the page in two places.**"

What it felt like was this. Under the pump's check, which he had learned four weeks ago to hear through, there is a sound that is not the pump — a low uneven thing, a counted sort of sound, like a thumb along the edge of a plate. It came up out of the frame's head while his hand was on it, and it was the anteroom's count arriving as a sound, and **he knew what it was**, and knowing it did not stop it and did not make it smaller and told him nothing useful at all.

**And the hand burned.** Not hot. Burning, the way it had burned on the night of the reading in the nineteenth week, and worse in the way that a thing you have had before is worse, because he knew the shape of it now.

**The grease went on.** His left hand stopped closing two-thirds of the way through and stayed like that, and he finished the last third with his palm and his thumb and his forearm, and nobody in that room said a word about it, and the clerk wrote down that nobody said a word about it.

Then he took his hand off, and the room was a room, and the mark was a line of chalk and a smear of gland grease on the dressed face of a jamb at the height of a hand, where anybody coming in through that door would see it.

"**Say the limit,**" said Renn Vale.

He said it in the air engineer's words, which he had not written down and had not been given on a page, and the room believed him, which is what the air engineer had said would happen and had arranged.

"**It carries the head of this frame and the clay packed in it and nothing else, and the head of that frame is carrying nothing, and the mark is being asked to carry the absence of a load. It holds that head where it is. If the head ever takes a real weight — a stone, a beam, a man, a bar — then it is not holding, and everything I have just said is out of date.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in the air engineer's words, said by him, and that she gave him the words and why.**"

---

**The sixth bell came, and the board, and the thirteenth thing on it, and it took a long time.**

Tams Quill came down with the chalk in his left hand and his right one open and flat in his lap, and read both columns, and named no day, and gave no reason for not naming one.

"**The crew are at fifty-three and a half, and I am not going to say why they moved yesterday because a clerk said it better in a room than I give it, and I am not saying it again this week. And a man of twenty-two is forty-seven days and forty-seven nights into a count that is his own, and I read it out because the sixth bell reads what is on the board and not what a room is feeling.**

"**The boundary is at a hundred and fifty-seven and a quarter, and that is four yesterday and three today, and forty-three since the keeper signed, and I have walked it and it adds, and both columns reconcile and they have reconciled for eight weeks.**"

Then he stopped with the chalk up, which he has done twice, and Dalla Sen said "**Go on,**" and he did not.

"**There is a thirteenth thing on this board. I want the room to know what it is before I put it down, and I want it to know that I have known it since the fourth bell and have been standing here a quarter of an hour.**"

"**Say it,**" said Marek Venn.

"**You put a mark on a stone at the fourth bell,**" said Tams Quill. "**And a mark is not a reading. I want that in the room's hearing, and in the clerk's hand, and in yours. You have not read anything. You have not looked at anything. You put grease on a jamb.**"

"**No,**" said Tams Quill, and put the chalk down against the board. "**And it is not a null either, because a null is a thing a man went and looked for and did not get, and you did not go and look for anything. And it is not the claim, and the claim is still a claim and its condition is still not met, and I have not moved it and I am not going to.**"

He wrote it on the board in his own hand, on the side with nothing over the top of it.

"**And I am not going to head that column, and that is five batches running, and I have got a worse one and I am not pretending I have not. A heading is a claim about what a column is for, and the day I can check it is the first day one of these does not fit. I have just put a ninth thing in it, and I can tell you what the ninth is and what the eighth is, and I cannot tell you the first and the second in one sentence without using the word *things*, and a man using that word at the ninth item has run out of column.**

"**So no heading. And in a month one of you will ask me again, and I will give you the same sentence, and it will be one item worse, and the day I cannot give you a reason at all is the day you should stop asking me and be frightened.**"

Nobody was frightened, and nobody asked.

"**Twelve entries were eleven nulls and one claim. It is now eleven nulls, one claim and one mark, and the arithmetic is nine in the ordinary column and four in the one with nothing over the top of it, and the mark is entered on its own terms and is not a null and is not the claim, and anybody who wants it moved into the ordinary column can come and move it themselves.**"

---

**After the sixth bell he sat on the anteroom step with that hand open on his knee and could not get it shut,** and the count was in the room again under the pump's check, and he knew what it was.

**The frame was not fixed.** It was a finger and a bit out of true and going a finger a week in the cold, and it was now being asked to stop by a line of chalk and gland grease that a man had put on it with a hand that could not close, with a condition written in another woman's hand and a limit written in a third woman's mouth and given to him to say.

**Nothing in this building is safer than it was this morning.** The shoring still holds and it is still stone and lime and one wedge out of seven. The packing is still clay. Nine children are two hundred feet away at the far end of a corridor that is shut today, and a school corner with a wet floor is shut with a wedge that came out of a school door, and the water in the wall of the room they used to sit in is the only drinking water in this Hearthhold.

**He said in a room that he was not going to do it, and he had not done it since, and he did it this afternoon for an afternoon and a hand.**

**The grease mark was on the jamb** where anybody coming through that door would see it, and it was going to be seen, and the woman who handed him the sentence had known that when she handed it over.
