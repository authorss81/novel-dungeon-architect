# Chapter 97 — The Charter

It was written on the back of a butcher's sheet in Mikka Hale's hand, it had an office's number at the top of it, and it had no name on it, and the reason it had no name was the first thing said in the room and the reason it was on butcher's paper at all was the second.

"**I am not going to write this on anything else,**" Mikka Hale said, "and the reason is that the last thing this boundary wrote that went out of the building was on a sheet of a schoolteacher's, and the thing before that was on nothing at all, and I have decided in a kitchen that a thing that is going to be read in a room by a stranger for four years ought to be on something you can scrub.

"And the reason there is no name on it is the keeper's and I am putting it in my own hand because I am the one holding the pen and I am not going to let a man argue me out of it in a room before anybody has read a line.

"**A charter with a name at the top of it is a charter with a holder. The whole of the last four days has been about that and I have been in this building nineteen weeks and I am the cook and I am the person who has to wash the thing off the board afterwards, and I am telling this room that the top of that sheet is going to be an office's number and nothing else, and the number is not a name, and I would like somebody to say that is correct.**"

"**That is correct,**" said Bel Ordry, "and I am not going to say it twice, and the clerk has it."

---

Dalla Sen read the whole of it out, and then the room did the thing that the release was done with and that this room has been practising for nineteen weeks: **it objected to it, out loud, line by line, before anybody picked up the chalk again**, and the objections are in the book in the hands of the people who made them, and the corrections are in five hands, and the thing that is on the table at the sixth bell is not the thing that was read out at the first bell.

"**One.** The way up the middle passage stays open to the people who live behind it, in two crossings, four and then two, every day in seven, at a walk, and **the gate is not to be held closed for a delivery.**"

---

"**Objection,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing. "**Four and then two is mine and it is a maximum and a maximum of four permits two, and I will not have it written as though it were a count of men that a stranger can add to.** Put *four at a time* and then put *and the second crossing is two*, because in a year somebody will read *four and then two* as a pair and this building has had a page read wrong at a counter once already."

Struck, and written in her own hand, and repeated.

"**Two.** No name is entered against a route in a register kept by the office, and this boundary will give none."

"**Objection,**" said Sefu Aran, from the wheel. "**Not from us. From the office. As it is written, it binds this boundary and binds nobody else, and a clause that binds the man who writes it and not the man he writes it about is the exact shape of the thing we are refusing.**

"Put it as: *no holder of a route in this Hearthhold is entered by the office against any work here, and this boundary will give no name for any work here, and the two are one clause and not two.*"

Struck, and written in the keeper's hand, and the clerk entered that a clause was rewritten by the man it bound and that this is the first time in nineteen weeks that a man has corrected a document against himself.

"**Three.** The reason, in full: the objects of care in this Hearthhold are a stair, a lip, a valve, and a woman's covers, and none of them can be entered against a man, and a name against a route is a letter forty days on from the top of this passage to a person alone on a stair."

Silence, and then the clerk said: "**That is the objection to a charter and I have read this room's refusals for nineteen weeks and that is the first time a refusal has been written into a thing that is going to be given to somebody.**"

"Yes," said Nera Oduya. "**And I want one word changed and I want to be the one who changes it and I want it in my hand.**

"*None of them can be entered against a man* is a claim about four things, and I know the three, and **the covers are not mine and the covers are not the boundary's, and there is a woman in this building who has put three conditions on paper and the third of them is that nobody is to be sent, and she has never been asked whether her covers may be used as an argument.**

"So take the covers out. **Three things: a stair, a lip and a valve.** And the reason still stands, and it stands better, because a valve is a handle and a stair is a hole and a lip is a body, and there is no version of a register that makes those three into a man's to hold."

Struck, and written in her own hand, and nobody argued, and Dalla Sen entered that the air engineer removed a thing from a document in order not to use a person who had not been asked, and that the room let her.

"**Four.** The office may see the maintenance plan once a season, in this boundary, in a room, to a person, and it is not carried out of the room, and the fourth column of it is not headed, and the reason for that is written on the plan itself."

"**Objection, and it is mine and it is the whole of what I have,**" said Bel Ordry. "**Once a season is a date and a date is a thing a man can be given a day for, and I would rather they came when they came and asked.**

"Put it: *when the office asks, in a room, to a person.* **And I want the second half in full, because the plan is mine and the fourth column is the reason I took it and it goes up a road with the thing:** *and the keeper will show the fourth column, and the fourth column is empty, and it has no heading, and it is true as at the day in the top corner, and the keeper may put something in it and the person who does it has to be in the room.*"

Struck, and written in the keeper's hand, and repeated.

"**Five.** The water-lock at the second gate is not this boundary's and is not claimed by it and is not discharged, and four households are carrying it, and this is named in the charter so that it is in a file and not only in a room."

"**Objection,**" said Mikka Hale. "**It is a water-lock and it is four households and it is not a thing to put in a charter that a kitchen hands out. And I am not going to have our own water handed up a road in a document about a door.**

"Take it out of the charter and put it in a second thing, in my words, in a hand that is not a clerk's, and let the two go up together, and the second thing says: *this boundary has found two jars a day out of its own wall and it is using them and it is not sending them, and the four households at the second gate have nothing, and we know it, and it is on the page.*"

Struck, and written in the cook's hand, and the clerk entered that the cook took a thing out of a legal instrument because it did not want to hand its own water to a stranger in a document about somebody else's, and that the sheet and the page now disagree by one number, and that the cook said she would rather they disagreed than that a document about a door should carry a number about somebody else's water.

"**Six.** No guild instrument may name a person against a route, and a guild bond does not become a licence over anything in this Hearthhold. **Seven.** Form 9-C stands unattended, and nothing in this touches the stair, and the stair is not a route under this charter and is not in it. **Eight.** The four hundred paces are released and the office's store order is void and nothing is owed by anybody. **Nine.** The ninth room, the four treads, the wash rooms, the school corner's mouth and the blank line are not in this charter, and that is deliberate."

"**I will say that last one out loud myself, because a charter that mentions a room is a charter about a room, and a document about a room travels,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I have been thinking about it for two days and I am not going to have a woman in a house four days up a road holding a page that names her covers in a clause about a door.**

"And I would like it read out that **this Hearthhold's ninth room has no row on a plan and a stranger's charter does not have a row for it either, and that between those two facts there is a great deal of nothing, and I did not sleep on the second one.**"

---

**Then the person who would not sign it,** and there was no argument afterwards and there has not been one since.

They asked Iven Tallo at the fourth bell, in the anteroom, in front of the clerk, and they asked him because he was the man who had refused the guild's draft and the room wanted to be able to say that the man who refused the other one had signed this one.

"**No,** he said.

"And I am not going to explain it, because I explained it on Friday and I explained it well and I am not going to do it twice, and I am not going to be asked twice, and the clerk can enter *no reason given* if she wants and I will not stop her."

"It is in the book," said Dalla Sen. "**And I have entered *no reason given* against a man the way I entered it against a woman on a litter in the seventeenth week, and the two of you have never met and you have now got the same line, and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

Then he said the reason anyway, half an hour later, to Marek Venn, on the bench, in a voice that did not carry past four feet, and Marek Venn wrote it in a book of his own and showed it to nobody, which was the second time he had done that in this building and the first time he had done it about himself.

*That this is not the guild's charter and it is not a bond and there is no word in it that I swear to, and I have eight months of not knowing what I swore, and a document with no oath in it can still be held against a man who is in it, and I have spent four months watching a boy in this building be paid in a column with no name on it and I am not going to be the second man to put his name on something and then have to say in a year that he did not know what it was.*

**So Iven Tallo did not sign, and nobody argued with him, and the charter was signed without him, and his refusal is in the book in his own hand with no reason given, and he is not a defector and he is not a martyr and he was told neither in a room.**

---

Marek Venn refused as well, and it took nine seconds, and it was the shortest refusal anybody has made in this Hearthhold and it was not cold.

"**I am not signing it because I am leaving this Hearthhold and everybody in this room knows roughly when,**" he said, "and a signature by a man who is leaving is a name against a route, and the entire document is about not doing that, and it is written in butcher's pencil, and I am not going to be the first man in this book to sign something in pencil."

Harlan Merrow said, from the end of the table, "**That is the first refusal since Friday I did not see coming, and it is the only one that costs the office nothing, and I am going to say something and I want it entered as a stranger's sentence and not as a boundary's.**

"**The office cannot grant this. It cannot refuse it either.** A boundary instrument needs nothing from us; it needs a keeper and a wheel and a record, and you have all three, and there is nobody in this file who can say that a Hearthhold's own keeper may not agree with the people whose road it is. What we do with it is *receive* it, and put it in a file, and cite it, and go to four other Hearthholds and ask them for clause four.

"And clause four is the whole of what you have given us and it is worth more than you think and less than you want, and I am going to say that plainly because I have been honest since I walked in and I would like to go on a road having been honest to the last.

"**The three that accept will have timber by the month after next. The three that accept will be able to pay a boy. The three that accept will be able to shut a route when a clerk at a counter says so, and they will do it, and they will think they are being careful.**

"And you will be cited, in a file, in a year, as a boundary that refused the Cartwrights' maintenance undertaking and left itself with a jar of water out of a wall.

"**I am going to recommend it. I do not expect it to be granted. Both of those are true and I have said them both.**"

---

Sefu Aran signed as the boundary's route keeper, in his own hand, and then six people signed under him, and the order of it was read out before anybody picked up the pen, because the keeper does not sign a civic instrument by himself and a room that lets him is a room that has gone back to a wheel.

"**The keeper first, then the person who keeps the water, then the person who keeps the road, then the school, then the clerk, then the keeper of the page, and the carpenter, and the air engineer has not signed and is not going to be asked to.**

"**The air engineer does not sign anything in this Hearthhold. I am not asking and I am not asking anybody to wonder out loud why, because there are four reasons and every one of them has been said out loud in this room already and a fifth reason would be me being a man who wants a signature.**

"And I am signing first, and I want the reason in the book in my own words, and it is not noble: **a charter signed by six people and a keeper is a charter six people can refuse, and the day that happens the road stays open anyway, because the road is not the paper, the road is a way up a passage and I hold the cord and the wheel and the bell and none of those are paper.**"

They signed at the fifth bell. Seven signatures on butcher's paper, an office's number at the top of it, no name anywhere on it, and the fourth clause of it saying that the boundary will show a person a plan in a room when the office asks, and nothing in the whole of it that could be carried to a counter and used to find a room in this Hearthhold, **which was the argument Bel Ordry had made on the first day about a document that cannot be picked up, being true for the wrong reason, in a document that can be.**

At the sixth bell the plan came out of Bel Ordry's coat in four parts and was laid on the table, and Renn Vale read the seventh term out from her slate in her own words, and the four parts were laid one on top of the other and nobody counted them because the clerk counted them and said nine rows and a date and a fourth column with a reason on it in two hands, and then the room stood and it was the end of the second day's business and the corridor was shut and the pot was ten days and had not moved for five days, and the water had come out of a wall and been in this kitchen's pot for five days, and a charter was signed on the back of a sheet you could scrub, and it was not the guild's, and it was not a form, and a boy of twenty-two was thirty-four days into a count in a column with no heading on it, and a man with a suspended bond had refused to sign it in four words and had not been argued with, and the slab under the lip had not moved in the cold and had not been looked at.
