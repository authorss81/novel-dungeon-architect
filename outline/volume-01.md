# Volume 01 Outline

## Volume identity

- **Title:** The Room That Cannot Carry Us
- **Chapter range:** 1–50
- **Central promise:** Marek can keep a small group alive long enough to reach the Hearthholds, but no room he builds is safe until he accounts for air, water, escape, and change.
- **Starting genre proof:** ordinary civil-engineering work is interrupted by a real collapse; the first dungeon room is treated as a failing building before it is treated as a treasure site.
- **Ending type:** earned escape with a civic cost, not a permanent solution.

## Central pressure

Marek Venn is trapped below Marrowgate with a small public-works survey crew after a pressure seam opens in a dry service tunnel. The route behind them is unstable, the rescue team cannot reach them, and every room they enter may be a dead end. They must reach a stable chamber near a Hearthhold entrance before the shifting load, water, and exhausted bodies make rescue impossible.

## Starting state

Marek is a 32-year-old civil engineer with no reliable supernatural ability. He is carrying a damaged brass survey rule belonging to his missing father, Alden Venn. His ordinary work and reputation are already under pressure after disputed inspection decisions and a supervisor's request to sign a false partial-closure certificate; the opening collapse happens during the first inspection, before he has been blamed for it. He knows how to shoring, drainage, evacuation, and confined-space rescue; he does not know how the Lattice's rule-lines work or why its rooms repeat human routines.

The opening party contains six people: Marek, Leena Orr, a public-works survey technician; Iven Tallo, a Cartwright route carpenter; Pell Roke, a fourteen-year-old junior route messenger and courier's younger brother; Oren Sill, a surface laborer; and Tams Quill, a surface laborer. Nera Oduya is not part of the initial group. She is a Hearthhold air engineer who appears at the far end of the first usable route and becomes the first person to reject Marek's recorded air plan for his failed refuge. Sefu Aran, an elected Hearthhold boundary route keeper, answers the first air-keeper signal and retains an independent civic veto over admission.

## Starting relationships

- Marek and Leena trust his measurements more than the office's account of the collapse; Leena is the first person to tell him that his confidence is not evidence.
- Marek and Iven begin in professional conflict: Iven knows which repairs guild crews can finish, while Marek asks who will maintain them after the crew leaves.
- Pell depends on Marek for a route out and refuses to be treated as a liability.
- Nera begins as a skeptical counterpart, not a helper. On first meeting she challenges the recorded air plan of Marek's failed refuge because its ventilation path fails; Sefu Aran, the local route keeper, can separately refuse admission.
- The Cartwright clearance system treats the group as an unlicensed route liability before it treats them as people.

## Starting power level

Ordinary civil engineering, practical materials knowledge, and a first fragile impression of moving force. The first volume may use ordinary timber shoring, chalk, rope, measurements, and rescue technique. It must not grant Marek a reliable bearing mark or a named power stage before the final chapters.

## Major locations

- Marrowgate Public Works dry service tunnel and inspection line.
- The collapsed surface shaft and the first impossible pressure seam.
- The Cinder Steps: sloped terraces, unstable stairs, a failed pump room, and a sealed transit room.
- The first domestic Lattice room containing traces of a kitchen, school, or shelter.
- A returning-stair faultform and its shifting route.
- The Hearthhold boundary, including the first maintained air and water works.

## Major factions and people

- The trapped public-works crew and laborers, who need rescue but cannot become props in Marek's plans.
- The Cartwright Guilds, represented by Iven's practical knowledge and an early clearance notice; their guild system is a pressure, not a single evil faction.
- Dael Harrow, a clearance master who treats the route as unlicensed property and begins the first institutional opposition.
- The first Hearthhold residents and Nera Oduya, who need the route but do not owe Marek obedience.
- An old Cairn plate marked with an empty single-seat symbol. It is a mystery clue, not a new power or a named antagonist.

## Escalation sequence

1. **Routine work and collapse:** Marek completes a familiar inspection, notices a physical inconsistency, orders a closure, and is caught when the tunnel gives way. The collapse is costly and physically real; no one can dismiss it as a magical dream.
2. **Immediate shelter:** The group uses ordinary rescue craft to make a first lean-to and discovers that the Lattice's rooms change under occupancy. Marek receives a brief, unreliable sense of force and chooses to keep investigating rather than wait for a rescue that is not coming.
3. **False safety:** The strongest-looking room passes an empty inspection but fails a full-room test. Marek must dismantle his own work, lose supplies, and accept that a structure can be stable and still kill the people inside it.
4. **Mystery and route:** A domestic room, old evacuation markings, and the empty-seat plate reveal that the Lattice was built for civic use. A returning-stair faultform makes the obvious exit dangerous, while guild workers argue that one licensed operator should be able to close or reopen every route. The group must work as a coordinated rescue crew rather than accept either rule as self-evident.
5. **Hearthhold threshold:** Nera's team opens a controlled route from the far side. The survivors reach a stable room, but the route is now claimed, watched, and dependent on maintenance Marek cannot control.

## Midpoint reversal

Around Chapter 25, after the first false refuge has been abandoned, Marek's team completes a larger two-level shelter intended to become a public Hearthhold room. It passes an empty inspection and has a stronger air plan. During a full occupancy drill, an old school-gallery floor and a crowd surge redirect the load into the lower corridor. The shelter is not a simple trap this time; it is a structure that works for a family but fails when a neighborhood, a stretcher line, and an emergency exit share the same wing. Marek must decommission the upper gallery, open a shared corridor, and lose the group's remaining spare materials to keep the lower residents alive.

The reversal establishes the volume's rule at civic scale: a safe-looking room is not a safe room until occupancy, air, drainage, escape, and foreseeable change are tested together. The first batch's small refuge failure teaches the same rule before anyone calls the project public.

## Volume climax

Chapters 46–49 bring the group to the same returning-stair system after its first sighting has revealed only a local rule. It has now fully manifested as the only apparent route to the Hearthholds: the stair repeats its first landing and pulls the evacuation line back into a collapsing pressure pocket. Marek coordinates an ordinary timber brace, rope anchors, Nera's air guidance, Iven's craft knowledge, and Leena's survey marks to create a two-way route. He does not defeat the faultform by hitting it; he changes the load and gives the stair a different function to repeat.

The climax costs a crew member and forces the group to abandon salvage and equipment. The route is open for people and air, not for a cart or a private guild claim. Marek's hand has already lost fine control from the failed refuge, and the final crossing makes him depend on another person's readings.

## Concrete resolution

By Chapter 50, the survivors reach the Hearthhold boundary with a usable route, a partial map, and a damaged Cairn plate bearing an empty single-seat symbol. Nera and the Hearthhold residents insist on an air and occupancy check before admitting the group. The route is not a permanent escape and the guild begins a claim on it.

Volume 1 resolves the immediate survival promise: the group gets out of the Cinder Steps. It does not resolve whether the Lattice is a refuge, a prison, or a failed civic system. It opens the larger question of who can own a route through other people's homes.

## Required relationship and power changes

- Marek moves from ordinary engineering to a fragile first repeatable reading; he cannot yet place a bearing mark, and the first failed refuge leaves permanent fine-control loss in one hand.
- Marek learns that rescue plans need named evacuation authority and full-room occupancy tests.
- Iven becomes a reluctant craft mentor and a witness to the guild's early claim.
- Nera enters as a professional equal and rejects the recorded air plan of Marek's failed refuge for a correct air reason; Sefu Aran independently controls Hearthhold admission.
- Pell and Leena give the volume its human cost; the group does not remain anonymous.
- Dael Harrow and the Cartwright clearance system become the first institutional pressure after survival.

## Required world reveals

- The Lattice contains domestic and civic rooms, not only treasure.
- Rule-lines carry force, air, heat, or function through mineral seams.
- Faultforms are unresolved structural functions, not arbitrary monsters.
- The Room Checks are the minimum standard for a refuge.
- An old Cairn plate uses an empty single-seat symbol, but its meaning is not explained.
- Hearthholds are inhabited neighborhoods that maintain the Lattice themselves.
- Alden's survey rule reacts to a Cairn mark, but it is a damaged tool and reader, not a lineage token.

## Next-volume question

Who maintains the Hearthhold rooms, and why do the old plans show homes rather than treasure vaults? Can a route built by a rescue crew become a public resource without becoming a guild toll road?

## Final chapter image

At the Hearthhold threshold, the survivors stand in a maintained room while Nera opens a vent that Marek's plan had treated as optional. The old empty-seat plate rests under a work cloth, still unexplained. The first useful route home is also the first route someone else can close.
