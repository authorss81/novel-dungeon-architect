# Chapter 199 — Nothing Beside Them

The seventh day of the thirty-seventh week is a Sunday, and a seventh day is not a crossing day, so nobody came up the nine steps this morning and nobody went down them, and the arrangement has stood since the thirty-third week and has never once been tested, because there has never been anything to test it with. The school did not run and lost nothing. There are eleven people in this Hearthhold and all eleven were in an anteroom with a hole in the corner of the floor, and the passage at the top of the nine steps stayed shut the whole day, and it will open at the first bell, and the corridor underneath it will be shut again by the second, the way it is on every Monday.

So the morning had nothing come into it, and the room did the only thing a room can do with a morning that nothing comes into, which is to look at what it keeps.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the nineteenth reading, and the same as eighteen, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under the string to find out whether it was still tight, which is the standing practice and has been for nineteen mornings, and the thumb came out of it right.

"**The wall is where it has been,**" she said, "**and I am going to give you the other half of that in my own words before anybody asks me for it. The line under the string, along the join, where the lime let go in the thirty-fourth week, is not in one piece this morning. It is in two, with about the length of my hand between them, and which end is the wider one is mine to keep. There is no number on either of them and the rule stays in its belt, and I would like the clerk to enter all four of those refusals as four refusals and not as one.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the line is in two and that no figure has been given for it by anybody and that the folding rule is on a belt which is staying where it is, for the twenty-second morning running, and entered that a person who has been refused four things by a wall this week is the only person in this room who has been refused anything at all.**"

Renn Vale watched the line at the first bell, which on an open morning is her bell, and on a shut one it is the second, and on a seventh day there is no bell in it at all and she was on the landing at the first anyway.

"**I am going to be here on a day when there is nothing crossing, and the reason I am giving you does not make it a rule,**" she said. "**I gave one of those in the thirty-sixth week and it was a good one and it cost me the day after. The reason I am here is that a keeper who stops on the one day of the week she cannot do anything is a keeper who will start again on the Monday as though the Sunday had not happened, and this line has been watched every morning for four weeks and there is no version of watching it that is only worth doing when there is something to hear.**"

She was there about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times. The clerk entered a figure of nothing against it and entered that the figure of nothing on a seventh day was a different figure of nothing from the one on an open morning and was not going to be given a reason and was not going to be joined to the other one.

And then the second thing happened, and it was small, and it took about nine seconds.

"**It was not nothing,**" Renn Vale said, from the middle of the floor, without going back to the wall. "**There is no description of it and there is no second report. That is the whole of the report and you are not getting a second one out of me this morning, and the reason I am giving for not giving you a second one is that a thing reported twice in one morning becomes a thing this room knows instead of a thing a woman heard.**"

The pump got two strokes in.

"**What it was not,**" said Jo Verrick, "**was the pump and what it was not was the water, because I have heard both of those for nineteen mornings and I know what they sound like on this side of a wall. And my ear stays out of that gap and so does my thumb, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said the second of those out loud in a room where nobody had asked me, because the last time I put a thumb in that join it came out of it wrong.**"

Nobody asked her to.

The anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and they are said on a seventh day as well, and they were said here at the second bell, which is when they are said on a shut day, because a seventh day is not a shut day and is not an open day and the only honest hour for them on it is the one a shut day uses. The count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and nobody read it, and the clerk entered that he had named it and read nothing, which is the fourth day running she has entered that and the first one on a day when there is nothing to be frightened of. The pump was attended, and stopped on a rising water line chalked on the frame, and the line has still not moved.

Then the room got to the third thing, and the third thing came out of the kitchen-line doorway from a woman with a slate under her arm who had been in the room since the first bell and had not said anything, which is not what she does on an ordinary morning.

"**Two of them are shut,**" said Nella Bray. "**Two Hollowmarks. Correctly, on the schedule, with the day in the first column and the second column empty because there is nobody to put in it, and the seals are the Compact's and not ours. That is the whole of what I have and I am going to give it to you the way I would give it to a person at a counter, which is all of it, in the order, with no opinion in it.**"

"**Which two,**" said Bel Ordry from the step, before anybody else had got a whole sentence out.

"**I was not told and I did not ask, and that is the shape of it for this week,**" she said. "**And here is the reason for the not-asking, and it is a counter's reason and not a moral one. A gate that a person uses every day is not shut in a morning. Both of the ones that are shut are ones nobody in this building has been through, and that is the whole of what I can tell you about them, and it is more than I have ever been allowed to say about a closure in nineteen years.**"

Nobody improved on it, and then Bel Ordry gave the half against, and it was not half against anything in the room.

"**And the reason it was correct is the sixth clause,**" he said, "**and the sixth clause is ours. Where a boundary cannot show that a room's condition is being kept, the office shall not treat the room as kept, and this boundary cannot show that a room's condition is being kept, on purpose, in writing, in a plan with a first column that has been a permission and not a count since the second week, and a clerk who said out loud on Friday that she was not going to give a room its number back.**"

"**So the office has done the one thing this boundary has spent thirty-seven weeks arguing it should be allowed to refuse, which is to treat a room it cannot verify as a room it is not responsible for, and it has used our own argument to do it, and it is right, and the argument is the argument, and the reason this boundary cannot argue against it is that the argument it would use is the argument it made.**"

Dalla Sen wrote for about half a minute and did not read any of it back.

"**And the cost of that, said out loud by the woman who paid it, and not by me,**" and she turned the slate over on the trestle so that nothing was on it, "**is that on Friday morning this room put a price on what it had done. A number said out loud in this anteroom becomes a number this room has agreed to. So nobody in this building can say how many people are in the anteroom on a school morning, and the only figure anybody can find is a ten on a stranger's sheet, and I know the real one because I feed people. That was the price and I said it in my own mouth before anybody decided on anything.**"

"**And what has come up that passage this morning is the bill,**" she said, "**and nobody in this room should be ashamed of it, because a woman at a counter did her work on an ordinary day with an ordinary pen and the office is entitled to act on a rule it wrote and is right about. The bill is for a number this boundary would not give.**"

"**And the half against that, which is mine, and it is the only thing I have got and it is not much,**" said Bel Ordry. "**The bill is not for the number. The bill is for thirty-seven weeks of being right about a thing that turned out to have a price, and a boundary cannot refuse a price by being right about it, and I have been standing on this step for a week telling a room that a decision not to know is a decision, and it is a decision, and decisions are what a schedule is made of.**"

Then Dalla Sen asked for the book, and nobody had asked her to, and she wrote for most of a bell, and the room watched a clerk do the thing she does and does not do.

She wrote the first fact. Then she turned the page a long way forward, and wrote the second fact. Then she turned the page forward again, and wrote the third one, and she did not write a reason beside any of them, and the space where a reason goes was empty in all three places, and she did not come back to it once.

"**I am entering them one at a time,**" she said, "**and I am entering them with nothing beside them on purpose, and I am going to say out loud why, because a clerk who puts a reason beside a fact is doing the safest thing in the world and the safest thing in the world is what has made every bad map in this district.**"

"**Two Hollowmarks are shut. The line along a join in a party wall is in two places instead of one. On the seventh morning a thing in that wall was not nothing. That is the whole of what is in this book this morning and no two of them are next to each other and none of them has a reason beside it, and there is not going to be one.**"

"**And here is what I am refusing, out loud, so that a person in a year can check it. I am not joining the second of them to the nine-hundred-year-old dowel, or to the charter, or to the mouth at the end of the school corner, or to the four steps nobody in this building has gone down, or to the room four steps below the far side of that landing, or to the air engineer's theory, or to the plate on the shelf above that bench. Seven things, named in a room, in this order, and I am not joining any of them to the first or the third, and if somebody in this room wants to do that on a day when I am not here then I would like it entered that it was possible.**"

Nobody joined them to anything.

"**And the reason I have done it this way is not virtue,**" she said. "**The reason is that three true things in a row with nothing between them is the only shape I know of that a person can pick up later and use without a motive. Any other shape — two of them together, or three of them with a line under them — hands whoever finds it a motive to go looking for a fourth.**"

Bel Ordry gave the half against that, and he gave it from the step without coming down it.

"**An empty space where a reason goes is not a neutral space. It is a space with nothing in it, and a person in a year is going to come along with a pen and find three facts in a clerk's book with three empty reasons beside them, and they are going to fill all three in, and they are going to fill them in in the order the facts are in, and they are going to think they have found out something.**"

"**And the answer to that is that they will have found out that this boundary had three things it could not explain in the same week,**" said Dalla Sen, "**which is true, and which is a thing, and which costs this building nothing that has not already been spent. I would rather it were wrong in a year and empty now than full now and wrong in a year, and that is not a principle, it is a preference, and I am allowed a preference.**"

Then the air engineer came down one of her three steps and stopped, which is the second of the three and is as far as she comes for air, and she had the dyed cloth out and not out.

"**I gave this room a word once,**" she said, "**on the seventh day of the thirty-fourth week, and I said twice would make it a thing this room knows instead of a word a woman carried in from somewhere else, and I am not saying it a second time this morning and I am not saying a different word either.**"

"**And the reason, because somebody will ask me for one and they may as well have it in my mouth. The word I gave was for a thing that is happening because a room is finishing something it never finished. I do not know that this is that. I have got two facts and neither of them is mine, and a woman who reads walls has told me the line is in two pieces and a keeper has told me there was a sound, and that is three people in a room describing a thing and not one of them has been on the other side of it.**"

She did not go nearer the wall and she did not go further from it either.

"**And the woman who makes those three things into one thing will not be me, because I have watched a room do that to a finding once and the finding came out the other side of it with a name on it that nobody in the room had given it. I have four numbers and not a fifth this morning, and the fifth stays in my mouth, and my two conditions are not met and nobody has asked me about either of them, and I am not asking anybody to ask me.**"

Nobody asked her.

"**And the other thing, which is a fact and not a request, and I am putting it in the room the way I put the last one in, and nobody is going to do anything with it.**" She did not move. "**Two of the three facts in that clerk's book are about air. The line in that wall is where the air of this building goes. And I have certified the air in nine rooms of this Hearthhold and I have never certified the air on the other side of that join, because there is no way into it from this side that I would send anybody through, and I have not said so in this room before and I am saying it now because two things that nobody joined together have just been in the same book and I would like it written down that they were in the same book.**"

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell with his book shut under his forearm.

He had wanted, since the second week of this building, to be the man who found out. It had taken him thirty-eight weeks to work out that the wanting and the finding were two different trades, and that this building had spent all thirty-eight weeks refusing the second one on the grounds that the first one was not safe to have in a room.

The three facts in the clerk's book were the first time in thirty-eight weeks that three true things had gone into a page in this building with nothing between them. He sat at the end of a trestle and looked at the space between the pages and thought that it was the best piece of writing he had seen in this building and that it contained nothing at all. He did not know whether that was a victory or the most expensive kind of losing, and Bel Ordry had used those exact words about a different instrument on a Friday and had not been talking about this.

He did not say any of that. What he said was the arithmetic, because the arithmetic was the one thing in the room that was his and it cost nothing.

"**The three of them have no day in common and no room in common,**" he said. "**Two of them are about a surface and one of them is about a wall in this building. None of them is a reason for either of the others and I will not pretend to a room that one of them is, and I would like it entered that the man who found out what is behind that wall has had three mornings in a row to say something about it and has said the arithmetic instead.**"

"**Entered, in his words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that he did not say it kindly and that nobody in this room expected him to.**"

Then Tams Quill read the board at the sixth bell because there was a bell and he reads a board at a bell, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and eight of them not, and the eight are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it. He gave no reason for the column and she did not ask him for one.

"**And the reason the board is walked on a morning like this and not skipped,**" she said, "**is that a man who reads a board is a man who is looking at eighteen things that are not rooms, and this morning three things that are not rooms happened and a board is where a person goes to find out whether a thing belongs on it, and the answer was no six times this week and it is no this morning, and I am entering the no and I am not entering a seventh reason for it because I did not ask and he did not give one.**"

The sixth bell came and the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither of them was read out, and the reason went in beside them: a seventh day is a day nothing crosses on, and on a seventh morning a clerk enters three things that she cannot join, and a clerk who reads a total out loud over three unjoined facts is telling a room that the three have been dealt with.

One today and two hundred and ninety-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and fifteen and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that on its own line, in the ordinary hand, and was not read, and no reason was given for that either, and the line has no heading over it and is not going to get one. Wen Pask is one hundred and thirty-four days and one hundred and thirty-four nights. It is not going to be one hundred and thirty-five.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a room with three things in it that nobody can explain has nothing to hand five men, and a crew asked to go and look at a thing that cannot be named would be a crew asked to have an opinion, and this boundary does not put opinions in five men's heads on a Sunday.

The pot is two days, the seam gave nothing on Friday and nothing yesterday and nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with nothing on it, and a book on the trestle with three pages turned forward in it and three spaces left empty, and a piece of chalk beside the book that was not picked up once in the whole of a morning by the hand it belongs to. On the table was a stranger's sheet with a wax head at each end of it, and above the bench a plate wrapped in work cloth on a shelf, thirty-seven weeks unopened, and in the pocket of a coat on the hook a brass rule that had said nothing for twenty-eight weeks.

Nobody went through anything that day. Nobody put a hand, a tool or a tape into the gap. Eleven people went to bed in a building with two ways out of it, one of which is a word, and neither of them had been touched, and one of them had four days of walking in it and was four days away.
