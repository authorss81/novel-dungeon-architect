# Novel Review — Volume 01, Batch 0005

## Scope and provenance

No planned beat, no death, no hand-over, and no ending constraint needed to change.

## Second repair pass — six defects the first pass missed

A later independent re-derivation of the state found six defects the first repair had not caught, three of them blocking and one of those a repair that had only been half applied. All six are now applied. The prose was preserved and edited in place; no chapter was restarted, no plot beat moved, and no death, hand-over, or ending constraint changed.

- **R1 (blocking, and a failed repair) — a leftover duplicate draft in Chapter 47.** The B5 correction left both versions of the margin sentence in place, back to back: *"**Two minutes** of margin, and two minutes is nothing…"* immediately followed by *"**Six minutes** of margin, and six minutes is nothing…"* The six-minute version is arithmetically impossible against the sentence before it — thirty-two minutes of crossing in thirty-four minutes of pump leaves two — and Chapter 48 then cashes in the two-minute figure. The six-minute sentence is deleted. This was the only near-duplicate pair in the batch and there is now none.
- **R2 (blocking) — the hand-over instrument committed a dead man.** Oren Sill dies in Chapter 48 and the state records five survivors, but Chapter 50 had Marek say *"done by six people who are going to leave"*, *"the six of us have a maintenance obligation"*, and the ledger clause read *the six of them hold the maintenance of what they built*. The page the whole volume builds toward was wrong about the number of obligors, and nobody in a room that corrects every other number noticed. The second of Tams's two public corrections is now **the number of them**: he puts two fingers on the tally board and says *there are five of us in this room, say the five*, and the room does not write it down, because Dalla Sen's hand stays on the pen and it is not a thing in the ledger, it is a thing in the room. Sefu's paragraph keeps the historic six and names the standing five (*six people from above built a part of it, and the five of them who are here do not hold any of it*). The tally's six and Chapter 50's *six strangers* and *six people had now added a floor of a school and a lip with no post under it and a dead man on a stair* are all correct as past-tense history and are untouched.
- **R3 (blocking) — the anteroom cleared in two incompatible times.** Chapter 48 said all ten were out in a minute and a half *and* that the litter took four minutes and six, and the litter is one of the ten. Chapter 49 then repeated the minute and a half as a **counted, ledger-recorded** fact, so the error was propagated into the state-backed account rather than caught. All three places now keep the two figures apart: the **nine on their feet** clear the kitchen line in a minute and a half, and the **litter** goes out first on four bearers and the anteroom is not clear of it until four minutes and six, and the litter sets the number. Chapter 50's closure is scoped the same way, with the litter still coming down the corridor when the pump comes off the lever.
- **R4 (continuity) — the day-register / week-register collision was only partly fixed.** Two sentences still dated an event *inside* the batch to a day number, which is impossible against the ninth week: Iven's *"I am not going to start again on the forty-first day"* in Chapter 46, and his attribution of the first shore to *"the forty-fifth day"* in Chapter 48, when the first shore is Chapter 45 of the same batch. Both are now referred to by what they are. The **calendar is now locked in the state files**: the volume ends in the ninth week, the highest day number in the prose is the forty-sixth, every day number is a past event, and no day number may be attached to an in-batch event.
- **R5 (continuity) — Chapter 43 ran four descents against a plan that says *once*.** Nera's first plan carries the word *once* on purpose and states that a second trip would be a different document, and Chapter 43 then did trip one (aborted) and three more on the same terms with no re-papering. Since OT-41 and the Volume 02 prompt both build a job on the air plan saying *once*, the contradiction was being carried forward as canon. The extra descents are now lawful and paid for: **Nera writes a second document that night**, three lines, cutting the time under the six from eleven minutes to five, with the reason given aloud — eleven under a six is not a margin and only ever looked like one on a page — Dalla copies it and reads it back, the **first card stays on the valve board with *once* still on it** and the line *first descent, this card, and it did not finish* added beneath, and Jo enters it against the seat without moving her estimate. A **third document** is written on the morning of the fourth trip, which Nera will not let Jo read out because Jo is in the housing. *Once* therefore survives, and the Volume 02 retrieval now needs a fourth document nobody has written.
- **R6 (continuity) — the terms-reading ordinal was off by one.** The fifth reading is Chapter 45, the sixth is Chapter 46, so Chapter 47's *"for the sixth time"* is now the **seventh**.

Craft, from the same pass: the plinth-reader rule was repeated verbatim for twenty-six words between Chapter 49 and Chapter 50; Chapter 50's slate now says it as slate — *a reader who will not come off the plinth is not a reader, it is a post* — which is the same rule in Renn's hand and a sharper instrument than Mikka's rota. The first repair record's claim that *nine weeks* had been reduced to 49 was wrong; the count after this pass is **52**, and the motif was reduced, not removed.

## Blocking repairs applied

- **B1 — a phantom timber length.** The length left in the flooded pump gallery was **four foot nine** in three places and **five foot nine** in three others, including two lines in Chapter 48 where Iven and Dalla gave different lengths for the same object in the same scene, and Chapter 43 said it "went back down" when it had never come up at all. All three are now **five foot nine**, and Chapter 43 states that it never came up.
- **B2 — the volume's central material object contradicted its own build.** Chapter 49's closing tally put the three recovered lengths **in the anteroom**; Chapters 44, 45, 46 and 48 all put them **in the shore**. The tally now reads: three lengths in the shore on the common landing (three foot four, two foot nine, a stub), **four foot eleven and a bit of offcut** in the anteroom — the figure Iven gives in Chapter 48 and which the tally had dropped — and the five foot nine under water.
- **B3 — the shore's geometry was impossible.** The foot was **six feet** back from the wall and the head **four feet** up: a rake of seven foot two and a half against six foot one of timber. The foot is now **four foot seven** in both the mark-taking and Renn's recorded words, which is where six foot one of timber stands a four-foot head, and which is also a better shore angle. The correction is in the record, because it is the kind of number Leena's discipline exists to catch.
- **B4 — a three-foot-four offcut called "six feet of hardwood"** in Chapter 45's own explanation of why the crossing test was slow. Corrected to the end of a three-foot-four in its hands.
- **B5 — the relay's arithmetic was out by four minutes and Chapter 44's margin was built on the error.** Eight crossings at four minutes is **thirty-two**, not twenty-eight. Chapter 47 now says **two minutes of margin, and two minutes is nothing**, which is a worse number for the room and is exactly the sentence Chapter 48 then cashes in when the pump comes off four minutes early. - **B6 — "five seconds better" was three.** Chapter 46's reason for the figure also did not describe the comparison it was attached to. Both are corrected: the forty-four was a turn, and a line in the block takes the fraction out of a return.
- **B7 — Chapter 48 invented a twenty-eighth stroke** on a pump whose locked interval is twenty-six. Jo now comes off **the twenty-fourth stroke instead of the twenty-sixth**, and the paragraph's logic closes: the six minutes still run in full and the four minutes come out of the pumping, which is what the eight-crossing plan cannot afford.
- **B8 — the Thursday crossing could not produce "seven people still on the near side."** The school's nine children cannot fill whole crossings. - **B9 — "ninety-two and a half" hours was not derivable from anything** and inverted the finding Tams is arguing. Both instances are now **forty-six**, and Tams says he read it twice before he believed it.
- **B10 — Chapter 50's slate printed five terms and referred to six.** The cart refusal is now on the hand-over slate in Mikka's own words, with the reason she asked for it, and the closure is the sixth.
- **B11 — Chapter 48 named no threshold-bell deputy and no wet-seam watch, on a stair period when the corridor was open.** The Thursday is a school day, so the sixth watch at the waist is a live post, and it is now held — **by Renn Vale**, who says the whole of the distinction out loud before she takes it, and **Ruk Sella rings the bell for the third time in the batch**, asked in person and repeating the terms back. **Ilsa Norr counts at the head of the crossing** in Renn's place, and the passage adds the fact that one of the four people who could stop that work was on the far side of a slab for the best part of the morning because nobody had asked her whether she would be needed here, which nobody had an answer to and which is in the ledger with a time on it. This is the best thing the review found and it is now on the page.
- **B12 — Chapter 42 said the black cord had never been released; Chapters 18 and 44 say it was.** Corrected to *Sefu has not released since the eighteenth day*, and the line inventory now records that the stair's own restriction cord is a **separate short length** taken off that same hook in Chapter 18, still tied, and re-tied only by Renn and Sefu.
- **B13 — a garbled clause in Chapter 41's key scene**, in which Jo was recorded as having said something to Jo. Corrected: she said it afterward to Dalla, and to nobody else.
- **B14 — three sentences put incompatible clocks in the same breath.** The batch carries a week clock that is continuous with Chapter 39 and a day-number register that is not. ## Watch items carried forward, not defects

- **W24 — chapter length.** Nine of ten chapters run above the 2,100–3,200 baseline the Batch 0003 review recorded, and Chapter 45 is 5,045 words. The review's judgement is that the constraint is the shape and not the sentences: the batch's pressure types are action, craft, civic conflict and one death, but every one of them is delivered through a page somebody reads back. Chapter 45 was trimmed where the prose was expository and no original scene was cut. **The next batch should decide whether Volume 02's charter argument can carry a chapter that turns on a person doing a job rather than a room agreeing to a line** (C17), because the shape will get worse before it gets better.
- **W25 — repetition density.** *Read back* appears **37 times** in this batch against twelve in Batch 0004, and **26** paragraph openings across the ten chapters begin with a *Nobody …* construction (C2, C3), five of them in Chapter 48 alone. Both are the same class of finding as Batch 0004's C19 for *own hand*. Three instances of the *Nobody …* opening were rewritten and one *read back* was moved out of the narration, but the density is a symptom of a batch in which people write things down, and it should be watched rather than stripped. The second repair pass added one of each and did not pretend otherwise.
- **C12 — the two four minutes.** The route's crossing is four minutes and Leena's litter through two foot eight is four minutes and six. The number is a deliberate echo, but on the page it originally read as a collision. **Left as it stands, and recorded as an author decision**, because the echo is doing real work: the route's four minutes is Leena's number with the six seconds taken off it, and a reader who notices the collision has found the batch's argument. If a future review reports it as a defect, the fix is one clause in Chapter 47 making the rhyme explicit, not a change to either number. **R3 strengthens this rather than threatening it**: the litter's four minutes and six is now also the anteroom's clearance number, because a litter is what sets a clearance, so the six seconds do double duty as the fastest time a person in that building can be moved and as the number the route takes off her.
- **C15 — the narrator aside in Chapter 48.** The line about the best forty seconds of Marek Venn's life lands *before* Oren Sill goes, not after, and so it is not sentimentalising the death. Left as it stands and recorded so that a second reader knows it was a decision and not an oversight.
