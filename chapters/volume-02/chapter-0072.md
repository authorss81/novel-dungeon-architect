# Chapter 72 — Nine Rows

Ilsa Norr gave her part first and she gave it in the order it was on the thing, and Dalla Sen wrote it as she spoke and read it back three times because Ilsa made her, and the third reading was the one that is in the book.

"**It is not a drawing of a building.** That is the first thing and I have thought about it since the fourth bell and I am certain of it and Nera is not, so write down that we differ. It is a list. It is one long table drawn on a sheet about the length of my arm and a hand wide, and the table is divided into three columns, and there are nine lines and every line is a room in this Hearthhold that I have stood in."

"Which rooms," said Sefu Aran.

"That is the next part and I am coming to it, and I want the room to understand that I have not got a map and I have got nine lines and a table, and that is a different thing, and the reason it is a different thing is the second column."

"Read the columns," said Dalla Sen.

"**First column: how many people.** And I want this written exactly, because everybody in this room has been waiting eight weeks for a column with a name in it and this one has got a **number** in it. A one, a two, a four, a nine. Nobody's names. Not initials. Not anyone's. Just how many, and I have had my hand on that page and I have gone looking for a name for about a minute and there is not one, and I am telling you that so that nobody goes looking at eight o'clock tonight and thinks they found one.

"**Second column: when.** And when is not a day of a week. There is no seventh day and there is no fifth day and there is not one single number in the when column anywhere on that page. It is a **condition**. Written out longhand. Wet. Dark. Cold. First. Last. And then on the ninth line the word is *none*."

"And the third column," said Renn Vale, from the upper landing, where she had come and stood without being asked and without going any further down.

"**Third column is what is done in the room.** And the third column is empty on six of the nine lines."

The anteroom did the thing it did.

"Empty," said Bel Ordry. "Not short. Empty."

"Empty," said Ilsa Norr. "And I am going to say the thing I have not said all afternoon, because I have had it since about a quarter past four and I have been standing on it for an hour, and if I do not say it in the next minute I am going to lose my nerve, which I do about twice a day.

"**On six of nine rooms, whoever drew that thing wrote down how many people live there and what the conditions are, and then did not write down what anybody does.**"

Nobody moved.

"I know what that sounds like," she said, "and I am not going to let anybody in this room finish my sentence for me, because there are about four possible sentences and four of them are wrong. It does not mean nobody did anything. It might mean the man who drew it thought the answer was the first two columns and I would give him the third week of his life for it, because the answer to *how do you keep a room* is **how many people are in it and what the weather is doing**, and if you have that on a wall you do not need a third column, because you already know.

"And it does not mean treasure, and I know that somebody in this room has been waiting eleven weeks for a room with a door drawn on it and gold behind the door, and there is nothing of the kind, and I would like that said out loud today so that it can stop being waited for."

"Say it," said Marek Venn, from the end, before he knew he was going to.

"**There is no treasure on it. It is a schedule of keeping a place to live in, and it is a schedule, and schedules are not maps and they do not show you rooms. They show you that somebody expected to be here.**"

---

They went through it line by line for the better part of an hour, and it is worth saying that the room did it properly, because five weeks ago it would not have.

Mikka Hale read her own line and made Dalla Sen correct it twice. "It says the kitchen, and it says four, and the when column says *wet*, and there is nothing in the third column. I have got four people on my line in a pot and I have got one, and the number is four, and the number is wrong by three, and I am not going to have a number wrong by three in a book. Write *four when the room is in use* and I will put a line under it and say so, because a pot of four is a different thing from a kitchen of four and whoever drew that meant the room and not the pot."

"Seconded," said Ilsa Norr, "and enter that two of us corrected the air engineer's and the cook's lines in the same hour and that the air engineer's is still not corrected."

Nera had not corrected hers. That was the first thing anybody noticed and nobody said so for a while.

Leena Orr had come to the front of the room on the litter, which she could do for two hours and no more and had been at the anteroom for a hundred minutes of the two, and she had a slate on her knees and she had not written anything on it in front of anybody, and she said the thing at about the second hour, and she said it slowly, because she is careful and has been for eleven weeks.

"Stop for a minute," she said. "All of you. I want one word checked and I want it checked before anybody builds anything on nine lines.

"**Is it to a scale?**"

The room turned round.

"Because if it is, then a table is a drawing, whatever Ilsa Norr says about it, and it can be laid on the floor of this anteroom and two people with a cord and two nails can put every one of those nine lines against a wall in this building and find out whether they are where she says they are. And if it is not to a scale, then it is a list with a shape on it, and the shape is decoration, and **a person in four years is going to be shown a page with nine rooms on it and no way to find one of them, and that is the most dangerous kind of old document there is.** I have seen a survey that was not a survey put a crew under a floor for two days."

"It is to a scale," said Nera Oduya.

"You have not laid it on a floor."

"I have not laid it on a floor because I was not allowed to take it out of the room and I was not going to ask to have it taken out of the room," said Nera. "But I can tell you it is to a scale from the thing itself, and I will tell you how, and then you can all stop me if I am wrong.

"**Every line has its neighbours at the right distances.** The kitchen line is nearer the store line than the store line is to the stair. The school line is about a door's width from the corridor and the corridor line is between it and the pump. Draw a line through nine of them at their true distances and you get the shape of this level the way you already know it, in order, with the odd one out being — "

She stopped.

"Say it," said Renn Vale.

"**The ninth line is not in the order.**" Nera's voice did not change at all, which was how everybody in that room knew she had arrived somewhere. "And it is not in the order because it is not in this level. It is down."

---

"Down how much," said Sefu Aran.

"Four steps," said Nera Oduya.

There is a silence in that anteroom that is not describable and is not repeatable, and the only thing in it that moved was the wheel under the floor, and it went round once and a half and everybody heard it and nobody mentioned it.

"Whose four steps," said Renn Vale, from the upper landing.

"**Ours.**" Nera did not move and did not put her board down. "I have fitted eight of the nine lines to this level by eye and by memory and I have been doing it since the fourth bell and I have checked it twice, and the eighth line is the wash rooms, and the ninth line is below the ninth tread of the stair that goes down off the far side of the fixed first landing, behind the rail, on the far side, past the four steps nobody has walked and is not walked this month on my own condition.

"And the ninth line has a **one** in the count and **none** in the when column and **nothing at all** in the third column, and the when column says *none* on that line and *none* nowhere else on the page, and I have looked at that word for ten minutes and I am telling you that I cannot read it, and I am telling you that I am the person in this room who most wants it to say something, and that is exactly why I will not read it for you."

"Then do not read it," said Renn Vale.

"Then let me say what I have already said out loud three times in my own head and once in this room," said Nera, "and let the room do what it likes with it.

"**This boundary has been keeping that room since the ninth week.**"

---

The anteroom went cold in a way that had nothing to do with December.

"Since the ninth week," said Nera, "by crossing it. Four at a time, four minutes, six crossings a week, on the days the school does not run, at the first bell and after the fifth, with a bearer on the lip and a plinth reader and a string and a bell, and every one of those crossings has gone over a lip that is packed and not held, with a person under it, and **we have done it six times a week for four weeks and we have called it carrying water.**

"I am not saying we knew. I am saying there is a row on that page with a one in it and no day in it, and there has been a person going over that lip once a day for four weeks, and I have spent four weeks not publishing a rate because I will not stand behind a number, and I have just been handed a document with a count on it and no rate on it at all, and **I want it to be true, and that is the sentence I asked to be written in the book at the second bell this morning, and this is me finding out what it is for.**"

"Stop," said Renn Vale.

"No."

"**Stop, Nera.**" She had not come down three steps and she did not come down three steps now, and nobody in that anteroom expected her to and the whole sentence depended on her not doing it. "I am going to say the thing that has to be said and then I am going to stop talking, and I want it in my own hand and read out, and I want it in the book before anybody agrees with me.

"That row says one person. It does not say one person *walking over it*. **A room is not kept by crossing it.** A room is kept by somebody going into it, and there has not been a person in that room in the whole time I have been in this Hearthhold, and the day somebody goes down those four treads, the keeping of that room begins, and it will not stop, because that is what a room is.

"And so I am saying the other half, and I am saying it before anybody can be kind to me about it. I have written a term on a slate in this building that says if it turns out to be a door then it is a door in a building I keep and **nobody goes down it, and that decision is made now, in the cold, so that in the summer nobody can say *we did not know*.** I wrote that in the eleventh week and I have not withdrawn it and I am not withdrawing it because a woman has read a page.

"So: no. Not one crossing a week of a room. Not a rota, not a day, not a name, not a *one* in a column. **The sixth crossing is water, and it is four people and a pail, and it goes up and it comes back, and if a page from before any of us were alive turns that into a keeping, then the page is not telling us about a room. It is telling us what the last people who lived here were frightened of, and I am not going to be frightened on a Tuesday because there is a one in a column.**"

"Read it out," said Dalla Sen, and read it out, and Nera Oduya made her start at the beginning and did it twice, and Dalla Sen read Renn's seventh term and the eighth after it, both in Renn's hand, back to back, and the room heard the two of them together the way it had heard the lip and the seventh term together at the fifth bell every day for a fortnight.

---

"Also," said Nera, and picked her board up, "there is nothing else I can tell you and I am going to be short about it.

"**There is no air on that page.** No vent, no shaft, no grille, no baffle, no rate and no figure of any kind, and it goes against a nine-line table of a building that has a stair going down to a room, and a building with a stair going down to a room has an air question, and it is not on the page, and there is no second page, and the nine boxes in that store are registers and not plans and I have now seen all nine of them and they are registers.

"So the air that used to come up out of the school corner is still not accounted for, and it got less accounted for this afternoon, and I have got four cards and a valve and a shaft I cannot certify, and I am telling you that a plan with no air on it is not a smaller document than a plan with air on it. **It is a document that somebody kept, and air is the thing you cannot keep.**"

"Which means they did not have any," said Bel Ordry.

"Which means they did not write any down," said Nera Oduya. "**Which is not the same, and I have spent eleven weeks teaching this room the difference, and I am not going to be the one who drops it today.**"

---

Late, near the sixth bell, with the school gone up the nine steps and the anteroom down to nine people and the pot standing where it stood, Leena Orr asked Dalla Sen to read the whole of it again from the top, and Dalla Sen did, and the finding at the end of it was not about the plan.

"Eight of the nine lines have a count and a condition and nothing to say what is done," said the clerk, reading. "The ninth line has a count of one, a condition of *none*, and nothing at all. The eight are rooms in this building that people work in. The ninth is a room at the bottom of four treads that nobody has walked and is not walked this month.

"And here is what I want said at the fifth bell every day from today, in my own hand and in the air engineer's, because it is the only arithmetic in this description and it is the one I am frightened of.

"**Eight rooms in this building are kept because people work in them. One room on that page is not kept, and has a person in it anyway, and nobody has been near it, and the only thing anybody has ever done about it is walk over the top of it and call it water.**"

"Twice a day," said Dalla Sen, "and read out, and I will say it in the same breath as Mikka Hale's number and Renn Vale's seventh term, and I am not going to put a name against the ninth line, and if a name ever goes on it I want the person whose hand wrote it to be in the room when it does."
