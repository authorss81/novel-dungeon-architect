# Chapter 150 — The Question With Thirty People On It

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the twenty-ninth week, a Friday, and the school ran for the twenty-second morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and a boy of fourteen read sixteen numbers out loud in a room and said nothing about any of them, and a courier asked a question out loud that she had asked in this building before and that nobody had answered then and that nobody answered now, and the board was walked, and a clerk read a count of eighty-five at a bell and said it out loud, and a clerk asked a man a question in a room at the seventh bell and did not ask him twice.**

Twenty-second morning, and the nine went in at the fourth bell, and one of them went in sitting down, and the strip under the ninth hearth stone was holding, and the two flags had not moved, and Jo Verrick said the standing term out loud in the doorway and a physician said it out of his own hand against hers and Ilsa Norr said yes to it, and Dalla Sen entered that the term is the same in two hands, and entered that she had been entering that same sentence for four weeks and that she was not going to stop entering it on the day a page got read out to a stranger.

Open for five weeks, that room is, and it is open under a page with sixteen rows on it and no name anywhere, and a physician's refusal to certify it is in a clerk's book with his name against it, and a girl of nine was on the fourth step of the anteroom with a strip of crate board round her foot and her bone where a physician put it and it had moved, and her name is Orla Kern and she is the fourth of the nine and she is on the page, and two people had said in a room that it had not moved and one of them had said so this week.

Pell Roke read the first column out loud at the second bell, because a column gets read when a number has gone into it, and there are four new numbers' worth of rows on that page and no new numbers, and the clerk had said what she was doing before she did it.

"**Sixteen, and four of them are new rows, and I am not going to tell you what the numbers are and you are not going to be told, and I want all sixteen in one breath because a number read on its own is a fact about a column and a number read in a list is a fact about a page, and the room has heard me say that once before and it did not improve anybody and I am saying it again anyway."

"**Ten. Four. Eight. Ten. Six. Nine. Five. Two. And a dash. And ten. And four. And six. And ten. And ten. And ten. And ten."

Nothing in the room filled in after it, and nobody asked him about any of the sixteen, and four of them are the same number and he said them in the same voice as the rest of them.

"**The second column."

"**Sixteen conditions with no number and no day of the week on any of them, and the tenth is a condition that says the strip under the ninth hearth stone is still holding, and it is said out loud in a room every morning this room is used and four mornings a week in an empty room."

"**The third column."

"**Fifteen lines filled and one empty," he said, "**and I am not going to say which one is empty because the man who keeps it has said in a room that he is not going to say, and I read numbers and that is the end of what I do."

"**The fourth column."

He looked at it, and it was empty, and it had been empty when he read it a month ago, and he had said four words about it then that are on a page in a clerk's hand.

"**There is nothing in it."

**At the third bell a courier asked a question out loud in a room, and she said before she asked it that she had asked it in this building before and that nobody had answered it then, and that she was not going to be insulted by that.**

"**I asked this room a question on the fifth day of the twenty-seventh week at the seventh bell and nine people were in it and nobody answered me, and the air engineer said she had half of it and would not give it, and the clerk entered that the two questions are adjacent and not the same and that she had been asked to put them next to each other and would not, and that they are next to each other in the book by accident and not by hand."

"**And I am not going to be one of the ones who asks a thing twice to be answered twice, so I am going to say what has changed since, and it is not me and it is not her, and it is a piece of paper."

"**On Tuesday thirty-one people from this Hearthhold went up a market road as ordinary residents with nobody at the front of it, and I walked at the back with a bundle that was not mine, and no clerk on that road asked anybody's name. And on Tuesday afternoon a woman at a counter took a form off a board because my mark is not a holder and cannot be entered in a register, and she said in four other sentences that the board is a courtesy, that the seal stands, and that the office does not know the sheet was on the board, and that it will be back in a month by a clerk who does not know about Tuesday."

"**So I have got a road. That is what has changed. I have been asking this question for two years about a road I have never had anybody else's people on, and on Tuesday I had thirty-one of them on it and I had them on it as ordinary residents and not as a load, and the form came off the board at the end of it, and I am not going to pretend that the second thing happened because of the first thing, because it did not, and a competent woman took a piece of paper off a board because she could not use it."

"**Here is the question and I am going to ask it exactly as I asked it the first time, because I have changed twice this month and I am not going to change the wording."

"**Can a route be a public good when the people who use it live on different sides of the law?"

Nine people were in that anteroom and the corridor was open behind them and the school had run a bell at the far end of it, and nobody answered it.

"**I have half of it," said Nera Oduya, from the second step.

Nothing in that room filled in after it, the way nothing had filled in after it a fortnight before, and nothing filled in after the question either.

"**And I am going to say the same three sentences I said then, because they are the same three sentences and I have not got a fourth one and I am not going to manufacture one for a room that has been good to me."

"**I have had half of it since the fourth day of the eleventh week. Half of what I know is about a room. What she has asked is about a road. The two are adjacent and not the same and half of my half does not answer it, and I would like that entered in those words and entered exactly."

"**My condition is not met. It was approached in the twenty-fourth week and it was approached again in the twenty-sixth and it has not been met, and a plan with sixteen rows in it and a river on a fill at a waist is not the thing that meets it, and anybody in this room who thinks a good month is going to meet it should look at what this boundary did in a bad one, which was write a page, refuse three rows, and let a woman strike a line out of it in nine seconds without giving a reason."

"**And nobody has asked me. Nobody is going to ask me. I would like the clerk to enter that a room in which somebody could have asked me sat with nine people in it for a minute and a half and did not."

Dalla Sen entered all of it, and entered the question in the asker's own words and read it out twice, and entered that it was asked in a room and not answered in a room, and then she said the part that was hers and had been hers for a fortnight.

"**And entered that she asked me a fortnight ago to put two questions next to each other and I would not, and that they are next to each other in this book by accident and not by hand, and that this morning nobody asked me to do it again, and that a clerk who has spent twenty-eight weeks putting a reason beside two facts and nothing between them on purpose is not going to be the one who joins two questions together on a Friday morning in the twenty-ninth week because a road came good."

"**And I am entering that it came good. A route was used by an ordinary resident of this Hearthhold on Tuesday and the form came off a board and the office conceded nothing in a word, and this boundary has decided it will not fill those two lines in, and nobody in this room has changed its mind about that and nobody is going to, and that is not a victory and a courier said so in this room yesterday and I am entering her sentence because I would like it in my book in her words in case a person in a month reads it and thinks we won something."

"**And a week is not a month. A week is not a month. I am writing it twice because I want the second one to be the one a person reads."

**At the sixth bell the board was walked, and it has sixteen entries on it now, and the column is still unheaded, and the count gave no new reason and said why in one sentence.**

The chalk was in his left hand and the right one was open and flat in his lap, as it has been since the eleventh week, and he read both columns and named no day and gave no reason for not naming one, and he said the base under both columns was not going to be re-derived in the last week of anything.

"**Two today and a hundred and sixty-five since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and eighty-two and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks, and I have walked that board for twenty-nine weeks and I have never once re-derived that base and I am not going to start in the last week of a volume."

"**The crew are at sixty-three and a half. Asked three times in twenty-nine weeks, on a named asking, in a room, with the terms out loud, and a person named who could be told no, and the three who could not be asked named with the reason for each before the asking, all three times, and the reasons were not the same reasons twice."

**Sixteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, a plan and a condition. Nine in the ordinary column and seven in the one with nothing over the top of it, and the condition is in there because it was tested and it held, and it is the first thing anybody in this building has put on that board that is a sentence somebody can stop.

"**And the column is not headed. Eighth batch, and I gave the eighth reason on the first day of the twenty-eighth week and I said that a man who has eight reasons and gives the seventh is not being careful, he is being lazy, and I am not going to give a ninth reason this week, and Dalla is not going to ask me, and if she does I am going to say the week getting away from me and she has that in her hand already."

Dalla Sen read the sixteen entries out and read her own reading of them back and entered that the count had said the base was not going to be re-derived, and entered that she was not going to ask him.

"**The pot is ten and has not gone up since the fifth day of the nineteenth week. The jars are two a day and are never added to the pot. The water is on the fill at the waist and it has not been off it since the Thursday of the twenty-sixth week and it is not moving, and there is still no number for that fill in this building and there is not going to be one, and three people have now given that reason out loud in a room and it is in this clerk's book in three hands and the order is not a finding, it is three people agreeing to be frightened in public."

"**The grate at the low end is off and there is no second one. Two flags and a length of the crew's own spent line are over that channel, and the line is the crew's and it is spent and it is not an hour and it never was, and I entered a man's two and a half days in the boundary's column instead and I gave the reason out loud on Friday and I am not going to give it again this week."

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-five days and eighty-five nights into a count that is his own. I said on the second day of the twenty-sixth week that it was not going to stop and that I was going to keep writing it, and I have written it every day for four weeks, and it is eighty-five, and it is not going to be eighty-six, and I am going to keep writing it, and a man from a surface federation read it out of my book on Thursday and did not say a kind thing about it, and I have entered that I was glad, and this is the second time I have entered that and I am not going to make a practice of it."

---

**At the seventh bell the clerk asked a man a question in a room and did not ask him twice, and nobody in that room answered it either, and she entered it in the shape of a question and not in the shape of a finding.**

"**You said on the fourth day of this month that you were not a post and that no page was going to get your name on it," said Dalla Sen, "**and I entered it, and I read it out in this room on Tuesday, and nobody argued with it. And I am going to ask you one thing and I am going to ask it in a room and in front of nine people, because I have been a clerk in this building for twenty-nine weeks and I have found that a question asked in a corridor is a different animal from a question asked in a room, and I have not asked you this one and I am not going to ask you it twice."

"**What are you going to do when the weather turns?"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything, and Marek Venn did not answer it, and the pump got three strokes in, and Dalla Sen did not ask him again.

"**Entered, and read once, and I am not reading it twice because there is nothing in it to compare. Entered that I asked him what he is going to do when the weather turns, in a room, at the seventh bell, with nine people in it, and that he did not answer, and that I did not ask him twice, and that I am not going to enter a reason for either of those, and that anybody in this room who wants to supply one is going to have to put their own name against it and Dalla Sen is not going to do it for them."

**On the fifth day of the twenty-ninth week of a Hearthhold under a street, in a corridor that is shut on the first, second and fourth days of a week and open on the third, fifth and sixth, and on the seventh day of which it is not a crossing day and nobody will know what is on the floor of it.**

**A schoolroom at the kitchen-line end with nine children in it, one of them sitting down, on nine hearth stones with one of the nine propped on a strip of crate board and two flags, and the fire in the corner of that room not lit for nine weeks, and the light in it off nine stones.**

**A room that was shut in the twenty-fourth week with eleven people in it, and is open now, and is open under a page of sixteen rows with a date in the top corner and no name anywhere on it and no signature anywhere on it at all, a first column that has never held a count of anybody, a second column with no number and no day on any of the sixteen, a third column in fifteen hands and one line empty that the man who keeps it has said in a room he is not going to name, and a fourth column empty and unheaded with a reason in it in two hands and a modal struck out of one of them; four rows of that page holding a network, and one number at the end of a chain, and the end of the chain is a word.**

**A corridor at the low end with two flags and a length of the crew's own spent line over a channel with no grate in its frame, and a page in a clerk's book that says somebody stands at the low end, which is blank, and has been blank for twenty-nine weeks, and carries three conditions and the third of them says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and nobody has been asked, and nobody has been sent, and nobody is going near that floor, and the condition was broken and the page is still blank and the two facts sit next to each other in a clerk's hand on purpose with nothing between them.**

**A mark on the outer service doorframe of the anteroom, which is off the wall, has been off the wall since the twenty-third week, and was declared stopped in a room before anybody stood in a channel, and is not going back on, and a carpenter has said in a room that he would not put it there.**

**Four damaged marks and one undamaged groove on a lintel above the open mouth of a school corner that has not been used since the twenty-second week, read two ways by two people, one of the four marks being two strokes, and it has answered neither of them, and no mark has been cut in the stone anywhere in this building, and nobody has been on the four treads and nobody is going to be, and the ninth line is a room four steps below the far side of a landing with a one and a *none* and no work and it has no row on the page and it is not in the charter and it is not on the second sheet.**

**A road open that cannot be shut for a delivery, four days up the middle passage and forty days on from the top of it to a guild file, a halfpenny sheet at a store four days away with a hundred copies of it and three other Hearthholds expected to accept, a form off a board at a gate four days up that road and a board still standing and a seal still on the road and a sheet that a clerk will put back in a month because she does not know about Tuesday, and a courier's own sheet with four names on it going the other way with a woman who is owed a spring by nobody and was not paid it as a gift, and a form with two lines and a space in each of them that this building has decided it will not fill in and has three times refused to fill in in front of people from another office.**

**A brass survey rule in a coat on a hook that has said nothing for longer than sixteen weeks, and a wrapped plate on a shelf above a bench that has been unopened for twenty-nine weeks, and six pages with no date on them going up a passage with a competent man who is right about the first column, and four households at a second gate up the middle passage who have nothing, and a water-lock shut for a survey of a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood that nobody has said how long will take.**

**A board with sixteen entries on it, nine in the ordinary column and seven in the column with nothing over the top of it, and no heading, and a crew that has been asked three times in twenty-nine weeks with the terms out loud, and a room whose condition is checked every morning it is used and four mornings a week in an empty room by a woman saying a sentence to a building about a stone nobody is standing near.**

**A count in the column with nothing over the top of it, kept by a clerk, at eighty-five, and not going to be eighty-six.**

**Under a lip there is still a person, packed and not held, with two timber shores on a common landing above it and a post under it that has never been built.
