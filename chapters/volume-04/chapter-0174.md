# Chapter 174 — The Column For A Person

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the thirty-fourth week, a Tuesday, and the school did not run and lost nothing, and a printed schedule came down the middle passage in a carrier's hand with four columns on it and the second of them headed for the person who will be present, and this time nobody struck anything out of it, and the room read it against a document of its own instead, and a second person compared the two.**

**The third room on the sheet was named out loud in that anteroom, and it is a room nine children are taught in. Nobody wrote a name in the column. The air engineer was argued with in her own mouth by a man who had been in the building two days, and she did not concede, and she set a second condition, and nobody asked her about it in a room.**

The carrier came up on foot at the second bell and said what he was before anybody asked him, and put a printed sheet face up on the anteroom table, and put his hand flat on it the way a man does when he is about to say that a thing on it is not his.

"**Pettin Rahl's office, second printing. It came down the passage with a load of nothing else and nobody met me on the road, which I am told is unusual and I have not checked.**"

"**Say what is on it before anybody touches it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Four columns. Time, place, the thing to be looked at, and the second column is headed for the person who will be present.**" He took his hand off it. "**Three rooms under it. Two are struck. One is not.**"

"**Say the covering on that sheet,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it before anybody reads a word of the rest, and say it flat, and do not soften it, because if I am going to ask you a question about it in a room I would rather you had said it first.**"

"**The covering is struck,**" said the carrier. "**It was struck before the sheet came into this room. It is struck clean, in a hand that is not the printer's, and there is a room named on the sheet under it, and I was told by the man who put the sheet in my hand that I was not to ask about it and not to look at it and to bring it, and I have brought it.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, and she did not write for about four seconds. "**Entered that the covering was struck before this sheet came into this room, and that it was struck by somebody, and that nobody in this building struck it. I will find out nothing about who and ask nothing. There is no reason in the space where a reason would go, and this is the ninth time I have entered that, and the count of them in this book is not a number I read out.**"

Nobody asked. A man who keeps a page looked at the sheet for a while and then said, out loud, that he had a question about it, and Dalla Sen said that he did not, and he said that he did, and she said that she had heard him, and he put the question away, and Dalla Sen entered that.

Then she did the thing that made the morning a fact in a room instead of an argument about a stranger's paper.

"**Nobody is going to strike anything on that sheet,**" she said. "**A woman struck a room out of a printed schedule in this room on the fourth day of the twenty-ninth week in nine seconds and gave no reason, and I have written in this book since then that striking a thing is a lie with a line through it, and it does not get done today either, and not because the second printing is different. It does not get done because this boundary is not going to be the people who make a document shorter.**"

"**Then what are we going to do with it,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because doing nothing with a printed schedule is how a thing becomes this boundary's practice, and you have spent a month teaching me that difference.**"

"**We are going to read it against a document.**" She went to the shelf and came back with the coat, and Bel Ordry took the coat out of her hands, which he has not done in this room, and neither of them said anything about it. "**The plan is four parts in sixteen rows and the second column of every row is conditions with no number and no day on them, and they were written by the people who keep the rooms, and all sixteen of them are filled, and I have never once read them out in a room and neither am I today.**"

"**Then what is the second column for,**" said the carrier.

"**That is the whole of it, and you have asked it in about four seconds and I would like a moment.**" She put the two sheets down side by side, the printed one and four pages of a plan on a table, and squared them, and her hands were not steady and nobody mentioned it. "**A column headed for the person who will be present is filled in before the day. It is filled in by somebody who is not in the room. It says a name and the name goes and stands somewhere, and if the name does not go then a person finds out about it afterwards from a piece of paper, which is the machine. Our second column is never filled in before anything, and it is written by the person who keeps the room, and it has no name in it, and nobody can tell from either of them who is supposed to be there.**"

"**Then they are not the same instrument,**" said Nera Oduya, from the third step, "**and a clerk who has just put them side by side has found a difference and not a contradiction, and I would like it said that way.**"

"**Say the half against it, and then somebody who is not me reads both of them out loud and says whether that is the same as what I have said,**" said Dalla Sen.

Jo Verrick read the printed column and the plan's second column out loud, both of them, in that order, and then said the comparison, and it was hers and not Dalla Sen's.

"**They are both columns and one of them is for a person and one of them is for a wall, and they are not the same, and the difference is that the printed one is filled in by somebody who is not going to be there.**"

"**Entered that the second person said it and I said it first and hers is better and shorter,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And entered that a comparison in this room is not a finding, and that I am entering both words, the word I used and the word she used.**"

"**And entered that nobody wrote a name in that column today and nobody is going to, and that the page in a coat is the only instrument in this building with a column for a person in it, and that it is empty, and that it is going to stay empty.**"

Marek Venn had been standing at the end of the table with his own book shut for an hour. The thing he had been waiting for had arrived, and it was a column with a heading over it and a space in it where a person went, and it was a piece of paper four days up a road, and there was nothing in the Lattice that could do anything about it.

That was new. Thirty-four weeks in this building and the difficulty had always been inside the walls. A wall was a thing you could read, a shim was a thing you could cut, a room was a thing you could empty into the next one.
A column on a printed sheet is none of those, and the only instrument in this building that has ever had to meet one is a person with a page in a coat. He opened his own book, shut it again without writing in it, and said nothing at all for an hour.


Then the carrier said the third room, because he had been asked to and because he had said he would.

"**The third room is the one at the kitchen-line end. It is the one a printed sheet calls a schoolroom. It is the third of three and it is the one that was not struck, and I am reading it off the sheet and I would like a room to know that I read it off the sheet and not out of a list of my own.**"

The room did not make a sound. A woman who keeps that room was standing at the kitchen-line doorway with a slate in her hand and had been in the anteroom since the first bell, and nobody had thought to move her and nobody moved her now.

"**Say it again,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The room at the kitchen-line end,**" said the carrier. "**On the printed sheet. In the second column. Not struck.**"

"**Entered, and entered that it is a fact in a room and not a revelation. And entered that it is a room nine children are taught in on the three days the corridor is open, and that it has a wall on the other side with a brace in it. And entered that this boundary has not been asked about it by anybody, because nobody has come up the passage and asked it anything.**"

"**Are you going to write a row for it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**No,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The plan has sixteen rows and there is no name anywhere on it, and the one line in it that is empty is the keeper's and he will not fill it, and I am not going to fill it for him this week either. And a row for that room is not a row this week. It gets no row, and it is not going to get one because a piece of paper four days up a road has printed it.**"

"**Say the other half, because nobody in this room is going to be kind to me,**" said Mero Senn, from the doorway. "**I have refused in writing to certify that room and I stand in the same anteroom as the clerk, and neither of those things is going to keep a printed sheet from having it in a column, and I would like that said this morning by a person and not found out in a month.**"

"**Say the whole of it, then, and I will enter all of it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Say that a room with nine children in it is now in two documents in this building, one of them ours and one of them theirs, and that a person is not safer for being written down twice, and that I will not be the man who says the sheet is wrong, because the sheet has not claimed anything about that room. It has put a space where a person could go.**"

Nobody filled the space. The carrier looked at the column, then at the woman in the doorway, then at the column again. A man reads a column on a printed sheet four hundred times a year in his ordinary work.

Marek Venn watched him decide not to write one word in it, and understood that he was watching a competent man being told no by a room and taking the no. That has happened in this anteroom perhaps six times in thirty-four weeks, and never once to a stranger.

Then Sefu Aran said the thing he had come up the steps to say, and he said it to the room and not to the clerk, and he had thought about it for two days, which Dalla Sen entered.

"**Say it in your own words and do not soften it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say whether you are arguing with me or with the sheet.**"

"**Neither. I am arguing with the word *keeping*.**" He had his hands loose and he was looking at the four columns. "**This boundary has kept a brace it built. It has kept a store it does not own, and a man who keeps that store is four days up a passage. It has kept a road it does not control, and a form on a board four days up that road. And it has kept a keeper it did not have, which is me, and I was not here for any of it, and I would like that in your book in those words, and it does not count for anything, and I am not pretending it does.**"

"**Say the four of them, and then say what follows from them, because that is a clerk's shape and a man who comes back is allowed one,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**What follows from them is that this boundary is keeping itself. And a boundary that is keeping itself is a boundary that can be shown to be the kind of thing a road can be asked of. That is the whole of my argument and I have had it in my mouth since Friday and I have been waiting for a piece of paper to put it next to.**"

Nera Oduya came down the three steps, all of them, and stopped on the anteroom floor.

"**Then I am going to answer it, and I am going to answer it in the first person, and a room has been taught that by me for thirty-four weeks, so.**"

"**Say it as a person who is right and not as a person who is being difficult,**" said Renn Vale.

"**I am going to be right.**" She had the valve board against her hip and she was not holding it like a person holding a thing to work with. "**Keeping itself and being asked to open a road while something is going wrong four days up a passage are not the same sentence. They are two sentences and the second one has a hole in it the shape of a store with no fir left in it, and nobody hurries me into the first sentence with a printed sheet that has four columns on it and no dates.**"

"**Say the four households, and say the gate, and do not put a figure on the gate,**" said Bel Ordry, and his voice was not steady either, and nobody mentioned that.

"**Four households at a second gate have nothing. They have had nothing since a water-lock was shut for a survey of a sill, and the survey has not been finished, and nobody has said how long. I will say no length of time either, because the moment I put a length of time on that gate it is a schedule, and I do not want it to be a schedule.**" She put the board down on the table, which she has not done in that room. "**And a boundary with a pot at nine days and four households at a second gate with nothing is not the sentence in my condition. My condition says *when this boundary is keeping itself* and I wrote it in the eleventh week and I have watched it get closer for eight weeks and it is not the same thing, and it will not be pretended to in order to get it out of the way.**"

"**Then say what is the same thing, because a room has been living in the gap between your condition and the word keeping for a fortnight and somebody has to name the gap,**" said Renn Vale.

"**I am not naming the gap, and the reason is that the moment I name the gap I am the person who decides what is in it, and I have spent thirty-four weeks refusing to certify a room and I am not going to start handing out the parts of a sentence I did not write.**" She picked the board up. "**And here is the second condition, and I am saying it twice. The first time is a sentence and the second time is a fact. I will not certify a building that is keeping itself. I will certify a building that has said no to something it could have said yes to in the same week. A boundary that has said no to nothing has not been shown to be able to say it, and the room I have been standing outside of for a month is a room that has said yes to everything anybody ever put in front of it.**"

"**I will not certify a building that has said no to nothing.**"

"**Entered, twice, in her words, with no number and no day on it, and entered that it is her second condition and that the first one is not withdrawn and is not met,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And entered that nobody asked her. And entered that I have wanted to ask her every morning for a fortnight and have not, and that the not asking is the practice in this building and is not the same as not caring, and I would like somebody in a year to be able to tell those two apart.**"

Then she was wrong, and she said it before anybody else had got to it, which is what she has done once in this book and which nobody in that room was going to be gentle about the second time.

"**And I have been wrong about the air in that room and I found it out four minutes ago while I was standing here not saying anything,**" said Nera Oduya. "**I have set a limit on the air in a room and I have been carrying the number about for a fortnight, and I have never once been in that room on a morning when there were nine children in it.**"

Nobody spoke.

"**I have certified the air in a room the way you certify a thing you have to certify, which is in the morning, at the first bell, in an empty room, with the fire out, and nine children go into that room on the three days the corridor is open, and I have not been in it on one of those three days in eight weeks.**" She looked at the woman in the doorway. "**I have been certifying the air of a room that is not the room the nine of them are in. That is the sentence and it is a bad one and I am the person who wrote the limit, so it is mine.**"

"**Say the half against it,**" said the woman in the doorway, in the voice she uses for a condition.

"**The half against it is that a room with nine children in it and a fire out is a room with a different air from a room with nine children in it and a fire in it, and the fire has not been lit in eleven weeks, and I did not know that when I wrote the limit and I do know it now."**

"**So the number I have been carrying is the number for the room as it is and not the number for the room as it will be on Wednesday, and it does not follow that my number is wrong, and I am not going to be told that it is, by a room that wants to be comforted.**"

"**Nobody was going to tell you that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I will not, and entered in her words both halves in that order, and entered that the clerk has consoled her once in this book and will not twice, and that the second time she nearly did and looked at the first time instead.**"

The pot was still at nine days at the fourth bell, and the cook said the arithmetic out loud the way she does. It was worse than the arithmetic of a crew of five, because a printed sheet had been in that room for two hours and nobody in it had eaten anything.

The four households at a second gate still had nothing, and no figure was attached to that gate by anybody.

At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total in the book and read neither of them out, did not walk the board, and the count was read at one hundred and nine.

"**Three today and two hundred and thirty since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and forty-seven and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**I entered those and I did not read them, and the reason is that a printed sheet has been open on that table since the second bell with a column on it headed for a person, and a clerk does not read out a total in a room with a document like that on the table and pretend the total is the same kind of thing.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one. The pot is nine. The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and nine days and one hundred and nine nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and ten, and I have said in eighteen weeks running that I am going to keep writing it.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is that a sheet came down the passage with a column for a person on it, and I spent two hours this morning finding out that a board is a column with a heading over it, and a board does not get walked in the same day that a room has found out what the difference is.**"

**A printed schedule came down the middle passage with four columns on it and the second of them headed for the person who will be present, and this boundary struck nothing out of it.**

**A clerk put it side by side with the second column of a plan, and a pump maintainer read both out loud, and said they were not the same instrument, because the printed one is filled in by somebody who is not going to be there.**

**The third room on the sheet is the room at the kitchen-line end, in the second column, not struck, and nobody wrote a name in that column and nobody is going to, and the plan has sixteen rows and that room is not going to get a seventeenth.**

**And an elected keeper who had been in the building two days argued in a room that a boundary which keeps a brace, a store, a road and a keeper is a boundary that can be asked.**

**The air engineer said those are not the same sentence, and set the second condition she has set this month, and said it twice, and then said out loud, before anybody could be kind to her about it, that she has been certifying the air of a room she has not stood in on a morning nine children were in it. Nobody consoled her.**
