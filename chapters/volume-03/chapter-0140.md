# Chapter 140 — A Place A Person Can Be Killed

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the twenty-seventh week, a Friday, and the school ran for the eighteenth morning, and a man identified the second way out of this building and said out loud in a room that it is a place a person can be killed, and then a courier asked a question this boundary has been carrying since the first week of the twentieth week and nobody answered it, and the board was not walked, and a clerk read a count of seventy-five at a bell and said it out loud.**

**No row on the plan describes the low end of this corridor, and the refusal of that row is in a clerk's book with a reason in it and the reason is a physician's, and the reason for the refusal is why there was nobody standing at the low end of this corridor on the fourth day of the twenty-fifth week.**

He had it out on the anteroom table at the second bell and it was not a drawing, because there is no timber and there is no paper that is not butcher's paper and there is nobody in this building who can draw. It was a length of the spent nine-metre line laid on the floor of the anteroom from the anteroom step to the wash-room wall, and a lamp, and a piece of stone from the hearth, and Jo Verrick's own two scratches on it.

"**I am going to do this with a piece of rope and a lump of stone and I am going to say the reason out loud first, because the last thing anybody in this building did with a floor was a stove and it was the right call and it cost a girl of nine her foot."

"**This is the second way out of this building. It is the only one there is. The stair goes down to a landing and there is a slab over eleven feet that is packed and not held and there is a person under it and the post under that has never been built. The mouth in the school corner is open and has been since the first week of the twenty-sixth week and it is warm and it goes into the throat and nobody in this building has ever stood in it."

"**And this is the other one, and it is not a route and I am not going to call it a route in a room."

"**The channel at the low end of that corridor goes down about a foot and a half under the frame the grate came out of, and then it goes away toward the shaft. If a person went into that channel and went along it in the dark, at the lowest place in this building, with nothing to stand on, then they would be going underneath a corridor that has water standing on the fill at the waist and a foot of water round two flags, and they would be underneath eleven feet of corridor that nobody has ever put a load on and nobody has ever measured."

"**And here is why it is a place a person can be killed, and I am going to say all five of them and I am not going to shorten it into a reason to stop."

"**One: the water comes up the channel before it comes up the corridor, so anybody in that channel is a foot and a half lower than the floor, and the water in it does not have to be deep to kill a person. Two: there is no grate in that frame, so there is nothing on the floor of that corridor to say where the channel is, and I know where it is because I have a lamp and Jo Verrick's scratches, and a person at a market day would not. Three: a person holding that opening would be standing at the lowest point of a corridor that fills, under two flags and a length of the crew's own spent line, with no second grate in this Hearthhold and a wall in lime four days up the middle passage. Four: the page in this clerk's book that says somebody stands at the low end of this corridor has been blank for twenty-seven weeks, and the woman who wrote the two sentences on it has three conditions and the third one is that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and she has never been asked and is never going to be asked and nobody in this building is going near her floor to find out whether the conditions are met."

"**And five, and this is the one I found on Monday and did not understand until this morning."

"**Renn refused a row for that place. I asked for one and she said no and a woman with a sling found the shape of the reason and a physician gave the other half of it out loud and it went in a clerk's book. A row is a promise. A promise about a place where nobody can stand is a lie told in a column."

"**And I have been on the other side of that since Monday, and a promise about a place where nobody can stand is exactly how a place where nobody can stand became a place where a man of twenty-two went down into a channel in the dark to see where the water is."

"**So the refusal was right and the refusal is the reason, and I am not going to have anybody in this room take either half of that out.**"

For a while the room was quiet, and the pump got four strokes in, and a woman from the lower level said the sentence that made the whole of it stop.

"**Say the fifth one again,**" said Leena Orr, "**and then I am going to say one sentence, and it is mine, and I have not said it since the twentieth week, and I am not saying it because it is clever. I am saying it because a man has laid a length of rope on the floor of a room and pointed at it."

"**A list is not a location."

"**That is the whole of it and it has been sitting in this building for a fortnight with a reason in a clerk's book. You have got five reasons and they are good and every one of them is about a hole and about a body, and not one of them is a place a person can stand.**"

"**Then say what I should have said,**" said Marek Venn, "**and I will not defend the rope.**"

"**You should have said: a way along the channel is a place a person can be killed, and the only way to use it is to stand in it, and the only way to stand in it is to send somebody, and the third condition on that page says nobody is to be sent."

"**So there is no way out of this building and the honest thing is to say that on a Friday morning with a room full of people, and then to say the next thing, which is the thing I have not said and have been not saying since the first day of the twenty-fourth week."

"**We have one room with water in the floor that is being taught in, one room with a hole under a stone that nine children sit beside, one anteroom with a word for a second way out, one hole under a corridor floor with a dead man in it, and a waist that three people have agreed for twenty-seven weeks not to measure, and a wall in lime in a camp store in the month after next. That is the whole of this Hearthhold. And I am a schoolteacher and I want to say that a building like that is not a thing people live in. It is a thing they are in."

"**And the second thing, and it is worse, and I am going to say it because I am the one who keeps the room and I am not going to be a woman who has been brave about a floor and silent about a page."

"**There is a plan in a coat in this building with twelve rows on it and it has no row for that hole. And a boundary that writes down what it can promise, and keeps the list short, and puts a refusal in a book with a reason in it, has just found out that the place it did not promise anything about is the place a man died. And I do not have a better system than that and I am not sure anybody does, and I am not going to pretend the page is not a part of it.**"

---

**Eighteen mornings in, the school ran in a room with a re-adopted condition in the second column of its row and a strip of crate board under a propped hearth stone, and the strip was holding, and the fire was not lit, and the light in that room was off nine stones.**

**At the first bell the two of them stood in the doorway of the school corner, which is open and has been open since the first week of the twenty-sixth week and is not used and is not going to be used, and looked at the lintel above its mouth, which has four marks and a groove in it that are damaged.**

Renn Vale read the four marks out of the light, one at a time, and then Marek Venn read them again from under them at an angle, and they were not the same four marks.

"**Two of these are the same mark and I have read them both ways and the fifth stroke is a stroke and not a stop,**" said Renn Vale.

"**The fifth stroke is a stop,**" said Marek Venn, "**or it is the middle of a letter and there is no letter. I have read that lintel in this building for twenty-seven weeks and I have got two readings and I have got no third one, and I am not going to cut a mark in the stone to find out and I would like it entered that two people read it differently and that it has not answered either of us."

"**And the groove is a groove. It is older than the four marks and it goes under them and it is the only thing up there that is not damaged, and I have looked at it twice and I am not going to look at it a third time in a week, and there is a wrapped plate on a shelf in this anteroom that has been on that shelf for twenty-seven weeks and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it.**"

No mark was cut in the stone anywhere in this building, and nobody went down the four treads.

---

**A courier asked the question this boundary has been carrying since the first week of the twentieth week, in a room, out loud, and it was not answered, and the person who has half of it was in the room and did not give the other half.**

She said it at the seventh bell with nine people in the anteroom and the market day in the morning after, and she said it to the room and not to the man who had spent the morning on a hole in a floor.

"**I am going to ask a question and I am not going to answer it and I am not going to pretend the question is about the water."

"**Can a route be a public good when the people who use it live on different sides of the law?"

"**I have been running a road for two years for people who do not have a permit and a post and a name anybody can send for, and my answer to it every day of those two years is yes, and I have never once had it tested, and it is about to be tested tomorrow at a gate where there is a form with two lines and a space in each of them, and this morning there is a woman in this room who has answered a question of mine in a way I did not expect and I want to say that before I ask, because she has not been thanked and I did not thank her either and I noticed at the time."

"**So. The question. Can a route be a public thing when the people who use it are people a form is written for?**"

"**I have half of that,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps up.

Nothing in the room filled in after it.

"**I have had half of it since the fourth day of the eleventh week and I have said so out loud in this room once and did not say it. And I am not going to say it now, and nobody in this room is going to ask me, and Dalla has that in her hand in my own words and it is a decision and not a fact."

"**And I am going to say the only part that is mine to say about the question she has just asked, and it is this: half of what I know is about a room, and what she has asked is about a road, and the two are not the same and half of my half does not answer it. I would like that read back and I would like it read back exactly, because somebody in this room is going to want to put the two next to each other and the clerk has been putting things next to each other on purpose for twenty-seven weeks and I am asking her to do it this once, and to say in the book that she did it and what she did."

"**And I am not going to be hurried. My condition is not met, I have said what it is in a room before and I am not going to say it again today, and a plan with twelve rows in it and a river of water at the waist is not the thing that meets it, and anybody who thinks a bad month is going to meet it should look at what this boundary did in a good month, which was put a row on a page and refused three and let a woman strike a line out in nine seconds.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**the question, in the asker's own words, and read twice. Entered that it was asked in a room and not answered in a room. Entered that the air engineer has said she has half of it and did not give it and that her condition is not met and that nobody asked her."

"**And entered that the two questions are adjacent and not the same, and that I have been putting a reason beside two facts and nothing between them for twenty-seven weeks and that this time I have been asked to put two questions next to each other and I am not going to, and that they are next to each other in this book by accident and not by hand, and I would like whoever reads this in a year to notice that and to do with it what they think is right.**"

It was not answered. The pump's check came off. The market day was tomorrow and the seventh day is not a crossing day.

---

**On the fifth day of the twenty-seventh week the board was not walked, and the clerk entered that she had not walked it, and the count gave the reason from the bench without being asked and entered it himself, and neither of them improved it.**

"**I am not walking it today and I am not giving a reason and Dalla is not going to ask me for one and if she does I will say the week getting away from me again and she has that in her hand already."

"**The column is not headed. Seven batches. The plan is in it and it is the second thing on that board that is not about a wall, and I have got nothing to add to it today and the day to be frightened is the day I have something to add to it and not the day I have nothing, and I have said the second half of that out loud once and I am not going to say it again this week.**"

Dalla Sen entered it in his words and read it out once, and did not read it a second time, and entered that she had read it once.

"**And at the sixth bell the clerk read the six things and the day's number and the count, and read the last of it twice, and the room did not fill in after it, and nobody asked her anything, and the week had gone on without any of it being forgiven.**"

"**Three today and a hundred and thirty-seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and fifty-four and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks, and the base under both columns is a hundred and seventeen and a quarter and it has been that every week since the first week of the twentieth week and I have not re-derived it once."

"**The crew are at fifty-nine and a half, and they have been asked twice in twenty-seven weeks, on a named asking in a room with the terms out loud and a person named who could be told no, and the three who could not be asked were named with the reason for each, and the column has not moved since the second day of this month."

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two a day and are never added to the pot. The water is on the fill at the waist and there is no number for that fill in this building. The grate at the low end is off and there is no second one and two flags and a length of spent line are over the hole, and the line is the crew's and it is spent and it is not an hour and it is not in the column."

"**And the count."

"**Wen Pask is seventy-five days and seventy-five nights into a count that is his own."

"**And I said on the second day of last month that it was not going to stop and that I was going to keep writing it, and I have written every day since and I am writing it now, and there is a man in this building who has spent a fortnight being right about a page and a hole in a floor and he is right about both, and I want that in the book next to this number, and not joined to it.**"

---

**In the anteroom, on the anteroom table, in a coat four hands down a wall in a room nobody was in, was a plan on butcher's paper in four parts with a date in the top corner and no name anywhere, and twelve rows, and a first column that had never held a count of anybody, and a second column with no number and no day on any of the twelve, and a third column with eleven lines filled and one empty that the man who keeps it will not name, and a fourth column empty and unheaded with a reason on it in two hands and a modal struck out of one of them; and one row with three hands on it and an objection written above a proposal; and a plan that five people refused to sign and which moved about forty people out of two rooms in fifty minutes on a Thursday morning.**

**In the school corner a mouth stood open and warm with four damaged marks and an undamaged groove in the lintel above it that two people read differently, and no mark was cut in the stone anywhere in this building, and nobody went down the four treads.**

**And a girl of nine was on the fourth step with her foot across a strip of crate board bound with a piece of an old apron, and a physician had said her bone is where he put it and it has moved and two people have not said in a room that it has not moved.**

**And a brass survey rule was in a coat on a hook and had said nothing for longer than fifteen weeks, and a wrapped plate was on a shelf above the bench and had been unopened for twenty-seven weeks, and a stair went down to a landing with two timber shores on it and a slab over eleven feet that was packed and not held, and a person was still under it, and the post under that had never been built.**

**And four households at a second gate up the middle passage had nothing, and a water-lock was shut for a survey of a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood that nobody had said how long would take, and nobody in this building had been asked and nobody was going to be.**

**And in the morning there was the market day, the sixth day of this week, and a board at a gate on a road had two lines and a space in each of them, and this boundary would not fill them in, and had not decided what it would do instead, and the only other way out of this building is under a corridor floor at the lowest point in it, where a man of twenty-two went down in the dark to see where the water is, and where a page in a clerk's hand has been blank for twenty-seven weeks with three conditions on it and no name against any of them, and where a woman has never been asked and will never be sent for and whose floor nobody is going near.**
