# Chapter 119 — The Office Writes A Rule

**The fourth day of the twenty-third week was shut for the corridor and the woman with the permit came back up the middle passage with a form in her hand, and the corridor had been shut for three weeks and it was the first day in this Hearthhold that two things from outside it were in the same room, and neither of them was a person who had come to take anything.**

Hollis Ashe introduced herself again before anybody asked, because that is who she is, and she put her tally on the table and left it there, and she said the thing she had said the first time before she said anything else.

"**My permit does not cover this. I have not obtained anything that covers this. I have written a procedure where I work and it does not apply to you and I am bringing it to you anyway, and I want to say the next part out loud because it is the part that matters. I have not got a power over this room. I have got a form with four lines on it and a man who saw something and wrote it down in a book, and neither of those things is a power and both of them are permanent.**"

"**Then say what he saw,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**A mark. On the dressed face of a doorframe, at the height of a hand, a line of chalk and a smear of the grease a pump gland puts on a shaft, about a foot long, made on the second day of this week by a person who is not on my list and is not on any list, and it was on the stone at eleven o'clock on Tuesday and it was gone on Wednesday evening, and a road hand going to a survey camp stood in a doorway for four seconds and looked at it, and he is a good hand, and he wrote down what he saw, which is what he is for.**"

"**Which is your form,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Which is my form. One. An inscription, bearing or otherwise, observed on a structure within a boundary beneath a surface inspection line. Two. The date observed. Three. The condition under which it acts, in the observer's words or the holder's. Four. The limit under which it acts, in the same words. Five. The person who keeps it.**"

Not one person in that room said anything for about four seconds, and one of those seconds was the clerk finding that she had written *the holder's* in her own ledger, on the ninth page, in a hand that was hers, from the second day of the week before last, when she was copying Bel Ordry's four sentences out fair for the second time and had mistyped a word.

"**Strike it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I am not striking it, I am telling you it is there and it is in a book that does not go up a road and I am going to enter it and enter that I found it and did not find it on purpose.**"

"**Enter both,**" said Renn Vale. "**Enter the word and enter that the clerk found it herself and nobody sent her looking. And then say the other thing out loud, because it is the other thing and not the word.**"

---

**The air engineer's nine seconds happened before anybody had finished the sentence, and this time she did not explain, because she had explained twice.**

**Near the bottom of the form was a line headed for rooms and fittings in the structure under inspection,** and Hollis Ashe's clerk had filled it in, in a small competent hand, with the anteroom, the anteroom's outer service door, its frame, and — on the same line, after a bracket — *the wash rooms.*

Nera Oduya read the form upside down from three steps, on her way past, and said one word, and Dalla Sen watched her do it and did not get a full sentence out of her.

"**Out,**" said Nera Oduya.

"**That is a line on a form put in by my office and it is a line my office put in correctly,**" said Hollis Ashe.

"**Yes. Out.**"

The question was not asked twice. Bel Ordry did ask her why, and she looked at him, and he put his hand down.

"**I struck it out in the last week of the eighteenth week and I struck it out again in the second week of this month, and I gave the reason both times, and both times nobody in either room could produce one sentence in which anybody in this building has ever asked her. I am not going to give the reason a third time in front of a stranger. It is not that it is dangerous to write down. It is that this building has never once asked a woman for one thing and I am not going to let a form on a road be the thing that makes us look like we have started.**"

She took the clerk's chalk out of Dalla Sen's hand — Dalla Sen let her — and struck the bracket and the two words under it, in Nera Oduya's own hand, in nine seconds, and gave it back, and went up three steps, and did not say anything else about it, and Dalla Sen entered that it was the third time and entered that the air engineer had not given the reason a third time and entered that she had been asked why by the keeper of the plan and had not answered him.

---

**Then Hollis Ashe did the thing this building will be arguing about for a month, and she did it because she is competent and bounded and not a villain, and she did it in about eleven seconds, and nobody stopped her.**

On the anteroom table, face up, was the market route's form.

Marek Venn stood on the anteroom step and listened to a competent woman from another office say out loud that she had no standing in anything on that table, and he understood the whole of the sentence, and the whole of the sentence was a thing about himself that a stranger had said in a room for nothing.

**The mark had been off the wall for a day and a half and it was already in a book four days up the middle passage, with a date on it that was a small lie and a post on it that a road was going to come looking for, and none of that had happened to him. It had happened to a boundary.** He had put grease on a stone with a hand that will not close, and a woman had handed him a sentence to say, and neither of those facts had travelled, and something smaller than either of them had, and it had travelled because it was written down in a place he was not.

It had been there since the second day of this week. Dalla Sen had put it there and had said out loud, before anybody asked her, that she had chosen to put it there. It was a printed sheet with seven lines on it and a clerk's mark, and it had a line for a name and a line for a person responsible for the load and a line for a signature.

Hollis Ashe read it. All of it, standing up, at the table, without being asked and without touching it, in the amount of time it takes to read a page.

Not one person in that room stopped her.

"**This is not mine,**" she said. "**I want that said first and by somebody else, because I have been in a room for a month where nothing that happened to me was mine. I have never seen this before this morning and I have no office in it and no part in it and no standing in it, and I am going to read it out in a room where it was left out on a table on purpose, and I am going to say that I understand why it was left out on a table on purpose, and I am not going to pretend I do not.**"

"**Say the part that matters,**" said Renn Vale.

"**The part that matters is that it is readable, and it has been readable for two days, and there is one of me and there are offices on this road with a great many more, and the moment a form like that is left face up on a table in a room where a person from another office is standing, it stops being yours.**

"**And I did not come in here and take it off the table. I want that entered, because I could have and I did not, and the reason I did not is that it is not my table and it is not my form and I have spent sixteen years learning that the day a competent person takes another institution's paper off another institution's table is the day both institutions are wrong.**"

Then Dalla Sen said the sentence the morning turns on, and she said it standing, in the flat voice, and it was not an apology and it was not a defence.

"**I chose to put it there,**" she said. "**On the second day, out loud, before anybody asked, and I have said that three times in three days and I am going to say it a fourth time and then I am going to stop saying it, because I have understood this week what the sentence is for. It is for me. A rule in a drawer is a rule nobody has to look at, and I said that, and I was talking about our four sentences and I was talking about my own comfort and not about a table. And the four sentences are in a coat and the market form was on the table and I did not see the difference and I have been the clerk of this building for twenty-two weeks.**"

"**Then put it away,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**No,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I am not putting it away. I chose to put it face up and I am going to leave it face up, and I am going to write on the back of the day-board in my own hand that a woman from another office has read it in this room and that nobody stopped her, and I am going to put the reason beside it and nothing between it, and the reason is: this boundary is going to be read from outside whether it is on a table or in a drawer, and the only thing it gets to choose is whether a stranger finds it in a room with people in it.**"

---

**And the mark was asked about, and the answer was a condition and a limit and no name, and the clerk wrote all three out in words, and Hollis Ashe read her own form back and filled in three of the five lines in the room in front of everybody.**

"**Condition,**" said Dalla Sen, reading her own hand, "**and I am reading the room's words and not mine: the mark is on while the head of that frame is out of true, and it is off when the head has not been further out of true than a line on the jamb, through a night and a morning, and the woman who keeps that room's terms takes it off and nobody argues with her about it.**

"**Limit, and these are the air engineer's words and a man said them out loud in a room on the second day of this week and the clerk has them: it carries the head of that frame and the clay packed in it and nothing else, and the head of that frame is carrying nothing, and the mark is being asked to carry the absence of a load. It holds that head where it is. If the head ever takes a real weight — a stone, a beam, a man, a bar — then it is not holding, and everything in this sentence is out of date.**

"**And it is stopped. It came off that jamb at the sixth bell yesterday in front of eleven people and it is not on the stone and the head is a finger and a bit out of true and the second way out of that room is a word.**"

Hollis Ashe wrote all three lines down in a hand that was not in a hurry, and then she put her pen down beside the form, and said:

"**Three of my five lines are now filled and they are the three that are about the work, and my office will be satisfied, and my office is a good office and I am going to recommend that this is closed.**

"**And I am going to tell this room what my office is going to do about the other two, because I would rather it heard it from me at a table than worked it out in a month.**"

"**Say it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Line two is a date. I put the date in because I am required to and because I would put it in. It is the only line on that form that is a lie and it is a small one.**

"**And line five is the person who keeps it, and I have not filled it in, and here is why, and it is the whole reason I came up that passage instead of sending it.**"

Her hand went flat on the empty line, without writing on it.

"**I could fill it with a name. There is one man in this Hearthhold who put that mark there and nine people in this room know which one and I know which one, and I did not write it on this form and I am not going to, and I would like the room to understand that I did not write it because of your charter and not because of my office.**

"**And there is a second thing I could fill it in with that is worse, and my clerk has already filled it in once, on a field sheet, in a camp four days up the passage, and I did not see it until this morning. He has written *the person who keeps the anteroom's terms.* That is not a name. That is a title, and a title sounds careful, and a title is worse than a name, because a name is a person and a title is a post, and a post can be asked for by a body that has never met the person who holds it.**

"**In a month there is going to be a woman on a road with a piece of paper in her hand asking after the person who keeps the anteroom's terms, and she is not going to be from my office. And somebody in this Hearthhold is going to have to decide whether to be that person in a room with a stranger in it, and the woman who keeps those terms has spent five months being extremely careful not to be anybody's holder of anything, and I have just handed a road a post with her name-shaped hole in it and I could not stop myself because it was the accurate word.**"

Renn Vale stood on the landing and did not move for about four seconds.

"**Then I want the field sheet corrected,**" she said. "**And I want it corrected in a way that takes the title out and does not put a name in, and I want somebody to tell me what is left when you take a person and a post out of a line.**

"**And I want it said out loud, in this room, by the person who keeps the terms, that there is no post. There is a woman with a slate and seven terms and an eighth who is here on the days she is here. And if a body on a road sends for a post, this boundary will answer it the way it has answered everything else this month, which is that there is nothing here to be sent for, and I will say it myself at a counter if I have to.**

"**And I want it entered that I would rather be named and refused than titled and found, and that I know which of those two is better and I am not sure which of the two I would survive.**"

---

**And at the sixth bell the board was not walked, because the anteroom had a woman in it from another office and the count does not read the board to a stranger, and Dalla Sen said so out loud and it was the first time in twenty-two weeks that the six things had not been read at the sixth bell, and she entered that, and entered that she had chosen to say it in front of the woman rather than after she had gone.**

**The crew were at fifty-three and a half and had not moved.** The boundary was at a hundred and eighty-six and a quarter, and a man of twenty-two was fifty-four days and fifty-four nights into a count that was his own.

Hollis Ashe left at the seventh bell with her tally, her permit, and a form with three of five lines filled in by a clerk of a boundary that had no procedure for her, and she went down nine steps and along a corridor two foot eight wide over eleven feet of shaft that is packed and not held, at a walk, and did not put a hand on the fill at the waist, and nobody walked her out past it because the corridor was shut.

**And what this boundary had gained between Tuesday and Thursday, and it is worth writing down in one place because the clerk would not put it next to anything, is this.**

A mark was put on a doorframe by a man who had said in a room that he would not do it, and it was seen by a stranger for four seconds, and a description of it is now in a book four days up the middle passage, with a date on it that is a small lie, and a post on it that a road is going to come looking for. The mark is off the stone. **The record of the mark is not off anything.**

**And four sentences that were written for one office came out of a coat** and were read out loud in a room by a keeper of a page, with a woman from another office standing at the end of the sentence, and nobody in this building can take a rule back once it has been read in a room by a person it was not written for.

**And a printed form that a clerk put face up on a table on purpose was read by a stranger in four seconds and cannot be un-read, and the clerk who put it there has said out loud that she is going to leave it there, and that is the first thing this building has done that it cannot reverse, and it was not done by a guild and it was not done by the Compact. It was done by a woman who wanted a rule to be looked at.**
