# Chapter 153 — A Store With A Keeper In It

**Open for the corridor on the third day of the thirtieth week, a Wednesday, and the school ran for the twenty-third morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and a clerk asked five people for three hours of a piece of work in a room with the terms out loud, and named the three she was not going to ask and the reason for each before she asked anybody anything, and the reasons were not the reasons she gave in the week before last.**

**Three hours, asked for in a room with the terms out loud: the crew's column moved by three, from sixty-three and a half to sixty-six and a half, and two pairs of hands went four days up the middle passage to stand in a store that is not this building and came back and said that a store is a room.**

At the fourth bell the nine went in and one of them went in sitting down, and the strip of crate board under the ninth hearth stone was holding and the two flags had not moved, and Jo Verrick said the standing term out loud in the doorway the way she has said it every morning since a physician gave it to her, and Mero Senn said it out of his own hand against hers, and Ilsa Norr said yes to it, and Dalla Sen entered that the term is the same in two hands.

Ten weeks now without a fire in the corner of that room, and Mikka Hale asked to be asked about it for the sixth time this month and refused it for the sixth time, in one sentence, standing in the doorway with the fire-frame off behind her.

They were in for the morning and out of it again inside half an hour, the way they were now, and a heel had been rubbing the clay beside the strip of crate board under the ninth hearth stone for four weeks and had left a mark you could read from the doorway.

"**No. Not this month, not next month, and not when a physician comes down nine steps again and says a word about a floor. I will not light a fire on nine hearths in a room with a bed a hand and a half down under one of them, and I have been asked six times and I would like to be asked a seventh and refused a seventh.**"

"**And the children are cold, and I would like that written down in the same place as the refusal and not in a different one, because the cold is the trade and I made it and I am not going to have it kept somewhere quiet.**"

Then the clerk had the anteroom at the second bell and said what she was going to do before she did it.

"**The crew have been asked three times in thirty weeks. Three times it was a work, and three times the terms were said out loud in a room and a person was named who could be told no, and I am asking a fourth time and I am going to name the three I am not going to ask and the reason for each before I ask anybody anything, because that is the practice, and because the reasons have changed twice in a month and I am not going to use an old reason in a new month.**"

"**Leena Orr. Her right shoulder is in a sling and it takes no weight and that has not changed and I am not going to pretend it has. The reason is not the shoulder. The reason is that on Thursday the man on the step is going to say out loud in a room which wall of this building can take a push, and the person who has to be asked about that is the woman who keeps the room on the other side of that wall, and a woman who is going to have to answer that question in a room is not a pair of hands in the same room on the same morning.**"

"**Pell Roke. He is fourteen. The reason is not his age and I want that in the book, because his age is the reason everybody expects. The reason is that he reads numbers, and on Wednesday of this week he is going to read a column of figures out of a store's list in this anteroom, and the figures in it are not counts of anything, and if a boy of fourteen is the one who reads a set of figures aloud in a room then that boy is the person in this building that a figure belongs to, and I have watched this boundary spend a month being frightened of exactly that.**"

"**Tams Quill. His right hand is open and flat and it is not a working hand and that has not changed. The reason is that he is the person who reads the board, and the board is where a thing that has been done gets entered, and this work is about finding out what a thing is, and if the man who finds out what a thing is is also the man who writes down what it turned out to be then there is nobody left in this building who can stand in a room and say that the record is not the man.**"

The three reasons were not argued with and not improved on, and Dalla Sen entered each of them as it was given, and then she asked the two.

While she was saying them the anteroom had filled to the wrong shape. Four women had come off the step and stood along the wall that kept the wet out, Bel Ordry had turned his page over and shut it, and the two men who were going to be asked had not sat down and had not moved closer together than they had been standing before she started.

"**Iven Tallo. Three hours, and I am naming the split before you do, because you always do and I would rather it was in my hand than yours. Two hours of yours and one hour of Marek Venn's. You go first.**"

"**Say why you go first, and if the reason is pride say pride,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The reason is not pride and I am glad you asked, because I would have said pride and it would have been half true.**" He did not put his hands in his pockets, which is what he does. "**A carpenter looking at a pile of beech is a different thing from an engineer looking at a pile of beech. I have been in this building thirty weeks and I have watched a man stand in a room and be the man in the room, and everybody in the room agrees with him because he is the man in the room, and that is the thing I have been talking about since the twenty-second week and have never once been able to talk about in a room where he was in it.**"

"**So I go first and I look at it, and I do not speak for it, and I come back and say what a pile of beech is and what a pile of beech is not, and the man who is the man in the room says what it will be used for, and if he gets it wrong then a carpenter said a thing that a room heard and not a man agreeing with a carpenter.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that the distinction is real and that I am entering it because it is real and not because it was kind.**"

Then she gave the terms, and they were out loud, and Renn Vale was on the landing and had heard them, and Renn Vale said one word from up there, which was *yes*, and then told the clerk to come and hear the rest of it because she was going to be the person who could be told no and she wanted to be told no-able in the same breath.

"**Three hours. Four days up the middle passage to the middle gate and then a store at the camp, and the second gate's distance is stated nowhere in this and I am not going to have a figure put on it, and the road to a guild file is forty days on from the top of the passage and this work does not go there.**"

"**The work is not building and there is no building in it. The work is to stand in a room that is not this building and find out what a store is. In that room you say out loud what is in it and how it is kept and who can be asked for a piece of it and who cannot, and the person who keeps that store has to agree with you or say why not.**"

"**You write nothing. You bring nothing in. You take no wedge and no bar and you do not go near the waist's third one, which is up a quarter of an inch and a bit and has not been touched in a month.**"

"**And the last of the terms, and it is the important one and I have thought about it for two days. Nobody in this Hearthhold's name goes into that store's book. If that store keeps a list, the list is asked for and read out in a room and it comes back with no name on it, and if it comes back with a name on it then it does not come back and you both come home and say so.**"

"**That is not in the crew's practice and it is the same practice. A store's list is not a carrier's book. A carrier's book is a different book and it has a person in it and this boundary has been refusing to be a person in one of those for a month.**"

Then the man who keeps the page said the half of the morning nobody had come to yet, and he said it standing, with his hand on the date he does not say, and it was the thing the crew went up the passage to find out about.

"**Before anybody goes. Read the word out.**"

"**Which word.**"

"**The one the carpenter struck out of a plan's back in the twenty-eighth week.**"

Iven Tallo looked at the anteroom table for a while and did not answer straight away, and the pump got two strokes in, and when he answered he answered like a man reading out something he had written himself and was not proud of.

"**I struck it. In the twenty-eighth week, on the back of a day-board, in my own words, and Dalla entered it in the order I said it and said the order was the finding, and I have had a month to find out whether I was right.**"

"**I was right about the word. A brace is a support joined to a mark, and there is not one mark in this building that is not off the wall, and I said then that a thing with no mark in it is four rooms and a floor, and that is still true.**"

"**I was wrong about one of the reasons and it is the second one and I am going to say it now because we are all going to be in a room with a pile of beech in a fortnight and somebody is going to use my sentence without the part I am about to add.**"

"**I said: a thing called temporary is a thing a man in a bad month is allowed to stop maintaining.** That is right. **And I said: a brace is a support joined to a mark. And there is a mark in this building. It is not chalk and it is not mine and it is not on a doorframe. It is in the lintel over the mouth of that school corner and it has been there since before the ninth room was given a name, and it is four marks and a groove, and two people have read it two ways and it has answered nobody.**"

"**So the word is not wrong because there is no mark. The word is wrong because I have never been in a room with a brace in it and I do not know what a man in a bad month would do with one, and I said a true thing in a bad month about a thing I had not seen, and I have been carrying that for a month and this is where I put it down.**"

"**Do not put my name on the page,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The word is not going back on. It goes in the book in your words with the second half of it in your words, and both halves, in that order.**"

"**Then somebody is going to have to say the mark's limit,**" said Marek Venn, **"and I am going to say now, before anybody asks me, that I cannot.**"

"**A bearing mark tells a rule-line which way to carry a limited force, and one mark has one direction and one stated limit, and that limit is in the thing itself and it comes out of it. I put one on a jamb in the twenty-second week and a woman on a landing took it off with a cloth and would not let me take it off myself.**"

"**The mark in that lintel is not mine and I have not read it and I cannot read it, and a thing I cannot read does not come with a limit I can say out loud.**"

He said it to the step rather than to her, which was where he had been looking for some time.

"**So the limit on this, if this happens, is not going to be a figure of mine in a room. It is going to be a condition written by the person who keeps the room it can hurt, in her own hand, with no number and no day on it, and she can be stopped and she can stop it, and that is the only honest place a limit can come from when the mark is nine hundred years old and damaged and does not say.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that he said it before anybody asked, and enter that it is the third time in thirty weeks that a man in this building has said out loud that the thing he cannot do is the thing somebody else is going to have to do, and that I am entering the count of those and not the reasons, because he has given me the reasons four times and I have them all.**"

---

**Two pairs of hands went four days up the middle passage to a store at a breaking camp, and came back, and what they came back with was not a figure and not a refusal and not a document.**

Iven Tallo said it in the anteroom at the fifth bell in the order he had walked it, and nobody wrote it down, and Dalla Sen wrote it down anyway because she is the clerk and said so before she did it.

"**It is a lean-to of dressed stone and a timber frame against the cut face, and it is dry, and it is a room, and it has a keeper in it.**"

"**The keeper is a man of forty-nine who has kept it for eleven years and did not tell me that, and I asked him before I asked anything else, because I have been in a room with a store's worth of beech in my head for two days and that is the first question you ask a man.**"

"**And he said no.**"

"**To what.**"

"**To the timber. Not to the asking. To the timber, and he said it in a room and he was not being difficult, and he gave me a reason.**"

"**The reason was that it is not his to give, because the camp owns it until the camp closes, and the camp closes when the last of the season's work is done, and he does not know what day that is and neither does anybody above him.**"

"**And then he said a thing I have not stopped turning over. He said: bring the boundary's own keeper and I will listen to the terms.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**There is no rest of it. He said bring the keeper. And then he asked me one question before we left, and it was not about beech, and I am not going to pretend to this room that I know what it was about.**"

"**What was it.**"

"**He asked whether we had a person in the building who could say no to a thing on behalf of somebody who was not going to be here. And I said we had several and I named none of them. And he said: then bring one.**"

Marek Venn had been on the step with his right hand flat on the back of the bench and his left one open on his knee, and he had said the sentence about the limit and had not moved since, and the thing he said next was short and it was the only thing he said in the whole of the fourth bell.

He had spent a fortnight on that step working out what a paper on a cart had to do with a room nine children were taught in, and it had taken him the whole of the walk up nine steps to find out that the question was not his.

"**A store is a room.**"

"**Say it again in a month when it is not the answer to anything,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**A store is a room,**" he said, "**and a room has a person in it who keeps it, and a person can be asked, and the person can say no.**"

"**And that is not a discovery. That is what Renn has been doing to a corridor for thirty weeks with a slate on her knee, and nobody has ever once applied it to a heap of wood.**"

"**And I have been standing in this anteroom for a fortnight thinking about a piece of paper on a cart and a bedroll on a bench, and the whole of the two of them is that this boundary will not be given anything by anybody it has to name. And a man with eleven years of beech in a lean-to will give it to this boundary, and be able to take it back, and be asked in a room like a person and not like a gate.**"

"**Enter that as a possibility and not as a decision,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that a carpenter came down four days of road and was told to bring a keeper, and that a clerk has written that down, and that I am not going to read it out at a sixth bell as though the week before last's questions have all been answered.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read six things and the day's number and the count, and the number moved, and the board was not walked.**

"**Four today and a hundred and seventy-four since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and ninety-one and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks.**"

"**The crew are at sixty-six and a half. Up three, and it is the fourth time anybody in this building has asked the five of us for anything since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and I am going to say the thing I have said the other three times and then I am going to say the new part.**"

"**The new part is that a carpenter and an engineer went four days up a road and looked at a heap of wood and came back with the same sentence, which is that a heap of wood is not a thing you can have and the only person who can answer the question is one they have never met. Eleven years of it. Two hours of mine and one hour of his.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water went off the fill at the waist on the second day of this week and it has not been on it since, and Jo has not told me where it went, and I have not asked her, and I am entering that I did not ask her.**"

"**And I am putting two sentences next to each other in this book on purpose and with a reason. A man on the step said in a room this morning that he does not intend to say which of two prices he would pay. One of those is a number and the other one is not, and I have spent thirty weeks learning that those are the two different kinds of thing in this building.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-eight days and eighty-eight nights into a count that is his own.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is not that it was busy. Two men went four days up a road today and I could not walk a board in a room with a clerk who had just been entered into a store's list by a man I have never met. A board is walked by a person who has nothing else on them and I have had four days of somebody else's road in my book since the first bell.**"

---

**On the fourth day of this week a man on the anteroom step is going to say in a room which wall of this building can take a push, and the clerk has entered that the limit on the thing is not going to be a figure of his, and the carpenter has given back half of a sentence he wrote in the twenty-eighth week, and two wedges at a school-door head are still holding that door.**

**Four days up the middle passage there is a lean-to of dressed stone and a timber frame against a cut face, and eleven years of beech in it, and a man who said bring your keeper, and there is no wall in lime in this Hearthhold and the month after next is this one.**
