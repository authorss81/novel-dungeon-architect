# Chapter 147 — The Word Is Not A Way Out

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the twenty-ninth week, a Tuesday, and the school did not run, and the woman who keeps the road opened the corridor again on the same term with the same condition and no number and no day on it, and the last of the movement went through four rooms, and the air engineer shut the air gate a second time and stopped a holding whose limit had not been met, and a man who had been in a channel since Friday afternoon came up out of it and then walked out of this building the ordinary way, in daylight, in front of everybody, and the second way out of the room he came out of was a word he said himself and it opened nothing.**

**Nothing was forgiven and nothing was repaired. The cost is permanent and it is cumulative, and it is going to be there for the rest of his life, and a physician said the only true thing anybody said about it and then said the other thing.**

Renn Vale answered the question at the first bell and she answered it in four words and then said the rest of it because four words is not an answer anybody can put in a book.

"**I will open it again. Same terms, same four, same four minutes, same bearer at the near lip, same condition, and the condition is still a person at the low end and there is one."

"**And I said on Monday that I am the person who opens it and nobody else in this building can, and I am not going to soften that by saying that anybody could learn it. A woman can learn it in a year and a man could learn it in an afternoon and I would rather it took a year."

Through in twelves and then the rest went the movement, and the anteroom went over its number for a second hour, and about a girl of nine sat on the anteroom step with a strip of crate board round her foot and did not go up the nine steps, and a physician stood at the bottom of them and did not go up either.

Then the air engineer stopped the holding, and the limit had not been met, and she said so before she did it.

"**I am going to do a thing now and I want the two halves of it said in this room in this order, and I want them entered in that order, and the second half is the one that matters."

"**First half: the limit on that opening is that it is held while there is water standing on the fill at the waist. The water is on the fill. It went off the fill for about an hour yesterday afternoon and came back on it before the pump came off, and I entered that at the time and I want it entered again now. The limit has not been met. That holding is not out of condition. Nobody in this building is entitled to say that it has failed, and I am not going to be the one who says it."

"**Second half: I am stopping it anyway, and the reason has nothing to do with the water, and I am going to say the reason in the shape of a person and not in the shape of a risk."

"**A person in that channel cannot be reached. That has been said out loud in this room twice this week and it is the truest thing anybody has said about a floor. And so there is no sentence that can be said to a man in that channel, and there is no way to tell him the water is up, and there is no way to tell him anything, and there is no way to know whether he is in it. And a holding whose only condition is a thing on a fill somewhere else in the building is not a holding. It is a man in a hole, and the condition is on the wrong side of the floor from him."

"**So I am stopping it because a person who cannot be told to come out is not a person holding an opening. He is a person in a hole with a lamp. And I am the person who may turn that wheel and I am turning it."

She shut the air gate for a second time and held it shut for longer than she had the first time, and the whole of the low end of that corridor sat in air that could not leave by the bottom of the shaft, and Jo Verrick got down onto the flags at the frame with a lamp in her hand and a woman on each side of her, and a man in a channel a foot and a half below a corridor floor got himself up onto a sill, and then got himself up through a frame with no grate in it, and two people took his weight and did not carry him.

He stood up in the corridor and could not stand up in the corridor for a while, and nobody helped him and nobody said anything to him, and after a while he did it again.

Then he walked the length of that corridor on his own, past the dip, under the jaw of the ninth hearth stone's room with the nine children not in it, and up through the school door onto the common landing, and down nine steps to the anteroom, and the anteroom was over its number and its outer service door was packed with stone and clay and a finger and a bit out of true, and Renn Vale said its terms out loud and he said the word and Dalla Sen repeated it.

"**Ten including one litter for no more than two hours," said Renn Vale. "**The outer service door. The pump attended. And the air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it."

"**Door," said Marek Venn.

"**Door," said Dalla Sen, and wrote it down, and it opened nothing.

Out of it on his own two feet, in front of everybody, Marek Venn went, and nobody carried him and nobody thanked him, and the anteroom's second way out was a word said by the person who was going to be in the room and repeated by the clerk, and it did not open a door, and he opened the door.

---

**Then the woman who keeps the road said the sentence this whole volume has been walking toward for four weeks, and it was against her own practice, and she said it in a room and not in a corridor.**

"**I have got to say a thing and I have been carrying it since Thursday and I am not going to be a woman who holds it for a week."

"**The second way out of that anteroom is a word. It has been a word since I signed this boundary's charter and it is the ugliest rule anybody in this building has made, and I made it, and I have defended it in four rooms in a fortnight. And it opened nothing on the Friday in the twenty-fourth week when eleven people said it in a corridor one at a time. And it opened nothing on the Thursday of the twenty-seventh week when that room went over its number with the word unsaid in it and the only way out of it was nine steps and a word."

"**And it opened nothing this morning. He is outside that door because he can walk, and not because anybody said a word, and the word was the same word it has been for twenty-eight weeks and it did not do one single thing for a man who has been in a channel since Friday afternoon."

"**And I have spent twenty-eight weeks pretending otherwise, and the reason I have pretended otherwise is that a word is the only thing I can give a room without a name on it. That is the whole of the practice and it is a real thing and it has kept women like me from being the one a doctor looks at. And a word is not a way out. A person is a way out. I have been signing a rule that says a person does not count, for twenty-eight weeks, in a building where a person is the only thing that has ever brought anybody out of anywhere."

"**And I am not taking it off. I am going to keep the word in the terms, because I have got nine people in this building who have said it out loud and been told that saying it was the thing that made them safe, and I am not going to take that off them in a week because a man came up a channel this morning."

Mero Senn said the two things and neither of them was the one anybody in the room wanted, and he said them standing at the bottom of the nine steps where he had been standing all morning.

"**A man of twenty-two was found in that channel in the twenty-fifth week, under a grate that had been levered off with something the width of a dead stick, and the clerk has written in this book in her own words that nobody sent him, and there is no page in this Hearthhold that says where any person is at any hour, and she has been clerk for twenty-eight weeks."

"**And a man went down the same channel on Friday and came up it on Tuesday, and nobody sent him either, and those two facts are going to sit on the same page and they are not the same fact, and I am going to say the second half of that out loud today while it has gone well, because nobody is going to say it at a funeral and this is not going to be a funeral."

"**The second one is not a good thing that happened. It is a thing that went well. And that is a different kind of sentence and it is the kind I am paid to notice and I have noticed it and I am not going to let a room turn it into the other one while it is still warm."

"**And the third condition on that page has been broken. The page is blank. It was blank before and it is blank now and it is going to be blank on Wednesday when a woman from another office sits in this anteroom and asks to be in the room while a page is read out loud. And nobody has been asked and nobody has been sent and nobody has gone near that floor, and I am going to say the last part of it and then I am going to stop talking."

"**In a year somebody is going to write a sentence about this that makes it sound like a decision. It was a decision. It was also two and a half days of a man in a hole for the price of four sentences a week about a stone. And both of those are going to be true and only one of them is going to be in the sentence, and that is true of every plan anybody has ever written, and it is the first time in twenty-two years that I have watched a room do it in front of me."

Marek Venn said what was left and he said it from the anteroom step with his left hand open on his knee, which it now is, and which was not.

"**I cannot go down there again."

"**That is not modesty and it is not a figure. My right hand has not closed since the nineteenth week and my left hand closed this morning about a lamp and it has not shut since, and there is a mark on the inside of my forearm from the frame and it will be there in a month, and my knee does not bend past about half, and I have not slept since Friday and I am not going to be asked about any of that because I am not going to be able to answer it properly and I would rather say that than be interesting."

"**And I want three things entered and I am going to say them in the flat voice and not the other one."

"**One: I do not know why I cannot. I know that I cannot the way I know that this hand does not shut, and I am not going to find out, and I would like it written down that the reason I am not going to find out is that a man who says *I could probably do it again* in a room in a month is the man who recommended that a room be shut in the twenty-fourth week, and I have already been that man once."

"**Two: I said on Thursday, before anything had happened, that I was not a post and that no page was going to get my name on it, and Dalla entered it, and it is in the book, and I want it read now, in this room, and I want to know whether anybody in this room wants to argue with it."

"**Nobody in this room wanted to argue with it. I am going to let that stand and I am not going to be grateful about it because gratitude is a way of saying *thank you for not making me be something* and I am not going to be a thing."

"**Three, and this is the last of it. The limit on that holding was not met and it was stopped by a woman with a wheel who did not stop it because the limit was met. I have spent twenty-eight weeks in a building trying to be the man who is right about a floor, and on Friday I said in this room that the person who decides when a holding stops being safe would not be me, and I said it in a speech, and a speech is cheap, and this morning a woman turned a wheel she has never turned in twenty-eight weeks and did not ask anybody, and that is what it looks like when it is not a speech."

"**And I am not going to be able to do that again either, and I do not know that, and I have not been told, and I am not going to be told, and I would like the clerk to enter that a man has just said in a room that he is going to be told things about himself by other people for the rest of his life and that he is not going to be consulted on any of it, and that he is not a post and that is the only standing he has got."

Dalla Sen read the entry of Thursday back out of her own book, word for word, and the room checked it, and nobody argued with it, and she read it a second time because that is what this building does and because nobody in the room wanted to.

"**Entered. And I am entering a sixth thing, which is that I have been a clerk for twenty-eight weeks and I have entered about nine hundred things in this ledger and there is not one entry in it that says a person is not to be sent, except mine, from the twenty-fifth week, about a man of twenty-two."

"**And the reason I am entering that is that on Thursday I entered that nobody sent him, and on Thursday I entered the difference between that and the other thing, and I called the difference the whole of what this boundary has learned in twenty-eight weeks, and I said I would not be asked whether it was a large one."

"**It was a small one. It was the smallest thing in this ledger. And the two halves of it sat next to each other on the same page for a fortnight and nothing happened on Wednesday, and nothing is going to happen on Wednesday, and in a year somebody will read them and think there was a difference in the kind."

"**There was not. There was a man going into a hole because he said he would, and a clerk writing down that nobody sent him, and that is a very old way of doing things and this building has known about it since the twentieth week and did not do it differently this time either."

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked, and the count read it in the same breath as the day's number, and nobody in that anteroom filled in after either of them, and a physician did not fill in after anything for about a minute and a half.**

The count read the board off the wall with the chalk held the way he holds it now, in the left hand, with the right one open and flat in his lap, and he read both columns and named no day and gave no reason for not naming one.

"**Four today and a hundred and fifty-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and seventy-five and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at sixty-three and a half and have not moved since Wednesday."

"**Fifteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row and a plan. Nine in the ordinary column and six in the one with nothing over the top of it, and the column is not headed, and I have nothing to add to that this month and I am going to say so and then I am going to shut up about it for the rest of the week."

"**And I am going to say one thing about the six in that column and I am going to say it once. Eleven of the fifteen on this board are nothings. A nothing is a reading that found nothing. And there is no place in this Hearthhold for a thing that found nothing except that column, and I have been putting them in it for twenty-eight weeks, and the only reason that column is safe is that nobody can say what is supposed to be in it."

"**And on Friday afternoon a man went down into a hole in a building where a page says nobody goes, and he came up on Tuesday, and it is going in the boundary's column, and it is not going in that one, and that is correct, and I want it said in this room by me before anybody in a month puts it in the wrong place and calls it a finding."

The fifteen entries were read out by the clerk and read again by her, and she entered that the count had said a thing once and that she was not going to ask him to say it twice.

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is on the fill at the waist and it is not moving, and it went off the fill for about an hour on Friday afternoon and came back on it before the pump came off, and there is still no number for that fill in this building."

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-two days and eighty-two nights into a count that is his own, and I am writing it, and I have said out loud in three weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and this week I said it in a room with a man in it who has just come up a channel, and nobody thanked me, and a physician stood at the bottom of nine steps for a minute and a half afterwards and did not fill in, and I entered that, because I enter everything, and because a clerk who only enters the good things is not a clerk."

---

**A man stood outside the outer service door of a room that is a finger and a bit out of true and packed with stone and clay and is holding and not fixed, and his left hand was open, and his forearm had a mark on it, and the anteroom was over its number behind him and the fifth of its five terms has been broken since the middle of last month and has been entered three times and a clerk has this week said out loud that the difference between the last three weeks of this book and everything before them is smaller than it looks like.**

**Blank, still, is the page in the clerk's book that says somebody stands at the low end of the corridor. It will be blank on Wednesday, and the third of the three conditions on it has been broken, and nobody has been asked, and nobody is going near that floor, and there is a word in this Hearthhold that everybody in it has said out loud at least once and it is not a way out, and a woman on a landing has said so in a room and has not taken it out of the terms of a room because nine people in this building have been told that saying it made them safe.

**On Wednesday morning Hollis Ashe is going to sit in this anteroom and ask to be in the room while a page is read out loud, and the page has twelve rows on it and no name anywhere, and a courier is going to take thirty-one people up a market road with nobody at the front of it, and there is a form at the end of that road with two lines and a space in each of them, and this building has decided that it will not fill them in.
