# Character Bible

## 1. Lead: Marek Venn

### Baseline

- **Age at opening:** 32.
- **Occupation:** Civil and structural engineer for Marrowgate Public Works; trained in shoring, drainage, confined-space rescue, temporary bridges, and evacuation planning.
- **Appearance and habits:** Lean, sun-darkened from years of surface work, with split fingertips, a shallow scar across one palm, and a habit of tapping surfaces twice before trusting them. He carries a folding rule, grease pencil, chalk, and his father's worn brass survey rule.
- **Temperament:** Patient with people in danger and impatient with anyone who calls an unexamined structure safe. He can become rigid under criticism because he has buried the habit of saying “I do not know” beneath professional confidence.
- **Immediate material stakes:** His ordinary career, his mother's trust, and his ability to return to the surface are all threatened by the first collapse. He begins the story wanting a route home more than a movement, a cause, or a title.

### External want

Marek wants to survive the Lattice and return to Marrowgate with proof that the tunnel failure was not ordinary negligence. As he meets the people living below, the want expands: he wants a route that lets residents leave, supplies that reach them, and a place where his work cannot be privately seized by a guild or a surface government. By the final volumes, he wants the Lattice to be governed by the people who live in it.

### Internal need

Marek must replace the belief that personal control is the same as personal responsibility with the ability to share authority, accept correction, and build systems that survive his absence. He does not need to become less competent. He needs to become less possessive about competence.

### Flaw

He is a control-seeker who expresses care through taking responsibility. He will not leave a problem unowned, so he takes on problems that require other kinds of expertise. He is also afraid of being seen as an ordinary frightened man, so he hides uncertainty until the hidden uncertainty becomes a public failure.

### Fear

Marek fears repeating Alden's disappearance and becoming the person who designed a system in which other people die quietly. His deepest fear is not being trapped; it is leaving behind a structure that looks safe because he needed it to look complete.

### Lie he believes at the beginning

“If I can see the whole load path, I can keep the people in it alive.”

### Truth he earns

A structure becomes safe when its load can be understood, maintained, challenged, and shared by the people who depend on it. A builder is part of that structure, not its owner.

### Competence and limits

Marek is excellent at reading physical weakness, sequencing repairs, and identifying the order of work. He is poor at magical theory, political coalition-building, air chemistry, and anticipating how people will negotiate a route after he leaves. His engineering can tell him that a wall is failing; it cannot tell him whether a person will panic, lie, or choose to disobey.

### Physical and emotional cost arc

Early overuse gives him headaches, vertigo, and brief phantom sounds of stone settling. Repeatedly carrying a load without enough help leaves **load echoes**: he feels phantom stress in empty rooms and loses balance on stairs. The first failed refuge leaves permanent fine-control loss in one hand, so later delicate work requires a second pair of hands. By the final volume, recurring symptoms make solitary readings unsafe; he has intermittent tinnitus and unstable balance, and the final shock will make the sensory damage permanent. He survives with a damaged sense of balance, permanent tinnitus in one ear, and the knowledge that he can no longer work safely alone. He does not lose his ability to build; he loses the fantasy that building alone is the same as leadership.

### Relationship to Alden Venn

Alden's disappearance is a personal mystery, not a secret father who secretly trained Marek for a destiny. Marek resents the years of unanswered questions and the professional legend that grew around Alden's final rescue. He learns that Alden died while staying with stranded workers in a collapsing sector. Alden's last usable design was a communal shelter that could be maintained by people who had never met him. Marek's grief changes when he realizes his father chose responsibility for strangers without abandoning the family he could no longer reach.

## 2. Core supporting cast

### Nera Oduya

- **Age:** 28.
- **Role:** Ventilation and air-quality engineer from the Hearthhold Middle; trained to maintain pumps, baffles, heat baffles, and emergency air routes.
- **Appearance and habits:** Compact, quick-moving, and allergic to careless certainty. She carries a strip of dyed cloth that changes color when smoke or dust changes its composition. She speaks bluntly when someone has ignored her measurements.
- **Want:** Make the next winter survivable for the Hearthhold without surrendering its air routes to a guild or a surface inspector.
- **Flaw:** She treats dependence as a trap and sometimes refuses needed help because accepting it feels like losing control. She is as capable of misreading people as Marek is of misreading loads.
- **Initial relationship with Marek:** Professional antagonism. When they first meet, Nera reads the sketch and measurements Marek has carried from his failed refuge and calls it a “closed box with a beautiful floor”; she refuses to certify its air path until it is redesigned. The local Hearthhold route keeper, Sefu Aran, can separately veto admission. They respect each other only after both predictions fail or hold under real conditions.
- **Arc:** From an independent specialist who distrusts surface engineers to a public steward who can negotiate, teach, and delegate. She never becomes Marek's support character; her air network and public mandate remain independent.
- **Romance policy:** A slow-burn romantic partnership, without magical mate bonds or instant attraction. Their intimacy grows through disagreement, shared risk, and choosing to keep separate professional roles.

### Iven Tallo

- **Age:** 36.
- **Role:** Senior route carpenter and former Cartwright warden; a practical mentor who knows joinwood, shoring, guild contracts, and the habits of dangerous crews.
- **Want:** Make the guilds safer for workers without destroying the only institution that can train and supply them.
- **Flaw:** He believes a competent authority can manage people better than people can manage themselves. He is willing to accept controlled danger when a public alternative is not ready.
- **Initial relationship with Marek:** Resentful respect. Iven knows how to build a brace that lasts a season; Marek asks why it must fail next year. Their conflict teaches Marek that maintenance and trust are part of design.
- **Arc:** He defects from the Ninefold Charter, loses his guild standing, and eventually helps found a worker-led public works school. He remains skeptical of romanticizing sacrifice and insists that every public design include pay, rest, and replacement parts.

### Talla Roke

- **Age:** 22.
- **Role:** Courier, salvage picker, and route scout from the Hearthholds; grows into a public route keeper.
- **Want:** Guarantee her younger siblings clean water and a route that does not require permission from a distant guild.
- **Flaw:** She takes every risk personally and treats asking for help as a kind of theft. Her boldness creates early successes and early losses.
- **Initial relationship with Marek:** Suspicion. She knows the real occupancy of rooms better than his public-works diagrams do and repeatedly exposes his assumptions.
- **Arc:** From a person who uses the Lattice for individual escape to a negotiator who understands that a route is a public promise. She becomes one of the first elected Hearthhold route stewards and later exposes a corruption in Marek's own administration.

### Mero Senn

- **Age:** 45.
- **Role:** Rescue physician and trauma surgeon who has treated collapse injuries on both the surface and below.
- **Want:** Make sure the dead are counted honestly and the living are not asked to accept preventable death as a necessary cost.
- **Flaw:** He distrusts ambitious projects because he has seen their human debris. He can become so focused on triage that he denies a community the chance to choose a slower repair.
- **Initial relationship with Marek:** Mutual professional friction. Mero accepts Marek's calculations only when he has a named evacuation plan and a person who can carry the injured.
- **Arc:** He becomes the moral and medical check on the new city, not a symbolic voice for safety. He later forces Marek to accept that a distributed system can still fail and that accountability matters more than a comforting explanation.

### Cassian Rook

- **Age:** 49.
- **Role:** Warden-General of the Seatwarden order; former structural engineer and one of the Lattice's most capable rescuers.
- **Want:** Prevent another Sundering by making one trained authority responsible for every load, route, and emergency decision.
- **Public virtue:** He saves thousands, maintains several dangerous sectors, and genuinely understands the failure of uncoordinated crowds.
- **Flaw:** He confuses accountability with obedience. He treats dissent as a structural weakness and is willing to make a small group bear the cost of a large system.
- **Initial relationship with Marek:** Indirect at first through a signed rescue charter, then a severe mentorship. Cassian teaches Marek that a route can be elegant and lethal, and Marek teaches Cassian that a safe system still needs people who can refuse it.
- **Arc:** Rook becomes the principal human rival, not a hidden final enemy. In the last volume he is forced to surrender personal control but is not killed; he survives as a bounded steward whose expertise is public and whose authority can be revoked.
- **Final ideological status:** He is neither absolved nor reduced to a villain. He has to live with proof that care and control can coexist in a person and still fail as a governing principle.

## 2a. First-volume supporting figures

- **Leena Orr, 26:** public-works survey technician and Marek's closest surface colleague. She is practical, blunt, and physically limited by a shoulder injury after the collapse. Her continuing arc is learning to trust a shared plan without surrendering her own judgment.
- **Pell Roke, 14:** Talla's younger brother and a junior route messenger who survives the first crossing with a left ankle injured when a returning-stair step settles beneath him in Chapter 8. He gives the first volume its child-centered cost and later refuses to become a mascot for the Hearthholds.
- **Oren Sill, 34:** a surface laborer in the trapped crew and a dependable second bearing/support hand. He is established in the opening chapters as a stretcher and shoring worker, survives the first batch, and dies during the later Hearthhold crossing because no route can carry everyone at once. His death is permanent and is not later erased or converted into a secret power source.
- **Tams Quill, 29:** a surface laborer in the six-person opening party. Tams handles the second chock line, test loads, and ordinary rest rotations, giving the crew's unnamed labor work a named human presence.
- **Sefu Aran, 34:** an elected Hearthhold boundary route keeper and air-gate operator. Sefu answers the first boundary signal in Chapter 7, can veto civic admission independently of Nera's air expertise, and makes the distinction between a safe mechanism and a permitted route explicit.
- **Dael Harrow, 41:** Cartwright clearance master who treats safety records as property and who believes he is protecting workers by making unauthorized routes legally unusable. He is a local institutional antagonist, not a secret final villain.

## 3. Family and civic relationships

### Iona Venn, Marek's mother

Iona is a school caretaker in Marrowgate who has spent fourteen years maintaining a small public archive of Alden's rescue reports. She is not a waiting woman. She argues with the office that ruled Alden's disappearance unresolved, refuses to confuse “missing” with “abandoned,” and makes Marek confront the family cost of his habit of disappearing into work. Their relationship is strained because Marek measures family loyalty as support for his decisions; by the end she supports the city but does not approve of every choice he makes.

### The Hearthhold as a relationship

The central relationship is between Marek and the residents he initially treats as occupants or load measurements. The Hearthholds force him to learn that a safe design without consent is only a cage with better engineering. The city's eventual self-government is the relationship made public.

## 4. Antagonist ladder

The opposition grows from ordinary pressure to a deliberately seeded structural threat:

1. **Route gatekeepers and bad employers:** Dael Harrow, a Cartwright clearance master, profits from inaccurate route seals and can abandon a crew under a plausible emergency order.
2. **The Ninefold Charter:** a guild institution that turns private expertise into exclusive access and can starve a Hearthhold of materials without declaring war.
3. **The Quiet Hands:** a sabotage network that attacks public infrastructure to prevent the Lattice from becoming another centralized refuge. Their fear is understandable; their immediate harm is not.
4. **The Seatwardens:** custodians who use emergency powers to close routes, centralize records, and place Cassian Rook in control of the Loom Deep.
5. **The One-Seat protocol:** the ancient Cairn Directorate's failed solution. It is the final structural antagonist and is present in early clues, old records, and political argument before the last volume.

Cassian is the ladder's human face. The One-Seat is the ladder's final pressure. No new final enemy is introduced after the opening volumes.

## 5. Relationship milestones across the series

These are emotional and political beats, not mandatory romance scenes:

- **Marek and Nera:** professional friction; a first successful joint repair; a public argument over whose safety calculation governs; a separation caused by competing jurisdictions; a reunion as equal planners; a private partnership that leaves both with independent public roles.
- **Marek and Iven:** borrowed craft; a first betrayal by a guild order; mutual respect after Marek admits a design failure; co-founding a public works school; disagreement over whether emergency authority should ever be permanent.
- **Marek and Talla:** protector and skeptic; a rescue that violates her autonomy; Talla's independent leadership; her challenge to Marek's first public office; a later election in which she defeats his preferred candidate on route policy.
- **Marek and Mero:** professional trust; a dispute over acceptable casualties; shared accountability after a failed project; Mero's testimony at the final civic trial.
- **Marek and Cassian:** admiration; competition; a genuine disagreement over a rescue; a shared experience of a buried-city emergency; ideological rupture; partial collaboration; public surrender and guarded cooperation after the climax.
- **Marek and Iona:** unresolved grief becomes honest communication; Iona supports the city without absolving Marek's workaholism; their final conversation recognizes that a person can return as someone different.

## 6. Character boundaries

- No character is defined only as a reward, mentor, love interest, villain, or exposition device.
- Nera has a full political and technical arc independent of romance.
- Talla and Iona can make decisions that cost Marek materially.
- Cassian's competence is real and must be shown in action.
- The supporting cast can disagree with the series' preferred political answer.
- No harem structure is permitted.
- Romance, when it appears, must arise through choices, consequences, and time, never from a magical bond or instant attraction.
