# Chapter 116 — The Bench Is To Be Kept Clear

**The first day of the twenty-third week was shut for the corridor and the school did not run, and the boundary spent the morning doing the one thing it had never done, which was to put a question to a room that had been sealed by other people, and to do it properly, which took four bells of argument before anybody picked up a chalk.**

**The sealed archive is at Hearthhold Middle,** and it is sealed on the outside by a guild inventory and on the inside by the Middle's own route keeper, and the two seals are different, and one of them is a lock and the other one is a person, and the difference has mattered exactly once in twenty-two weeks, which is now.

Bel Ordry had it on the anteroom table at the first bell and it was four lines long and Dalla Sen read it standing and did not read it twice, which she does with everything, so somebody said so and she read it twice.

"**One question, in a room, to a person, and only one, and we do not get to ask it twice.**"

"**Say what it is,**" said Renn Vale.

"**It is this. Is there a room in your inventory where the sheet and the room disagree, and what does the sheet say the room is for?**"

Nera Oduya, from three steps: "**That is two questions.**"

"**It is one question and an instruction about how to answer it. If they answer the second half first we take it and we do not send back for the first, because we get one.**"

"**Then it is two questions in a coat,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and I know exactly which one a sealed room will answer and it will be the one that is easier, and the one that is easier is a list of rooms and the one we want is a single room, and they will give us the list.**"

"**Then say the one we want,**" said Bel Ordry.

Mikka Hale put her hand flat on the table, on the plan, which she is not supposed to do and does.

"**Go back a step. It is not a list and it is not a single room, and both of you are trying to be clever in a direction, and I will tell you what I actually want and then you can argue with it.**"

They waited.

"**I want to know whether a room in a sealed archive has ever been written down as being one thing while it was plainly another, by somebody who had been in it. Not a drawing. Not a description. Somebody wrote *this is for that* on a sheet about a room they had stood in. And I want to know what they called it, because the calling is the part.**"

Bel Ordry wrote it out longhand and it was one sentence and it had a verb in it and Dalla Sen said it out a second time and Renn Vale gave the answer she always gives, which is that the clerk is not to improve it.

"**Then find me the person,**" said Renn Vale, "**and the person has to be a person and not a sheet, because a sheet in that room is a thing a stranger can hold and this building has already sent one of those up a road this month and I have not stopped thinking about it.**

"**And the question goes in a room and not down a corridor, and it is not asked through a child, and it is asked by a person who can be told no, and if the person says no then the boundary has asked nobody and we enter that we asked nobody and we do not ask again.**"

"**Then I will ask it,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am not going to be able to answer it for about three weeks and I am not going to count, and Dalla Sen is not going to write a figure on it either, and if either of us puts a number on this it is struck.**"

---

**Dalla Sen went into the school store at the second bell for a blank sheet, because there is no paper in this Hearthhold that is not already something.**

**Smallest room in the building, at the school-door end of the corridor,** it is locked, and the key stays in the room, and it now holds a shelf on two beech lengths laid across two pails, and the dowel, and nothing else. The nine register boxes are on hearth stones two hundred feet away and the plan's two parts are in a coat.

She came out after four minutes with nothing and stood in the doorway and said so, and then said the other thing, and she said it to the room and not to the anteroom.

"**There is no blank paper in this Hearthhold. There is the plan in four parts, the charter, the second sheet, four sentences and a clerk's fair copy, six pages of ledger, a slate, and a man whose own book is in his own coat. And I have just gone through a locked room with a nine-hundred-year-old schedule rolled up in it looking for a piece of paper to ask a question on, and there is none, and I am going to write this question on the back of a day-board and if anybody wants to say something about the condition of the reverse of a day-board in this building they can say it now.**"

"**Write it on the back,**" said Mikka Hale. "**I write the days on the front. There is nowhere else.**"

---

**The answer came up the middle passage on a courier's hand some weeks later and it was not dated, and Dalla Sen read it out in the anteroom at the fifth bell with the room at its number, and it was said twice, and then she said it in her own words, which is the thing she does.**

**The Middle's route keeper had written it himself, in his own hand,** and it was four lines, and the second line was a line off a sheet nine hundred years old.

"**I have had a sealed room in front of me for nineteen years and I have never had a question put to it that I could not answer out of the index in a morning, and I have not thought that was strange until this week.**

"**Here is the one I would give you. Room eleven of the lower row. The inventory enters it as a records room and it is a records room and there are records in it. Under the entry, on the same sheet, in a hand I cannot date and have stopped trying to date, there is one more line and it says: the bench is to be kept clear.**

"**There is no bench in room eleven. There has never been a bench in room eleven in any inventory, and I have had the room open for an inventory in my own time and I stood in it.**

"**That is the whole of what I have. I do not know what it means, I have had it in front of me for nineteen years and I did not know until this week that I did not know, and I am not going to tell you it means anything, because you did not ask me what it means and because I would only be guessing in a worse hand than yours.**"

For a while there was no sound in the room at all, and the pump went on, and the sixth minute of the low end of the corridor came and went with nobody standing at the low end of it.

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Dalla Sen. "**You have written four lines and I want to know that those are all of it and that you have not got a fifth.**"

"**That is all of it,**" said Bel Ordry, reading it, "**and he has written that he is not going to tell us what it means, and he is a stranger to us and he has refused us a thing in advance, in writing, and I am going to enter that and I am not going to argue with it, because he is a keeper of a room and I am a keeper of a page and he is the older of us.**"

"**And the clerk wants a fifth line,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am going to say what I want it to say and I am going to say it in my own words and not in his. I want it to say: he was asked one question and he has answered it and the answer is a fact about a room and not a cause, and this boundary entered that on the day it was read, and the two of them are the same day and not two days.**"

---

**And the air engineer was in the room, and had been in the room the whole time, and said nothing at all for about nine minutes, and the clerk entered that she had said nothing and where she was standing.**

"**You were here,**" said Dalla Sen, not as a question, and Nera Oduya said: "**I was here. I am going to say the professional thing and it is four words and then I am going to stop.**

"**A room is being maintained as a bench.**"

"**That is four words and I am not going to let anybody in this room build anything on it,**" she said. "**I have had a theory about this building since the fourth day of the eleventh week and it is worse than not knowing and I have not said it and I am not going to say it because a bench on a sheet in a sealed room is not my theory, it is a stranger's line under an inventory entry, and I would be reaching.**

"**And the condition is not met. I asked that it be entered that the fourth thing bound to *when this boundary is keeping itself* was approached in a room this week and was not met, and that I was in the room when it was approached and that nobody asked me anything.**"

Marek Venn had the question in his mouth and had had it for about a minute, and it was the fourth question he had wanted to ask her in twenty-two weeks, and the three before it had all been about a valve, and he put it down because she had said *nobody asked me anything* in the tone of a woman who has been counting, and because a man who has just been told that nobody in a room ever asks should be the last one in it.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, and wrote it, and read it back, and Nera Oduya said: "**And enter that I said *a room is being maintained as a bench* and that it is four words and that I stopped on purpose and that a room that lets a person stop is a room that is keeping itself better than any of the rooms that are shut.**"

Then the clerk did the last part of it herself, standing, with the room at its number, and it is in the book in her hand, and it is the reason nobody in this boundary is ever going to be allowed to make this into a theory.

"**Three facts and they are going on a page and there is nothing joining them.**

"**One. A sheet in a sealed archive at Hearthhold Middle carries a line about keeping a bench clear, and there is no bench in the room the sheet is about.**"

"**Two. The first column of our own plan is a permission and not a count of people, and it has been a permission for twenty-two weeks.**"

"**Three. A schoolroom floor in this building is dry this week and the reason is a line of grease on a jamb, and the reason is a person who can take it off.**

"**I am putting those three next to each other with a reason beside them and nothing between them, and the reason is not a reason, and the reason is that I am putting them near each other so that a reader in a year cannot make them into a finding. If somebody in this room can join those three, they can say so now, in the room, and the clerk will write it down and it will be the clerk's own line and not mine.**"

The room said nothing back. The anteroom was full, and the shut school corner was open at the far end of a corridor with a dry floor in it and nobody in it, and the two jars a day were two jars a day, maybe three, and the pot was ten days and had been ten days for twenty-two days and had never once gone up in that time, and a man of twenty-two was fifty-one days and fifty-one nights into a count that was his own, and a bench nobody in this boundary has ever owned is being kept clear four days up a middle passage in a room with records in it.
