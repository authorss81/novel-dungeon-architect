# Chapter 215 — What The Plan Costs

The fifth day of the forty-first week is a Friday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the forty-eighth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and nine people who were in standing water three days ago sat at the far end of that room at a table of their own with a slate lying face up on it, and one of them had worked out how many of them there were and had told the cook, and the cook had not put it anywhere.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the thirty-fifth reading, and the same as thirty-four, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked her, and the count of mornings is thirty-five and is the same count as the wall's, and the folding rule is in a belt and has been on a belt for thirty-five mornings.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is twenty.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it was right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and the clerk entered that she had written it out eleven mornings running without changing a word of it, which is the first time she has entered a thing she did not change as though it were worth entering. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. And the sixth was the pump, which is the only instrument in this building with a rule on it that this room did not write, and it ran, and on the same floor an air engineer was describing a nine-hundred-year-old door and a clerk was writing down the cost of it in the space where reasons go.

Then the air engineer stood at the end of the trestle with her back to the wall and a piece of chalk in her hand and no dyed cloth anywhere about her, and nobody had called her and she had not been asked and she had come and stood there, and Dalla Sen entered that she was not asked.

"**I have got a plan,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and I am going to set it out in the order I set out everything else in, which is terms first and asking second, except that this is not a term and I am not asking anybody for anything, and I am going to do it in one go without stopping, and if I stop in the middle of it I will lose the thread of it and I have lost the thread of it twice this week already.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything.

"**The problem. There is a chamber at the bottom of a passage with four fathoms of water in it and a room of shelves above the water, and both of them have been closed for nine hundred years, and the air in them is nine hundred years old and it is not going anywhere. A body of water going into a space takes the air out of it and pushes it up whatever is above the water. That is not a fear and it is not a theory, that is what a body of water does, and it is why the passage has been cold at the kitchen-line end since Tuesday and why four of the nine went up nine steps on Wednesday and nobody in this room thought about it until the child came down them.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The half against is that I do not know what is in that air and neither does anybody in this room, and I am not going to guess at it in front of you, and everything I am about to say assumes it is fit to be breathed and it may not be, and if it is not then this plan is not a plan, it is a way of getting a bad thing to a particular room faster.**" She put the chalk down flat on the trestle. "**Entered, in her own words, and I have asked the clerk to enter it before the plan and not after.**"

"**Then the plan,**" said Dalla Sen, with her hand near the book.

"**Four closed and two opened,**" said Nera Oduya. "**Closed: the low vent off the corner of this anteroom, which has been drawing cold since Tuesday and has been drawing it since before I came here. Closed: the broken louvre over the kitchen line, which is not a vent any more, it is a hole, and a hole that cannot be shut is a vent the building does not have. Closed: the seam vent off the far end, because the head of water is on that side of the party wall three days in seven and I am not putting a vent on a wall that has a head of water on it. Closed: the fourth is the riser at the top of the nine steps, and I want that shut for about an hour and no longer.**"

"**That last one is the one that matters,**" said Renn Vale, from the middle of the floor.

"**That last one is the whole of it,**" Nera Oduya said. "**Because with those four shut, everything the chamber pushes out has one way to go, and the one way it goes is up the shaft that serves this anteroom and the room at the kitchen-line end, and the air those two rooms breathe gets pushed at them instead of being drawn through a hole in a corner. It is efficient. It is more than efficient. It means that the rooms with people in them are being served by a body of water nine hundred years old pushing at them, and there is no fire in this building to fight and no stove to fight it with and nobody has to carry anything up any steps.**"

"**Then the cost,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The cost is the door,**" said Nera Oduya.

"**Say the half against the cost,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Say the cost,**" Nera Oduya said, "**and I am going to say it now, before anybody asks me for it, and I am going to say it in the first person and not in the third, because I wrote it out yesterday and I read it in the third person and it sounded like somebody else's plan.**"

She straightened up off the wall.

"**For the two opened to work, the passage below has to be shut. There is a door in it. It is a pressure door: it is cut for a door, it has a channel cut in the jamb, it has sockets worn on one side only, and it has been there since the building was made, which is about nine hundred years, and it was put there so that a man could close a room that was under water and go on working in the room next to it. That was its use. The use ended about nine hundred years ago. The door stayed, because a door is a thing that does the last thing anybody told it to do, and nobody has told it anything in nine hundred years and it is still a door and it is still cut for one.**"

"**And how long does it take,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and who shuts it and who opens it.**"

"**A windlass and two people and about four minutes,**" said Nera Oduya. "**I have not turned it. I have looked at it. I have stood under it for about ten minutes with a lamp and worked out which way it takes the pressure, and I am telling you what I know and I am telling you the shape of what I do not know in the same breath, because the second one is the one that is going to cost somebody.**"

"**Then say the cost,**" said Dalla Sen.

Nera Oduya did not answer for about four seconds.

"**The cost is a person and not a room,**" she said. "**Everything on the far side of that door is cut off while it is shut, and that includes the pumping station on that line, and that includes the drain, and it includes anybody standing in either of them. So the cost of my plan is this: if there is anybody on the far side of that door when it shuts, then the cost is that person, and not a room, and not the air, and not the water. I am telling you the cost before you ask me for it because I would rather this room had it in its mouth than had it turn up in a corridor in a fortnight. I have had it in my mouth for two days.**"

The anteroom was very quiet, and the pump was between strokes, and the child at the kitchen-line end had come down the nine steps at the fourth bell and was sitting on the bench at the anvil end with her shoes off, and nobody had noticed that either.

"**Say the half against,**" said Bel Ordry, and he came down two steps.

"**A room cannot see a cost in the same breath as a plan,**" he said. "**That is the half against and I have said it before in this room about other instruments and I am not going to pretend I have not. The clerk reads the second part of a thing out loud and the room hears a number, and a number is where a room starts, and a cost is not a number, a cost is a person, and a room that has been handed a person in the same breath as a good idea is a room that will not be able to do arithmetic for the rest of the morning. And I am going to say the rest of it, which is that this door has been standing there for nine hundred years doing the last thing anybody told it to do, and I have spent forty weeks in this building explaining that a rule is not a mark and a mark is not a rule, and I am now watching a room be handed a nine-hundred-year-old door and call it an instrument, and Bel Ordry argued in this room six days ago for starting a pump against a nine-hundred-year-old groove and said he would do it again in nine hundred years and said he is not a person anybody should take advice from about a pump.**"

"**Then say the half against that,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The half against is that I am right,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and that a door that has been shut for nine hundred years and is cut for a door is the only instrument in this building that anybody has ever handed anybody, and that this room has spent three volumes refusing instruments and is about to get one out of a hole in the ground, and that the man who has been refusing is the man who is going to be asked to make it work, and he has not been asked yet, and I have noticed.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything about that last sentence, and the clerk entered it in the words of the man who said it and did not add anything to it.

"**Then the clerk is going to say the thing none of you have said,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am going to say it into the space where a reason goes, because that is what the space is for, and I have not written in that space in six weeks.**"

"**Say it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**This room is not stopping her,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the reason this room is not stopping her is not fear and it is not stupidity and I am entering both of those so that nobody in a year can put it down to fear. The reason is that this room has spent forty weeks refusing plans and has nothing to put in their place. It has refused a plan eight times and every one of the eight times it was asked *who keeps it*, and it never once was asked *what is it for*, and it has been able to do that because nothing has ever come down this corridor that was good enough to be worth taking instead of a refusal. And this morning something has come down this corridor that protects every person it names. The anteroom. The two rooms off the far end with six people in them and one latching door. The room at the kitchen-line end with nine children in it four mornings in seven. And nine strangers at a table at the end of that room. She has named all four and the cost is one person on the far side of a door and there is nobody on the far side of that door at present.**"

She stopped and looked at the book.

"**A room that cannot tell the difference between a refusal and an abdication has no business asking a competent person to be worse than she is,**" she said. "**And that is the reason. I would like it entered that a clerk wrote it and not an air engineer, and that she is not proud of it, and that she has been a clerk for forty weeks and this is the first entry she has made in that space that is not a rule and not a figure, and that the two of them have been in here all along and nobody in this room could read either of them.**"

"**Then this room has to be told what it is agreeing to,**" said Mero Senn, from the frame of the far door, with his bag against the wall, "**and the thing I am going to say is the only medical thing in this morning and I am going to say it once.**"

"**Say it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**A plan with a named injured person in it is not a plan,**" said Mero Senn. "**She has named a person. She has not named an injury, and there is not one yet, and I am the man who would name it if there were, and I have not named it because I do not know of one, and that is the correct condition for me to be standing here. I want it in the book that I heard the cost and did not object, and that a man who has said no to plans in this room for nine weeks heard a cost described to his face and did not object, and that if there is an injury on the far side of that door then I will be the one who stops the work and not the clerk and not the keeper and not the man who has the shape.**"

"**Entered, in the physician's words, and entered that nobody had asked him,**" said Dalla Sen.

For about an hour after that nobody came into the anteroom and nobody went out of it, and at about the ninth bell the air engineer went back up her three steps and sat down on the second one, and nobody went up after her, and at the end of that hour Marek Venn understood two things that he had not understood on the previous four days.

The first was that she had not asked him for anything. Forty weeks in this building and she had struck a line out of a guild's claim before anybody asked, and had refused a man who could have got a sentence said for her in four seconds, and had stood at the end of a trestle this morning and set out a plan that turns on a windlass and four minutes and a length of time she cannot name, and she had done all of that within sight of the only man in this building who could build the thing her plan depends on, and she had not said one word to him, and he understood that this was not modesty and it was not a slight and it was not the air engineer's four numbers and not a fifth, and that she had simply decided that the cost she had named was a person and that adding a request would have made it two.

The second was worse, and he had it about four minutes later and did not tell anybody: **that he could have built it.** Not the door — the thing the door is there for. A frame across the head of a wet passage to take the pressure so that a windlass and two people could put a nine-hundred-year-old door where it was cut to go. It was the ordinary work. It was a brace, and he had a stage, and a stage is not a reading and it is not a panel and it is a physical support joined to a bearing mark and nothing else, and the standing rule in this building is that no mark is cut in the stone anywhere in it, and a bearing mark in this building is a thing two people read differently and that answers nothing.

And so the only man in that room who could have made her plan good was a man with no post, no room, no watch, a left hand that does not close, a knee that does not bend past about half, and a rule against him that is forty weeks old and has never once been argued.

He sat at the end of the trestle with his shut book under his forearm and did not say any of it, and Dalla Sen entered that he was asked nothing and said nothing, which is true, and which is not the whole of what happened on that afternoon, and the clerk entered the first half and did not enter the second, and the second half is in this sentence and nowhere else.

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither was read, and the reason went in beside them: a room which has been handed a good plan and a cost in the same breath does not want two numbers out loud this afternoon, and a clerk who has spent a whole morning being the reason a room did not stop a woman is not going to be the second voice in the room about her.

"**Seven today and three hundred and fifty-three since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and seventy and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

The count went in underneath that in the ordinary hand and was entered and not read, and the reason was given and was that a clerk who reads a number out loud on the morning she has written a reason in the space where reasons go is a clerk who is telling the room which of the two she did first, and the number is one hundred and sixty, and it has a zero in it, and four people in that room could have said so and none of them did, and she did not offer it, and the clerk entered that nobody had and that she had not and that the two of those facts were the same fact.

Wen Pask is one hundred and sixty days and one hundred and sixty nights, forty-nine weeks of a clerk saying out loud that she is going to keep writing it down. It is not going to be one hundred and sixty-one.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a clerk has written one sentence into a column made for rules for the first time in six weeks, and a board is a column, and a clerk does not walk one on the afternoon she has discovered what two of them are for.

The crew are at eighty and did not move again, and the reason for the day is in the book, and it is that a foreman said on the previous day that he will not ask for his four and a half hours before the open days, and the first of those is tomorrow.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning, and nobody measured it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for twenty-one weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a plan is in this room that protects four rooms and costs one person and has nobody on the far side of the door at present.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a room at the kitchen-line end with nine children in it in the morning and nine strangers in it in the afternoon, and a piece of chalk lying flat on a table that had not been used to mark anything, and a man at the end of a trestle who had worked out inside about ninety seconds that he was the only person in the building who could do the thing the room was about to need, and who had not been asked, and who would not have said yes.
