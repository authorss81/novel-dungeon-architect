# Chapter 151 — The Sheet Is Back On The Board

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the thirtieth week, a Monday, and the school did not run, and a road hand came down the middle passage with a wooden tally and said what she was before anybody was asked her, and she had two things and neither of them was a thing anybody had sent for.**

**A sheet is back on a board four days up a road with a seal number on it that is not the number it had before, and a cart is standing at the middle gate with a paper on it, and the news came down the passage before the timber did, and nobody in this building is entitled to a thing a sheet promised a hundred people.**

Down went the tally on the anteroom table where anybody could see it, the way she has put it down for thirty weeks, and she said her name, which she has done every time and which is the only name she gives anybody, and then she said what she came down the passage to say and she said it in the order it had happened, because that is also what she does.

"**The sheet is on the board. The one that came off. It came back on the Saturday and I did not put it up, and neither did anybody I could see.**"

She put two fingers on the table corner nearest the door, not on the paper, and left them there.

"**There is a seal number on the corner of it now, and the number is not the number it had before. I have the old one off the copy I wrote down for myself and the new one off the board with my own eyes, and I am not going to give you either of them, because a seal number is a thing you can be found by.**"

"**I am telling you it is back and I am telling you it is different, and the different is the part I came down for.**"

Bel Ordry said the reason out loud before he asked the next question, because that is what the man who keeps a page does, and the reason was a sentence Pettin Rahl had said in this anteroom on a Thursday in the last month of a week.

"**A regime is not a threat. It is a schedule. And a thing which is scheduled is not a thing that is refused.**"

"**Write that down as his words and not as ours,**" said Dalla Sen, and her chalk was already moving.

**"Write down under it that a woman at a counter told a courier in this building, in her own words, that a sheet comes back on a board in a month by a clerk who does not know about the Tuesday.**"

"**The courier wrote the words *a week is not a month* twice into my book, so that the second one is the one a person reads. I have read both of them, and the woman was right, and I am not going to pretend in a clerk's hand that I thought otherwise when I wrote it.**"

Her other hand went flat on the table, on the wood, not on anything anybody had written.

"**There is a cart at the middle gate. I did not see the timber. I saw a cart and a man reading a paper on it and a second man who was not reading anything. That is all I have got and I am not going to go up that road and look at a cart for you, because I have got a tally and I have got a road and looking at carts is not in either of them.**"

"**Say what is in a road,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing.

"**Going up it and coming down it and the weight of what I am carrying, which is not nothing, because the tally is oak and a tally is not paper.**"

Not one person in that anteroom said a kind thing about the tally, and the cook, who has been in this building thirty weeks and has a pot at ten days, said the sentence that turned the morning into a fact instead of a grievance.

"**A hundred copies,**" said Mikka Hale. "**That is what the halfpenny sheet said at the store four days up that passage. A hundred copies, and timber in the month after next, and three other Hearthholds expected to accept, and this one cited. And the month after next is this month. A hundred copies of a sheet is not a delivery. It is a thing that was printed, and I have printed things in my life and not one of them ever turned up.**"

"**And the three that took it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The three that took it have timber.**" She put the back of her hand flat on the table. "**That is what the sheet was for. I do not think anybody sat anywhere and meant anything by it. I think a man wrote a fourth paragraph he has been refused three times and put timber behind it and printed it a hundred times and sent it to four Hearthholds and the four that answered are the four that have it. And a boundary that is cited in a file is a boundary that somebody is going to say a word about, and a word is all it has ever taken.**"

Then Talla Roke came off the step, where she had been standing since the first bell with her three behind her and her hands loose, and she said the thing she was going to say before anybody got to it, which is the shape of her in this anteroom now.

"**I am not here about a boy. I have said that every day I have been in this building and I am saying it on a Monday morning because it is a Monday and people get busy.**"

"**Say the other half,**" said Marek Venn.

"**There is no other half of that one. Here is mine.**" She had the tally of her own in her hand, the wooden one, and she turned it over once and did not put it down. "**A form and a load are the same instrument. I have been telling this room for a month that a road becomes a toll gate the first time somebody puts a piece of paper on it, and I have been right, and here is the paper coming the other way.**"

"**A load without a form is not a load. It is a thing on a road that a man with a tally counts. And the moment that cart is moving, this Hearthhold is a place that something is being taken out of by somebody, and the somebody is a form, and the form has two lines in it, and I know what the two lines are because I have stood in front of them.**"

"**So I am going to say the whole of the thing in one sentence and then I am going to stand somewhere else. A boundary that will not fill in two lines is a boundary that cannot be given anything by anybody who needs two lines filled in. That is not a moral position. That is a *shape*, and the shape has been in this building since the twenty-ninth week and everybody in it knew it and nobody said it out loud, because saying it out loud on a Monday makes it a fact instead of an argument.**"

"**I have got a bundle sitting in this anteroom since the fourth day of the twenty-fifth week that cannot leave this building for the same reason a cart cannot come up the middle passage. It is a bedroll and a belt and half a bread he did not eat. And I have never once said that out loud in a room, because the man it belongs to does not live here and it is not mine to make into a speech.**"

The room made it into no speech at all, and Dalla Sen entered that a bundle had been in the anteroom for four weeks and entered that a courier had said so out loud in a room for the first time, and entered that she was not going to ask the courier for the man's name because the clerk's book has a bundle in it and a name would be a different kind of entry.

Then Marek Venn said the thing he had been not saying since a Friday in the last month of a week, and he said it in the shape he had it and not in a better one.

"**A clerk asked me in a room ten days ago what I am going to do when the weather turns, and I did not answer, and she did not ask me twice, and it is in her book as a question and not as a finding.**"

"**I have had a fortnight with that and I have been carrying it about, and the answer is a piece of paper on a cart, and I do not like it. I want to say that in a room in those words and I want it entered, because I have spent a month being a man who is careful in front of people and careful is now the thing I am least able to afford.**"

"**I am not the man who decides this. I want to say the other half of that too, because I said it ten days ago and it has not stopped being true in a fortnight. There are four women in this room who keep a room and a man who keeps a page and a clerk who keeps the arithmetic, and there is a woman on that landing who shuts a corridor, and the whole of what I can be asked for is a set of figures and a set of opinions about where they go.**"

"**I have got no post and I have never asked for one and I said ten days ago in a room that no page was going to get my name on it, and I am not going to break that in a month because a cart is standing at a gate.**"

He put the heel of his left hand on the edge of the table to get his weight off the bench behind him, and the hand did not close on the edge, and he took the weight on his forearm instead without any sign that he had noticed doing it.

"**What I want entered is this. If that cart comes up the middle passage it comes up it as a load with a person responsible for it, and the person responsible for it is going to be a person this boundary names, and a person this boundary names is a person four days up a road can be sent for.**"

"**And if we do not name one then the cart does not come up. That is a wall in lime that does not get built, and there is a room at the kitchen-line end of this corridor with nine children in it three days in seven, and a sill packed in clay that is the only thing holding that floor up, and it has been shrinking for thirty weeks.**"

"**Those are the two things. I have said both of them. I am not going to say which one I think is worse because I do not know and I have said in this room that I do not know better than anybody else in it.**"

For the length of four strokes of the pump the anteroom said nothing, and then the road hand, who had been standing at the table the whole time with her oak tally in her hand and had not sat down, said the thing that made the morning a Monday and not a crisis.

"**I am going back up at the second bell. There is a second crossing at the waist and I have got a tally and I am not carrying anything up it that is not mine. If you want to know anything about that cart you will have to ask the man who counts the crossings, and he is not me, and neither is the clerk, and neither is any of you.**"

"**Say her name,**" said Bel Ordry, **"and she is not going to give it, and we are not going to ask twice, and I would like that entered as a practice and not as an oversight.**"

"**I have been on this road for two years and nobody has asked me for my name once,**" she said, "**and I would like that in your book in that order, the asking first and the not-asking second, and I am going up now.**"

**At the sixth bell the board was walked, because a road hand had been in the room and a board is walked by a person who has nothing else on them, and the count read both columns and named no day and gave no reason for not naming one.**

Chalk in his left hand and the right one open and flat in his lap, and he had the ninth reason and would not spend it before the middle of the week, and he said so, and Dalla Sen entered that and entered that she was not going to ask him for it before the middle of the week either.

"**Three today and a hundred and sixty-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and eighty-five and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks. That counter has been twelve for a month and it is thirteen now and I am going to write it once and stop noticing it.**"

"**The crew are at sixty-three and a half and have not moved since Wednesday of the week before last, and I am not going to tell this room that a road hand coming down a passage is a work anybody asked five people for.**"

"**Sixteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, a plan and a condition. Nine in the ordinary column and seven in the one with nothing over the top of it. The mark is not coming off because the mark stopping is a fact about the mark and not about the entry, and the claim's condition has not been met, and both of those are going to be said by me every month until one of them stops being true.**"

"**And the column is not headed. Ninth batch, and I have got the ninth reason and it is a good one and I am not spending it before Thursday, and Dalla has that in her hand, and if she asks me before Thursday I am going to say the week got away from me.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is on the fill at the waist and it is not moving, and there is still no number for that fill in this building and there is not going to be one, and I have said that five times and I am saying it a sixth because nobody has found me a better one.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-six days and eighty-six nights into a count that is his own, and I said in five weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and I am writing it.**"

The chalk stopped for the length of one stroke and started again.

"**It is eighty-six, and it is not going to be eighty-seven. I have entered that a bundle has been in this anteroom for four weeks and that a courier said so out loud in a room today. I am not putting the two numbers next to each other, and nobody has asked me to, and I would have done it on my own if I had thought of it.**"

---

**On the first day of the thirtieth week a Hearthhold under a street of a federation learned two things on a Monday morning, and neither of them came up the middle passage, and both of them came down it.**

**A sheet is on a board four days up a road with a different seal number on it, and nobody put it up.**

**A cart is standing at the middle gate with a paper on it, and a hundred copies of a halfpenny sheet promised a boundary timber in the month after next.**

**The month after next is now, and a boundary that will not fill in two lines cannot be given a thing by anybody who needs two lines filled in.**

**There is a room at the kitchen-line end of a corridor with nine hearth stones in it and one of the nine propped on a strip of crate board, and a fire that has not been lit in ten weeks.**

**In a man's mouth and not out of it, the ninth reason he will not put a heading over a column, and he is keeping it for Thursday.**
