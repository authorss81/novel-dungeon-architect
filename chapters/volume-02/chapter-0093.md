# Chapter 93 — Three Readings

Three lines, and a whole morning spent arguing over them, and Jo Verrick said afterwards that this was the correct ratio and that anybody who thought it was slow had not yet tried to sell water to people who have run out of it.

They had used three vessels because three vessels was what they had: the pot, a pail, and a jar out of the clinic's store that Mikka Hale had asked the clinic for and had been refused and then had been given. The first reading was off the joint into the pot at the fourth bell on the Tuesday. The second was off the wet stone into the pail at the first bell on the Wednesday, with the pump not yet running. The third was off the joint again into the jar at the fifth bell on the Wednesday, with the pump running its full window.

Dalla Sen read the three lines out at the sixth bell on the Wednesday, in Jo Verrick's words, in her own hand, with the times on them.

---

"**One. The fourth bell on the second day, pot, straight off the joint, clear, no smell, no colour.**" She stopped. "That is the clerk's word *clear* and I want it challenged in the room before I read the other two."

"**Challenge it,**" said Dalla Sen.

---

Mikka Hale came in with the cloth in her hand and did not sit down, and she had had two days to think about a word.

"**It is not a word you can put on a page about water.** That is the whole of it. *Clear* is a word you can put on a page about a pane of glass. You can say what water is not — you can say there is no colour in it, you can say there is no smell in it, you can say it has been in a covered vessel and it has not been in anybody's mouth. **You cannot say clean, because clean is a thing you have got to be able to prove every day of your life, and I have proved nothing.** I have a jar of wet rock water and three mornings."

"And I am not going to argue the word with a cook," said Jo Verrick, from the frame, "**because she is right and because I wrote it.**"

"You wrote it because you were in a hurry and the hurry was mine," said Mikka Hale. "I had the number go to eleven on a Sunday and I have been in a hurry since the Sunday, and you wrote what I asked for, and I asked for a word that means nothing. So strike it. Strike *clear* and put *no colour* and *no smell* and *not drunk* on the three lines, in my hand, and have the clerk repeat it, and then let the water engineer have the other two."

---

"**Then there is the second line and it is mine and it is the hour,**" said Nera Oduya, and she came down two steps and did not come down three.

"The second reading is the first bell on the third day with the pump **off**, and the first and third are at the fourth and the fifth with the pump **on its window**, and the page says *pump ran* against the first line and nothing at all against the second, and a clerk reading that page in a year is going to take the second line for the odd one out when **the second line is the only one of the three that is a reading of the water at all.** Every other two are readings of the water while a machine was pushing this building's air around, and you cannot compare a number taken inside a pump's window to a number taken outside one.

"**So the page is not wrong. The page is incomplete, and an incomplete page on water is a page that lies by being silent.** Put the hour on all three, and the pump's state on all three, and the vessel, and the word *not drunk* on all three until somebody says otherwise in a room and somebody else writes it."

"And that is not an instrument," said Tams Quill. "**That is a thing you can be hung for and it is the first instrument in this building in eighteen weeks that anybody can carry out of it, and it is in your own hand and I am not going to stop you writing it.**"

"I am not writing a form," said Nera Oduya. "**I am writing three lines of a page that already exists, in the hand of a person who will keep it, and I would like that distinction entered because in a year it is the only thing standing between a record and a form.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it out, and then Mikka Hale came and struck *clear* out of all three lines with a single stroke and put *no colour, no smell, not drunk* in her own hand on all three, and the clerk repeated the new wording and then repeated the striking as well, because a clerk who repeats a correction without repeating the strike is a clerk who has corrected nothing.

---

Then the water went into the pot, at the sixth bell on the Wednesday, after the fourth reading, which was not a reading but a decision.

"**Say the order of it out loud,**" said Bel Ordry, "because I have held this page for four days and I am not going to be the man who put a bucket in a kitchen in a hurry.

"Found — a boy of twenty-two counting pails, in his own words, nine days late, and it is in the book in his words and not in mine. Located — a joint in dressed stone behind the outer service door, four threads, a leaf of lime, a wall nobody has looked at because everybody had their hand on it. Tested — three readings, three vessels, three hours, one of them taken at an hour when the pump was not running, and that is the only one of the three that means anything and the other two are in the book anyway. Recorded — in five hands, read out, and **one word struck out and put back in a different hand.**"

"And then in use," said Mikka Hale, "and not before, and **that is the order and it took four days and it is the first time in this Hearthhold that anything new has been drunk, and I would like everybody to understand that the reason it took four days is that four women had to be in a room and one of them did not agree with a word.**"

---

The first person to drink it was Jo Verrick, and she did it standing at the frame with the pot in her hand, and she drank a cupful in front of the room without tasting it properly, which is what a person does who has already put *clean* on a page and wants the page to be wrong.

"**I wrote that word first,** she said, "and I would like it entered that I drank it before the clerk finished writing down what I had said about the time of day, because I am not going to be a woman who corrects a record and then waits to find out if she was right to."

Renn Vale would not drink it until the three lines were on the page, and drank it then, standing on the landing, and handed the cup down to Bel Ordry without a word, and Bel Ordry drank it and said that it tasted of stone and that he had expected it to and that a man who has expected it is not a witness, which is the only objection he made to it and he made it about himself.

Mikka Hale put a cupful in the pot at the seventh bell and the pot was a pot of water and it was ten days of a kitchen and one cupful of a wall did not move it by a day, and she said so.

"**The school is going to ask me,**" said Ilsa Norr, from the end of the corridor, and she had not asked and everybody knew she had not asked.

"No," said Mikka Hale. "**Nine children do not get a wall.** Two jars a day is a pot for eleven people and a cook, and the day I give it to a school I am running a kitchen on a thing I have tested once, and the first person to sicken on it will be a child and the thing will be in your hand and not mine."

Then the numbers, and it was Mikka Hale who would not add them, and she would not add them out loud three times.

"**The board says ten days.** It said eleven this morning and it says ten tonight and it has gone nineteen, sixteen, fifteen, fourteen, thirteen, twelve, eleven, ten, and it has never once gone up, and I have not put a figure on it in my own hand since the third day of the sixteenth week and I am not going to start.

"**And the seam gives us two jarfuls a day. Maybe three.** And I am not going to add two jars to ten, and here is the reason and I have had it since Monday and I am saying it now before anybody suggests it in a month.

"**If I add them then I have one number and it is a sum of two things, and the day one of the two stops I will not be able to say which.** And the day one of them stops is the day this pot goes from ten days to nothing in a single line, and the line will be the shortest one in this book and it will be in my hand, and I will not have it. So: ten, and the jars, and the two are read out separately every fifth bell, and they are not added by me and nobody in this kitchen is to add them."

"Entered in her own words and read out," said Dalla Sen, "and I am putting my hand to the fact that she said *nobody in this kitchen is to add them* and that I will not add them either, and that is the clerk refusing an office, which is a thing that has never happened in this building before and I am not going to make a habit of it."

---

At the fourth bell of the same afternoon, with the pump stopped, the nine children at the far end of the corridor with their teacher, the school door shut, the anteroom shut and empty, Nera Oduya on the valve with her hand on the release and Renn Vale on the landing and Bel Ordry at the school door with both hands flat on it, **the school corner's mouth was opened a hand's width.**

"It is shut after one bell," Nera said, "whatever happens, and I am saying that now so that nobody can be tempted by a good result, and if it is shut early I am the one who shuts it and not the man at the school door."

**What came out of it was warm,** and that was the thing nobody had expected, because everything anybody had said about that mouth for eighteen weeks had assumed it was the cold end.

It was not going out. Nera had a hand on the valve and a knuckle-head of the pump frame and one thread of burnt wick, and she said, twice, before anybody asked her anything: **it is going into the throat, not out of it, and I have been wrong about the direction of that air in every account I have given since the collapse, and I am not going to say the word *sink* in this room because a man two rooms above the clinic return has been standing at a bar for a week listening to this building.**

"What I will say," she said, "is that there is a warm current coming out of the school corner and going down the throat, and there is a cold current coming off four treads nobody has walked in nine hundred years, and **those are not the same current, and I have claimed that draught four times in three weeks and this is the first time both of them have been in one room with me, and I still do not know what either of them is.**

"I will not call either of them ventilation. I will not call either of them a shaft. And I am not publishing a rate, in a cold month, off a hand's width, with a bar running."

At the fifth bell the mouth was shut and the shutter was back and the school door was opened and the nine children came out of it, and nothing about that afternoon is on any page as a result.

---

At the sixth bell the outer service door went back on, on four rusted pins, packed behind with stone and clay out of the rubble, and Jo Verrick put the chalk line on the frame where it goes and said the same sentence twice to two people: **it is holding and it is not fixed, and it is in the book as a thing that is holding.**

**So: the corridor open on the third day of the eighteenth week and shut on the other two, and the school running one day,** and the pot was ten, and a jar of water stood on the bench under Renn Vale's eye for the fourth night in a row, and nobody in this building had yet put the number ten next to the number two in a column, and that was the correct decision and it was made by a cook.
