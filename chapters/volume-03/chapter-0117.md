# Chapter 117 — The First Form

**The second day of the twenty-third week was shut for the corridor and a man came up the middle passage with a printed sheet in his hand and eleven other boundaries on it, and by the fourth bell this boundary had understood that something it had been saying about doors for five months had just arrived on a road.**

**The man was called Pettin Rahl** and he read forms for a living, which is a trade, and he said what he was before anybody asked.

"**I carry the paper. I am not the office. I have carried this to eleven places on this road and eight of them have taken it off my hand and put it on a wall, and one asked me whether it is compulsory, and I have told all of them the same thing and I am going to tell you the same thing and then I am going to stand by the door and you can ask me anything you want about the paper.**"

"**Say the thing,**" said Dalla Sen, who had the chalk up.

"**The form goes on a board at the market gate and it is not a toll and it does not cost anything, and it is not voluntary, and the word in it for not doing it is *unattended*, which is a word that has been done to me twice on a road this month by people who did not mean it the way I take it.**" He said it flatly. "**I am not complaining. I am telling you what it is so that you are not finding out about it later from somebody with a reason.**"

Then he put it on the anteroom table, which he was not supposed to do, and Bel Ordry said so, and he said he knew, and Bel Ordry let it stay, and Dalla Sen entered that.

---

**They read it out loud, one line at a time, and the room objected to five of the seven lines, and the fifth objection was the one that mattered, and it was made by the woman who had said the thing in the first place.**

"**It is not a toll gate yet,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, and she had the slate under her arm and she was reading off it and she was not reading a slate, she was reading a form on a table from four feet away. "**And it is the exact shape of one, and the air engineer said so in this room in the last week of the nineteenth week, and I wrote it down in this book, and I said in the fourteenth week of the twentieth that nobody had read it since, and I was talking about something else and I was wrong about what I was talking about.**"

"**Go on,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps.

"**I am not going on. I am going to say what I read out of my own book and then I am going to pass it to somebody else.**"

"**She said that a halfpenny sheet, a clay packing that shrinks, a word that has to be said in a room, and a frame that goes a finger a week is how a door becomes a toll gate,**" said Dalla Sen, reading it in her own hand, "**and I wrote it down, and I have not read it since, and there is a printed sheet on this table with a line on it for a name.**"

"**Then read the form,**" said Mikka Hale, "**all of it, and say what each line is going to be used for, and let the room be as unpleasant about it as it is going to be.**"

So they read it out, and Dalla Sen read each line and stopped after it, and the room argued after every one, and no line was marked until it had been argued.

**One.** *A person using the market road with any load states their business at the gate before the last of the road traffic.*

Renn Vale's objection, and it was about the words and not about the requirement: "**It does not say a person. It says *a person using the market road with any load*, and a person with nothing is not on this form at all, and the four households at the second gate carry water up four sets of steps and that is a load and they are people, and the gate at the bottom of that road is on a road this boundary does not have anything to do with.**"

"**Nobody is filling it in for them,**" said Bel Ordry. "**That is the point I am going to come to.**"

**Two.** *Name of the person carrying.*

"**Object,**" said Mikka Hale, "**and it is the only objection I have got left in this building, so I am going to say it properly. A document about a room travels. Everybody in this room has spent five months on that sentence and it is the reason the plan is in a coat. And this line is not a document about a room. It is a document about a man on a road, and it goes to a gate, and a man who has filled in a line like that can be found by it, and I have watched a keeper of a page refuse to put his ring on a sheet for exactly this reason and I am not going to be the only person in this room who thinks the ring was right.**

"**And there is no date on it either. I looked, and there is no date, and a sheet with no date on it has been sitting in this building since the nineteenth week and we still cannot answer it in time.**"

**Three.** *Number of persons.*

Bel Ordry read it twice before he said anything about it, which Dalla Sen entered.

"**This is a count,**" he said. "**And a count on a form at a gate is a number anybody may use, and this boundary has spent a week finding out that a number on a page is a permission and not a population, and I am not going to stand in a room and tell a man that the number on his form is a permission. It is a number. It will be a number somebody counts on a gate. It is the only line on this sheet that I believe.**"

**Four.** *What is carried.*

Nera Oduya came down the four steps for this one and went no further, and she said the thing that made the room go quiet.

"**This is the line and I want the room to hear why. The first thing anybody in this Hearthhold would ever put on it is *water*. The second thing would be *nine children*. The third thing would be *nothing, passing through*. And all three of those are true on the same morning and on most mornings, and a clerk at a gate who writes one of them has written down a true thing about us and has not been asked what the other two were.**

"**And the fourth thing anybody in this building would put on it is *a mark*, and I am not going to say the rest of that and the clerk is not going to ask me.**"

**Five.** *The person responsible for the load.*

"**Object,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and this is the fifth line and it is the one that is the whole sheet. A document that names a holder names a person. This boundary's charter has no name on it and a written reason for having no name, and every instrument we have for five months has been built on the fact that a name on a route is a name forty days away on a road. And this line is a form with a box for a holder, printed, with a clerk's mark on it, going to every boundary on this road.**"

**Six.** *Signature.*

"**I have already said my piece about rings,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to stop, and it is this: the fifth line and the sixth line are the same line, and there are two of them, and a form with a line for a name and then a line for a name again is a form that is asking twice on purpose, and the office that printed it is competent, and the office is competent, and that is the entire difficulty.**"

Iven Tallo said the sentence the room needed and said it from the bench.

"**It is a good sheet. I have read forty of them in my trade and it is a good sheet, and the spacing is right and the order is right and somebody thought about the order. There is not a word in it that is wrong. If I were a guild clerk writing the form I would want somebody to sign it, because a road with a load on it and nobody responsible for the load is a road where the next thing that happens is a man is killed and there is nobody to ask.**"

"**And you are not going to argue with that,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**No. Because it is true, and because I have been the only man in a room for eight months who nobody had to think about, and I know exactly what it is like to be a trade nobody asks and I am not going to be the man in this room who says a good sheet is a bad sheet.**"

**Seven.** *Countersigned at the gate by a person of that office.*

"**That one is not objectionable,**" said Renn Vale, and the room was surprised, and she let it be surprising for a moment. "**That one is a person at a counter doing a job. I would rather have a name on that than not. Put that in and leave it and stop arguing about it, because we are going to run out of things we are sure about and I would like one thing left in the room that is only good.**"

---

**And then the fifth objection, the one that mattered, was made by the man who keeps the page, and it was about a hole.**

"**Clause four of this charter says the office may see the plan when the office asks, in a room, to a person. The office in that clause is the Cartwright office. It is one office and it is named in the document and it is the only one it means.**

"**And this is a form from a market gate. I do not know whose gate it is. I have written a procedure for one other office that the charter does not mention, four sentences, on a Friday, in a room, and that procedure has been read off the anteroom table by a man in this building and was got half wrong by him, and I am not going to do that twice and I am not going to write a second procedure in a fortnight for a second office either, and the reason is the same reason I gave then and it is a good reason: a document that gets amended every time it is inconvenient is the guild's counter-draft with better manners.**"

"**Then what is there,**" said Mikka Hale. "**There is a form at a gate on a road and no rule of ours covers it and we have to be in a room with a man holding it.**"

"**There is a rule of ours,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and it is a rule with a hole in the middle of it, and the hole is that the four sentences name one office and no other body, and this building has now had two bodies come to it that the four sentences do not name, and both of them were competent, and one of them said out loud that she would write a procedure of her own and I would like it entered that I did not stop her.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the boundary has four instruments and no procedure for either of the two bodies it has not written one for, and that a procedure is a rule a stranger can use, and that the boundary is going to answer this form with the four sentences it has, and that the four sentences do not say what happens when the person reading them is a market gate.**"

---

**At the fourth bell they wrote the answer, and it is two sentences with no name and no signature on them, and it did not go up the passage that day.**

**The first one is the shape the boundary has used since the charter,** and Dalla Sen said it out twice and compared the two.

"**Nobody in this Hearthhold may be entered against a route. That is a charter and it is signed by seven people and it has no name on it and the reason it has no name on it is written on the charter.**"

**The second one was Mikka Hale's,** and it took her four minutes and she would not let anybody improve it, and the clerk said it out against what had been written and the cook said yes.

"**And if a person is going to write a name at a gate, write the water first and the people second and the mark never, and write what the bottom of the road looks like and not only the top, and if there is no room on the sheet for that then the sheet is the wrong shape and I am not going to pretend it is the clerk's fault.**"

"**And nobody is going to carry that up the passage,**" said Renn Vale, "**because a man on a road has to be asked by a person and a sheet on a counter is not a person, and we have already proved that this month at four days' walk and it cost two true sentences and a woman of sixty-one standing in a doorway.**"

Marek Venn put his hand out to the column and then took it back, and nobody saw him do it except the clerk, and the clerk entered that she had seen it and had not written it down, which is a thing she has done twice in twenty-two weeks and one of the twices was about a man who could not go up a road.

He had wanted to say *then let me take it*, and he had a hand that could hold a sheet, and there is a difference between a man who can carry a piece of paper four days up a passage and a man who can be useful at the end of it, and he knew the difference, and it was the whole of what he had learned in five months, and it had cost him a fortnight of deciding and a burning hand and a mark that was off a wall by Thursday.

"**Then it goes up on a market day,**" said Bel Ordry, "**with a person who is going that way anyway, and I want it entered that I have not got a person who is going that way, and that we are waiting for a market day and not sending anybody.**"

---

Pettin Rahl stood by the anteroom door for the whole of it, which nobody asked him to do, and when it was over he said one thing about the form and went down the nine steps.

"**You have got it wrong and I am going to tell you so because you have been honest with me and there is a gate at the bottom of that road with a board on it and a clerk who is going to put your sheet up whether it is the right shape or not.**

"**The form is not compulsory. It is *attended*. A load that has not been stated at the gate is a load that has not been attended. And the word that does that work is the same word as the one on your water-lock sheet, and I have seen it on four gates in a month, and every office that has printed it means something different by it, and none of them has ever asked anybody what this boundary means by it.**"

A man of twenty-two was fifty-two days and fifty-two nights into a count that is his own, and the boundary stood at a hundred and seventy-nine and a quarter, and Tams Quill had walked it that afternoon and named no day.

He went down the steps on a crutch of his own and into the corridor that was shut, past a waist with a wedge up a quarter of an inch, and he did not go over it, because a man carrying a form is not an excuse, and the form went onto the anteroom table and stayed there, face up, where anybody in this building could read it, **including a clerk who had chosen to put it there, and this time she said so out loud before anybody asked her.**
