# Chapter 144 — The Person It Is About Is In The Room

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the twenty-eighth week, a Thursday, and the school did not run, and four pages written by a woman from a surface federation were read out in an anteroom and decided upon, and a sheet with a title on it went back into a coat unaltered, and a clerk noticed out loud that the air engineer had stopped making a man say how he knows, and the air engineer answered about the work.**

**What the room found out at the third bell was this: a network of four rooms cannot be used on any day of the week, and the reason it cannot is a page that has been blank for twenty-eight weeks with three conditions on it, and the third of them says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and nobody is going to ask the woman who wrote it.**

Hollis Ashe came up the middle passage at the first bell with her tally left on the anteroom table where anybody could see it, and she said what she was before anybody asked her, and she sat down facing the door.

"**Stonelight Compact, route inspection. Forty-one. Sixteen years. And I have carried four pages up and down this passage for a fortnight and nobody has read them, and that is a thing I did to myself and I would like it in your clerk's book in those words. I am here on the third day of this week, not the fourth, because the corridor is shut today. On the third day of next week I would like to be in the room, and that is the whole of what I came to ask for.**"

Bel Ordry said the reason out loud before he put the four pages on the table, because that is what he does, and because a page from another office in this building is the most dangerous object in it.

"**Clause four of this boundary's charter is a right for the Cartwright office, in a room, to a person, and neither of you has ever been that office. We have refused to write a second procedure and we are not writing one this week. We are going to read these four pages, decide, and tell you the decision in a room, and you may take it up the passage in your own hand and not ours. Everything a stranger puts on this table gets read out in this anteroom within a day, and I would rather it got read out by the people who keep the rooms than by the people who keep the books.**"

Dalla Sen read the first three pages out loud, and the fourth was not a page, it was a sentence Hollis Ashe read herself.

"**One. A boundary may publish, for anybody who asks in a room, a statement of what each of its rooms is permitted to hold and what condition each of those rooms is under, in a form with no name on it.**"

**Two. A boundary may decline to show the document itself while showing the statement, and may say no to a person who wants the document, and saying no is not a refusal to answer.**

**Three. A person from another office may be present while the statement is read, and may ask, and may be told no, and the whole of it is not a thing that is given to the office and kept.**

**Not on the page is the fourth thing. I wrote a procedure that assumes the boundary wants to be looked at, and I could not write one for a boundary that does not want to be looked at, because there is nothing in one.**

Dalla Sen entered all four, and entered that the fourth was not on the page, and that the clerk had no category for a sentence a person says with her own mouth shut and does not write down. Then she said out loud that the reply sheet was on the table, and nobody had asked for it to be.

"**Read the line,**" she said, "**and then the answer that was given to it, and then the thing nobody in this room has said out loud. The line is for a name, or a description sufficient to identify, the person by whom the mark's condition is kept. The answer was given in nine words by a woman on this landing and went up the road on paper. The description exists, in a field book four days up the middle passage, and it is a title, and the title is *the person who keeps the anteroom's terms*, and it has been there since the twenty-second week. And the person it describes is standing on the landing with her arms folded and is not going anywhere. This is the first time in twenty-eight weeks that a document in this building has got a person into it who is in the room, and it got her in through a title and not through a name, and nobody sent for her.**"

Renn Vale said the thing the room had been walking toward since the second bell, and she said it flat, and she was not angry, and that was worse.

"**Then the title is wrong and it is going to stay wrong, and I would like all of you to hear that as a decision and not as an oversight. If I write a correction, this boundary has written a document for an office it has refused to write a document for, and the document is a procedure, and Bel Ordry has said in this room that a document amended every time it is inconvenient is the guild's counter-draft with better manners, and he is right. If nobody writes a correction, the title goes up that road and gets filed, and in a month somebody has a piece of paper with *the person who keeps the anteroom's terms* on it and can point at it, and somebody in an office who has never met me will put a name in it, because a title is a post and a post is a thing that gets filled. I cannot stop either of those, so I have picked the one that is mine. And the half against me, unasked: there is a third thing available, it is the best one, it is on the first of that woman's four pages, and if this boundary uses it then the page in my hand stops being a title somebody can send for and becomes a room anybody can ask about in a room. And I want that. And I am not going to ask for it, because a woman who has spent four weeks being a handle does not get to choose the shape of the handle, and if I ask for it in a room you will all say yes in a way that means something else and I will spend the rest of my life not knowing which.**"

Bel Ordry entered that in his own hand and entered that the woman on the landing had just said she would not be the person who put anything in the fourth column. Hollis Ashe said one sentence, and it was the shortest thing anybody said in that anteroom all morning.

"**I have been trying to write a page that could be shown for four weeks and I have not managed it, and a woman on your landing has just written the reason I could not, and I am going to write it down in my own book in her words, and I am not going to use her name.**"

---

**At the second bell the clerk noticed something out loud, in a room, in front of a stranger, and it was the fourth week she had been carrying it and the first time she had said it.**

Marek Venn had given her two figures that morning, the number the anteroom can hold and for how long, and the number that can go on the waist in the pump's window, and Dalla Sen had taken both out of her own book and compared them with what was written and had said *that holds* twice.

"**I want to say a thing and I want it in the book. I have been going to say *say how you know* to that man for four weeks. I said it to him in a corridor in the twenty-fourth week and I made him say it, and I said it four times last month when I had his figures read against an original, and every time I said it I was doing the thing this building does, which is make the person who holds the figure say where it came from so that the room can weigh it. And I have not said it once this month. He has given me two figures today and I have read both against the original and I have not asked him a single thing about how he got them, and I noticed at the fourth stroke of the pump that I had not, and he had not noticed that I had not, and I am a clerk and I am supposed to be the person who notices that. So I am noticing it. Out loud. Now.**"

Marek Venn had been in the room for all of it and said one sentence.

"**Then say it to me, Dalla, and I will answer it the way I answered it in the corridor.**"

"**No,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I have told you I noticed. That is a different thing and I am not going to pretend it is the same, and if I say the words now it will be because a stranger is in the room, and that is what a room with a stranger in it is for.**"

Nera Oduya was on the second step and had heard the whole of it, and she answered before anybody asked her, and she answered about the work.

"**Nothing changed in me, and I am going to say the professional reason for that and nothing else about it. On the third day of the twenty-fourth week I made him say how he knew because his figure was the only figure in that room that had nothing to agree with, and I was right to doubt it, and the doubt is what a room is for. There are two handholds on that page now. One is a man's and one is a room's, and a room's third column is what a person does every morning, and a schoolteacher's hand in a column cannot be adjusted by anybody who finds it inconvenient. That is a better check than a man being made to defend himself in a corridor. So if I make him say how he knows in front of a room that has a room in it, I am not checking him. I am telling the room that the man is the thing that can be doubted and the room is not. And that is not true, and I would have been doing it for the shape of the thing I did in the twenty-fourth week, and I do not want that job for the rest of my life.**"

She went back up the second step. Nobody thanked her. Renn Vale, who had something in her mouth at the second bell and had held it, did not say it, and the clerk entered that a woman who might have said a kind thing did not, and entered that she was entering it on purpose and did not know why.

---

**At the third bell the network was found to be unusable on any day of the week, and the reason it was unusable was a page with nothing in it.**

Renn Vale put it in the room as a fact and not as an objection, because she is a person who keeps a road and the arithmetic had been done overnight and the arithmetic was against her own yes.

"**On Tuesday I said yes to opening the waist for a full crossing and I said the reason for the yes in the same breath, and I have not stopped saying yes. Here is what the yes does not get me. The water comes up the channel before it comes up the corridor, and that is the air engineer's and has been hers for twenty-eight weeks, and the low end of that corridor is the lowest point in this building, and there is nothing on that floor to say where the channel is, because the grate came off it in the twenty-fifth week and there is no second grate in this Hearthhold. So I said on Tuesday that I will not open the waist on a day anybody at this end tells me somebody is going to be at the low end, and the only way the low end is watched is if somebody is there. And the page that says somebody stands there has been blank for twenty-eight weeks and the third of its three conditions is that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and I am not going to ask the woman who wrote those two sentences whether the conditions are met, because nobody is, and that has been the rule since the fifteenth week and it is the only rule in this building I have never once argued with anybody about. So the waist does not open on a day anybody is at the low end, and on a day nobody is at the low end there is a man of twenty-two in that channel. Your four rooms and your door are a network that can only be used on a day when nobody is watching the one place in this building that has killed a person.**"

For about a minute the room said nothing.

Leena Orr said it from the lower level with her good arm through the doorway, and she said the reason against it before the reason for it, and Dalla Sen's chalk was moving before the sentence finished.

"**On Monday of the week before last I found the shape of the refusal of that row, and the shape was that every row on that page is a place somebody is going to be, and the low end of that corridor is the only place in this Hearthhold nobody is ever going to be in. I said it and I still believe it. And the other half, in the book whether anybody wants it or not. If nobody is ever going to stand there, then that place is the only place in this building with nothing watching it, and a place with nothing watching it is how Wen Pask came out of a channel, and he was twenty-two, and nobody sent him, and Dalla has that in her hand in her own words. So I am not against this because it is dangerous. I am against it because it is the reason a page in a clerk's book has been blank for twenty-eight weeks, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that the blank page and the refusal of the row are the same decision.**"

Bel Ordry asked the physician a question nobody in that room had ever asked him, and he asked it as the man who keeps a page and not as a man asking for a rule.

"**Mero. Not whether a plan with a number in it is acceptable, because you have been asked that four times in three weeks and you have given the same answer four times and I am not going to be a fifth. Is there a version of this where nobody is in that channel.**"

Mero Senn put the bag down, which he has not done in this room in a fortnight, and said the answer before the reason, and did not pick a side.

"**No. And I am not going to make the search sound like a principle, because I have been looking for a week and have not found one. Three things I know, and none is an argument. That channel is a foot and a half below that floor. There are eleven feet of corridor over the far end of it that nobody in this building has ever put a load on. And a person in that channel cannot be seen from the corridor and cannot be reached from it, and the only way anybody reaches a person who cannot be reached is by changing the air around them. And the air at that end of this building belongs to one person, and she is standing on the second step of those nine steps, and she has just answered a question about a figure and she did not do it for me.**"

Out loud, into that minute, a courier said the thing four people in that room had been carrying since the first day of the twenty-sixth week and had not said.

"**I have been careful about this since the sixth day of last month and being careful has got a bundle sitting in an anteroom. Nobody is being sent. I said on the day I came up this passage that I am not here about the water, and not here about the school, and not here about a boy, and I am not going to change that because a hole has appeared in a Tuesday's arithmetic. I am not sending anybody, and my three are not being sent. If a person goes into that channel on Friday, it is a person who went, and it is not a shift, and not a rota, and not the crew, and not a favour, and not an hour in anybody's column. And if thirty-one people go up my road then they go as ordinary residents with nobody at the front of them, and the form at the end of it does not get filled in by me or by anybody else.**"

Ilsa Norr came down two steps, which she has not done in this room in four weeks, and stopped the argument with a sentence that was not about engineering.

"**I am not going to be the person who asks for it. I closed a room with eleven people in it in the twenty-fourth week on the advice of a competent man, and I wrote the change to my own room in my own hand, and the room forgave nobody and blamed nobody, and it cost a girl of nine her foot, and I have not been able to put it down since. And if a person goes into that channel on Friday to make a network work, then in three months there will be a plan in a coat with a row about a person at the low end of that corridor, and the person will have a name against it, because a person gets a name when there is a post, and a post is what a blank page becomes when somebody fills it in. And I am not going to be the person who fills it in. So I am saying no to the network on Wednesday if that is what it takes, and I am saying it now so nobody can tell me afterwards they did not know.**"

Marek Venn had been against the anteroom wall since the second bell and had said nothing, and what he had been doing was an arithmetic he is good at and this time could not finish, which is a thing that has happened to him twice in twenty-eight weeks.

"**I am not going to stand here and tell this room that I volunteered, because I have not, and a man with a book of his own and a figure in it that he gave to a woman and not to a page does not get to make a speech about a hole he put a length of rope on a week ago. The arithmetic is four lines and none of them is a design. One: this network is four rooms and a door, and it is worth having, and if it can only be used on a day when nobody is watching the low end then it is four rooms and a hope. Two: there is no grate in that frame and no second grate in this Hearthhold and a wall in lime four days up the middle passage in the month after next. Three: nobody is going to be sent, and I am not going to pretend that a person who is not sent has been treated differently from a person who is, because a man who says that in a room is a man who has found a way to be sent.**"

"**And four, which nobody has said, because everybody in this room has been polite about it. The person who can go down there is me, and the reason is not that I am brave and not that I am the engineer. The reason is that I am the only person in this building who does not keep a room, and does not stand a watch, and is not on a rota, and is not one of the five of the crew, and is not a resident of this Hearthhold by anything except that I am standing in it. Which means that if I go, no keeper's condition is broken and no third column is out of date. And it means that in three weeks this room will have invented a post, and I would like it entered now, by a clerk, that I have said all of that out loud before it happened, and that I am not a post and there is no page with my name on it and there is not going to be one.**"

"**And Renn is right, and it has to be said again because I said it on the Friday of the twenty-seventh week and I am not going to be the man who only says the half that flatters him. A person at the low end of that corridor is a place a person can be killed. It is the second way out of this building and it is the only one there is, and the refusal of a row for it is correct, and the refusal is the reason there was nobody standing there, and both are true at the same time and I am not going to have anybody take either half out. So I am going to go, and nobody is sending me, and those are not the same sentence, and Dalla is going to write them as two entries and not as one, and I would like her to write mine as the thinnest thing she has ever entered in this book.**"

Dalla Sen wrote two entries and read them out, and they were two entries, and the second one said that no person had been sent.

"**Entered, and it is going to be read twice. Entered that on the third day of the twenty-eighth week a man said in a room that he would go into a channel at the low end of the corridor on the fifth day, and that he said it out loud before anybody had asked him to, and that the terms of the going had not been settled at the time he said it. Entered that nobody sent him, and that the word *sent* has been used in this building in the last four weeks to mean two different things. On the second day of the twenty-fifth week it meant that a clerk wrote in a book that nobody sent a man of twenty-two to his death. Today it means that no keeper in this Hearthhold has been asked to put a person in a place. And the difference between the two is the whole of what this boundary has learned in twenty-eight weeks, and I am entering the difference as a difference, and I am not going to be asked whether it is a large one. And entered that the third condition on a blank page has not been softened and has not been broken by a decision. It is going to be broken by a man who is going to say he went, and the page is going to be blank when he comes up the steps, and nobody is going near that floor, and nobody is going to ask.**"

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the reason she gave was better than the reason anybody in the room expected, and the board was not walked at all.**

"**Three today and a hundred and forty-nine since the keeper signed, and the boundary is at two hundred and sixty-six and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at sixty-three and a half. I entered both of those and I read out the count, and I did not read the other two, and I want the reason in my own hand and I want it to be the real one and not a good one. There is a sheet from a surface office lying on this table with a title on it that describes a woman standing on my landing, and I was not going to stand up at a sixth bell and read numbers out of my own book with that lying there. It was not dignity. It was housekeeping.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is on the fill at the waist and it has not been off it and it is not moving, and there is no number for that fill in this building and there is not going to be one. The count. Wen Pask is seventy-nine days and seventy-nine nights into a count that is his own, and I am entering that a page has been blank for twenty-eight weeks, and that a man has offered to go and stand in it, and I am entering the offer and not entering the page, because the page is not mine and never has been. And I did not walk the board, because a board is walked by a person who has nothing else on them, and I have had four pages and a title and a hole in a floor on this table since the first bell.**"

---

**A woman from another office went up the middle passage with a decision in her own hand, and a woman on a landing did not ask for a thing, and a man who does not keep a room said out loud in front of a clerk and a stranger that he was not a post and there was not going to be a page with his name on it, and Dalla Sen entered both halves of that in two lines and read them twice.**

**Under two flags and a length of the crew's own spent line, the low end of that corridor has a channel in the bottom of it with silt in that, and the page that says somebody stands there is blank, and the third condition on it says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and nobody has been asked, and nobody is going to be.**
