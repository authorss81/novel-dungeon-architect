# Chapter 96 — In Her Own Hand

It came up the middle passage in the first day of the nineteenth week with a courier the clerk could not describe afterwards, and it was a single sheet and it was not for this boundary, and Talla Roke's name was on it in her own hand with three other names under hers.

Bel Ordry read the first four lines of it, put it down on the table, and said, "**No.**

"I am not being cold. I am telling you what it is. **The moment I take that sheet it becomes a page about this boundary, in my coat, and it is not about this boundary. It is about a woman at a counter and four households at the bottom of four sets of steps and three people who are not in this building.** And I have been a keeper of nothing for eighteen weeks and I have been good at it, and the reason I was good at it is that I never once held a thing that was somebody else's.

"Somebody has to read it out to me. Dalla. Out loud, and then I will hear it and not have it, and then it goes back down the passage with whoever brought it, and if anybody asks me in a year what was on it I will be able to say what I heard and where I was standing."

Dalla Sen read it out, standing, in the anteroom, at the first bell, and it is in the book in her hand as *read out and not held*, and this is what it said.

---

"**I run paper and small goods between the second gate and four households and the Hearthholds past it, on my own account, with Bevin Tace, Osha Kell and Merrit Amm, and not through any guild counter.**

"**No bond. No register. No counter. I pay them and they pay me and we are not a house.**

"**I am not asking this boundary for anything and there is nothing in this sheet that is addressed to it.**

"**I am writing it to say this: if a paper comes to you off a road with a mark of ours on it, it is ours, and it is not the guild's, and it did not pass a counter, and it did not cost a person a fee. If a paper comes to you off a road and it does not have our mark, it came from a counter, and the counter is the one that wants paying.**

"**There is no name of yours on this and I have not put one there, and the boy is not on this and he is not coming.**

"**Write the bottom of the steps and I will carry it. Not a page. Two sentences, if you have two, and I will say both of them to whoever asks me at the bottom and I will not put a third one on for anybody.**"

---

For a moment the anteroom was quiet, and then the clerk said, "**Thirty-three days and thirty-three nights, and entered in the column with no heading, and it is his.**"

"And I will say the other half, because somebody always has to," said Wen Pask. "**A sheet with three names on it and a woman's is not the same as a sheet with one name on it and an office at the top of it. I have read four hundred of the ones with an office at the top. This one is four people who have put their names next to each other and that is the whole of the difference and it is not a small one, and I am twenty-two and I have been in this Hearthhold thirty-three days and I know that because the clerk keeps it.**"

The room did not argue with that, and the sheet went back down the passage, and Bel Ordry never held it, and at the third bell the anteroom had a jar of water out of a wall in it and a sentence it had not yet written.

---

Two hours and three drafts, and the clerk wrote the third one, and Bel Ordry did not sign it, and that is in the book in his own words.

"**I will not sign it,** and I would like the reason read out, because it is the same sentence as the ruling and I would like the room to hear the two of them next to each other.

"**A keeper of a page is not a holder of a route, and a keeper of a sentence is not a holder of a road, and a man who signs a thing that goes up a road is a man who has put himself against it. She has written three other people's names on her sheet and I am not going to be a fourth name on a page I cannot hold.**

"And I would like it entered that the sentence goes out of this building over the signature of nobody, and that a woman at the bottom of four sets of steps is going to be told something by a person with no name at the top of it, and that this is the first thing this Hearthhold has ever sent that a stranger could not file."

Mikka Hale stopped them before it went, and stopped them on one word, and the clerk repeated it.

"**Nothing.** Say *nothing* again, Dalla, and then say what it means, because I have kept a pot for nineteen weeks and I know exactly what a cook means by nothing, and what she means is *there is no arrangement and nobody is pretending*.

"And that is what the two sentences say, and it is the first time a thing has gone up a road out of this building that has said out loud what it does not have, and I would like the room to sit with how small that is and how long it took."

Then they read it out at the fifth bell, and it is in the clerk's hand, and it is two sentences, and neither of them is a promise.

"**The way up the middle passage to this boundary is not shut against the people who live behind it, it has not been shut since the second week, and it will not be shut for a delivery, and nobody will be asked for anything to come up it.**

"**And at the bottom of those four sets of steps there is nothing, and we have not sent anybody, and we have not got anything, and we are not going to say otherwise at a counter.**"

---

Then Wen Pask asked the question, and he asked it out loud and in front of people, which is the thing he has got from nobody in this building, and it is a thing that has been expensive for him.

"Does it carry people."

Harlan Merrow's draft was on the table in its flat case and the room went quieter than it had been all month.

"**It carries people or it does not,** and the answer is on the sheet, and the answer is no, and I put that word down twice before the sheet went and I am not going to take it back," said Talla Roke's sheet, in the clerk's hand, in a room in which Talla Roke was not. "**And I am not going to explain it here, because if I explain it in this room then the explanation is this boundary's, and it goes up a road in this boundary's mouth, and mine is a woman with three names on a piece of paper.**

"So I will say the two things and the room can do what it likes with them.

"**One: no. Not this month, and not as a favour, and not to a boy.**

"And that is not me being hard on him. **It is that a boy of twenty-two without a bond who gets on a road with a woman he has not met is a thing a woman has to carry, and I carry paper, and there are four households at the bottom of four steps who cannot get paper up those steps if the paper stops halfway.** And it is not the guild's word either. I have been on roads for nine years and I know what it is when a route is a route and when it is a man in a house.

"And **two,** and this is the whole of what I have to say about the boy and it is in the sheet and the clerk has read it: **he is not on it, and he was not offered, and if he is standing in a room waiting for a woman he has never met to say something about his trade then somebody in this room has told him the wrong thing and it was not me.**"

---

Iven Tallo said the hard thing at the fourth bell, and he said it to the boy and not to the room, and the room heard all of it.

"**Then take the wage and do not take the road, and do not think the road is a trade.

"There is no money in this Hearthhold. I have looked for it since the first week and there is none, and a wage in coin is a thing this boundary cannot do and I am not going to let anybody pretend otherwise in a room to make a boy feel better.

"**So the wage is this: your food, your bed, which you have and have had, and the hours you work, entered in the book as work and not as a favour, at the same rate the cook's own book keeps. That is a wage. It is not a trade. A trade is a bond and a bond is money and a man has to be paid in order to be let hold a chisel in a town, and this boundary cannot make a bond and I cannot either, and the fourth paragraph is the only door in the whole of this file that opens on one and I have refused it and will refuse it again.

"So: **you will be paid, and you will not be a carpenter, and both of those are true, and if the second one of them has to be true for a year then it has to be true for a year.** Do not let anybody in this room — *and I am including the man with the counter* — tell you that being kept is the same as being trained. It is not. Being kept is what this Hearthhold is good at."

---

Into the column with no heading the wage went, and it was the first wage that had ever been in that column, and Tams Quill wrote it in his left hand and did not head the column, and gave the reason out loud, and the reason was new.

"**I have refused to head this since the twelfth week and I am not going to stop now, and the reason has changed and I would like the room to have the new one, because the old one was about being praised and the new one is worse.

"**If this column had a heading, it would have a name at the top of it, and a column with a name at the top of it that holds wages is a column somebody can point at.** And the first man to point at it in a bad month is not a thief and is not a villain. **He is a man with a knife out at the throat of a building that cannot pay anybody, and he will say: there, that is what we pay.** And the next man will say it louder, because the last one got away with it.

"So the column stays without a name, and the wage is in it, and in a year nobody can be shown a heading and told what this boundary owes.

"And I have been thinking about it since the water came out of the wall, and the other half of the reason is that **a wage that can be pointed at is a wage that can be stopped, and the man who stops it will not be the man who is owed. It will be a man forty days away with a form.**"

"Entered in the count's own words and read out," said Dalla Sen, "and I would like it entered in mine too, that **the column with no heading now holds a leg, a bundle, thirty-three days and thirty-three nights, a half hinge, a line refused, a page that has gone out of sight, and a wage, and it is the only column in this book with a wage in it and a wage is the only thing anybody in this building is actually owed.**

"And the figure on the crew's side has not moved since the tenth day and the crew's hours have not gone up for the water, and I want that read out, because Renn said it and the keeper said it and nobody else was going to."

"**The crew's hours do not go up because the building got lucky,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing. "**The water was found by a resident and it is carried by residents and it is recorded by residents and the maintenance obligation is not a container that fills up when a thing goes well. The crew's figure is forty-eight and a half and it has been for ten days and it will not move for a jar of water off a wall.**"

"Nor for a charter," said Sefu Aran, from the wheel. "**I have been asked and the answer is no, and I am putting it in the book tonight so that nobody can come and find me in a month having discovered a different answer.** The hours do not move for a good week. They move for a work. **A good week is not a work.**"
