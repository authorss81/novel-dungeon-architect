# Chapter 42 — A Plan for a Room With Water In It

Nera Oduya wrote the air plan for the lower pump gallery on a slate, standing up, with four people objecting to it, and it took most of two days, and it was not a rating, and it was not a document anybody was going to be able to use on a Tuesday.

It could not be written before the hand was looked at, because the hand decided who the plan was for.

Tams Quill came in at the first bell on the second morning with the same sling on, torn out of a length of Ilsa Norr's old apron, and he had not asked anybody to look and nobody had told him it was the day, and he stood in the middle of the anteroom while Mikka Hale took the sling off and turned the hand over in hers and opened the two fingers as far as they went.

They went about as far as a finger and a half.

Nobody said anything for a while.

"Right," said Jo Verrick, who was the one who had written the number down in the thirty-seventh day, because she had asked for it, because she would not put a figure on a page with nothing behind it. "Say what you said you would say, at the time, and not before it."

"It is a month and not nine days," Tams said. "That is what I said I would say, and I am saying it, and I am saying it now, and I would like it written down now and not at the end of the month, because I have watched two people in this room find out what they thought about a thing at the same time as they found out it was true, and I am not doing that."

Dalla Sen wrote it in her own hand, with the time on it, and read it back.

"Right," said Jo again, and then, to nobody: "So the lever is Oren's. Not for the rest of today. For three more weeks, and we will look again at the end of the month, and I am not writing a figure on the page because the figure is his to change and mine to ask for."

"He does not want it in writing," Marek said.

"He does not want a figure in writing, and he wants the date in writing, and those are two different things and you have been in this building nine weeks and you still think they are the same," Jo said, and then stopped, because she had said it unkindly, and she did not take it back, and she said the other half out loud too, which was the thing she had learned to do in the thirty-ninth day. "I am sorry. That was unkind and it was also true. Both of those go in the same place."

Tams put the sling back on himself, which nobody offered to do, and went to the tally board, and turned it round.

"Three weeks," he said. "I am not on the lever, I am on the count, and the count is going to be a man on a lever for three weeks working out a rhythm with his hands and his back and nothing else to think about, and I would like somebody in this room to write down that the person on that lever is going to get tired, because in about ten days he is going to be the tiredest man in this boundary and the only difference is that nobody will be able to see it on him."

"I will write it," Dalla said, and wrote it, and Tams read it back and made her add *and the crew does not have a relief bearer and the boundary may not put one on the rota*, which she wrote, in his words, and did not argue with, because it was the rule she had made herself on the thirty-eighth day.

---

"Ask me," Nera said.

They were in the anteroom, and the anteroom was the only place she would do it in, because the anteroom had the outer service door and the valve string and the ledger bench and the bench with the wrapped plate on it, and because a plan for a flooded room that was written anywhere else in this building would turn into a piece of paper that got carried about.

"I am not going to come and ask you," Marek said.

"Then it is not written." She had the cloth off its hook and she was holding it and not folding it, which was what she did. "I said it in the fortieth day and I am saying it again. I will write an air plan for that gallery when a person has asked to go into it and has said what they are going in there for, and not one day before, and not for a reason that is a shape or a route or a word somebody liked. If you tell me you are going in for the stair, that is not a reason I can put my name to. Say the reason or do not ask."

"I am going in for the jack," Marek said.

"That is a thing."

"It is the only mass in this building that is not somebody's wall, and it is the only thing in this building that can be put under a shore, and I have said that sentence out loud in this room before and I am saying it again and it is still true and it is still not a work."

Jo put her chalk down.

"It is not a work," she said. "Iven's condition, from the twelfth day, in his own words in this room, is that the bulk tools stay recorded below unless a *named work* requires one. The jack is a jack. A named work is a thing with a person on the end of it and a date on it and a thing that will be different when it is finished. So say the work, and if the work is a stair, then say a stair, and then we will find out what the room says about that."

Marek stood there for a second, and the anteroom watched him do it, because it was the thing he did and it was not a small thing.

"The work is this," he said. "The stair's lower side is a line-block and the line is not there and there is a piece of a wash door in the gap doing a stop's work. I want to give that line something on this side to be made fast to, so that when the slab returns the return goes into a shore standing on a floor that already exists, instead of into a hole nobody can see into. And that shore needs something under its foot that is not a wall and not a fill and not a lying parapet slab, and the only thing in this building that is that thing is in a room with six weeks of water in it."

"That is a work," Jo said, after a moment. "That is a work with a beginning and an end and something that will be different. It is a proposal, though, and it is a proposal about a stair, and a proposal about a stair is not a thing anybody in this boundary has agreed to, and I am not going to let the air plan for a room be written on the back of an agreement that does not exist."

"I am not asking for the agreement," Marek said. "I am asking whether you will write the plan for the room."

"No," Nera said. "I will write the plan for the room, and on the first line of it I will write that the work is a proposal and that drawing the water out of that room is lawful and separate, and if the proposal never goes anywhere then the water still comes down and the jack still comes up and the school corner's fall still has to be cut. And you will not like the first line, and I am writing it anyway."

"Write it," Marek said.

Dalla read it back, twice, and copied it into the ledger, and then read out the line she had copied, because that is the practice, and the line was: *Draw-down and recovery of the public-works cache. The work the cache is for is a proposal before this boundary and is not agreed by anybody in this boundary. This plan covers the room, the draw, and the person, and does not cover the proposal.*

Then Sefu Aran, who had been standing at the wheel the whole of the two days, read his own paragraph, separately, as he does, and had it marked separately, and would not have it argued with.

"My part says that work in these rooms is consented to room by room by the people who keep the rooms," he said, "and it says the crew is not tenants and not neighbours and not residents, and it says that if a stair begins to be worked on, nobody is bound by anything they did not agree to. None of those three sentences is changed by a plan for a flooded room. What *is* changed by it is this: from today there is a room in this boundary that a person goes into, and that room has a keeper, and the keeper is me, and I have not been asked by anybody to hold a watch in it and I am not holding one, and the plan says so, and the plan also says that a person coming up out of that room comes up into my count and I count him."

He stopped, and looked at the slate.

"And the second half, which is the half I am not going to let go past. Everything I just said assumes the work is a proposal. If the work stops being a proposal, then that sentence of mine is the one that has to be read again, and it will be read again by me, in this room, and I will not need anybody's permission to read it."

"Noted," Dalla said, and wrote it, and Sefu made her put the word *separately* beside it, which she did.

Leena Orr had the next objection, and it was not about authority.

"You have written an air plan and there is no time in it," she said. "That is not a small thing, and I want it said out loud, because every plan anybody in this building has read off a board in nine weeks has had a duration on it, and yours has got a word instead, and the word is *interval*, and an interval is not a length. Nera, how long is a person in that gallery?"

"I do not know," Nera said.

"Then say that on it."

"It is on it. It says the person is out before the lever comes off, and it does not say how long the person is in, and it does not say how long the person *can* be in, and I will not put a number on it, because I have never been in that room and neither has anybody in this room since the ninth week, and a number I cannot stand behind is how the last air plan in my life went wrong." She did not soften it, and then she did, in the specific way she had which was not softening at all. "There is one number I will put on it, and it is the only one. The pump goes twenty-six strokes and stops for six minutes and goes nineteen more. The six minutes are the part with the water up and the air in this boundary going the wrong way and the stair pushing through the shaft under the anteroom. So: no person is in that gallery, and nothing of the crew's is in that gallery, in the six minutes after the lever comes off. Not once. Not for a lamp. Not for a second."

"Write it as a condition and not a time," Jo said, "so that anybody reading it in a year knows it was a thing somebody decided and not a thing somebody measured."

"That is the whole of my art," Nera said, "and I have never once been thanked for it."

---

The plan came to nine lines and it took two days, and the lines were the last two days' work and the arguments were the two days before those, and nobody in that anteroom was ever going to be allowed to see the lines without hearing the arguments again, so Dalla Sen read the whole thing out twice on the same afternoon and made Jo Verrick stand next to her while she did it.

Nobody in the anteroom would have allowed the lines to appear on their own. Jo read them, one at a time, and four people took them apart.

The first line was the interval, and Mikka Hale objected to it on the grounds that the crew would go down hungry and a hungry man in water is a man who comes up too fast. Her own refusal from the thirty-eighth day stood and she repeated it: she was not feeding a work party, feeding a work party is a wage with a pot on it. The crew ate what the kitchen ate and when the kitchen ate. Tams wrote that he would eat less and said so in his own column, and Dalla recorded the words *he will eat less* with his name on it, and nobody softened it into a hardship.

The second line was the isolation: the delivery check shut except while drawing, the pump's air chamber isolated, the outer service door open, the stair-side grille shut, the bypass hatch latched, and the anteroom's cloth on its string. That line was not argued with. It was the whole of nine weeks of argument, arriving at once.

The third was the cord. Eight feet of draw cord, Nera's own, off the valve board, released by her for the third time in seven weeks, and she released it herself in front of witnesses and wrote the release and the reason, which was that the bay's working depth from the ladder foot was six feet, and there is no other line in this building, and the hand line is on a cracked school-door head, and the bell rope is Dalla's, and the black cord is forty feet on a hook that Sefu has not released since the eighteenth day.

"Write that last clause in," Nera said. "Write that somebody asked Sefu about the black cord in the second week and was told no, and has not asked again. I want the next person who stands here to know it was asked once and not twice."

The fourth line was the turn. If the cloth moves, the person is out, the well is closed, the gallery refloods, and the day is over. Not shortened. Over. And Jo made Nera say the difference out loud for the room, because *over* and *shortened* had been confused once already in this building and had cost a shift and a shutter.

The fifth was the finding, and Nera's own, and the one she would not take back: whatever the shut at the school corner pushes the air into, it does not push it into a room she maintains. "I am not going to be able to tell you where it goes. I am going to be able to tell you where I can see it, and that is not the same thing, and I am not going to write a plan that pretends it is."

The sixth was the seat. And this one came out of Jo, hard, and out of Nera's own mouth, and it is the line that the whole of the last three weeks has been standing on.

"Drawing that room down means the delivery check is open for the whole draw," Nera said. "It is the only thing in this building that makes that check chatter and pack. Four cleanings have been paid for out of Jo's shutter route and there are four left, and I have said *or fewer* out loud in this room twice, and I am not the person who decides how they are spent. I do not maintain that pump. Jo maintains that pump. The seat is Jo's, and the draw is a proposal, and the cost of the draw is half a day of a route that pays her, and she is the one who has to say yes with her own mouth."

"I have already said yes," Jo said, "and Dalla has written it, and I said yes before anybody asked me, which I would like noted, because I have been the one asked in this building for six weeks and I have got used to it."

"Noted," Dalla said, and read it back.

The seventh was the person, and it was the line the room fought hardest over, and it was not settled that day and Jo Verrick wrote it as open and read it back as open and refused, in her own hand, to let anybody close it in a hurry. Pell Roke, from the bench by the anteroom step, where he had been for two days with a slate and his ear, said the one thing that the four of them who had been arguing it had all missed, and he said it the way he said everything, which is once and without ornament: "You are all arguing about who goes down it. Nobody has said how the other one gets out."

The eighth was what happens to the tools, and it was not a line of Nera's at all, and she gave it away, because it was not hers.

"It is Iven's condition and Dalla's record and the register," Nera said. "The jack is public-works equipment and it is guild-frozen pending a hearing and it is under my inventory, and the condition is that it stays recorded below unless a named work requires one, and that any courier wanting it moved gives a written order saying where it goes and who receives it. So the plan cannot say who carries the jack up. It can only say that if the jack comes up, it is *received* — by a person, named, in the ledger, with a time, and the receiver is not one of you six, because the six of you are not a party to a hearing about a public-works jack and cannot be the receiver of one."

"Who is the receiver?" Marek said.

"Not me and not Jo and not the valve," Nera said. "It is the boundary, and the boundary's receiver for anything that comes up out of that room is its keeper, and the keeper is Sefu, and the keeper's clerk writes it, and if the keeper says no then it does not come up, and none of you get to be disappointed about it in front of him."

Sefu said, "That is right, and I want it read back, and I want it read back here and not in a hearing."

Then the ninth line, which Nera read last, and which is the reason it is called a plan and not a rating.

"This is not a certification," she said. "A certification says a room can be occupied and how many and for how long, and I have written one of those once in this building, for the anteroom, and it is on the valve board, and it is ten people including one litter for two hours with the outer door open and the pump attended. This is not that. This is a method for a room that one person is going into once, in the dark, in water, for eleven minutes, to take four things off a shelf. It is the only kind of plan I will write in this building while I cannot see the bottom of that room, and I would like the next person who reads it to know that it is a method and not a promise, and that I have put the word *once* on it on purpose."

"Once," Iven said. "You are giving me one trip."

"I am giving you one trip, and on the second trip I will know a great deal more than I do now, and on the second trip the plan will be a different document, and the second trip is not this week's problem." She hung the cloth on its hook. "And I would like it said out loud, by somebody, that if a person is still in that gallery when the lever comes off then the person has to come up on an eight-foot cord with a jack in the other hand, and the cord is eight feet and the bay is six and there is nothing to hold. I have not put that in the plan because it is not a line of the plan. It is a line of the reason the plan is short."

Nobody argued, and the plan went onto the valve board in Nera's hand, and Dalla copied it into the ledger and read the copy back, and the copy was not the plan and the plan stayed where Nera had put it, and at the bottom of the ledger entry Dalla wrote, in her own hand, and read it out:

*The plan is for a room. It is not for the stair. The stair is a proposal and the plan says so on its first line, and the plan was not written to get the stair agreed to, and if the stair is never agreed to then the water still comes down and the jack still comes up and the school corner's fall still gets cut. Requested by Marek Venn. Reason given aloud: the screw jack. Second reason given aloud, at my request, after the first: the jack is the only mass in this building that is not somebody's wall. Both recorded. Nera Oduya.*

And in the anteroom, afterwards, Tams turned the tally board round one more time and put the five public-works items on it in a column, because a column is a place where somebody can see what it costs, and he wrote under the column, in the largest hand he had, *NONE OF IT IS OURS*, and then went to find out what Oren Sill wanted for supper, since a man on a lever for three more weeks was owed a pot before anything else was.
