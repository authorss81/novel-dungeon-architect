# Chapter 157 — A Post With A Name In A Register

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the thirty-first week, a Tuesday, and the school did not run, and a carrier came up the middle passage with a stiff cover and a proper seal on it and Iven Tallo's name in the second line, and the name on the paper was the whole of the difficulty and everybody in the anteroom found that out inside a minute.**

**It has come back for the fourth time in this building and this time it pays in coin, and it does not pay him and it does not refuse him either, and the whole difference between those two is a man standing in a room refusing to say either.**

A different carrier this time, and the paper came up in a stiff cover with a proper seal on it, and he read the outside of it before he handed it over, and that is the whole of what he said.

"**It is not urgent and it has no day on it. I am to hand it to a named person and wait, and I am to wait as long as is reasonable, and I would like it established in your clerk's book that I have said the second part of that out loud before I handed anything over.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a carrier is now establishing the shape of his own patience in my book before he hands over a paper, and that I have been clerk in this building for thirty weeks and this is the first time one has done it and I am going to notice it and not improve on it.**"

Iven Tallo took the stiff cover off the anteroom table where Bel Ordry put it, and read it standing, and nobody sat down, and when he had read it he did not give it to Dalla Sen and he did not put it down.

"**It is a route-holder's post.**"

"**Say the whole of it out loud,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because everything a stranger puts on this table gets read out in this anteroom within a day, and this one has your name in it and that is a different kind of document and it gets read out louder.**"

"**Route-holder, for the working life. A bond lodged with the office and held. A wage in coin on the first and the fifteenth, which I have not had in money since I was twenty-two. Hours entered against a named route in a register kept by the office, and the register shows them, and anybody can look at the register and see that I was there.**"

"**An apprentice back on the roll. Not on a suspension and not on a supervision fee. On the roll, with a number, and his name on it and not *the apprentice of* anything.**"

"**And the fourth paragraph, which I have refused three times, in full and not shortened, because a man has come four days up a road and he should not be given a smaller version of it than anybody else in this building has had.**"

He read it out of the carrier's own sheet, in the anteroom, standing, in the order it was printed, and Dalla Sen entered every word of it and entered that it was the fourth time and that the three refusals before it were on a page in the same book.

Then he stopped reading, and held the sheet, and said the reason he was not going to say the obvious thing, and it was not noble and he said so first.

"**I am not going to refuse it.**"

"**Say why, and say it in a carpenter's words, because if I do not get the reason right this morning then nobody in this room will ever get it right and I will be a man in a story.**"

"**Because a refusal given before a thing has been understood is a thing a man says to look clean, and I have watched a man do it in this building, and it was me, in the twenty-second week, about the sill.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in his own words, and enter that he has named a refusal of his own in a room where nobody asked him to name one.**"

"**And I am not accepting it either, and I want that in the book in the same breath, because a man who sits in a room holding a piece of paper for a week is already halfway to holding it.**"

"**The date I refuse it is not today. Today I have not understood it. There are two things in it I have not understood and I have been in this building thirty weeks and I do not think I am going to understand them in a room with nine people in it.**"

Then Dalla Sen did the thing she had never done in this building, and she did it in front of a carrier with a proper seal on his cover, and what she did was read a document against itself and find the hole, and the hole was not the guild's.

Both of the carrier's hands went flat on the cover the moment it was on the table, the way a man holds a thing he has come to be a witness to, and they did not come off again for the rest of the morning.

"**I am going to read the outside of this against the inside of it and I am going to do it in this room and not in a corridor, and I am going to say when I am doing it so that the man who brought it can hear me do it.**"

"**Inside: a post in a named route, in a register kept by an office, with a named holder, in exchange for a bond and a fee and a year of attendance.**"

"**Outside, on the sheet, in the second line, where a person goes: this boundary.**"

"**Read the second line again.**"

"**This boundary,**" said Iven Tallo.

"**Then the post is in this Hearthhold,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and this boundary's own charter says in terms, in a clause argued out loud in the nineteenth week before a mark was made, that no holder of a route in this Hearthhold is to be entered by the office against any work here, and that the two are one clause and not two.**"

"**And the guild knows that.**"

"**The guild knows that, and the guild is offering a post inside a boundary that has refused it three times, and it is offering it in a stiff cover with a proper seal on it, and it came up a passage in a carrier's hand.**"

Renn Vale came down two steps for the first time in two days, which she does not do, and she said the sentence that made the morning a different morning from the one it had started as.

"**Then it is not a post. It is a hand out of a gap in a clause, and the hand is wearing a bond and a wage and an apprentice's number on it, and a person in a bad month is going to hold the wage and not look at the hand.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Marek Venn.

"**The rest of it is that I have spent a month telling this building that a name on a page about a room is a handle, and a title is a post is a box a letter goes into, and a post is what a blank page becomes when somebody fills it in. And this is a post. Somebody has drawn it, in a stiff cover, and put my name in it, and it is lawful, and it is in this Hearthhold, and it would be the first post in this building since the ninth room was given a name and a row, and the row was refused.**"

"**And the person it will find,**" she said, "**is not me. It is a boy on a bench at the top of nine steps with a crutch and an ear against a stone, and it is going to find him because his master's name is going into a register and a boy's roll number goes with it.**"

"**And I am not going to be the woman who says in a room that a boy is not a route to anything, because a boy is a route to a roll, and everybody in this building knows it and one of them has said it out loud and it was not me.**"

There was no improving on that, and Iven Tallo said the thing that came next in his own words and asked for it to be entered, and it was the sentence that made the offer into what it actually was.

"**Where is the post.**"

"**Say it again.**"

"**Where is the post. That is my question and it is the only one I have got, and I have not been able to ask it for a fortnight and I asked it this morning because the room had a carrier in it and I could not ask it on my own.**"

It came out of the carrier before anybody asked, which is what a good carrier does.

"**The first survey camp above the water, at the third holding. It is four days up the middle passage and then a day and a half on a cart road, and the camp is not yours and is not a Hearthhold, and it has beech in it and a wall in lime going up on a foundation that is nine weeks old, and it took the sheet.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**The camp is one of the three that took the halfpenny sheet. It has had timber since the sheet came out. It has been able to shut the road at the third holding when a clerk at a counter says so since the sheet came out, and it has not had to shut it yet, and the clerk at the counter is a competent woman who did not know about the Tuesday.**"

Then Bel Ordry said the half of the morning that nobody else was going to say, and he said it in his own shape, standing, with his hand on the date he does not say.

"**I want to say the part that is against him and I want to say it before anybody argues with him, and I am aware of what I am doing.**"

"**A boundary that has timber can shut a route when a clerk at a counter says so. That is not a guess. That is the rule that a hundred copies of a halfpenny sheet were printed for, and the rule is in that carrier's hand on that table in the fourth paragraph, and it works in both directions.**"

"**And a man who has been in this building thirty weeks, and who took a third of the crew's hours, and who knows more about a sill and a joint and a channel in this Hearthhold than anybody except two people in it, is being offered a bond and a wage and an apprentice to take to a camp four and a half days up a road.**"

"**And it is at the exact moment that a wall in lime is the only thing anybody has said is the answer to a floor that nine children sit on.**"

"**I am not saying he wants it. I am saying the shape of the thing is the shape of the thing.**"

Iven Tallo said one sentence, and it was about a boy's foot, and it was nine words long, and he asked for it to be entered exactly as he said it and not improved.

Nobody in the anteroom had said anything for some time, and out in the corridor a bell that had not been due went over, and the man who had asked to be asked did not look up.

"**The first one out of a room with eleven people in it was a girl of nine.**"

"**Entered in nine words in his own words and not improved,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the name of the girl is on a page in this book and that the keeper of the page has said the half of the morning that is against him and I have entered his half first.**"

Then the carrier asked the only question he had been waiting two days to ask, and he asked it as a man doing a job and not as a man being clever.

"**Am I to take an answer back?**"

"**Yes,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and the answer is that a man in this room has not refused and has not accepted, and I want that in the cover of your stiff envelope in the carrier's own hand, because you will be the only person in four days who knows that both of those things are true.**"

"**I will write it on the outside and not inside,**" said the carrier, "**and I will not put the name against it, and I will hand it back to the same man in the same room, and I will say that I did, and if anybody asks me up the road what I carried I will tell them I carried a piece of paper and a thing that was not a refusal.**"

"**That is a good carrier,**" said Talla Roke, "**and I want to say something about him before he goes, and it is not a kind thing and it is not about him.**"

"**A carrier who will carry a not-refusal back up a road four days without a name on it is the first person in a month who has come down that passage and refused to become an office. And that is not praise. It is a description of the gap.**"

"**Everybody in this room is refusing to be an office, and there is a carrier on this table who has figured out how not to be one, and a man with a tally on a road, and a woman at a counter who took a sheet off a board. That is four people this month, and every one of them found their own way of doing it, and none of them found the same way.**"

"**Enter that in my words and do not improve on it,**" she said, **"and enter that I have been asked this question twice in a month and nobody has answered it, and that a carrier has just described four answers to it in a room in four days without being asked a single question about any of them.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the count was read, and the board was not walked by either of them, and the reason the clerk gave was that a man's name was on a piece of paper in the room.**

"**Four today and a hundred and eighty-six since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and three and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks. The crew are at sixty-six and a half.**"

"**I entered those and I did not read them, and the reason is that a piece of paper with a name in it came up four days of road in a carrier's hand this morning and I entered a sentence of a carpenter's in it, and I am not going to stand up at a sixth bell and read a number out loud over a room that has just spent the morning finding out that a boy's roll number is a route.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is not on the fill and has not been since the second day of this week and there is no number for it and nobody has told me where it went.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is ninety-two days and ninety-two nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be ninety-three, and I have said in six weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and I have written it in six weeks running and nobody has thanked me in six weeks running and I have stopped expecting that and I am not going to stop writing it.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the count did not walk it either, and his reason is his own and I am entering it in his words because he gave it out loud and he is entitled to have it in my hand.**"

"**There is a piece of paper in this room with a man's name on it, and there is a column on this board with nothing over the top of it, and I am not going to be the man who puts the two next to each other in front of a room.**"

"**That is the third week running he has said something about that column that I have entered, and I am entering that too, and I am not going to let three of them accumulate into a policy without somebody saying so in a room.**"

---

**A carrier went back up the middle passage on Tuesday afternoon with a thing on the outside of a stiff cover that was not a refusal, and a man who came down four days a week ago with nothing in his hands is somewhere on a cart road with a boy on a roll, and a wall in lime is going up at the third holding on a foundation nine weeks old.**

**On the third day of this week the nine go in for the twenty-fifth morning and fourteen lines of a store's list get read out by a boy of fourteen, and a man with a lamp in his left hand stands at a frame at the low end of a corridor and looks at what is coming off it.**

**In a clerk's book, in a count's own words, is the ninth reason he will not head that column: there are rooms in this building that have never been asked what the person in them is for, and two of them are four days up a road.**
