# Chapter 213 — What Four Hundred Years Of Water Does To Paper

The third day of the forty-first week is a Wednesday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the forty-seventh morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door, and four fathoms of water with nine people in it were going up a rope at the same time two floors away, and nobody put those two facts next to each other because the clerk had asked not to.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the thirty-third reading, and the same as thirty-two, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked her, and the count of mornings is thirty-three and is the same count as the wall's, and the folding rule is in a belt and has been on a belt for thirty-three mornings.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is eighteen.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it was right, and the clerk entered that the cook was in the room and entered not one word about whether it was true. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing, and the clerk entered that she did not ask. The sixth was the pump, and it was attended and it was running, and it was the only thing in this building that had not stopped for nineteen weeks, and nobody in the room said that either, and the clerk entered that nobody had.

Then Emrys Callow stood up, which is what he does about once a week, and he had a cup in his hand that had not been in it for an hour.

"**It is Wednesday,**" he said.

Nobody in the anteroom needed to be told what Wednesday was. A clerk had written it in a book a fortnight ago in the words of the man who said it, and a physician had been asked not to take it apart, and a room had spent a fortnight not asking.

"**Then say it,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step.

"**The water is going to take four and a half hours off me today and I have had a fortnight to find a reason and I have got one and it is not a reason,**" said Emrys Callow. "**I am going to be in that water for four and a half hours and I am going to be the first man in this building to be in it, and I have not said that out loud to anybody in nine years, and I have not said it in a room in nine years, and I have said it in a room this morning and that is the whole of what has changed in nine years and it has changed because of a woman who reads walls and a woman who reads air and a rope.**"

He put the cup down.

"**That is not a reason for anything,**" he said. "**It is a thing about me. A man who wants to be first into the water is a man who has spent nine years watching five other men go first and has told himself it was a system and it was not, it was a queue, and I was at the front of it, and I have never said so.**"

"**Then say the half against,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The half against is that a foreman who goes into a room first is a foreman who is not there when his five come up,**" Emrys Callow said. "**And I know that and I have thought about it for a fortnight and I have not found a better sentence, and if somebody in this room has one they can have it after I have said the thing I have to say and not before.**"

Then Mero Senn took his bag off the wall and set it on the floor, which he has done twice in this building and the second time was three days ago.

"**I am not going to take it apart,**" he said, "**and I want it entered that I am not, and I want the reason entered, because a man who keeps a rule for a week and then breaks it on the day the rule was due is a man who is telling a room what the rule was for.**"

"**Go on,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**On Saturday I would have taken it apart. On Saturday I had four minutes and a bag against a wall and a man in a room who had been carrying a sentence for a fortnight, and I have taken men apart for less. He told me on Saturday that it was a sentence with a shape in it and was not to be taken apart, and a man who puts a shape in a sentence on purpose is a man who has thought about it longer than I have, and if I take it apart this morning then the shape was a performance and the fortnight was a queue of his as well.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything, and the clerk entered it in the physician's words, and entered that nobody had asked him.

"**Then it is a reason,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because a reason that has not been said in a room is not a reason, and it has now been said in a room, and the clerk would like it entered that she has been waiting two weeks to be allowed to write that sentence and has not written it once.**"

"**Entered,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step, "**and I would like a clerk to note that the crew's column does not move on the strength of a foreman's reasons about himself.**"

"**It does not,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the reason is in the book and the reason is that a foreman does not ask a crew for a work in a room where the work is not settled, and there is no work settled in this room this morning, and there are nine men coming up a rope who are not his.**"

Then the air engineer came down all three of her steps, which she does perhaps twice in five volumes, and she was already speaking before she got to the floor.

"**I agree,**" said Nera Oduya.

"**Say what you agree to,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I agree that it is a chamber,**" Nera Oduya said. "**I said yesterday that I would not call it one in a room in front of a book until somebody showed me what goes round it, and this morning somebody went into two rooms off it and came up with nine men and a foreman, and that is what goes round it, and I am not a fool about my own trade. A room that other people's work ends in is a room. It is not a chamber of a city yet and I am not going to say it is, and I will tell you why in one sentence and then I am going back up my steps.**"

"**Why,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Because a chamber is a room with an institution round it, and a building is a thing that stands up, and an institution is a thing that people do in the same place on purpose more than once,**" said Nera Oduya. "**A forge is a room. The thing people do in it is a trade, and in four hundred years it stops being a trade and starts being a word, and that is when it is a chamber. I have not got a word for that room and I am not going to make one up in a wet week, and I am not going to let this book have one before it has had a year.**"

She went back up her three steps and did not sit down.

Then a clerk at the end of a trestle said she was going down, and nobody stopped her, and she gave the reason before she was asked, which is her practice.

"**I am going down to look at the rooms off that gallery,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the reason is that a salvage claim cannot be answered by anybody without knowing what is in the rooms, and this boundary is owed a hearing in a room and the man who is owed it is one of the nine, and the honest answer to a claim over a room you have not been in is that you have not been in it, and that answer is now finished.**"

"**Then take somebody,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I am going to take a slate,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am not going to take a person, and the reason is that what is down there is a room full of shelves and I am the only person in this building whose trade is a room full of shelves, and if I take a witness then two people come up with two accounts and the first thing this book does with two accounts is compare them, and I have not got time to compare two accounts and neither has anybody else.**"

"**Say the half against,**" said Dalla Sen, to herself, and nobody laughed, because she does it in rooms and they are used to it.

"**The half against is that a clerk who goes into a room alone and comes back and writes down what she saw has nobody in the room to say she saw it differently,**" she said. "**Entered, in my own words, and entered that I chose it, and that the choice is mine and not this boundary's, and that a boundary that sent its clerk alone into a flooded room would be a boundary doing the thing it has refused to do for forty weeks, and it would be doing it with a person in wet clothes as the reason.**"

She went down at about the second bell with a slate and a stub of chalk and a piece of black cord, and that is where the clerk went and stayed for three hours.

Marek Venn sat at the end of the trestle for the whole of them with the shut book under his forearm and did about four things and three of them were not anything.

The first was that he took the shape out of his head, deliberately, the way a man empties a pocket, and put it on the table in front of himself where he could see all of it, and looked at it. One line. A cistern, a gallery, a pump station, and a rule on the line, and he had told a room on the fifth day of the fortieth week that he would rather two other people found out wrong in a room with no name on it than that he told them, and they had found out, and they had found out a chamber and nine men and a floor that does not dip, and he had given the only thing he had away in order to be wrong in the direction of other people, and he had been, and it had cost him a week and a half of being the man who found out and gained him nothing whatsoever except the right to sit here.

The second was that he worked out, sitting still, what a room of shelves four hundred years under water is worth, and then worked out that the question was not worth but was *whose*, and then had to stop, because a room that starts counting what a thing is worth in that particular way has begun to make an inventory, and an inventory is a column, and a column has a heading over it, and this boundary had spent forty weeks refusing six of those and had no reason to stop on a Wednesday afternoon.

The third was that he found out he was listening for the pump.

It ran from the second bell to the fifth, two people turning it, Renn Vale and Emrys Callow, and it made the sound it makes in the corner by the seam, and Marek Venn had been in this building for nine weeks and had decided some months ago that the sound had stopped being a sound in his head and had become the floor he stood on. It was not. He found out on that Wednesday that he had been listening for it every day since the Friday it stopped, and that when it started again on the Saturday of the fortieth week something in him had put itself down and stopped being tense, and that it had been doing that behind his back for a fortnight without consulting him.

The fourth thing he did was to notice that this building had a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle and a man who could read a wall and a woman who could read air and a clerk who could read a ruin, and that on the Friday of that week three of those four had put a sentence into a room that was the first sentence any of them had given in five volumes that was not a refusal, and that he was the fourth and had said nothing, and that the reason he had said nothing was that there was a woman asleep at the end of the cook's room with a lamp in her hand and nine men in the water, and the room was full enough.

She came back at about the fifth bell, and she was not wet, because the shelves are above the water, and she had been up at that height for three hours and her hands were white.

Then she sat down and put the slate on the trestle and did not turn it round.

"**Four fathoms to the floor of the aisle,**" she said. "**Three tiers of shelves cut into the dressed stone, a shelf to a tier, and the cut is dressed, and the same hand cut it as cut the walls, and the cutters did not mind about books, which tells you more about the building in a sentence than anything anybody said in it yesterday.**"

"**Then say what was on them,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Water damage,**" said Dalla Sen.

She said the two words and then stopped for long enough that the pump made four strokes.

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Four hundred years of it,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The top tier is gone. It is not torn and it is not burnt and it is not eaten, it is *fused*. Four hundred years of wet paper in a closed room presses itself into a single grey mass about the thickness of a hand and it keeps the shape of the shelf and it keeps, on the top of it, the shape of the spines. I could tell you there were eleven on the second tier of the west run and I could tell you that two of them were taller than the rest and I could tell you that the tall ones were bound and the short ones were not, and I could tell all of that from the shape of a thing that no longer has any writing in it, and I have just done that, in my head, standing in that aisle, and it took me about a minute.**"

"**Then that is useful,**" said Iven Tallo.

"**That is not useful,**" said Dalla Sen, "**that is me. What I can do with a ruin of paper is read the ruin. It tells me how many and in what order and in what condition and at what three heights the water stood, and the water stood three times and the salt is on the wall at all three, and the middle one is the highest, and there is a line of rust on the second tier of the east run about the height of my hand which I would want a person who reads metal to look at and I have not asked one and I am not going to today.**"

"**Then what is the half against,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The half against is that I have just spent four hours reading a room that four hundred years ago people were in every day to write things down, and I have come up with three water heights and a number of spines, and the thing that room was for is not in any of that, and it never was going to be, and I knew that when I went down and I went anyway, and I would like that entered as the half against and I would like it entered in the space where a reason goes.**"

She put her hand on the slate.

"**And there is one more thing and I am going to say it slowly.**"

Nobody in that anteroom moved.

"**Out of the top of the run on the east side, in what used to be a case, there is a single leaf that came out whole,**" said Dalla Sen. "**One. It is wet and it is not fused and it is not torn. It is a leaf and it has a back to it, which means it was bound, which means it was in with other leaves and the other leaves are the grey mass and this one is not, and I have not been able to account for it and I have been in that aisle for four hours thinking about it and I cannot.**"

"**Then read it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**No,**" said Dalla Sen, and the room did the thing it does about four times in a week, which was nothing, for about two seconds. "**And I am going to say the reason and it is the only professional thing I have said in this room in a month, and it is this. A thing that is wet cannot be read in a room and put in a book, because what it says at four fathoms is not what it will say when it is dry. Ink runs. I have watched it run. If I read that leaf this afternoon I will read it wrong, and I will be the only person alive who has read it, and there will be a version of it in my head that I cannot get rid of, and in a year when it is dry somebody who reads better than me will read it properly and I will be the fool who read it wet and I will have said so in a room first.**"

"**That is a good reason,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**It is a clerk's reason and it is the reason I am allowed,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I have thought about the other one for about an hour down there and I am not going to say it in this room, and I am not going to write it down either, and I would like somebody in a year to notice that a clerk went down into a flooded room with a slate and came up with one thing in her apron and entered two sentences about it and did not enter a third.**"

"**What goes in the book,**" said Renn Vale.

"**The condition, the order, the count, the three heights, and one sentence that a single leaf was recovered whole out of a case on the east run and is in my keeping,**" said Dalla Sen. "**Nothing about what it is. Nothing about what it says. Nothing about which run or which tier or which shelf, because in a year one of those is an address and an address is a place somebody goes, and I am not going to write down a place somebody goes.**"

"**Then you have written down that there is one and you have not written down where it is,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I have written down that there is one and I have not written down where it is, and I would like the clerk to enter that she entered the first half and did not enter the second half, and that the not-entering is the entry, and that if I am wrong about that then somebody can correct me in a year and I will not be here.**"

"**Entered, and entered that the clerk chose the omission and did not choose the accident,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**And the cost of the going,**" said Marek Venn, from the end of the trestle.

"**The cost of the going is that this morning I am the only person in this building who knows there is nothing in that gallery worth salvaging,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I have known it since the third hour down there, when I worked out that a salt line three hands deep on a wall means the water went in and out and did not stay. A man with a wooden tally came up those steps on the fourth day of the fortieth week and put four sentences on this trestle and one of them was that a claim covers salvage of water, timber, metal and salt-crust. Salt-crust is spore salt and it is worth carrying and it grows in dry galleries. I have been under four fathoms of standing water in a room that filled and emptied three times, and there is no dry gallery in it, and there is no timber in it, and there is nothing in it a person could lift.**"

She put both hands flat on the trestle.

"**So a guild's claim is sitting on this table over a room with nothing in it, and this boundary has known that since the third hour this afternoon and the office four days away has not, and the office is going to keep its claim, because the office does not know, and it is going to be a claim over an empty room for as long as it takes somebody to walk down there and find that out, and I am the person who found that out, and I did not want to have found that out.**"

"**Why not,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Because a clerk who knows a thing first is a clerk who owns a thing,**" said Dalla Sen. "**That is not a guild rule and it is not a Compact rule and it is in nobody's charter. It is what happens in a room. And this room has spent forty weeks explaining to offices that a person who can be found is not a keeper, and I have found a thing and I cannot give it away and I have not got a column to give it to and I am not going to make one. So it is mine, and it is in my apron, and one half of it is in this book and the other half is in my coat, and neither of those is anybody else's, and I have not got a single word to give a person in a year who finds out.**"

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither was read, and the reason went in beside them: a clerk who has spent the afternoon alone in a flooded room reading a ruin has not got a total in her and would say it wrong.

Six today and three hundred and forty-four since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and sixty-one and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that and was read out loud with them, and the reason was given, and the reason was that a day on which nine men came up a rope is a day on which a clerk is entitled to say a number she has been saying for forty-seven weeks and not think about it.

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty-eight days and one hundred and fifty-eight nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and fifty-nine, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for forty-seven weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in forty-seven weeks.**"

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a clerk has entered one thing in this book today and deliberately not entered a second, and a board is a column, and a clerk does not walk a column on a day when she has worked out for herself what a column is.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason is in the book, and it is that the foreman said the reason out loud in a room this morning and the asking has not been made, and a crew's column is a quantity of asking and not a quantity of a man's reasons about himself.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for nineteen weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure attached to that gate by anybody, and nine people came up a rope off a passage at the second gate today and none of them is anybody's.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a book with three water heights in it and one sentence about a leaf in a woman's coat, and a pot at three days, and a man who read walls sitting on the floor of a room because he had been in water for two hours and would not take a chair from anybody, and a physician's bag still on the floor where he had put it down.
