# Chapter 100 — Not On A Figure

On the fifth day of the nineteenth week the corridor was open, and nine children went through it at the first bell in twos at a walk, and the schoolteacher walked behind them, and before the second bell a courier came up the middle passage with a printed sheet that the middle gate's counter was selling for a halfpenny, and the sheet is the reason this does not end the way anybody in this room wanted it to.

It is headed *the Cartwrights' maintenance undertaking* and it is offered at the middle gate's store to any Hearthhold whose keeper applies, and it is dated in the office's own hand for the month after next, and it says in the fourth line, in the same words as the fourth head, that a named holder will be entered against a named route in a register kept by the office.

"**It is a halfpenny,**" said Nell Ferrin's note on the back of it, which was the only part of the document anybody in this room wanted, "**and the office has printed a hundred and I have counted them, because I do the count on Fridays, and I would like whoever is holding this to know that the office is not doing this because it thinks it is right. The office is doing this because the first one of you said no and three of them have not been asked yet and the office would rather ask three of them than find out whether it was wrong.**

"**And I am fifty-one and I have been on that counter nineteen years and I have watched a thing like this twice and both times it worked. I am not going to pretend I think it will fail.**"

"Entered," said Dalla Sen, "and the clerk would like it noted that the note is on the back of a halfpenny advertisement and not on the notice, and that is the fourth time a person at that counter has put the true sentence on the back of a piece of paper."

---

Tams Quill had walked the board twice and read both columns at the second bell, and he named no day and gave no reason, and the room had stopped asking him for one some weeks ago and had not been wrong to.

"**Fifty-two and a half,**" he said. "**Forty-eight and a half at the end of the seventeenth week, and four since, and every hour of the four is a work: two on the second day at the door and the haunch behind it, in the pump's window, asked for by the keeper with the terms out loud; and two on the fourth and fifth days on the fourth column and the counter-draft, in a room. The record and the three readings are in the other column with the cook's name and the clerk's on them, and not in this one.**"

"And the water cost the crew nothing. **The crew's hours did not go up for the water because the water was found by a resident and a resident's good week is not a work, and I want that in the book, because in a year somebody will try to prove that this crew saved a Hearthhold and the answer is that the crew's figure went up four hours and not one of the four was a jar of water, and the water came out of a wall on a Tuesday because a boy counted pails.**"

"And the other column," said Bel Ordry.

"**A hundred and seventeen and a quarter.** And the last figure anybody in this room heard out loud was a hundred and three and a quarter, and fourteen has gone on since, and here are the fourteen, and it is the only list that matters and it adds.

"Ilsa Norr, two — a school in a worse room and a word struck off the page. Mikka Hale, one — three readings, a pot, and two jars a day entered separately and never added to the pot. Jo Verrick, two — a joint, a wall, and a frame that came out a finger and a bit in eleven days. Renn Vale, one — a rating suspended, a door on four pins, and a rule about a word. Dalla Sen, two — a record of three readings, a counter-draft, and a charter. Bel Ordry, two — a page in four parts on a table, and a clause four. Isel Ferrow, one — a door off and a shoring. Nera Oduya, one — a line in the third column and a hand-over that is not a hand-over. Iven Tallo, one — a draft, read, and a refusal. Tams Quill, one — a board, walked twice.

"**Fourteen, and it adds, and I have done it twice, and both columns reconcile exactly and have reconciled for four weeks.**"

---

**Then the thing the room had been not doing for nineteen weeks was done,** which was looking at the two refusals that sat next to each other in the book, a count's refusal of arithmetic and a keeper's condition with no date on it, and the keeper came off the wheel and put both hands on the rail.

"**Not yet,**" he said. "That is what is in this book in my hand, and I am going to say what I am doing with it, and the room is going to hear me refuse to use it, and I want that entered before I start rather than after, because a keeper who decides in a room is a keeper who decided in a room.

"**The condition says the first day of the month the pot stops falling.**

"**The pot stopped falling on the third day of the eighteenth week.** It has been ten days for twelve days and it has not moved once, and I have not been able to stop looking at the board, and the count is right that a number that holds for twelve days is not a number that means anything — and I want him to hear me say that, because he has been telling me for a fortnight that my own notice made my own condition harder, and he was wrong, and it was a good argument, and **it is not the argument.**

"So: the condition has been met. In the strictest reading it was met on the third day, in a clerk's hand, in a kitchen, and nobody in this building moved, and I have not moved it, and I am not going to move it now, and I am going to say why, and this is the whole of my reason and it is the only one I have got.

"**A condition you can satisfy is not a condition. It is a date with a number on it.** And I set it because I wanted something in the world that this boundary could achieve, and I got one, and the thing I got is that a pot holds when a jar of water comes out of a wall, and I did not know that when I set it, and if I had known it I would not have set it, and **the condition is satisfied by luck and a condition satisfied by luck is not the same object as the thing I was trying to write down in a book.**

"So it stays where it is and nobody moves it and nobody is released on it."

"**Then who releases them,**" said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up, and she asked it in front of the room and entered that she had asked it, and that she had asked it because nineteen weeks of a book have been building to this and she was not going to let it be ended by a number.

Sefu Aran said:

"**I do, and not the condition, and out loud, in this room, with the reason, and the reason is not the pot and it is not the charter and it is not the water off the wall.**

"The obligation ends when the boundary can do it without you **and a person named by the boundary has said so out loud, in the room, with the reason.** Those are the words and they were the words in the twelfth week and the count wrote them on the board in his own hand and I have not liked them for four months.

"So, in the room, with the reason.

"**One: the boundary can do it. That is a fact and not a boast and I am going to give you the proof, which is that the boundary has done the last three days without a single person in this room who came up a shaft. A carpenter found a joint in a wall. A cook tested water. A schoolteacher told me a word was wrong. A clerk argued with a woman from a counter and won. A woman on a landing made a rule about a word. An air engineer who is leaving handed us a line in a column. I made a charter and four people signed it and one man refused to and was not argued with. Not one of those is a thing the crew did.

"**Two: I am the person the boundary names, and I am naming myself, in a room, out loud, and I want the clerk to enter that I named myself and that nobody proposed me and that I have been waiting four months for somebody to notice that a keeper who waits to be asked is a keeper who has not been named.**

"And the reason is not a number and it is not the pot. The reason is that **the boundary's terms are four at a time, and two crossings, and a bell that cannot be left, and a valve nobody has answered yet, and a stair that is not in any work, and eleven people. The five people I am releasing are the only five people in this Hearthhold who can do the work the boundary has just been given, and I am not releasing them because they are tired. I am releasing them because a boundary that cannot be left alone is a boundary that is still a rescue, and the whole of what we have argued for in nineteen weeks is that we are not that.**

"So the crew's maintenance obligation is **discharged**, in my hand, in this room, with the reason, on the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and I am not going to be thanked, and I am not going to be praised, and the count is going to say something about a figure in a minute and **I am asking him not to.**"

---

Tams Quill put the chalk down, which he has done four times in nineteen weeks, and he did not say the figure, and the clerk entered that he was asked not to and that he complied, and entered the reason in his own words.

"**He has asked me not to end an obligation on a number and I am not going to.**

"And I have refused for four months to end it on a figure, and I am not withdrawing that refusal, and nobody in this room should hear me say a figure and think the refusal has gone.

"**Mine is a refusal of arithmetic. His is a refusal of a condition. Neither of them has moved. The obligation ended this morning in a room, on a sentence, said by a man who named himself, with a reason, and the reason had nothing in it that could be measured.** And I would like it entered that the count's refusal and the keeper's refusal are both still standing in this book and the thing they were both about is over, **and that is the only way a refusal and an ending can sit on the same page without one of them being a lie.**"

**Nobody left, and nobody was asked to,** and Dalla Sen entered the fact that no person had said *stay* and no person had said *go*, and that it was the first time in nineteen weeks that five people in this Hearthhold were in it as something other than a debt, and that the count had read out the whole of it and the clerk had not been asked to.

---

**The sentence the middle gate's office had asked this boundary to say out loud, in a room, to its own people, before the first of the month,** had been written in a clerk's hand in a book since the fifth day of the seventeenth week, and Sefu Aran had said the answer was him.

He said it in the anteroom, at the fifth bell, with the room at its number and six people down the nine steps listening, and it was not a list, and it was read afterwards in the clerk's hand, and it is the last thing anybody said out loud in that room for a long time.

"**I will tell the four houses. It is me and it will be me and it is not a courier and it is not a page, and the answer I am giving them is the true one and it is not good, and I am going to give it in the same words I have given this room, because a thing softened on a road is worse than a thing refused.

"*Nothing comes. This boundary is not sending anybody and has not sent anybody and will not. There is a water-lock at the second gate with a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood and two hands a day for four days, and that timber is the office's and the office has no carpenter with a bond, and this boundary released it in writing and the release stands and the office is not to blame. The four households have their own water and their own four sets of steps and they have had them since the cold closed, and they did not ask this boundary for any of this.

"*And this boundary has found two jars a day out of its own wall and is using them and is not sending them, because it has two jars a day and a man of twenty-two and no second pair of hands, and if it gave them away it would have none, and the true sentence is that it looked after itself and four people carried water up four sets of steps, and those two things happened at the same time and one of them was this boundary's choice.*

"*I will say it in a room at the counter, to the four of them, in person, and not through a woman who has bad legs, and I will say it at the first of the month and not one day before, because a thing said early is a thing that gets repeated wrong on a road.*"

---

At the sixth bell that same evening the air engineer said the thing she had been carrying, and it is the last thing she says in this building for a long time, and it is the question this building has gone nineteen weeks without asking, said out loud in a room and not answered.

"**Two things and I am going to say them quickly and I am not going to explain either.**

"**One. A halfpenny sheet, a clay packing that shrinks, a word that has to be said in a room, and a frame that goes a finger a week: that is how a door becomes a toll gate.** Not a gate with a man at it. A gate with a **rule** at it that a man forty days away can be handed, and the person behind the door can pay in food and hours, and the person in front of the door can be sent a form. It does not need anybody to be wicked. It needs a page and a dry month and a person who did not come and answer for it.

"And this boundary has spent nineteen weeks finding out that the second half of that is a matter of a **clause four** and a plan in four parts, and I do not know whether we have won it. I think we have moved it. I have been in this building nineteen weeks and I have watched a man keep a page in his coat so that a stranger could not carry it out of a room, and **that is not a system. That is a man.**

"**And two.** This building's oldest book has nine rooms on it and not one name anywhere and no air on it at all, and I have told this room that a blank row in a new document is an omission and an omission reads as an accusation. I have been here nineteen weeks and I have looked at that dowel a hundred times.

"**And I have a theory about why it has kitchens in it and classrooms in it and beds in it and no treasuries, and I have had it since the fourth day of the eleventh week, and it is worse than not knowing, and I am not going to say it in a cold month to a room that writes things down.**

"You will get it when I am not standing at a valve, and not in a book you can carry up a road, and not in an anteroom with nine people in it. **So ask me again in a room when this boundary is keeping itself, and I will tell you, and I am telling you now that I will not be hurried into it by a plan.**"

No one asked her in a room that day. Dalla Sen entered the request, the refusal, and the condition, and the condition is *when this boundary is keeping itself*, and it is the fourth thing in this book that is waiting on that.

---

**Then the clerk read the six things at the sixth bell,** and the six were the same six with two more on them, and the two more were the pot and the charter, and nobody in the anteroom filled the space after the last one, which is what the six are for.

**And Bel Ordry did not take the plan away,** and this is the last of it and it is small.

"There is no name on it," he said, "and there is a date in the top corner, and the fourth column is empty and it has a reason on it in two hands, and there are nine rows and eight of the third column are filled and one is empty and I am not going to say which, and **it is a page and it is in my coat and it is going to be in my coat in the spring, and I asked to be suspicious of in a month, and it is nineteen days since I asked, and nobody has been suspicious of me, and I have noticed that too.**

"And the charter is on the scrub board and it is not a page. It has got a number on the top of it and no name anywhere, and the man with the counter is going up four days of road with a recommendation he does not expect to be granted, and a hundred halfpenny sheets are being sold at a counter four days from here, and three Hearthholds have timber in the month after next, and this one has a frame out a finger and a bit and a rule about a word and two jars a day out of a wall.

"**So the road is open and nobody may be charged for it, and that is the whole of what nineteen weeks has bought, and I would like somebody to look at it and tell me it is worth the eighth line of a column in a woman's hand.**"

Nobody told him, and Marek Venn, who was on the bench with his left hand in a cloth and who is the man in this room that leaves in the spring, sat and said nothing for a while, and then stood up, and then said the thing out loud that he had worked out on a corridor two foot eight wide over eleven feet of shaft and had not put in a book.

"**The road is not worth it,**" he said. "**That is the answer and you should have it from the man who wrote the plan's reason, and here it is. A road is a road, in a bad month as well as a good one, and nobody can point at the thing that built it. The road is not worth a line. The eighth line is a different object and I will not have the two run together in a room, because that is how a good answer gets used to pay for a bad one. The eighth line is a woman writing down what she thinks will go wrong in a room she is leaving, for a building that is not hers, in a hand that cannot be rubbed out. That is worth having. And a page in a coat is worth having exactly as much as the man in the coat, and he is thirty-five and he has a hinge of his mother's in a bag for seven weeks and he asked to be suspicious of in a month and nobody did it, and I have known him nineteen weeks and I would not bet the plan on him.**

"**So the road is open, and that is a fact and not a system, and the whole difference is worth saying out loud on the day it is got, because in a year the only thing anybody will remember is that the road was open and there will be a man who put the paper there.**"

And Bel Ordry looked at him, and did not ask him to take it back, which he had every right to do and had said he would not, and said: "**That is the first thing anybody has said to me in nineteen days that was not a question, and I am going to keep it, and it is going on the page in your words and not in mine.**"

Nobody told him. The school had gone through at the first bell and the corridor was open and the pot was ten days and had not gone up, and Wen Pask was thirty-five days and thirty-five nights into a count in a column with no heading on it, and the ninth line had no row and was not going to get one, and the four treads were dry and behind a rail and had not been on, and the brass survey rule was in the pocket of the coat on the hook and had said nothing for ten weeks, and the wrapped plate was on the shelf above the bench in its knotted bundle of work cloth with nothing joined to it, and the pot was ten days and had never once gone up, and a man with a suspended bond had refused to sign a charter in four words, and a courier was carrying two sentences up a road with no name at the top of them, and Talla Roke was not owed a spring by anybody and had not asked for one, and the anteroom's shored frame was holding and was not fixed, and the four hundred paces of nothing and the six pails a day and the seventh term and the ninth line and the lip's range were read out at the sixth bell, and a person was still under that lip.
