# Chapter 228 — What A Body Carried By Pressure Is

The fourth day of the forty-third week is a Thursday, the corridor is shut, and the school did not run and lost nothing, and at about the first bell four people in this anteroom said four different sentences about a heavy thing in a flooded channel, and by the fourth bell this room had decided that it was not going to break it, and the cost of that decision is standing in this room now and is not going to go away this week.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the forty-eighth reading, and the same as forty-seven, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, in the same words for the eleventh time, and the clerk entered it and entered that the woman who reads walls had been under a channel with a lamp in it the day before and had read a frame in a corridor at about a quarter past the first bell, and that neither of those two facts has been put beside the other.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is thirty-three.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it is right, and the cook was in the room and did not leave it, which is the third morning running and the clerk entered that a run of three is not yet a practice and that a clerk who has written that sentence twice in three mornings is beginning to use it as a place to put things. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The sixth was the pump, and it ran, and there is a channel under this floor and a body in the channel and neither of those is in the sixth term and the sixth term has not been changed in forty-two weeks.

Then a foreman with nine years of his own stood up at the anvil and said that he was going to take iron to it, and he said the four sentences that make that a plan and not a feeling, and nobody in the room interrupted him.

"**I have been in a room with nothing to say in it before,**" said Emrys Callow, "**and I have done this in it. There is a heavy thing lying in a stalled cut in a passage off the second gate and it is being carried by the water behind it, and every man who has ever shored a thing in a wet cut has got a bar out before he has got a sentence out, because a heavy thing in water is not a thing to think about, it is a thing to hold, and holding is what the trade is.**"

He put one hand on the iron.

"**And I have got four sentences and they are the four I would have to say to a crew and they are the reason I have not said I would do it. One: the cut is nine hundred years old and the stone round it is dressed and I have no idea what shape the far wall is. Two: the water behind that thing has a length I cannot name and neither can anybody in this room, and a length of water behind a thing is a length of force, and I do not know the force and I would be putting iron against a force I have not measured. Three: there are nine hundred years of scale in that channel and I would be guessing at the section, and a wedge in a channel you cannot see the section of is a wedge in a hole.**"

"**And four,**", and she did not look up.

"**And four, and this is the one I would like the room to have,**" Emrys Callow said, "**I said yesterday that the water is holding it up. A thing the water is holding up is a thing that goes where the water goes. If I put a bar under it in the right place I do not stop it, I only decide where it goes next, and I have not seen the next place, and neither has she, and neither has the woman who read the fall off a wall with a lamp in her hand and gave away a rate for nine hundred years because she could see it.**"

Nobody in that anteroom moved, and the pump made a stroke and then made another.

"**Then say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I have just told a room four reasons why I am not doing the thing I am the only man in this room who has any business doing,**" Emrys Callow said, "**and that a foreman who has four reasons is not being careful, a foreman who has four reasons is negotiating, and that this room has spent forty-two weeks refusing to turn a refusal into a plan and I have just tried to do it in a sentence with four numbered parts and I would like the clerk to enter that I noticed about halfway through.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that the four reasons stand and are not improved, and entered that the fifth thing he said is a thing about himself and not about the iron,**", and her voice was not different at all.

"**Then I am going to say the thing that says the same four reasons in a different trade,**" said Nera Oduya, from the second of her three steps, and she had a piece of chalk in her hand and had not marked anything with it for two days. "**A foreman has told this room he does not know the force. I am going to tell this room I know what carries it, and I know it because I can feel the shape of it and I have been feeling the shape of it since I was in a room with four fathoms of water in it in the ninth day of the forty-first week.**"

"**Say it,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**Water pressure,**" Nera Oduya said. "**That is the whole of the mechanism and it is the whole of the answer and neither of those is a discovery. A body in a flooded cut that will not go away is a body being held off the bed by a difference in the water, and the difference is the water coming down from somewhere behind it, and the water behind it is nine hundred years of a cistern that has been emptying into a gallery at a rate a person chose on purpose. It is not pushing because it is heavy. It is pushing because it has somewhere to go and the only somewhere to go is through a channel that is not taking it.**"

"**Then say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that this mechanism has a consequence and the consequence is the reason I have not come down off this step,**" Nera Oduya said. "**If the water behind that thing is what is holding it, then breaking it does not let the water through. It puts the pieces of it into the same channel with the same water behind them, and the pieces are nine hundred years of whatever that thing is made of, and the channel is not clear and nobody in this building knows how clear, and a channel with a heap in it takes the water the same amount and no more. You do not get a drain. You get the same drain with a different obstruction in it, and the obstruction is one this room can pick up with a bar, and that is the part I cannot put down this morning.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything for about as long as a pump takes to make four strokes.

"**Then say what would,**" said Emrys Callow, and he had not sat down and he was not going to.

"**Nothing in this room,**" Nera Oduya said. "**And I am going to say the rest of it and it is the sentence I have been carrying since Tuesday and it is a sentence about a rule and not about a body. This building found a rule nine days ago. A rule is a rate a person chose nine hundred years ago for the water to go down. The water is not going down at the rate anybody chose. So the rule is not broken. Nobody broke it. A channel that has been taking water at a chosen rate for nine hundred years has had something happen to it, and the thing that has happened to it is sitting in it, and the sitting is what has stopped it, and the stopping is not a fault, it is what a nine-hundred-year-old instruction does when a body gets into the middle of it.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I have said that twice this morning and both times it has sounded like a way of not doing anything,**" Nera Oduya said. "**And it is, partly. It is a way of not doing the easy thing. And I am not going to pretend that a mechanism I worked out at three removes with a lamp held by another person is a reason to do nothing, and I am going to say the real half against and it is this: the right answer to a thing that is holding itself up in a channel is to find out what the channel is supposed to do, and give the room something else to do, and move the force, and that is three works and this building has none of the three and I am not going to sit here and be the woman who found all three of them out in one morning.**"

"**Then say the cost of the room deciding not to,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The cost is that the drain does not happen,**" Nera Oduya said, "**on any day this building controls, and I want that said in the plainest words I have got, because the two men in this room who came four days for a turn-over are owed an answer and this is it. The water in that chamber is not going anywhere this boundary can reach. It will go down when the rain goes down, or it will go through a nine-hundred-year-old channel when the channel decides to, and a channel does not decide, and the only thing in this world that makes a channel take water is a rule and there is no second rule and nobody here is going to cut one, because cutting a channel is not something a boundary does in somebody else's water.**"

Then the physician came the two steps into the room, which he has done twice in five volumes, and he was not carrying his bag, and it was on the floor where he had left it, and nobody in the room noticed him leave it there and the clerk entered that nobody noticed.

"**Then I am going to stop the work,**" said Mero Senn, "**and I am going to say that I am not doing it with a veto and I would like that entered, because a veto is a thing a room can wait out and this cannot be waited out.**"

"**Then say how,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**With my own sentence,**" Mero Senn said. "**A plan with a named injured person in it is not a plan. I have said that in this room three times in a fortnight. There is no injured person. I have looked for one since Monday morning and I have not found one and I am not going to name a man in a room on the strength of what a foreman is about to do. So I am not using that sentence. I am using a different one and I am going to say where I got it and I got it at a counter in the fourth week of this volume from a man who had been in a collapse and who said to me that the men who died in that room were not the men who were under it. They were the men who had gone in after them to get a bar out.**"

Nothing came out of anybody in that room.

"**That is not a rule and it is not in any instrument and it is not in a charter and it is not in the fourth column of a page in a coat,**" Mero Senn said. "**It is a sentence a man said to me once. I am using it because the four reasons a foreman gave are about a force and a section and a length of water, and all four of those are things a man can be wrong about and can get somebody killed being wrong about, and I am the man in this room who is answerable for that, and I am not going to answer for it on the strength of a rule nobody has read.**"

"**Entered, in the physician's words, in the space where a reason goes, and entered that the sentence is not a rule and that he has said so himself, and entered that a man at a counter said it once and that this room has never heard it before and that the physician is not going to pretend to have invented it,**", with her hand near the book.

"**So the room is going to decide,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am going to say the shape of the decision out loud before anybody votes in it, because this building has spent forty-two weeks refusing to put two questions next to each other and I am not going to do that this afternoon. The shape is: a heavy thing in a stalled channel four days away is not going to be broken by this building this week. That is the decision. It is not a rule and it has no number and no day on it and it is not on the board and it is not in a column and it is not on anybody's slate. And the cost of it is in the room and the cost is that a thing is lying in nine hundred years of water and four people in this building are going to spend the rest of their working lives on a problem they have just decided not to touch.**"

"**Then say who is watching it,**" said Renn Vale, from the middle of the floor.

No one in that room took it up. for about as long as a pump takes to make six strokes.

"**Say it again,**" Dalla Sen said, and her voice was not different at all.

"**Somebody has to watch it,**" Renn Vale said. "**Not to hit it. To watch it. Because it is in a channel that runs under this pump and this pump has been running nineteen weeks against the low point of a thing that comes out of a cistern, and a woman read that fall off a wall nine days ago and the clerk has the thing on facing pages and not joined, and if that body shifts two hands one way or two hands the other way then the low point of that fall is somewhere it was not, and the next time a woman with a thumb looks under that chalk she is going to find a different number, and there is nobody in this building who will be able to say whether that is because the channel changed or because something in it moved.**"

"**Then say what watching it is,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**A person going down a wet stair at a first bell on a day the corridor is open and putting a lamp on it and coming back up,**" Renn Vale said, "**and that is a watch, and a watch is a rota, and a rota is a column, and a column has a heading over it, and I told this room on the second day of the forty-second week that a term which names a person is a term with a person standing at the end of it.**"

The anteroom was quiet for about as long as a pump takes to make three strokes.

"**So we have just built the thing,**" Bel Ordry said, "**and we have built it out of a decision not to do something, and I would like the clerk to enter that it is the first time in five volumes that this room has made an instrument out of refusing a thing, and that the instrument is a watch, and that nobody in this room has been named in it, and that I do not know how you watch a thing without naming somebody and I am not going to find out today.**"

"**Then I am going to say the half against the not-deciding,**" said Emrys Callow, and he had not sat down. "**I have been angry in this room once this week and I kept it out, and I am going to be angry now out loud, because I have had four reasons to be angry and I gave three of them away to the room in a numbered list and this is the fourth one. That body is in a channel that runs under the pump this boundary is proud of, and it is a thing that cannot be shored, and it cannot be moved, and it cannot be broken without a channel full of pieces, and it cannot be watched by anybody this room is allowed to name, and it is a thing that I have never in nine years met and that I cannot write a report about, and I am going back to a line of pumps four days up a middle passage in about a fortnight with nothing to put in a report but the word *waited*, and I said on the sixth day of the forty-first week that a crew that writes *waited* is a crew that gets sent somewhere else next time, and I have only just noticed that I have said that sentence in this room before, out loud, in another man's mouth, and that two men in two trades in the same fortnight have come to the same sentence about a different word for the same thing, and I would like the clerk to enter that I noticed it after I had said it and not before.**"

"**Then say the thing nobody wants to hear,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**I will hand you the word in a fortnight if you want it,**" Emrys Callow said, "**and that is not a threat and it is not leverage and it is the first thing I have said in five volumes that could be either, and I would like the clerk to enter that I know which it is and that I have said it anyway.**"

"**Entered, in the foreman's words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that a man has offered this room a word on a day when a room has decided not to do a thing and the reason he has offered it is that the room's not-doing has a price and the price has a face on it,**" said Dalla Sen, and she had her hand near the book and she did not use it, and the clerk entered that she had had her hand near the book and had not used it, and entered the reason, which is that a name against a watch is a heading over a column and she is not going to be the person who puts it there in the same month she wrote a name against a loss.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a room which has just decided not to break a thing in a channel four days away has just made a decision about a thing it cannot reach, and a board is a list of what this building found, and what this building found this week is a name that is not on it and a watch that is not on it, and a clerk is not walking a column on a day when the reason she is not walking it is the same shape as the reason for the decision.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning for the sixteenth morning running, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it and nobody explained it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for thirty-four weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a heavy thing is lying in a channel four days away in nine hundred years of water and this building has decided it is not going to break it, and has not decided who is going to look at it, and the clerk has entered both of those and has joined them to nothing.

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither was read, and the reason went in beside them, and the reason was that a room which has just spent a morning agreeing not to do a thing is not going to be read a total over, and that a clerk who has decided this afternoon not to put a name against a watch is not going to be the second voice in this room about anything.

Four today and four hundred and nine since the keeper signed. The boundary is at five hundred and twenty-six and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that on its own line, in the ordinary hand, and was entered and not read, and the reason was given, and it was that a room has said the word *watch* out loud for the first time in five volumes and the clerk is not going to read a number over that, and that is a reason about the clerk and not about the count. Wen Pask is one hundred and seventy-three days and one hundred and seventy-three nights, sixty-two weeks of a clerk saying out loud that she is going to keep writing it down. It is not going to be one hundred and seventy-four.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and it is the reason the foreman gave at the fourth bell in his own words, that a crew of his is not the work here and that the work on the far side of that door is on somebody else's paper, and that nine people have been in a room over a piece of paper, and that he is not going to be the tenth thing that happens to a man on somebody else's paper, and the clerk entered that she wrote that and that the last eleven words of it are the reason a carpenter gave in a room on the fourth day of the forty-first week, and that the two of them are in this book on facing pages and are not joined.

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell and had not said one word for about four hours, and what came up in him over that time was not a thing anybody else in the room had been carrying and it was the oldest thing he has.

He is the man who answers a cost with a thing he can make. Five volumes of it. A cost arrives in a room and he builds something. It is his whole trade and it is a good trade and it has never once been cowardice. In the ninth week of this volume he worked out how to take the pressure off a nine-hundred-year-old door with a frame and did not say it, and that was the first time in his life he had heard the two things at once — his instinct and his judgement — and had let judgement win, and it had felt like a wall.

Today he sat at the end of a trestle for four hours while three people in a room gave the correct answer three different ways, and the correct answer was: do not hit it, and do not stand in the passage, and do not name anybody, and do not build anything, and there is no frame in the world that does any of the four. And his whole body had spent those four hours waiting for the moment when somebody asked him what to do, so that he could say it, so that a thing could be made.

Nobody asked him.

And when the decision was made, and it was made against the thing he could have built, he felt — and he let himself feel it, which he had not expected to be able to do — a small clean disappointment. Not at the decision. At himself. A man who has spent five volumes refusing to be the person who decides, and who has a stage, and who has a shut book he has never once been asked for, and who has just heard a room agree not to do the one thing his hands are for, and whose first feeling about that agreement is that nobody needs him.

He put his hand flat on the book, and he did not take it off, and he thought about the second kind of man — the one who hears a cost and lets it stand — and he understood that the second kind of man is not the opposite of him. The second kind of man is the same man four hours later. And the difference is not courage and it is not humility and it is not a rule. It is that the second kind of man has worked out that being needed is a thing that happens to you and wanting it is a thing you do, and the two of those are different instruments, and the second kind of man keeps them apart.

He had been keeping them apart for eleven days. He had not known that was what he was doing.

At the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a physician's bag on the floor by the frame of the far door where nobody had noticed him leave it, and a folding rule on a trestle with mud on it, and a foreman standing at an anvil who had not sat down all morning, and a keeper on the middle of the floor who had built a watch out of a decision and had not been able to name a person in it, and a clerk with a name in her book against a loss and a hand that had gone near the book twice today and had not gone in twice, and a heavy thing in a channel four days away that is not going to be broken, and nine men at the far end of a cook's room who are owed a turn-over that is not going to happen, and a man at the end of a trestle who has worked out that he has not been needed since about the fourth bell and has not gone to sleep, and a word in the room that nobody has put a name against.
