# Chapter 229 — A Room People Work In On Purpose

The fifth day of the forty-third week is a Friday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the fifty-fourth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door, and at about the third bell a man who is owed five things on a slate and is on no board and in no column said out loud in a room what a drained gallery is for, and it was the first time in five volumes that anybody had said anything about what this building is for in this room, and the room did not take it and did not refuse it, and both of those were entered.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the forty-ninth reading, and the same as forty-eight, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, in the same words for the twelfth time, and the clerk entered it and entered that the same sentence is now twelve consecutive mornings and that a clerk who has entered a sentence twelve times running is either watching a practice being made or watching a groove being cut, and that she does not know which, and that she is going to let it run and not decide.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is thirty-four.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it is right, and the cook was in the room and did not leave it, which is the fourth morning running and the clerk entered that a run of four is a practice and that the practice started on a Tuesday and nobody in the room decided it, and that a clerk who has entered a run as a run has entered the first thing in this building that is neither a rule nor a refusal nor a debt, and that she did not enjoy entering it. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The sixth was the pump, and it ran, and there is a stalled channel under it and a decision about a body in it and the decision is not in the sixth term and the sixth term has not been changed in forty-two weeks.

The pump was started, two people turning it, and the anvil end of the room was empty for the first time in a week and half, because a man who has been at that anvil every morning of this volume was not at it, and the clerk entered that she had not asked where he was and that four other people in the room had not asked either, and entered that it is the first time in five volumes that a person in this room has gone to a post of his own accord and not been entered, and that the room has got a habit now of not asking, and that a habit is not a rule and she is not going to make it one.

Then Seth Wray came up the nine steps at about the third bell and stood at the end of the trestle, and he had the slate under his arm and a piece of charcoal in his other hand, and he did not put the slate down, which is the thing he has done every time he has spoken in this room.

"**I have got four lines on a slate that a woman at a counter wrote for me about me,**" he said, "**and none of the four has been answered, and I am not going to stand here and ask about them, and I am not going to stand here and not ask about them either. I have been in this building a fortnight and I have been answered once, and the once was a clerk putting my name in a book against a thing I did not decide, and I have not stopped thinking about it, and I would like that entered because a man who says he is not going to ask for a thing and then does not say it for a fortnight is a man who is not being fair to anybody in the room.**"

"**Say the thing you came up for,**", from the top of the nine steps.

"**I have been at four counters in nineteen years,**" said Seth Wray, "**and the word that goes on line four of a salvage form is *salvage*, and line four of a form of any kind is where the value goes, and I have spent my life putting a word on a line and being correct about it. And I have known since the ninth day of the fortieth week that there is nothing in that gallery worth the word. Water, timber, metal, salt-crust, and anything a person can take out and carry. There is no timber. There is no dry gallery. The salt-crust in there is spore salt in a room that has not been dry for four hundred years and a spore salt only grows where it is dry, and I did not know that on the ninth day of the fortieth week and I do know it now, and I have carried a form up four days and a road and back down four days with a word on it that is not true.**"

He turned the slate over and did not put it down.

"**That is not what I came up for. What I came up for is a sentence, and I have been carrying it since Wednesday of the forty-first week, which was a week before anybody in this room knew there was a chamber, and I have been carrying it because I heard a woman say it in this room and it went into my head the way a line on a form goes into a head, which is to say completely and without asking where it came from.**"

"**Then say the sentence,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**A chamber is a room with an institution round it, a building is a thing that stands up, and an institution is a thing people do in the same place on purpose more than once,**" said Seth Wray, "**and that is her sentence and not mine and I am going to hand it back to her before I use it, and she is on that step and she can say whether I have got it right.**"

Nera Oduya looked at him from the second of her three steps for about as long as it takes to say a sentence.

"**You have got it right,**" she said, "**and I am not going to improve on it, and I said it in this room on the third day of the forty-first week and I said it about a room with a course of stone round it at the height of my knee, and I want it entered that a salvage foreman has carried it for a fortnight and brought it back to a room it was said in, and that I did not know he had it and that I would not have asked.**"

"**Entered, and entered that she was not asked,**", and she did not look up.

"**Then here is mine, and it is mine, and it is about a thing worth nothing,**" said Seth Wray, "**and I have thought about saying it for two days and I have said nothing for two days, and a man who has said nothing for two days and then says a thing in a room has usually been waiting for somebody to say something first, and nobody in this room said anything, so I have not got a small reason and I have got a large one.**"

"**Say it,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**A gallery that is drained is a room people can work in.**" He put the charcoal down on the trestle and left his hand on it. "**That is all. And a workshop is a thing people work in on purpose more than once. And this boundary has spent six weeks refusing to claim a room it is standing on, and there is a claim on a trestle with a word on line four that is not true, and the office that wrote that claim is going to hold it until somebody tells them there is nothing in it, and they are not going to hear it from a boundary, because a boundary that answers a claim is a boundary that has admitted the claim was worth answering.**"

"**Then say the last part of it,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**The last part of it is that the room this building has been arguing about for six weeks is the only room anybody has found this year that could be worked in,**" Seth Wray said, "**and that I have spent twenty years filling in line four, and that I have known what a thing worth nothing looks like since the ninth day of the fortieth week, and that a person in this room read four walls and a floor and said a chamber built to be filled is not a cave, and she was right, and it has not been built to be filled. It has been built to be worked in, and there is nobody in this building who has said that sentence and there is nobody in this building who has asked for it.**"

Nobody in that anteroom moved, and the pump made a stroke and then made another.

"**And I know what I have just done,**" Seth Wray said, "**because I have watched a room for a fortnight. I have just said out loud, in a room, a sentence about what this building is for, and I have said it as a man with no post in it, and I have said it in a room that has not asked me for one, and I have not asked for one either. I am owed five things on this slate and a report I can write and a statement in this boundary's own hand, and I have not asked for any of them this morning, and I have spent the room's morning on a sentence instead, and I would like the clerk to enter that I did that and that it was mine to spend and that it was not a favour to anybody in this room including me.**"

"**Entered, in a foreman's words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that a man with no post in this building has said a sentence about what this building is for, and that it is the first such sentence in five volumes, and that it is not on the board and is not in a column and is not on anybody's slate, and that I am not going to be the second voice in this room about it,**" said Dalla Sen, and she wrote for about as long as it takes to say a sentence and then stopped and looked at what she had written and did not read it back.

"**Then say the half against it,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that a room that cannot refuse an offer is not a room,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and this room has refused everything for forty-two weeks and has never once had anything on offer, and a room that has spent that long refusing has a thing it is good at, and the thing it is good at is saying no, and a room that is good at saying no is a room that has a shape, and the shape is *no*, and a shape with nothing in it is a corner with a day in it, and I said the word *corner* in this room once in this volume in another man's mouth and I have been thinking about it every day since.**"

"**Then say what you are going to do with his sentence,**" Renn Vale said.

"**Nothing,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am going to say that out loud rather than leave it in the room, because a man with a page in a coat who lets a sentence sit in a room without putting it anywhere is a man who is hoarding it, and hoarding is what this room does and I am not going to be the only one doing it.**"

"**Then say the other half against, from the other side,**" Dalla Sen said, "**because a room that has refused everything for forty-two weeks has a good reason and I am the one who has written most of them down.**"

"**Entered, and I am not going to keep it in my own hand for once,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am going to say it once and I am going to say it badly. A boundary that refuses everything is a boundary that has decided that being wrong in a room is worse than being quiet in one, and that is not a noble thing and it is not a cowardly thing either. It is a thing this building learned on the sixth day of the thirty-ninth week when a sentence was said in a room and became a drawing, and it has been right about that for forty-two weeks and it has used the rightness to stop asking anybody for anything, and there is a man at the end of a trestle who has been at the end of a trestle for nine weeks for that reason and a carpenter at an anvil who has been at that anvil for five volumes and nobody thanked him for either, and a woman who reads walls who was right about a wall and nobody sent her, and an air engineer with two conditions that have not been asked about, and a cook who has refused this book four times.**"

No one in that room took it up., and the clerk entered it in her own words without improving it, and entered that she had not been asked, and entered that she has been the reason a room did not stop a woman on the fifth day of the forty-first week, and that a person in this room has entered two reasons in the space where reasons go in a fortnight, and that she did not join them.

"**Then I am going to say one thing and I am going to say it about myself and it is the last thing in my mouth this morning,**" said Bel Ordry, and he did not read the page out of his coat, "**and I have thought about it for an hour and I would like it entered that I thought about it for an hour. Foreman, I have been wrong in this building four times and I knew about three of them, and on the seventh day of the forty-second week I entered a fifth and said I did not know about it yet. I have known about it since Tuesday. It is this. Every time this room has refused a thing, it has been me, and I have been the half against, and I have been right in the room four times, and I have never once been the man who said *do it*. And I have just spent an hour finding out whether that is humility or a shape, and I have found out that it is a shape, and that a man who has been the half against for forty-two weeks has a standing, and that a standing is a post with no name on it, and this building has refused six posts on the record and has had one of them the entire time and it is me.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything, and the clerk entered it in the words of the man who said it, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that a page in a coat has been in this room for forty-two weeks and has never been on the board, and entered that the board is a list of what this building found, and that a man is not a thing a building finds.

"**Then let us be plain,**" said Emrys Callow, coming back through the kitchen-line doorway with an apron on and both hands black, "**because I have been standing in that kitchen for an hour and I have been listening, and I want it in the room that I have listened, and that a foreman who listens through a wall is a foreman who is not working, and I have not been working and there is nothing in this building for me to work on this morning.**"

"**Then say the thing you came back to say,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**I have been at the anvil forty-two weeks without being given one hour,**" Emrys Callow said, "**and a man has just said a sentence in this room about what this building is for, and a clerk has said the reason it has not been said is her, and a man with a page in a coat has said the reason he has never said it is him, and I am the third one, and I have been the third one for three volumes, and I am going to say the third one's sentence and it is not a good sentence and I am saying it anyway.**"

He wiped his hands on the apron and did not take it off.

"**If you build a workshop, then somebody has to keep it, and keeping it is work, and work in this building has gone on a line with nothing over the top of it for five volumes, and the first man in this room who says *give me the workshop to keep* has asked for a post, and this boundary has said no to six posts and is going to say no to a seventh, and I am going to be the one who says no, because I have said no four times in this room since the fourth day of the forty-first week and the fifth time is going to be about something that matters.**"

"**Then say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I have just said *give me the workshop to keep* without saying it, in a room, out loud, in the past tense, and that I have said it as a thing I have already decided, and that a foreman who decides in advance is a foreman who has already had the argument with himself and won, and that winning an argument with yourself is the one kind of winning that does not count in this building.**"

"**Entered, in the foreman's words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that a carpenter has said a seventh post out loud in a room and that the seventh post is the first one anybody in this building has ever refused before it was asked for,**", and her voice was not different at all.

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle for the whole of the morning and had not said one word, and what came up in him, in about the fourth hour, was not a thing anybody else in the room had been carrying.

A man with no post had said the future out loud. Not a plan — a sentence about what a room is for. And the room had heard it, and had not taken it, and had not refused it, and had spent the rest of the morning explaining to itself why it did not take it, and in the middle of that explanation a woman who keeps a book had put six of its own habits on a table in her own hand and a man with a page in a coat had worked out that his own refusal was a post, and a foreman had said the seventh no out loud before anybody asked him for it.

And the man who had said the future was the man with no post.

Marek Venn had carried a shape in his head since the second day of the fortieth week. One line, three rooms, a rule on the line. He had given it away in the second week of this volume so that two other people could find out, and it had cost him a fortnight of being the man who found out, and in the nine weeks since he had been the man in this room who is good at walls and bad at the other thing, and a clerk had entered a physician's word for what he had in the fourth week of this volume and had entered four times that his reasons were about other people, and a man with a page in a coat had noticed his standing out loud and had said he was not going to write it in a column.

And this morning the man who said what this building is for was a salvage foreman from a different district who is owed five things and has no post and did not ask for one.

He put his hand flat on the shut book. He could have said the sentence. He had had it since the second week of this volume, in a better shape, in a shape with a name in it that a woman at a counter could write on a piece of paper and send four days up a road, and he had given it away on purpose and he would give it away again. But there is a difference between a thing you give away and a thing you say first, and the difference had taken him nine weeks to find, and it is this: a thing you say first is a claim. A claim in this building is a drawing. A drawing is a plan. A plan has a person in it, and the person in it is a man with a shut book and a knee and a hand that does not close, and the whole of this morning had been four people taking apart their own instruments in a room and he was the fifth instrument and he had said nothing at all.

That was right. He was almost certain it was right. And he noticed, sitting at the end of a trestle on a Friday in the fifth week of a forty-third week, that being almost certain was the exact sensation he had had on the fourth day of the fortieth week, and that it had worn a groove, and that he had already found that groove once and had been proud of finding it, and that the second time round it did not feel like finding anything at all.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and did not read the count, and gave the reason for both, and the reason was that a room which has just had four of its own instruments taken apart in it is owed two numbers it can verify and nothing else, and that a clerk who entered a sentence this morning about what this building is for and who entered that she was not going to be the second voice in the room about it is not going to stand up and read a number over any of it.

Five today and four hundred and fourteen since the keeper signed. The boundary is at five hundred and thirty-one and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that on its own line, in the ordinary hand, and was entered and not read, and the reason was given, and it was that a clerk has entered seven refusals in a book in the space where a finding goes this morning and has not read a single one of them back, and that a clerk who has done that is not going to read a count out loud at the sixth bell either, and that is a reason about the clerk and not about the count. Wen Pask is one hundred and seventy-four days and one hundred and seventy-four nights, sixty-three weeks of a clerk saying out loud that she is going to keep writing it down. It is not going to be one hundred and seventy-five.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a man with no post said a sentence about what this building is for this morning, and a board is a list of what this building found, and what this building found this morning is not a thing and is not on it, and a clerk is not walking a column on a morning when a man has handed her a sentence that is not for her book.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman said in a room at the third bell that if this building builds a workshop then the first man who asks to keep it has asked for a post, and he is going to be the one who says no, and that he has said it before anybody asked, and the clerk entered that she wrote that and that a man who says no before he is asked has not been asked and has not refused anything, and that those are different, and that she has written both of those and does not know which of them is the finding.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning for the seventeenth morning running, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it and nobody explained it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for thirty-five weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a man came up nine steps this morning and said out loud in a room that a drained gallery is a room people can work in, and nobody in this building has an answer, and the pot is at three days and the answer would not have fixed that.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle with a piece of charcoal lying on it that a foreman from a different district had put down and had not picked up, and a slate with four unanswered things on it that he had not asked about, and a folding rule with mud on it, and a door at the bottom of a wet stair that was shut, and a stalled channel under this floor with a heavy thing in it that this building has decided not to break and has not decided who will watch, and a man at the end of a trestle who had had a better sentence in his head since the second week of this volume and had let a man with no post say the worse one first, and had not been thanked for that by anybody and had not asked.
