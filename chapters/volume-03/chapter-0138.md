# Chapter 138 — An Objection Stated First

**Open for the corridor on the third day of the twenty-seventh week, a Wednesday, and the school ran for the seventeenth morning, and a revision was written into the second column of the tenth row of a public plan in a man's hand, and the objection to it was written above it in a woman's hand, and the correction was written under it in a third hand, and then she put her hand to the bottom of his.**

**Not one person in that anteroom thanked her and not one asked her why, and the board was walked, and the market day is the sixth day of this week and this week has not got long left in it, and nobody in this building has decided anything about it.**

The nine went in at the fourth bell and one of them went in sitting down and the strip of crate board was holding and the two flags under the ninth hearth stone had not moved and Jo Verrick said the standing term out loud in the doorway at the first bell and Mero Senn read it out of his own hand against hers and Ilsa Norr said yes to the reading, and the fire in the corner was not lit and the light in the room was off nine stones.

And the ninth hearth stone had a re-adopted condition on it, in Renn Vale's hand, on a page that had said something else, and the room was taught in anyway.

---

**At the second bell the revision went into the plan, in his own hand, and it went in as a proposal and not a change, and the difference between those two things is the entire working practice of this boundary and a clerk said so out loud before anybody read it.**

"**Say the rule first,**" said Dalla Sen. "**Any change to a room people use is written by the person who will keep it. A man is not going to write a change into a row about a room he does not keep and I am not going to let him, and the man has not asked me to let him."

"**So what is on the page is a proposal, and a proposal is not a change, and it is not in force until the two hands underneath it are on it, and there are three hands on that page and one of them is not in favour."

"**Read it,**" she said to Marek Venn, "**and read it as it is written and not as you would say it.**"

He read it out from the anteroom step, standing, with the pump running under him, and it was eleven lines and it took him a while and he did not rush the middle of it.

"**I propose that the second column of the tenth row record that the condition written in that column was lifted before the first day of the twenty-fifth week, on a foot, and that the lift was decided by the person who keeps the second column and not by any other person in this building, and that the reason for the lift is in a clerk's book and is not on this page."

"**And I propose that the second column of the tenth row then carry a condition in place of the one that was lifted, and I am not proposing the condition, because I do not keep the room and I do not get to write what replaces it, and I am proposing that the person who does be asked to write one, and that it be one that is checked every morning the room is used."

"**That is all of it. I have not put a date on it and I have not put a number on it, and there is no figure in this proposal at all, and I would like the room to notice that a man who has spent twenty-seven weeks writing figures into columns has put eleven lines on a public page with nothing in them to count.**"

"**Say what is wrong with it,**" said Nera Oduya, from the anteroom step, and she was not three steps up. She was on the step. She had come down for this and nobody had asked her to and Dalla Sen entered that she had come down and entered that she had not been asked.

---

**She wrote the objection before she said a word of it, in her own hand, on the page, above his eleven lines, and she read it out afterwards, and it was the best argument anybody in that room had heard in a month, and it was about his own plan.**

"**I have written it above his lines and I am going to read it out and I am not going to argue with anybody about it and I am not going to be softened out of it by a room that likes him."

"**A page that shows a condition being lifted is a page that says every condition on it can be lifted."

"**That is all of it. The second column of this plan has twelve conditions in it and every one of them has no number and no day on it, and the reason for that is on this page in two hands, and the reason is that a condition you can satisfy on a schedule is a date with a number on it. And that reason is only worth anything if the conditions hold. And a page that carries one lift in it tells every person who reads it, in a year, from a counter, in an office, that this boundary's conditions are requests."

"**And there is a worse half and I am going to say it, and it is worse, and it is about what this page is for. This page is going to be shown to a federation. A woman from that federation stood in this room on Friday and said she has written four pages that assume this boundary wants to be looked at. And a page with a lift in it is a page that says *we will show you what we decide, and sometimes what we decide is to open a room that has a condition on it, and the condition was a child's foot*."

"**And that is true. And it is going to be the only sentence anybody in that office reads, because the other eleven lines are about clay, and clay is not a story."

"**So I object to the second half of your proposal, and not to the first half, and I want that distinction entered before I sign anything. The first half is that it happened. I have no objection at all to a page that says a thing happened. I object to a page that says a condition can be set aside, in a document whose entire claim is that its conditions are not set aside.**"

"**Then say the thing that is against you,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because this boundary learned that in the twentieth week and it is the only free thing in it.**"

"**The thing against me is that a condition that has never been lifted is not a condition. It is a sentence. And this boundary has had a page of sentences for twenty-seven weeks and two weeks ago a room was shut with eleven people in it on a Friday at the fifth bell and every sentence on that page was true the whole time."

"**So I am not asking for the lift to come off the page. I am asking for what replaces it to be worth more than the thing that was lifted, and I am not going to be the woman who says a condition should be impossible to break. I am the woman who says a condition should be hard to break and honest about having been broken, and there is a difference and it is the whole of my trade.**"

---

**A third hand wrote the correction, and that hand belonged to the person who keeps the room, and the condition is a better one than the one it replaced, and the man who proposed the revision did not write one word of it.**

Renn Vale wrote it in her own hand on the anteroom table, and she read it out twice, and Dalla Sen read it a third time against the page, and the room checked it the way this room checks everything.

"**The second column of the tenth row reads, and I am writing it now and it is mine and I am not going to be argued out of it by a federation or by a man with eleven lines or by a woman who came down a step for this."

"**This room is opened when the person who lays the sill and the person who holds the stones have both said in a room together that the clay under that sill has stopped shrinking."

"**And it is shut, without a vote and without a number and without anybody being sent, on the first morning on which the answer to this is no: *the strip under the ninth hearth stone is still holding.* Said out loud, in a room, every morning this room is used, by a person who is not named on this page and is not named anywhere in this building's record of who keeps what."

"**And if the answer is no twice, the nine come out, and they do not wait for a physician, and they do not wait for a meeting, and nobody in this building is to be told afterwards that they were told to wait.**"

"**Say the last of it out loud and slow,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I want the clerk to have the words and not the shape of them.**"

"**Read it back, Dalla,**" said Renn Vale, "**and then read it back a second time, and if it is the same both times then I have written a condition and not a wish, and if somebody in this room thinks there is a version of that condition with a day on it I would like to hear it now and not in a month.**"

Not one person in the room had one. Dalla Sen entered that nobody had one, and entered that a man who had proposed a revision of a page had stood at the step and said nothing while a woman he does not keep a room for wrote a better condition than the one he had asked to be replaced.

Then the third column of the tenth row was read out, and it was a schoolteacher's own hand from the third day of the last month, and it had not changed, and Ilsa Norr said: "**That is still mine and I still stand up for it and the condition above me has got harder since I wrote it and I am glad of it and I want that entered, because a week ago I would not have wanted it and a room that keeps a record of that is a room that can be argued with.**"

---

**Then she put her hand to the bottom of his, and it was not a signature of the plan, and Dalla Sen said so out loud before anybody could say it was.**

"**Say what this is and what it is not,**" said Renn Vale, "**because in a month somebody is going to find out that an air engineer's hand is on this boundary's public plan and they are going to say she put her name to a set of numbers."

"**This is not a signature of the plan. There is no signature of the plan. Five people were asked on Monday and five people said no and the clerk wrote it in a book and read it out twice. A signature of the plan is a person putting their name to twelve rows and being sent for."

"**This is a person putting her hand under a man's hand on one row, with her objection written above it, and it means she will answer for the objection and not be improved out of it. That is all. It is a smaller thing than a signature and it is a bigger thing than agreeing.**"

Nera Oduya read her own objection out of her own hand once, said it was hers and was not improved, and then put the chalk to the bottom of the man's eleven lines and signed under them, and did not say anything after, and nobody said a kind thing to her, and one woman who might have said one did not.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in her words and read out of her own hand again so that the room could hear it. Entered that the objection is above the proposal and the correction is under both. Entered that she has answered for the objection and not for the plan. Entered that nobody asked her why and that I wanted to ask her why and that I am not going to, and entered that the reason I am not going to is the same reason as the fourth column's, and the strike stays struck.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked and it took four minutes because the count was in a short mood, and the clerk read the six things and the day's number and the count and nothing else in the room spoke.**

"**Two today and a hundred and thirty since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and forty-seven and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half."

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water at the waist has not moved and the water at the low end is back in its channel and the grate is still off and there is no second one."

"**The count. Wen Pask is seventy-three days and seventy-three nights into a count that is his own.**"

"**Fifteen entries, eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row and a plan, nine in the ordinary column and six in the one with nothing over the top of it, and the column is not headed and the count has given his reason and is not going to give another this month, and I have walked a board in a room where an engineer has put her hand to a man who came here on Monday saying a document with a name on it is a handle, and I am not going to pretend to myself that those two things are not in the same room.**"

**And the plan was in four parts in a coat with a stronger condition in its second column than the one it replaced, and an objection written above a proposal, and three hands on one row out of twelve, and no name anywhere on the page and no signature anywhere on the plan, and the market day is the sixth day of this week and this week has not got long left in it, and the only road out of this Hearthhold for anybody who is not one of the five of the crew has a form on a board at a gate with two lines and a space in each of them, and this building has not decided anything about it.**
