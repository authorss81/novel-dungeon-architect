# Chapter 76 — The Word Maintainer

On the Saturday of the thirteenth week the water did not come in, and the reason was that Isel Ferrow was on his shutter route, and the reason he was on his shutter route was that a half-day of shutter work had gone to standing at a pump frame with his hands empty in the twelfth week and nobody had ever replaced it, because a half-day of shutter work is not money anybody in this building was going to be told about, and because a man with a shutter route will always be somewhere the shutter needs him.

The first crossing was due at the first bell on the ninth step with eight pails on a two-day's pot, and at the first bell Isel Ferrow was not on the near lip.

"He is at the third level," said Sefu Aran, from the wheel, having gone and looked, which a keeper does not do. "I have sent Bel Ordry. And I am writing this down myself and I am not going to hand it to the clerk, and here it is: *the first crossing of the day was not made at the first bell because the bearer at the near lip was on paid work, and the paid work is his, and it is not this boundary's, and it is the only reason there was a question at all.*"

The crossing went at the second bell and a half. Eight pails, four at a time, fifty-eight seconds off the near landing again, which Jo Verrick has said twice is the best figure in the record for a party with a load in its hands and is worse than the thirty-eight, and which she would rather nobody built anything on.

Mikka Hale's number that evening was **fourteen**, written on her own board in her own hand at the first bell and read out at the fifth bell by a person, and the reason she gave was the whole of it and it took about nine seconds.

"Eight pails short by three hours. The pot is short, not the water, because a pot can be run thin and I ran it thin. **I have got fourteen days and it was fifteen yesterday and it is fourteen tonight, and it is fourteen because a man went to earn his living instead of standing on a stone in the cold, and that is a completely ordinary thing for him to have done and it cost this Hearthhold a day.**"

"Entered," said Dalla Sen, "in her words, with the time on it, and the fifteen, the sixteen and the nineteen are in the book in the same column going back to the start, and I would like the clerk to point out that the column has been falling for a fortnight and has never once gone up, and that this is the first entry in it with a person's name and a reason and a road attached."

---

Then, at the fourth bell, Sefu Aran asked for the notice to be brought up from the bench and read out again, and Dalla Sen read the fourth paragraph, and the anteroom listened to it a second time and heard something it had not heard the first time.

"Right," said Bel Ordry. "Dalla. Come here and look at your own hand for a minute."

She came and looked.

"Since the sixth day of the twelfth week," he said, "this book has got in it: a hardwood inventory with an owner against every line. A man's estimate of what is left in a pump, in his own hand, with the time on it. A schoolteacher's rota and three items of her own cost. A keeper's calendar, four days in seven, in her own words, called a calendar. A record of four mornings of a bar against a wall with no conclusion in either direction. A count of six crossings in six cold mornings. A record of a boundary's arithmetic in two columns that both reconcile exactly. A cook's book of favours, begun this week. A closure with a reason in a woman's own hand. A shelf in a store with nine lines on it that is nine hundred years old and has just been described into a page of this one.

"**Every one of those is a record of somebody keeping something.** Not a name. Not a holder. Not a bond. A record of work, in a book, in a hand, with a day on it. And there is a notice upstairs — a fold of guild paper on a bench — that says the trade will supervise a boy for a year if a **named holder is entered against a named route, in a register kept by the office.**

"Do you see it? Not you — the room."

"I see it," said Dalla Sen.

"Say it, then, because I am not going to say a thing like that out loud before you have, and you have held this book for thirteen weeks and if anybody in this building is going to say it, it is you."

---

She had not gone pale. She had gone *careful*, which in Dalla Sen is worse, because Dalla Sen going careful is a woman looking for the word that will keep the page loadable.

"I did not write it," she said. "I want that said first, and I want it said before anybody is excited. I did not put it in and I would not have put it in and there is no version of this book that anybody could point at and say *there, the clerk did that.*

"**And I am the only person in this building who can put it in.**

"Everything else we have made in eleven weeks is in a room with a person in it. Jo's estimate is on a board in the anteroom. Mikka's book is in a kitchen. Renn's slate is on her own arm. Ilsa's three items are in her own words in my hand because she cannot write them down herself and that is the only thing in this book she has asked me to hold.

"And this is the book. **This is the only object in this Hearthhold that a person forty days away can hold.** And a person forty days away cannot hold a room, and cannot hold a woman at a frame, and cannot hold a woman's eleven words. But they can hold this.

"And in nine weeks' time a notice goes into a roll, and it does not need my name in it, and it does not need anybody's name in it, because what it needs is a **record that a route was kept**, by anybody, on any day, in any hand, and I have been writing one of those since the sixth week of the sixth month."

The wheel went round twice under the floor.

"Enter it," said Renn Vale, from the upper landing. "All of it. In her words. Now, while she is saying it, and read it out, and put the time on it, and I do not care that it is nine pages. **I have spent thirteen weeks insisting that a refusal is not policy, and the first time a refusal has got itself onto a page in a form that could be carried out of this building, it has not been mine and it has not been anybody's. It has been the clerk's, and she did it eight times a day out of duty, and she is the best person in this Hearthhold at writing and that is now the dangerous thing about her.**"

"Entered and read out," said Dalla Sen, and her voice went on the last four words and she let it, which she had never done in thirteen weeks, and Bel Ordry's face did something that nobody in the anteroom had seen it do before.

---

Sefu Aran came off the wheel, which he does not do, and the room stopped.

"Now the other half," he said, "and the other half is why I have spent four days not sleeping.

"**Everything we have done in eleven weeks has been to keep a route from having a name on it.** Renn refused the form's field. Dalla wrote *none reported*. I have said in a room that the day a keeper starts saying no to paper is the day a keeper starts keeping a file, and I meant it, and I would like somebody to notice that I was talking about paper and not about a book with our own days in it.

"And it has worked. Form nine C says **unattended**. No officer of any chartered office shall be required to enter, inspect, maintain, fund, or answer for any condition arising upon it. And that sentence is the reason nobody is ever going to help us and it is also the reason there is nothing on that stair for anybody to take.

"So look at what thirteen weeks has actually produced, in the two halves. **The stair is untouched and unwritable, and everything we love in this Hearthhold is in a book.** The wash rooms are in a book. The school is in a book. The kitchen is in a book. The pump is in a book. The ground under the waist is in a book. And the stair is the only thing we have got that is not, and it is the only thing we have got that is also the only way anything gets in.

"So the dependency is this and it is a shape and not a feeling, and Renn drew it on the landing two weeks ago and nobody has said it out loud since.

"**In thirteen weeks the people this boundary keeps have become the people this boundary keeps.** Not in a speech. In a rota. **The nine children are the ones who cross the corridor. The clinic is the reason the water crosses. The kitchen is the reason the stair is used at five in the morning. The woman who lifts the covers is the string on the stair.** Every single thing that gets carried through this building in the next four months is carried by somebody who is being carried, and there is no category in this book that separates those two people, and I have looked, and I have looked in both columns, and **there is no column that separates them and that is not a gap in the arithmetic, it is the arithmetic.**"

Somebody at the back said the word. Only one person said it and it went round the room and was never said again.

"**Employs.**" Bel Ordry said it into the middle of the floor. "I did not enjoy it and I am going to write it down in this book whether I enjoyed it or not."

---

"No," said Renn Vale.

The room turned round, because it had learned in eleven weeks that there was one voice in it that said no before the argument started, and it was the voice of a woman on a landing who had said no out loud about a night room, a deck, a four-foot remnant, a waiting room, a school store, a stair, a form, a slate, and a list.

"**No**, and I am going to say the whole of it in one breath, because we have a quarter of an hour before the covers and I am not having this dragged out.

"Two weeks ago on this landing I said that the choice was not between a floor and a charter. I said it and Dalla wrote it and it is in the book: **the choice was between a floor with a name on it and a floor without.** And this room has not chosen either, and I said it would find out what that costs when Jo opened a housing with a borrowed chisel.

"Here is what it costs. **The choice is not a floor. It is water.** It is the same choice with a different thing in it. It is between a floor with a name on it and a floor without, and a school with a name on it and a school without, and a kitchen with a name on it and a kitchen without, and a room at the wash-room end kept by a woman with nobody's work in it, **and a name on it is the only thing that has ever made anybody send anybody, and a date is half a name, and a hand is the other half, and Dalla Sen has both.**

"And the other half of it, and this is the half that is actually going to hurt somebody in this room, and I am saying it now so that it is on the page before it happens instead of after.

"**Jo Verrick's number is one.** One cleaning. A seat made out of a piece of wood that does not exist. And in a year's time somebody in an office reads a book that says what was done, and by whom, on what day, and the question they do not ask out loud, because offices do not ask things out loud, is the one I am going to ask.

"*Who was maintaining this in the twelfth week?* And this book will say: **nine people, a woman at a frame, a keeper, a cook, a teacher, a man on a bell, a woman on a string, a boy on a plinth, and a man with a bad hand who is not on the rota and was not asked.** Every line in their own writing. And every one of them is going to read it in four years in a room they are not in, and I would like somebody to tell me which of those people is a beneficiary."

Nobody answered, and nobody was supposed to, and the check hissed on its one note under the floor.

"**All of them," said Renn Vale. "**Every one. And that is not a scandal and it is not a discovery. **It is a public thing, being kept by the public, and that is the whole of the thing anybody in this Hearthhold has wanted since the seventh week, and I have been in this room thirteen weeks teaching it how to be careful and it turns out the careful part is the danger.**"

---

Nobody fixed it. That is what Dalla Sen entered, and she entered it in her own hand, and read it out twice, and it is the longest sentence the clerk has written in thirteen weeks.

"It is entered that on the sixth day of the thirteenth week this boundary established, in a room, in front of eleven people, that **every object of care in this Hearthhold is now recorded in a book that one person holds, in a form that can be carried out of this building, and that the only object of care in this Hearthhold that cannot be carried out of this building is a stair.**

"It is entered that this was not decided. It was arrived at, over eleven weeks, by nine people doing nine kinds of work correctly, and that no person in this room proposed it, and that it is now a fact and cannot be un-arrived at without somebody deciding to un-arrive at it.

"It is entered that the fourth paragraph of a guild notice read in this anteroom on the fifth day of this week provides for the supervision of a trade by a **named holder entered against a named route in a register kept by the office**, and that this boundary has not applied for it, and has not been asked for it, and that the clerk's own assessment is that the boundary has now built the evidence for it without knowing it was building anything.

"And it is entered that I wrote that assessment, and that I would have written it in the sixth week as a kindness and that in the thirteenth week it is a warning, **and that the difference between the two is not a change of mind and it is thirteen weeks.**"

"Read it out again," said Renn Vale, and Dalla Sen read it out again, and the anteroom heard it twice, and the pump came on under the floor, twenty-six strokes, six minutes off, nineteen, and the check hissed, and it was one note, and it was thin, and it was December.
