# Chapter 83 — Eleven Minutes Early

The pump was eleven minutes early on Wednesday because a woman was in a clinic and a boy of twenty-two was on a frame, and by the time anybody understood that, the water in the dip had come up over a floor two foot eight inches wide that stands on the edge of an eleven-foot shaft, and there was nobody standing at the low end of the corridor, and there was a school on the other side of it.

Here is what the morning was, in the order it happened, and the clerk wrote all of it down and read most of it out at the fifth bell.

At the first bell the corridor was open and the school came through, nine children and Ilsa Norr, four at a time and at a walk, three parties of four with a bearer at the near lip for the lot of it, and the crossing took the eleven minutes it has taken for five weeks. The plinth was read by Ruk Sella and the string by the woman from the wash rooms and the bell was Bel Ordry at the threshold and the bearer at the near lip was Isel Ferrow, and the plinth reader's word was a warning and not a stop, and it was not given.

At the second bell the pump went on, twenty-six strokes, and it was attended, two people, and the two were Mikka Hale and Wen Pask.

He had been asked the day before, at the fifth bell, in the anteroom, out loud, with the terms, by name, which is how everything in this building gets asked now, and Dalla Sen had read the ask back and Wen Pask had repeated the terms back the way a man repeats terms he intends to keep, and the terms were: *the frame is not a watch, you are not on a rota, you are not paid, and the hour goes in the column with no heading and not in the count's ordinary column, and if the frame wants stopping you put your hand flat on the wood and say nothing, and if you want to get off it you say so out loud and you get off it.* And he had said, in his own words, that a stoppage is a thing a man has to be able to do with his voice and not only with his hands, and that he had a good voice, and that he had not been asked for one hour of anything in fourteen days and would take the hour.

"It is not a wage," said Dalla Sen, and read it back, and he said yes he knew it was not a wage, and she entered that he knew.

At the second bell and about a minute past it, Mikka Hale was called to the clinic return.

---

She did not go at once and that is the part of it the room has never been able to get round, because she stood at the frame for the length of a count and a half with her hand still on the wood, and then she said the whole of it out loud to the anteroom, and it is in the book in her words.

"Somebody has to say this in a room or it does not happen, so I am going to say it and then I am going.

"I have been asked for a day this week at the clinic return and I have been putting it off since the second day, because the clinic is three days in seven and this is one of them, and the day I do not go on is the day there is nobody at the clinic return, and there is a woman on that side of the shut waist who has been sitting with a bad chest since before the collapse and she is not mine and she is not this boundary's and she is somebody's mother.

"And I have been running this frame for fifteen weeks with two people on it and I have never once asked who the second person was going to be today, and today it was a boy of twenty-two, and he has a leg and no bond, and I am not leaving him on a frame with a lever in it. **So: I am going, and the frame is going to be short, and the pump is going to be stopped before its cycle by the woman who maintains it, and the water in that dip is going to come up about eleven minutes early, and I am going to have written the number of the pot before I have finished saying this, and I would like somebody in this room to understand that I have not been arguing for it. I have been putting it off for two days.**"

She went down the nine steps at a walk and the anteroom heard her go and then heard nothing for a while.

---

Jo Verrick had the frame ten minutes later and she had it for the rest of the pump's run, and she did not start it.

"Twenty-six strokes, six minutes off, nineteen," she said, to anybody who wanted to hear it, and nobody in the anteroom wanted to hear it and all of them heard it. "And I am not starting it, and here is the whole of my reason and it takes nine seconds and none of it is a mystery.

"**This pump is stopped by two people and the second one is a woman on her way to a clinic and a boy of twenty-two with a leg. That is one person. I am one person. A frame with one person on it and a lever in it is not a pump that is run attended, it is a pump that a man has said yes to, and I have spent fifteen weeks telling this room that the difference between those two things is the whole of my trade.** So the pump is off and the dip comes up, and the dip is the pump's water, and the pump's water is the water that took the packing out of the waist in the ninth week, and I put that packing in, so this is mine twice and I would like it entered that I said so myself before anybody asked me."

"When does it come up," said Nera Oduya, from the top of three steps.

"It is coming up now," said Jo Verrick.

---

A minute and ten of them, and the room did not know about it for nine of them, and here is the reason and it is the reason the woman from the wash rooms gave at her own door and it turned out to be right before anybody had finished arguing about it.

Nobody was sent down that corridor. That was her third condition and it is in the book in her words — *do not send anybody to make it true, nobody down that corridor to check on me and nobody down it to check the water* — and Renn Vale enforced it in front of a full room at the fourth bell, and Bel Ordry did not go, and Tams Quill did not go with a slate, and Isel Ferrow stood at the near lip with his hands empty and did not go past it, and the man who wanted to go most was Marek Venn, and he did not go either, and nobody asked him to.

So the water came up under the low end of the corridor for as long as the pump stood off, and the sound of it was the sound it always makes, and **nobody in this building heard whether it changed, and nobody will ever know, and the two sentences on the review page did not come true and did not come false and are still the only thing standing in that line.**

What happened in the corridor instead was this. The school had gone through at the first bell and had not gone back, because the school goes through and stays on the anteroom's side until the fourth bell, and at about a quarter past the second bell the water came up over the low end and stood on the floor of a corridor two foot eight wide, and nine children and a schoolteacher were at the anteroom end of it with a lit lamp, and they were not in it.

And then Bel Ordry's bell went at the threshold — the school-end bell, rung once, which is a sound and not a word — and Ilsa Norr looked at the floor of the corridor from where she was standing and said, out loud, to nine children, in a flat voice, the thing she has spent fifteen weeks learning to say in a room with strangers in it.

"Nobody moves until I have looked at it."

She looked at it for a long time. Then she said, "Back to the anteroom, in twos, at a walk, and no talking, and we are having the whole of the morning in here and I will not have anybody ask me why."

---

Sefu Aran shut the corridor at the fourth bell and put the reason in his own hand, and the reason was not the ground and this is the first time in five weeks that has been true.

"Shut for the rest of the week, and not for the waist. The waist is where it has always been and the third wedge is where it has always been and none of that has moved this morning and I will not use it as a reason to close a road. **I am shutting it because the low end of that corridor filled with nine inches of standing water for eleven minutes this morning and there was nobody at it, and I am not putting that back on a rota in the same week.**"

"Read that back," said Dalla Sen, and read it back, and the anteroom heard *and I am not putting that back on a rota in the same week* twice.

Renn Vale said, from the landing: "Say the rest of it, keeper, because there is a clause in it and the clause is the whole of it."

Sefu looked up at her for a moment.

"The rest of it is that there is a blank line on the review page with two sentences on it, and the two sentences say that a woman will come when the sound changes, and there is a condition under them, and the condition is hers and the third one is *do not send anybody to make it true.* And I have just shut the corridor and I have not sent anybody, and I want it entered that the keeper of this boundary enforced a condition that cost him a road, in the same room, in the same breath, and did not go looking for somebody to make the second sentence true, and I am not going to."

"It is entered," said Dalla Sen.

"Entered in the keeper's words and read out again at the fifth bell."

---

Water came in two hours late in the day, because the crossing after the fifth was not made on a corridor that was shut, and eight pails went over the waist by hand in two crossings of four, and Iven Tallo priced it at the frame afterwards in his own hand, which is the way he does everything and the reason this room trusts him, and Dalla Sen opened a new page for it because it was the fourth week of the hand carry and the second of the water-carrying, and there is a difference between a cost and a habit and the clerk wanted the difference visible.

"Eight pails in two crossings of four, two people, at a walk, over a fill that has moved three times, and it is not a different job from last week and I am not going to pretend it is a new one. **It is an ordinary day.** It is an ordinary day now and it will be an ordinary day in a month, and that is the thing this Hearthhold has not got used to, and I am not going to let it get used to it quietly.

"Price. A day and a half of a shutter route for Isel, again, and the half-day that went to the pump frame in the twelfth week and was never replaced. A day of my own trade, unpaid, again. A day of school, because the corridor is shut and the school is in the anteroom and the anteroom holds ten including one litter and there are eleven in it and I am one of the eleven and I am the wrong shape for a litter. And a day of a pot, and I will come to that one last because the cook has got it."

"My number is twelve," said Mikka Hale.

She said it from the frame, with her hand flat on the wood, the way Jo Verrick had said hers.

"It is twelve. It was thirteen on Monday and it was thirteen on Tuesday and it is twelve tonight. And the reason is not the corridor, because the corridor has been shut three days in seven and four in seven since the fourth day of the eleventh week and this is the fifth time in eleven weeks and it has cost me a day twice in that time. **The reason is that the water arrived two hours late in the day and a pot can be run thin and I ran it thin, and I ran it thin because there were nine people in my anteroom with no school and I gave them the water that was left instead of the water I had planned for, and I made that decision at about the fourth bell on my own and I did not ask anybody and I would like that in the book, and I would like it in the book in the words *on my own*, because the last time that column went down it had a man's name and a road in it and this time it has got my name and nothing else.**"

"Entered," said Dalla Sen, "in her words, with the time on it, and the nineteen and the sixteen and the fifteen and the fourteen and the thirteen are above it in the same column going back to the start, and the column has never once gone up, and I am going to read all of them out at the fifth bell tonight because the clerk is not going to be the only one who hears it."

And the boundary's arithmetic went into Tams Quill's column with no heading, in his own left hand, and it went in as a page of figures, and he read it and did not round it and the number was fourteen and a half in the sixteenth week for Iven's own trade alone, and he said that out loud, and then he said the other half of it, which was the reason nobody had stopped him.

"**Fourteen and a half days of a carpenter's own unpaid work in one week, on top of twenty-nine hours and two days that were in the book at the end of the last month, and I have never once entered any of it in the crew's column, and I am not going to, and I want the room to know that I have checked whether I am allowed to and the answer is no, and I checked on purpose, because a man who checks a rule before he breaks it is a man who might break it next week and not notice.**"

---

Marek Venn had known about the six minutes since the seventh week and had never in fifteen weeks put a person at the low end of that corridor, and on Wednesday afternoon he sat on the anteroom bench and worked out the reason and it took him about four minutes and he did not tell anybody, and the reason was this.

He had rated this anteroom for ten people including one litter, with the outer service door open and the pump attended, and Nera or Sefu able to stop it. That rating was correct. He had built it in a month when the ground under the waist had not moved three times and the shaft had been a chimney and the pump had a bronze seat in it and the route was unattended and untouchable and the whole of the design depended on there being a machine in a room and a door out of a room.

And the design had been right, in the way he had designed it, and the failure had not been in the design at all. The failure was that a stoppage requires a second pair of hands, and the second pair of hands had cost a day of a cook's count and a boy's labour and eleven minutes of a corridor, and **the number of hands a room needs is a load, and he had priced the load in timber and in air and in drainage and had never once priced it in *people who are not there,*** and that was the thing nobody in this room had ever asked, and the ninth line of a nine-hundred-year-old sheet had asked it in the oldest possible way, with a count of one and no work in the column.

He put that down. Not on a slate. He put it down in his own head, and he wrote it nowhere, because there is nobody in this building to write it for, and the brass rule was in the coat on the hook and had said nothing for eight weeks, and a man who is leaving in a spring is the last person in a building who should be the one who starts keeping a private list.

At the fifth bell Dalla Sen read five things on one line, as Renn Vale had asked for in the twelfth week and had now had for three weeks running: **the lip's range, and the seventh term, and the ninth line, and the pot, and the blank on the review page with the two sentences on it instead of a name.**

She read the two sentences out twice.

And the anteroom heard them, and the whole of the argument of the last three weeks was in them, and every person in that room understood that the woman who had offered them had been forty feet away and a floor up with a lid off a cistern at the second bell, and that she had not come, and that nobody would ever be able to tell whether the sound had changed while she was lifting a cover, and that the page said *the moment that sound changes* and the page did not say *and we will know.*

"That is the condition working," said Renn Vale. "Not the promise. **The condition.** It is a thing I asked for and she set and I enforced this morning at a cost to a road, and I would do it again tomorrow, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to anybody that it did not cost the water, because it cost the water and I would rather that than the other thing."

"What is the other thing," said Bel Ordry.

"Sending somebody," said Renn Vale, "and calling it care."

Nobody answered that, and the anteroom went back to being an anteroom, and the pump stood stopped and would be started in the morning by two people because the boundary had spent Wednesday finding a second one of them by asking a man of twenty-two in a room, out loud, with the terms, and putting his hour in a column with no heading because there is no other column in this book for a man who is kept.
