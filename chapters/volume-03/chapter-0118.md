# Chapter 118 — Stopped In A Room

**The third day of the twenty-third week was a Wednesday and the corridor was open and the school ran at the far end of it, and at the sixth bell of that day a woman with a slate took a cloth off a doorframe in front of eleven people and declared a thing stopped, and the boundary's busiest room went back to being a room whose second way out is a word, and eleven people in that room did not see it, and the only person in the building who could have put the four facts side by side was standing in it with a piece of chalk in her hand.**

Jo Verrick put her rule against the jamb at the first bell and looked at a line of chalk, and did not say anything for about a minute, which for Jo Verrick is a paragraph.

"**Say it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**It has not moved.**" She took the rule away and put it back. "**And I am going to say the rest of it and I am not going to be pleased about it. It has not moved by a hair, and I laid the rule against this jamb at the first bell on the second day of this week with a hair out of one of the nine's heads across the chalk, and the hair is still across the chalk, and a hair is not a measurement and I have said that twice and I am saying it again.**

"**So a thing that has gone out a finger and a bit in a fortnight and a finger a week in the cold has not moved a hair in nine days, and nobody in this building knows why, and I have told that room twice that I do not like it and I am telling it a third time and I am going to be wrong about being worried, because I am worried, and being wrong about being worried is the best thing that has happened to me in three weeks.**"

Renn Vale read the condition out loud, standing on the landing, in her own hand, off her own page, and nobody stopped her.

"**The mark is on while the head of this frame is out of true. It is off when the head has not been further out of true than the line on this jamb, through a night and a morning, and I take it off and nobody argues with me about it.**"

"**Then it is met,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Then it is met,**" said Renn Vale, "**and the clerk is going to do this in the order the room does things in, and I am going to say that the first three are not going to take a minute and I am going to say that I have waited nine days to do them and that I am not going to do them quickly, because the last thing this building did quickly was a floor.**"

---

**Found, located, tested, recorded, and not before, and the room did it on the anteroom step with the corridor open behind it and the school at the far end of the corridor and the pump running.**

Found: a line of chalk and a line of gland grease, about a foot long, at the height of a hand, on the dressed face of the jamb, where anybody coming through that door can see it. Located: the head of the frame, a finger and a bit out of true, and no further. Tested: Jo Verrick's rule and a hair, twice, and the third reading after Renn Vale had read the condition out loud, because the count in this room is that you check after the condition has been said, not before.

Recorded: Dalla Sen, standing, with the room at its number.

"**Entered in the clerk's hand: the head of the anteroom's outer service door frame has not been further out of true than the line on the jamb, through a night and a morning, and the condition written by the woman who keeps this room's terms is met, and the woman who keeps this room's terms has said so out loud, and the air engineer is in the room and has been asked whether she objects and has said no and has said why, which is that the head is carrying nothing and there is nothing to object to carrying nothing for.**"

"**Then it comes off,**" said Renn Vale, "**and I take it off, and nobody is to be holding anything at that moment, and I want the room to know that I have thought about that and I have said it out loud on purpose.**"

Marek Venn said, from the step, "**I have not got a hand that is going to help and I am not going to pretend I have.**"

"**I know what you have got,**" said Renn Vale, "**and you are going to stand on that step and you are not going to touch it, and the reason you are not going to touch it is not that I would rather you did not. The reason is that this thing is mine. I agreed to it and I wrote the condition and I am taking it off, and if a man who put a thing on a wall is the one who takes it off, then in a year there is a man in this room who can put things on walls and take them off, and there is nothing on the wall to stop him.**"

That had not occurred to anybody in that room and Dalla Sen entered that it had not occurred to her either.

So Renn Vale took a cloth off the line of chalk and the smear of grease with a cloth, in front of eleven people, in about forty seconds, and she put the cloth in her own apron and not in anybody's hand, and the jamb underneath was a dressed stone jamb with a head on it that was a finger and a bit out of true, and it was going to be that again in three weeks, and nothing in the room changed that afternoon except that eleven people had watched a woman take a thing off a wall.

---

**And then the count came in, and it was the same count, and it did not stop, and he had known for nine days that it was not going to.**

It was under the pump's check, low and uneven and counted, like a thumb run along the edge of a plate, and Marek Venn had known what it was since the second day of the week before last and had been told by three people that it was a sound in a room he already had.

**The burning stopped.** That is the whole of what came off his hand, and it is worth being exact about, because there is a habit in this building of making these things larger than they are.

His left hand did not close that night. It closed on the second morning. It was serviceable on the fourth day. It is not what it was on the day before the mark, and he can still not do with it the one thing he used to do with it, and nobody in this building has ever seen him do that thing, so there is no way for anybody here to know what it is.

And the sound stayed.

"**Enter this,**" he said, to the clerk, "**and do not do it kindly, because I have watched you do a thing kindly this week with the air engineer's four words and I would rather have it plain.**"

"**Say the plain of it.**"

"**The thing I did on Tuesday cost me an afternoon and a hand and I do not have either of those back, and the sound in the room is still in the room, and I put a mark on a stone to tell a seam to hold a bit of nothing and it worked for nine days and the frame is where it was on Tuesday, and the sound is not the frame.**"

"**Then what is the sound,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I do not know,**" said Marek Venn. "**And I have not known since the fourth day of the nineteenth week and I have had nine days to guess and I have not guessed, and if a room wants the answer to that I would like it written in the book in my own words that I do not know and I am not going to fill the gap with a theory about a building.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it, and read it back, and then she stopped with the chalk up and said: "**I am going to ask you now and I have not asked you at the sixth bell, which is what I said I would not do, and the reason I can ask now is that the thing you were going to explain has stopped existing.**"

"**Ask.**"

"**No,**" she said, and put the chalk down. "**I have been clerk in this building for twenty-two weeks and I asked a woman for a piece of work in a room in the last week of the last week and she did it in four minutes and would not do more, and I wrote that down and I thought I had learned something by writing it. And I have just caught myself about to ask a man for an opinion, and an opinion is not a piece of work, and you do not get to have a thing explained in a book so that a reader can check it later. You get to have a condition and a limit and a mark on a page, and if the mark is off the wall then the mark is off the wall and the sentence in my book is a sentence about a thing that is not there any more.**

"**I would rather keep the sentence than have the explanation. Enter that I chose.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked and the mark stayed on it, and that was the fight and it was short.**

"**The crew are at fifty-three and a half and have not moved and I am not giving the reason, and a man of twenty-two is fifty-three days and fifty-three nights into a count that is his own, and the clerk reads it because the clerk reads what is on the board.**"

"**The boundary is at a hundred and eighty-three and a quarter, and that is four and three and five and four and three and four and three and four, and it adds, and both columns reconcile and they have reconciled for nine weeks.**"

Then Tams Quill got his chalk to the side of the board with nothing over the top of it and stopped with it a finger off the wood.

"**No,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I have not said anything.**"

"**You have your chalk an inch off the thirteenth thing and I am going to say the thing you are about to do so that you do not do it quietly.**"

"**I am not going to take it off,**" said Tams Quill. "**I have thought about it for two days and I am not going to, and here is the reason and the room is going to think it is a small one and it is not. The mark stopped this morning. That is a fact about the mark. It is not a fact about the entry. The entry is on this board because a man spent an afternoon and a hand on a doorframe and because I was standing in a room and did not know what to do with it, and I did not know what to do with it and I still do not, and that is a more honest thing to have on a board than a figure.**

"**And I am not going to take it off the day a woman took a cloth off a wall, because the day I take things off this board is the day this board starts being a record of what this boundary has done rather than of what has happened to it, and it was not that in the fourteenth week when a line was refused and it was not that in the nineteenth week and I am not going to be the man who changes what it is at the sixth bell.**"

"**Then the arithmetic stands,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The arithmetic stands. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark. Nine in the ordinary column and four in the one with nothing over the top of it. And it is five batches running that I have not headed that column and I am not going to give a reason this week because I gave one last week and it was the worst one I have got and I have not got another and I am not going to make one up in front of you.**"

"**That is a new reason,**" said Renn Vale.

"**That is not a reason, that is a man saying he has run out, and I am entering that the count said he had run out, in a room, at the sixth bell, in the fifth week of refusing.**"

He was not asked for a better one, because he had asked them to notice and nobody had, for four weeks, and Dalla Sen entered that too and entered that she noticed this time and did not do anything about it, and that is the first time in this book she has entered an omission.

---

**And then the air engineer went down the nine steps and along the corridor and into a room that has been shut for three weeks with a dry floor in it, and nobody went with her, and she was gone about ten minutes.**

**In the wall of that room, above where nine children used to sit, there is a mouth.** It is shut, and it has been shut for twenty-two weeks, and it is warm under the hand and it has never once been certified, and Nera Oduya has refused to publish a rate for it since the second week.

She came back up and said: "**I have left a strip of dyed cloth in the mouth at a width I would not have set.**"

Renn Vale said, "**Say the width.**"

"**I am not going to. That is the point of it.**"

Bel Ordry said, "**I would like somebody to object to that in a room, out loud, because I have known this building for a month and you have never once left a thing set the way you would have set it and told us not to touch it.**"

"**I would have set it narrower,**" said Nera Oduya. "**That is the whole of the objection. I have been down that room twice this week and the floor under the mouth is dry, and I do not know what a mouth does when the room under it stops being wet, and I have no measurement I trust, and there is a mark on a frame in this building that was holding a bit of nothing nine days and I never got a figure off it, and I do not trust a figure I did not take.**

"**So I have left it wider than I would have left it, and I would like it to be set by somebody who has to keep it rather than by me, and if a woman in this building whose room it is reads that and disagrees with me she is going to come up there and change it and I am not going to argue with her, and I am not going to be thanked either, because this is air and not a favour.**"

For a bit, nothing.

Then Renn Vale went down the nine steps, and along the corridor, and into the school corner, and came back, and said: "**It is narrower. And it is what I would have set. And I did not know that a week ago and I have no idea why I know it now and I am not going to look for a reason in a room with a mouth in the wall.**"

Nera Oduya said: "**Good,**" and nothing else, and went up to three steps, and Dalla Sen entered the whole of it in her own hand including the word *good* and including that nobody had thanked anybody.

---

**And at the seventh bell Marek Venn was on the anteroom step with his left hand open on his knee, and the anteroom was full, and the room was exactly the room it had been for twenty-two weeks.**

Ten including one litter for no more than two hours. The outer service door, on four rusted pins, packed with stone and clay, shored with stone and lime and a wedge out of seven. A head a finger and a bit out of true, going a finger a week in this cold, in three weeks when the clay is drier.

And a second way out that is a word.

"**The word is back,**" said Bel Ordry, to nobody in particular, because he was the man who had said it out loud in a room in the twentieth week and had understood four seconds too late that he had described a rule. "**The frame is where it was and the second way out is the word and the word is said in a room by the person who is going to be in the room and the clerk repeats it. That is where we are. We were here on Friday and we will be here next week and I have written that sentence down four times in five weeks and it has never once got shorter.**"

"**Then it goes back in the book as it was,**" said Dalla Sen, and wrote it, and read it out, and the room took it the way a room takes a thing that has been true for five weeks.

**And here is what did not happen in that room.**

Dalla Sen had spent five weeks putting facts next to each other on purpose, with a reason beside them and nothing between them, so that a reader in a year could not make them into a finding. She had done it for a closure and a school day, and for three facts and a bench nobody owned, and she had done it in her own hand and underlined nothing and improved nothing.

**She had in front of her, at the seventh bell of the third day of the twenty-third week, four facts that belong next to each other.** That a frame is a finger and a bit out of true and its second way out is a sentence. That the sentence was said out loud in a room to a woman with a permit and written down in her own hand. That this boundary has four instruments and no procedure for either of the two bodies it has not written one for, and the third one has a form with two lines for a name on it. And that the mark is off the jamb and the room has not changed.

**She did not put them next to each other. There was no reason. There was no fact in that room that would not have gone on the page beside another one, and she had the chalk in her hand, and she did not do it, and the six things were read without it.**

That was the first thing in five weeks that this clerk did not do, and she has never given a reason for it, and she was not asked.
