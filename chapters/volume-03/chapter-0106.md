# Chapter 106 — The Wheel Goes With The Keeper

The wheel went with the keeper, and the boundary discovered on the first day of the twenty-first week that it had been carrying the wheel in four other places for twenty weeks and had not once noticed.

Sefu Aran went up the middle passage before the light and nobody saw him off, because nobody in this building has ever seen anybody off and there is a rule about that which is not a rule but a habit, and the habit is that going is a thing you do to a corridor and not to a room.

**And then it was a Monday, and the corridor was shut, and the school was not running, and the pot was read at the fifth bell by a woman who was not waiting for anybody, and the pump ran attended from the second bell to the fifth by two people who had not been told to be careful and had decided to be careful, and nothing in this Hearthhold went wrong.**

That is the finding. It is the whole of the finding and it took the whole day to arrive and it is not a comfort, and the room was in a bad mood about it by the fourth bell.

---

**Dalla Sen read the two sentences at the third bell, standing, in the anteroom, with the room at its number, and she read them because the man who left them had asked her to read them to whoever would have to act on them, and there is a clerk in this building who is the person who has to act on them, and she did not think about that and she did it anyway.**

"**These are not mine. They are his, and he wrote them in the nineteenth week and he read them out in this room, and he left them with me and said read them to anybody who has to act on them. So I am reading them to you, and I am reading them standing up because I do not think a thing like this should be read sitting down, and I have never done that before in this room and I am not going to pretend I have.**

"**One. Nothing comes. This boundary is not sending anybody and has not sent anybody and will not. There is a water-lock at the second gate with a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood and two hands a day for four days, and that timber is the office's and the office has no carpenter with a bond, and this boundary released it in writing and the release stands and the office is not to blame. The four households have their own water and their own four sets of steps and they have had them since the cold closed, and they did not ask this boundary for any of this.**

"**Two. This boundary has found two jars a day out of its own wall and is using them and is not sending them, because it has two jars a day and a man of twenty-two and no second pair of hands, and if it gave them away it would have none. And the true sentence is that it looked after itself and four people carried water up four sets of steps, and those two things happened at the same time and one of them was this boundary's choice.**"

For a while nobody said anything, and then the pump's check came off, and six of the twenty-six went by, and the water in the low end of the corridor stood where it stands, and the sixth minute went, and nobody was standing at the low end of the corridor watching it, and the blank on the review page was still blank, and nobody was asked about it, and nobody was sent for, and the blank on the review page is still blank and is going to stay blank.

Then Mikka Hale said one thing and it was the only thing anybody said about the sentences for the rest of the day.

"**Read them again at the fifth bell.**"

"**They are read at the fifth bell,**" said Dalla Sen. "**That is what the six things are for.**

"**Then read those two at the fifth bell as well,**" said the cook, "**and I am not going to argue for it and I am not going to explain why. If I explain why it will sound like I have an argument and I have not got one. I want them read because a thing that is true and heard once is a thing that gets repeated wrong by the fourth person who hears it, and a thing that is true and read every day is a thing that four people say the same way.**"

**At the fifth bell Dalla Sen read them again, and at the fifth bell every day while the keeper is gone,** and the room decided that by Renn Vale, out loud, on the landing, in the same breath as the reading, and what she decided was narrow and she said so.

"**I decide that they stand and that they are read. I do not decide that anybody in this building is to do anything about them, because there is nothing in this building that reaches four days up the middle passage, and a room that decides what to do about four households' water is a room that has started sending somebody, and we are not sending anybody.**

"**And I decide it, and not the clerk and not the man going up the road, because it is my page and I can stop it and I can be stopped by it. Write all of that down and do not shorten it.**"

---

**And then Marek Venn did the thing nobody in this building has done since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, which is to go and find out what would happen, and it took him the whole of the fourth bell and it took him the whole of the fifth.**

He went round the boundary asking one question of everybody, in their own work, at their own station, and the question was: **if the keeper never came back, what would want his word.**

He found three things. He wrote all three in his own book and the book has them and they are these.

**One. The water-carrying.** Six pails a day, every day in seven, two crossings, four and then two, struck by the keeper in his own hand on the fourth day of the seventeenth week. He went and read it out loud to Renn Vale and she read it out loud to him, and it was right, and every figure on it was a figure that was hers, and it did not want a keeper at all.

**Two. The two hands a second sheet would need.** He asked the room who the second hand would be and got three names in about four seconds, and all three of them were people in this building, and **none of them was the man on the road, and all three of them already knew the answer and had not been asked.** That is a thing he wrote down and it is the sentence that made him put the chalk down afterwards.

**Three. The plan.** A page in four parts, in a coat, in a room, with a man in the room, and the man is here, and the man has been here for twenty weeks and has asked to be suspicious of in a month and has not been.

**All three had a substitute. Not one of the substitutes was a keeper.**

He came back down the nine steps at the sixth bell and said so out loud, and he said the part that was for the room and the part that was for himself, and Dalla Sen wrote both.

"**Then here is what I have found and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have got. This boundary does not need a keeper. It has three things that want a keeper's word and every one of them has somebody else in it, and the wheel is not the wheel, the wheel is a man who comes off a step and says a thing out loud, and everything he says can be said by other people, and they mostly do.**

"**I have wanted to be needed in this building for twenty weeks. I have been needed twice last week and both times it was a floor and a wet joint and a man with a rule who knew where to put his hand, and neither of those needed me, they needed a woman who had been kneeling on that flag since the eleventh week.**"

Nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him, and Dalla Sen entered that the room heard it and entered that nobody was asked to agree, which is a thing this room has had to learn twice.

---

**And at the sixth bell Tams Quill walked the board twice and read both columns and named no day and gave no reason, and put a number in the column with no heading, and did not head the column.**

"**The crew are at fifty-two and a half, and they have been at fifty-two and a half since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and they did not move today, and I am going to give the reason out loud because Renn Vale gave hers out loud and because two people in this room have now said the same thing about the same figure in two different words and it is time somebody put it in a column.**

"**The figure is not a debt and it is not a rota and it is not going to move by itself, and it moves when a person in a room asks the five of them for a work, with the terms said out loud and a person named who can be told no. Nobody has asked them for anything. That is the whole reason. It is not that they are tired and it is not that they are angry and it is not that they have offered twice this week and been stopped twice, which they have, and which is in the book.**"

"**The boundary's column,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**A hundred and forty and a quarter.** A hundred and seventeen and a quarter when the keeper signed, and nineteen in the week he went, and four today: Dalla Sen one, a reading and a reading again and a copy for a courier. Bel Ordry one, a page in a coat carried past three things that wanted a keeper's word. Renn Vale one, the road held for a week with a substitute named in her own hand. Me, one, a board, walked twice. **Four, and it adds, and I have done it twice, and both columns reconcile and they have not stopped.**"

Then he read the column with nothing over the top of it, the way he reads it: a leg, a bundle, **forty-one days and forty-one nights**, a half hinge, a line refused, a page gone out of sight, and a wage.

"**And it has no heading and I am not going to give it one, and nobody has asked me to today, and I would like it noticed that it is the fourth batch running and that I have a better reason each time and that a man with a better reason each time is a man who is running out of reasons, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that and say something about it before I have to.**"

Nobody said anything about it.

---

**At the seventh bell Marek Venn went up the nine steps to the anteroom bench and sat down, and there was nothing for him to do, and that was the second time in twenty weeks, and it was worse than the first.**

He had a book of his own and he wrote in it, and this is the first entry in that book about him and not about a plan.

> *Twenty weeks. I have been the man who goes in this building since the first week. He goes up the road this morning and the boundary carried on and the pump ran and the six minutes came and went and the pot was read at the fifth bell and the low end of the corridor stood in water with nobody watching it, exactly as it does when I am on a bench being useless with a cloth round my hand.*
>
> *And the second thing, which is worse. A room can hear the true thing about itself and go on to the fifth bell. It is not comfort and it is not all right. It is the boundary working. It is the thing we have been arguing for in nineteen weeks — that a room does not stop when a man goes up a road — and it worked, and I was the last man in this building that needed it to and I am the one it left standing on a stair with nothing to carry, and I do not know what to do with an evening.*

He read it back at the seventh bell to nobody, because that is the rule, and put the book away, and went down to the kitchen and ate, and the pot was ten days and had not gone up and was not going to.

**The shut school corner was still shut, and nobody opened it to look, and nobody has opened it since the day it was shut, and the marks on the lintel over the mouth are still there being four marks and one groove that two people read two ways.**
