## Required movement and chapter cards

### Chapter 11 — The Room That Holds the Terms

Open with the first ordinary consequence of the anteroom pass: the pump must be attended, Leena needs a dry place to recover, and the residents need a decision about the six outsiders. Marek's goal is to understand the actual admission procedure without pretending that a successful test equals a welcome. Resistance comes from Sefu's narrow authority, Nera's refusal to speak for residents, the practical concerns of Renn Vale, Dalla Sen, Jo Verrick, and Mikka Hale, and the guild order's attempt to turn maintenance into custody. Show a real conversation or work exchange, not a lore lecture. End with the residents requesting a named maintenance shift and a route record before any deeper admission.

### Chapter 12 — The Work That Keeps Water Down

Center the pump, air gate, water line, and the labor of keeping the temporary repair alive. Iven must explain the work to another person; Oren and Tams should perform the lifting and watch rotations; Pell can call readings but cannot fake mobility. Marek's goal is a safe maintenance rotation, not a permanent pump. Resistance is sediment, a chattering check, a difficult housing, and the temptation to leave the bulk tools behind without recording who owns them. Let Nera correct an air assumption and Marek revise a plan in public. End with the first resident-maintained shift and a new, named maintenance cost.

### Chapter 13 — A Charter Is Not a Signature

Make the missing route charter the center of a political and practical conflict. The old inspection charter covers the incline, not the service shaft or Hearthhold gate. Sefu explains who may refuse admission; Nera explains what the air crew can certify; Iven explains what the guild can and cannot freeze. Marek wants one clean agreement, but the residents need consent, withdrawal, and maintenance language. Include a disagreement over whether the crew's labor makes them beneficiaries, claimants, or simply temporary workers. End with a draft that is useful but incomplete, not a final public charter.

### Chapter 14 — The Hearthhold Beyond the Gate

Allow only a limited, consent-based look at the inhabited rooms beyond the anteroom. The route remains conditional; show kitchens, a clinic or school corner, ordinary maintenance, and residents who have to live with whatever the crew proposes. The goal is to learn what the Hearthholds call a safe room in daily practice, not to tour or claim a neighborhood. Resistance comes from a resident's refusal, a real domestic constraint, and Marek's old habit of treating a room as a load diagram. End with the crew gaining access to a work area, not citizenship or ownership.

### Chapter 15 — The Guild Line Is Closed

Bring Dael's order into ordinary consequences. The guild-controlled surface door is physically closed and the remaining guild-financed equipment is frozen or unusable. Iven's maintenance signature now matters: he must explain his work to people who may report him, while the crew cannot pretend the order is harmless paperwork. Nera and Sefu must keep their separate roles. Use political pressure and difficult choices rather than a new fight. End with a decision about what the crew will do if the guild refuses future parts, records, or rescue support.

### Chapter 16 — The Workday at the Boundary

Give the batch a recovery and ordinary-life chapter. The residents and crew share food, water discipline, watch rotations, tool repair, and the small domestic tasks that make an infrastructure claim believable. Show Pell's frustration with being protected and Leena's frustration with being treated as only a patient. Marek's goal is to learn what maintenance looks like when no crisis is currently occurring. Resistance comes from fatigue, conflicting schedules, and the residents' need to preserve their own routines. End with a relationship or work practice changing because the outsiders have stayed long enough to be useful, not because they have earned a free place.

### Chapter 17 — When the Air Path Changes

Create a structural or air pressure event using the established systems, not a new monster. A wet mineral seam, the pump housing, the air baffle, or the returning-stair pressure cycle can force a difficult choice. Nera's expertise and Marek's load observation must complement rather than duplicate each other. The goal is to keep residents and the injured crew safe while preserving the conditional route. Resistance includes a real reversal, a failed shortcut, or a neighbor room that cannot be ignored. End with a changed air/route plan and a new maintenance obligation, not a permanent solution.

### Chapter 18 — The Stair in Someone's House

Bring the returning stair into the local civic argument without resolving it. Its physical cycle, missing receiver, and old school/infirmary history must be treated as evidence of a damaged function, not as a monster to be killed. A resident or worker may know a different local history from the guild record; do not make that history a complete answer. Marek must choose whether to map the stair for a future route, warn the Hearthholds, or leave it closed. Pell's injury and Leena's shoulder must affect the decision. End with the stair explicitly closed or restricted, its condition recorded, and a clear reason to return to it later.

### Chapter 19 — The First Public Work Sheet

Make the crew's maintenance work legible to the people who will inherit it. Marek wants a clear plan; Leena wants the corrections visible; Iven must translate craft work without claiming authorship; Nera writes air limits; Sefu writes consent and stop conditions. The goal is a shared work sheet or interim maintenance agreement, not a permanent route charter. Resistance is disagreement over who pays, who can refuse, and what happens if the pump fails while the crew is asleep. End with a practical agreement signed or accepted by the relevant maintainers, including an explicit withdrawal or stop condition.

### Chapter 20 — A Route That Must Be Kept

Give the batch a complete local climax and decision. The conditional route is tested against an ordinary maintenance or pressure change, and the group must choose between a safe but limited continuation, a costly retreat toward the Cinder Steps, or a claim that would violate Hearthhold consent. The pump, air gate, returning-stair restriction, and legal order must all matter. Do not stage the Volume 01 returning-stair climax or reveal the empty seat's meaning. The batch ends with the six-person crew and the relevant Hearthhold maintainers agreeing on a conditional route, a named next review, and a maintenance obligation. It should be a meaningful decision and consequence, not a manufactured interruption.
