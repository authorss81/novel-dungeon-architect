## Part 2 — `outline/volume-03.md`

`outline/series.md` commits Volume 03 (*The Weight of a Door*, Chapters 101–150) to:

- **Central pressure:** a series of "safe" closures saves structures while trapping people, and the Hearthholds must decide whether to open dangerous routes.
- **Starting state:** the public charter is fragile and the relationship between surface regulators and underground residents is openly hostile.
- **Major locations:** a flood-prone school, a sealed archive, a market route, and the surface inspection line above the Cinder Steps.
- **Major factions/people:** Stonelight inspectors, Hearthhold families, guild route-clearers, and a Quiet Hands cell discovered after a sabotage.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Marek seals a leaking room to save its structure and accidentally cuts off the only ventilation and exit for a group of residents.
- **Escalation:** the resulting conflict makes him question whether public consent is a luxury during emergencies.
- **Volume climax:** he designs a multi-room evacuation network and stays behind to open a secondary route while Nera and Talla move people.
- **Concrete resolution:** the Hearthholds keep the room open under a public occupancy plan; the Ninefold Charter loses exclusive access to one essential route, but the Stonelight Compact threatens intervention.
- **Next-volume question:** can a route be a public good when the people who use it live on different sides of the law?
- **Power/relationship change:** Marek reaches the **Marker** stage; his first public failure is not hidden; Nera begins to trust his willingness to revise a plan.

**The starting state is not the one in the series outline, and the difference is the whole reason this outline is needed.** `outline/series.md` assumed Volume 03 begins with a fragile charter and open hostility. Volume 02 actually delivered a **signed charter with no holder against any route**, a plan on butcher's paper in four parts, a courier route outside the guild system, a **discharged** crew obligation, and guilds who are **competing rather than obstructing**. Write Volume 03's starting state out of what exists, and say plainly where it departs from the series outline and why the departure serves the ending.

**The Marker stage is owed in Volume 03 and nowhere else.** `bible/power-system.md` is the authority: *Marker: place a limited temporary bearing mark, with a visible target and bodily strain.* It must be **earned in the prose**, it must **arrive out of the work**, and it must cost. **No route change, no binding, no relay and no brace** — those are later stages. **The Reader stage reached in Chapter 99 is not repeatable**, Tams Quill entered it as a claim with a condition, and Marek Venn said out loud in advance that anybody who comes and asks him to read a boundary will be told no. **A later batch may not make the Reader reading repeatable, and may not quietly recover the right hand's fine control.**

**Do not introduce a new final enemy.** The Quiet Hands cell is on the antagonist ladder and is a Volume 03 commitment, not a new villain. The final antagonist remains the One-Seat protocol and Cassian Rook, per `outline/ending.md`.
