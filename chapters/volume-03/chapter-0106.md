# Chapter 106 — The Wheel Goes With The Keeper

The first day of the twenty-first week was shut for the corridor, which was a Monday, and the boundary ran all day without its elected keeper in it, and it ran perfectly well, and that is the finding and it is not a comfort.

Sefu Aran had gone up the middle passage at the first bell the day before with two true sentences in the clerk's book and nothing else in his hands, and nobody had walked him out past the waist because the corridor was open on a Friday and four pails were going over it in the pump's window, and **that was an accident of the calendar and not a decision, and Dalla Sen entered it as an accident of the calendar, and entered that she would remember it in a month.**

---

The first thing anybody said about it was at the second bell, from Renn Vale, on the landing, and it was not about the water or the school or the shut room.

"**Clause four says the keeper will show the fourth column,**" she said, "**and I have been up here for two hours working out whether that is Bel Ordry or Sefu Aran, and I have worked out that it is both, and that the two of them have never been the same man and I have always known it and never once said it in a room.**

"**The plan's keeper is Bel Ordry and he has been the keeper of it in every room that has ever read it. The civic keeper is Sefu Aran and he signs first on everything. And clause four is a sentence with one noun in it and two men in this building who could be it, and until a week ago the two men agreed without ever having to say so, and that is not a rule. That is a thing two men happened to be doing.**"

Bel Ordry said, from the anteroom table, "**Then it is a hole in the charter, and the charter is a week old, and I am not going to reopen a signed instrument over a noun.**"

"I am not asking you to reopen it. I am telling you that for five weeks this building has had two men in every sentence it has written and one of them went up four days of road yesterday morning at the first bell and I do not know one single thing in this Hearthhold that has a name on it that is not his or mine or both.**

"And I am not frightened of the boundary stopping," she said. "**I am frightened of it being the only thing that stopped because he was in it.**"

---

It did not stop. That is what the day was for.

The pump ran attended, second bell to fifth, two people, twenty-six strokes, six minutes off, nineteen, and the water came up the middle passage in two crossings, four and then two, at a walk, over a waist that moved three times and is not measured by three people on purpose. The six minutes after the lever came off came and went, and the water stood in the dip where it stands, two foot eight wide on the edge of eleven feet of shaft, and **nobody stood at the low end and watched it, because nobody has stood there since the sixteenth week and nobody ever will, and the blank on the review page had the two sentences on it and no name and it was blank at the sixth bell and it was blank at the seventh bell.**

The school was at the kitchen-line end and the corridor was shut, so the school did not run, and that is the arrangement and it has not changed in five months. The shut school corner was shut and **nobody in this building went and opened it to look, on the fourth day of a week in which a stranger had stood in it with a permit.**

The cook's number was ten days, and it had held at ten for longer than it had ever held, and it did not go up, and it was read out at the fifth bell in her words on her own board.

And at the sixth bell, at the anteroom, on the wheel, which nobody was standing at, Dalla Sen read the six things, and the six were the same six with two more on them, and the two more were the keeper's absence and the second sheet to be written on the second day, and nobody filled the space after the last one.

**Everything that had a person in it had a substitute in it, and not one of the substitutes was a keeper, and the reason every one of them existed is that Sefu Aran had said the words that made them, nineteen weeks ago, in a room, and had then gone.**

---

Marek Venn did the thing that nobody in this Hearthhold has done since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and he did it because he could not stand being in a room that was fine.

He went round the building with a piece of chalk and asked people the question he has spent five months asking, which is *what happens if this stops*, and he asked it about three things, and all three of them wanted a keeper's word, and all three of them had somebody standing in the gap, and not one of the three substitutes was a keeper.

**One. The civic wheel.** The cord and the wheel and the bell, which is the boundary's ability to say yes to a person and no to a person and to know which one it is at six in the morning.

Renn Vale stood at it for a bell and a half and then came off it.

"**I can hold it,**" she said, "**and I am not going to pretend I cannot, because I can and because refusing a thing I can do is a vanity and this building has four of those already.**

"**I can hold it because of what he said when he signed the charter, and I am going to read his words out because they are the only reason I can stand here, and they are in the book in his hand.** *The road is not the paper, the road is a way up a passage and I hold the cord and the wheel and the bell and none of those are paper.* **He said that in a room and I wrote it down, and it is the reason I have a wheel today, and it has been the reason for three weeks and I did not think about it once until this morning.**"

"Nobody thought about it once," said Bel Ordry, "**and that is not a fault. That is what it is for.**"

"**Then I am not a keeper,**" she said, "**I am a woman holding a wheel because a man said out loud that a wheel is not paper, and those are different, and the difference is going to matter the first time somebody wants me to do something I do not want to do, and on that day there is nobody above me to say no and there is nobody above him either, because he is on a road.**"

Dalla Sen entered that, in her own hand, and entered that it was the first entry in the book that named a gap rather than a work, and that she had a column for it and was not going to use one, because *a gap with a heading on it is a job and a job is what a person gets given at the fourth bell in a bad month.*

**Two. The water-carrying.** Six pails a day, every day in seven, two crossings, four and then two, at a walk, in the pump's window, on a fill that has moved three times.

The room read the seventh term out in Renn Vale's own words from her own slate, at the third bell, with her standing on the landing and nobody else in the room doing it, and it was right, and it did not require a keeper, and **she read it and then she said the thing that made the day.**

"**It is right and it is mine,**" she said, "**and I have been saying it is the boundary's for five months because the boundary is a nicer word than mine. The clerk writes it down as the boundary's because the charter is the boundary's. **I am not the boundary. I am a woman with seven terms and an eighth and a slate, and when this boundary stops, what stops first is me, and not because anybody took anything off me.**"

**Three. A signature.**

Dalla Sen found it at the fourth bell and it was the one that made the room sit still, because it was the smallest and it was the worst.

"**The second sheet,**" she said. "**The charter has seven hands on it and one of the seven is a man who went up the middle passage yesterday morning. The boundary is not reopening the charter, so the sheet goes under it, and the practice in this building for nineteen weeks has been that a thing under the charter carries the boundary's hands. We cannot get his.**

"**So we have to work out who signs it instead, and I have asked three people in the last hour and I have got three answers and I am not going to decide this and I am going to write down all three.**

"**Bel Ordry: he says a thing under the charter does not need the boundary's hands, it needs the hands of the people whose objects of care are in it, and that is a better rule than the one we have been using and he is right and I hate it.**

"**Mikka Hale: she says the air engineer does not sign anything in this Hearthhold and has said so out loud, and that therefore the sheet will have one signature on it and a second hand on it, and that a document with one signature and two hands is better than a document with seven signatures that means nothing.**

"**And Isel Ferrow, who I did not ask and who was standing there, said that a sheet with two names on it is a sheet two people can be sent to.**"

Nobody filled the space after that either.

Then Marek Venn said the fourth thing, which was not a gap, and which he had been carrying since the seventh bell on the day before.

"**The plan is a page in a coat in a room with a man in it,**" he said, "**and I have said in this room that a page in a coat is worth having exactly as much as the man in the coat, and I said it out loud in front of eleven people and it got written down, and I meant it.**

"**And this morning that man emptied his own school store on the second day and gave away the room, and on the third day he stood on the common landing and watched nine children walk over ground that three people will not measure, and this morning he read the standing arrangement out to a stranger in a room and then he did not ask for anything when it was finished. I have been in this building five months and I have watched him do less and less of the loud things for three weeks, and I do not know what that is, and I am not going to stand in a room and be the man who asks him why in front of the clerk.**"

Renn Vale said, "**That is the first thing you have said in a month that I did not already know.**"

---

The keeper's two true sentences were read out at the fifth bell, in a room, in this building, and the reason they were read out is that Sefu Aran had left them with the clerk to be read to whoever would have to act on them, and he had said that on the Friday and it had been entered.

Dalla Sen read them out of the book, in the clerk's hand, standing, and she did not put anything on either side of them and she did not say a word before or after.

*Nothing comes. This boundary is not sending anybody and has not sent anybody and will not. There is a water-lock at the second gate with a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood and two hands a day for four days, and that timber is the office's and the office has no carpenter with a bond, and this boundary released it in writing and the release stands and the office is not to blame. The four households have their own water and their own four sets of steps and they have had them since the cold closed, and they did not ask this boundary for any of this.*

*And this boundary has found two jars a day out of its own wall and is using them and is not sending them, because it has two jars a day and a man of twenty-two and no second pair of hands, and if it gave them away it would have none, and the true sentence is that it looked after itself and four people carried water up four sets of steps, and those two things happened at the same time and one of them was this boundary's choice.*

She read it and read the strike back, because there was no strike, and she said so.

Then the room decided two things and refused to decide a third.

The sentences stand, and they are read at the fifth bell every day the keeper is gone, and **the person who can stop that is Renn Vale**, and the reason she accepted it is that it costs her nothing and she wanted it and the clerk entered both halves of that.

And the room did not decide what anybody was to do about them, and Dalla Sen entered that in her own hand and said it out loud, because it is the kind of thing a clerk has to say.

"**There is nothing in this building that reaches four days up the middle passage. We are not sending a person, and we are not sending a page to stand in for a person, and we are not deciding anything about those four households, because we do not know one thing about their week that is not on this page, and this page is the boundary's own page and it is about us.**"

"Then what is the point of reading it," said Wen Pask, from the fourth step, who has no standing in this room and has had it anyway for five months.

"**Forty-one days and forty-one nights,**" said Tams Quill, from the bench, with his right hand open in his lap, "**and not a word of a head on the column, and I have said in this room that a column with a name on the top of it that holds wages is a column somebody can point at in a bad month, and I was right and I have got a worse one.**

"**That column now holds a wage and a man's count of his own days, and the man is paid. And a heading over a count of days is a rota. **And I have watched this building learn what a rota is, in a condition, in the fourteenth week, and a rota is the first half of a form.** So no, I am not going to head it, and if I ever do, somebody should take the board off the wall.**

"**And forty-one is in it in the clerk's hand and forty-one is not a date. It is a man counting, and I have not asked him what he is counting and he has not told me and it is not mine.**"

---

He walked the board at the seventh bell and named no day and gave no reason, and the crew's column was fifty-two and a half and had not moved for the ninth day, and the boundary's column was a hundred and twenty-three and three quarters, and the two items were Dalla Sen, one and a half, the keeper's two sentences read in a room and entered and a gap named instead of worked, and Renn Vale, one, the seventh term read out in her own words by herself and the wheel held by a person who said she was not a keeper.

"**And I am not going to say what the crew's figure is not,**" he said, "**because a man offered to carry nine boxes on the second day and was refused and the refusal is not a work, and a boundary that has not asked anybody for anything since the day the charter went on the board is a fact and not a failure, and I have said in this room that that number has never been a measure of us.**"

---

Marek Venn came down the nine steps at the eighth bell, which is later than anybody works, and sat on the anteroom bench, and found that he had nothing to do with the evening and had had nothing to do with it for some hours.

He had been useful four times this week. He had been right four times and useless four times and needed once, and the once had been a man reading a plan in a stranger's hands and saying *the word is door* and watching a woman write it down.

Nobody asked him for anything between the seventh bell and the ninth. He went and stood at the shut school corner's door with the wedge in it and listened to it not being a room, and then he went and sat on the bench at the top of the nine steps, and then he went to the kitchen and Mikka Hale gave him a bowl of something and did not talk to him, and he understood that she was doing the whole twenty weeks on purpose.

**He was not needed. He had been not needed for six weeks and he had not noticed because there is a difference between not being needed and being in a room where nothing needs doing, and he had been standing in the second one and calling it work.**

And the other half, which was harder and which he did not have a sentence for until the ninth bell: **a room had heard the true thing about itself at the fifth bell and gone on to the sixth bell.** Ten days of food. Two jars a day out of its own wall. Four people carrying water up four sets of steps. And then the pump's window had come round and Renn Vale had said *four and then two* and it had been done.

**That is the boundary working. That is not the boundary being all right.** Nobody had been in the room at the fifth bell and said anything about it and nobody was going to, and the boundary went on, and he wrote it in the book of his own that has nothing in it that anybody can be handed, and it was the first thing he had written in that book about himself.

*Twenty-one weeks. A boundary can hear the true sentence about itself and still do the pump's window. I do not know what that is yet. I know it is not all right, and I know the two of those are not the same, and there is no man in this building to ask because the man is on a road and because I am the one who is supposed to have the answer and I do not.*

The plan was on the anteroom table in four parts with two pails and a jar stopper on it because a stranger had been in the room on Friday and it had been out of the coat for a day and a night, and the fourth column was empty and had a reason on it in two hands, and the first column was a count with no name in it, and the anteroom was shut and empty and had ten on it.

The charter was on the scrub board with an office's number at the top of it and no name anywhere and eight clauses of which clause four had a noun in it that meant two men, one of whom was four days up a road with the only true sentence anybody in this Hearthhold has got left.

And the wall behind the anteroom's outer service door had four threads of water in it and a leaf-shaped stain the size of a hand, and it was the only drinking water in the building, and it was in the room next door to where nine children had been sitting for twenty weeks, and the charter named three objects of care and this boundary had four.
