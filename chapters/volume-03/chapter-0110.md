# Chapter 110 — The First Column

On the fifth day of the twenty-first week the corridor was open and the school was running in the worst room in the building and the fifth bell went the way it goes, and then a boy of fourteen read a column of numbers out loud in a room that was not full, and the boundary found out what it has been writing down.

**First the six things,** because the six things are read first, and the pot was ten days and had not gone up and was not going to, and the corridor was open, and the school was at the far end, and the shut school corner was shut, and a man of twenty-two was forty-five days and forty-five nights into a count that was his own.

Then Dalla Sen said, "**Numbers.**"

---

**Pell Roke was on the bench at the top of the nine steps with his crutch across his knees and his ear against the stone, and he was handed the kitchen part of the plan, and he read the first column out loud in the anteroom at the fifth bell, in order, nine rows, in a voice that did not go up at the ends.**

"**Ten. Four. Eight. Ten. Six. Nine. Five. Two. And a dash.**"

Nothing filled the space after it, and that is what the numbers are for.

Then Ilsa Norr said, from the fourth step, in the tone of a woman who has been thinking about a thing for two days and has run out of ways to not think about it:

"**Say the second column.**"

He read it. Nine conditions with no number and no day of the week on any of them, and the fourth of them is the word *wet*, and the eighth of them is empty, and the ninth of them is a dash.

"**Say the first column again.**"

"**Ten. Four. Eight. Ten. Six. Nine. Five. Two. And a dash.**"

"**Then say the count out loud for the room, Renn.**"

"**Ten,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, "**and it has been ten every time anybody has come through that door for twenty weeks, and it is empty now and it was empty at the first bell.**"

Then Dalla Sen put the chalk down on the table instead of in her hand, which she has done twice in twenty-one weeks, and said:

"**Stop. Nobody fill a school day or a water number or anything else. I am going to do a check and it is going to be done in this room and it is going to be done in chalk against a slate, and everybody is going to watch it and anybody can stop me at any point.**

"**We do not know what the numbers in the first column are numbers of.**"

---

**The check was done in front of the room and it took forty minutes and it checked three explanations and all three of them were possible, and the true one was not any of them.**

**One. A clerk miscopies.** Dalla Sen read the whole first column out of the kitchen part against the slate, twice, in the room, and the slate had nine numbers on it and the page had nine numbers on it and the slate was in her hand and the page was in Bel Ordry's hand and there is one clerk in this building and it is her. "**And I will tell you what my hand is on. I have never held a pen over that plan. The plan is in a schoolteacher's hand and then a cook's, and neither of them has ever let me near it. What I do is write down numbers that are said out loud in front of me, and I have written that column down eleven times and I have never once been asked what the numbers are numbers of, and that is not modesty, that is the whole of my job, and I have been very good at it.**"

**Two. A voice is misheard.** Renn Vale read the anteroom's row out loud, and Dalla Sen read it after her, and Bel Ordry read it a third time, and it was ten in three mouths, and the row has been said out loud in this room every time anybody has come through the door for twenty weeks. "**The number is not misheard. It is the most repeated number in this Hearthhold and it is said by me, twice a day, to a clerk, and it has never once been a question.**"

**Three. A room is miscounted.** The anteroom holds ten including one litter for no more than two hours. It was filled to ten at a drill and emptied at its declared time and the number was said then too. "**Nobody has ever been in that room over ten and nobody has ever been in it under ten unless it was empty, and empty is not a number anybody in this building has ever had to shout.**"

"**Then the check is finished and the check says the page is right,**" said Bel Ordry. "**Which means the number is a number of something, and the page is a true record, and this room has been transcribing it accurately for twenty-one weeks.**"

"**No,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The check says the page is right and the room is empty. Those are two facts and the page cannot tell one of them from the other, and that is the whole finding, and I would like it written before anybody says a clever thing about it.**"

---

**And that is when a voice came up the nine steps from the lower level, and it was not loud, because nobody in this building can shout up nine steps, and everybody in the anteroom heard all of it.**

"**A list is not a location.**

"**And a count is a list. And this page has been calling a permission a population since the day anybody in this building first picked up a chalk, and nobody in this building has ever once asked it a question, because a number that goes in a column and a number that goes in a room look the same on a page and they are not the same object and one of them is a thing you may do and the other one is a thing that is happening.**"

Marek Venn got to the top of the steps and looked down and did not go down them, because his right hand cannot hold a rail, and Leena Orr was on the lower level with her shoulder in its sling where she has been since the first week.

"**Whose number,**" he said.

"**That is my sentence and it is the only one I have got, and I am nineteen weeks into a room I cannot get out of and I have one.**"

"**Then take mine and I will take yours. What is a permitted occupancy.**"

"**It is what a room is allowed to hold,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps, and she was in the room and she had come down the four steps and she had not been asked, and then she stopped, and looked at the page, and did not go on for about four seconds. "**It is a number somebody set. It is not a number anybody counted. Those two things are the same figure and not the same number, and I have not said it for a month, and I am saying it now because somebody finally asked me what the number was.**"

---

**And then Dalla Sen did the other thing, which she had been holding since the sixth bell of the day before, and she did it in front of everybody, because she does not ask for a piece of work in a corridor.**

"**Nera Oduya. I am going to ask you for a piece of work and not for an opinion, and it is going on a page, and I want it in your own hand and I am not going to improve it.**"

"**Say what it is.**"

"**Write down what the second column is for. Nine rows, nine conditions, no number on any of them and no day of the week on any of them, and the fourth of them is the word *wet*. I want to know what a condition with no number on it is doing on a page that somebody has to keep, and I want it from the person who wrote eight of the nine lines of the third column and refused to sign the charter.**"

Nera Oduya wrote it in four minutes on the back of the anteroom table in her own hand, and it went onto the page under the second column, and nobody in that room read past the first line of it out loud. It is in the book in her hand. It is not about nine hundred years ago and it is not about anything she has a theory about, and it is the only work the air engineer was asked for in twenty weeks that she did not have to ask anybody permission to do.

"**That is four minutes,**" she said, "**and it should have taken nine, and I want the room to know that I had a great deal more to say and that you did not ask me for the rest of it.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the rest of it was not asked for, and that nobody in this room is going to ask.**"

And then the clerk did the last part of it herself, at the sixth bell, with the school store open, which is the only reason it could be done, and the school store is open, and it is open because nine register boxes are on hearth stones at the far end of the corridor and the plan's two parts are in a coat, and that is the whole reason and there is nothing else about it.

The dowel is a nine-row maintenance schedule on a wooden dowel in a locked school store, to a scale, and it has not been out of that store since the register boxes came out of it, with a count column with no names and a condition column with no days and a work column empty on six of nine lines. It has no air on it at all. It is undated and cannot be dated.

Its fourth row is the anteroom. Its count is a ten, and it is the highest number on the page.

Nera Oduya looked at it for a while and then said one sentence, and it was a professional sentence, and there is nothing in that room that knows what is standing behind it.

"**That page records a schedule. This page records a rating. And both of them have been writing down what a room is allowed to hold instead of what is in it, and I have looked at that dowel a hundred times and I am not going to say what I think it means in a cold month to a room that writes things down.**

"**My condition is not met and I did not come in here to have it met and nobody is going to ask me in a room, and I am not going to be hurried into it by a page, and I would like the clerk to enter that the fourth thing bound to *when this boundary is keeping itself* was approached in a room today and was not met, and that I was in the room.**"

Dalla Sen entered it. And then she entered four more lines under it, and she read them out, and the last one is the finding and it is not about nine hundred years ago.

"**A room with nine children in it has no row on the plan. It has no number, and it has no condition, and it has no third column, and it is not an omission, because nobody has ever written a row and left it blank. It is a room nobody has taught in and a room nobody has taught in has no number.**

"**And, as a schoolteacher said in this room on Wednesday, a blank row in a new document is an omission and an omission reads as an accusation. I said that about a row I have never written, and I am now saying it about a room with nine children in it on the fifth day of the twenty-first week, and nobody in this building has promised that room anything at all, and it is the first time I have understood why the ninth room has no row and it is not because two women are stubborn.**"

There was nothing to say to that in the anteroom, and the anteroom was full, and the sixth minute of the pump's cycle came and went with the blank on the review page still blank, and the four treads on the far side of the stair were dry behind a rail and nobody had been on them, and a person was still under a lip that was packed and not held.

---

**And Tams Quill walked the board twice, and read both columns, and named no day, and gave no reason, and put a number in the column with no heading, and did not head the column, and gave a different reason for not heading it than the one he gave last week, which is not what a better reason means and he is aware of that.**

"**The crew are at fifty-two and a half. They have not moved since the fifth day of the nineteenth week and the reason is still that nobody has asked them for anything, and I am not going to say that reason a fifth time.**

"**The boundary is at a hundred and fifty-three and a quarter.** A hundred and seventeen and a quarter when the keeper signed, and thirty-six since, and here they are by the day they were done. Four on the first day of the twentieth week, four on the second, four on the third, four on the fourth, three on the fifth. Four on the first day of the twenty-first, four on the second, four on the third, three on the fourth, two on the fifth. **Thirty-six, and it adds, and I have walked it twice, and both columns reconcile and they have reconciled for six weeks.**"

Then the column with no heading, which the count has now read out in full twice in a fortnight and will not itemise a third time, and **forty-five days and forty-five nights** is in it.

"**And no heading. And I have now refused four batches running, and I told this room on the Monday that a man with a better reason each time is a man who is running out of reasons, and I was not flattering myself then and I am not flattering myself now, and I have thought about it all week and I have got a worse one, and I am going to give it because I said I would and because a man who stops giving reasons is a man who has started enjoying it.**

"**A heading is not a name. A heading is a claim about what a column is for, and I have been putting a leg and a wage and a hinge and a man's count into the same column for five weeks, and every week I could tell you what the column is for, and every week the answer has been a little worse, and this week the honest answer is that I do not know what it is for. A column with a heading on it is a column somebody can check. And the day I can check it is the first day one of these seven things does not fit, and a thing that does not fit is a thing that gets left out, and the one that gets left out will be the one that is hardest to explain and it will be somebody, and it will be somebody in here.**"

"**Then do not head it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I will enter that I said do not head it, and that the reason was given, and that the reason is the reason.**"

"**Eleven nulls and one claim,**" said Tams Quill. "**Eleven, and the twelfth was a floor, and the twelfth is a null, and it is in the ordinary column in his own hand and it is in the ordinary column because a woman told him where to put his hand, and the arithmetic is nine in the ordinary column and three in the column with no heading, and the claim is still a claim and its condition is still not met, and I have not moved it and I am not going to.**"

---

**And Marek Venn said the last thing in the room, and it had three parts and the room did not argue with any of them, and it is in the book in his words and not improved.**

"**Two things I know, and one thing I do not.**

"**The first thing I know is that this boundary has just found out that a page can keep a number after the people have gone. Ten, in a room that has been empty every day for twenty-one weeks, in a hand that is careful, checked twice, in front of a room, against a page that is nine hundred years old and cannot be dated and that nobody in this building has ever had read out to it by anybody. I do not know why. I am not going to write a theory about it this week, and I want that in the book as a decision and not as modesty, because a man with a theory about it on Friday is a man with a theory about it in a month, and we do not have one.**

"**The second thing I know is that we have learned how to keep a room by making it a room nobody is in. The price was a wedge out of a school door and an afternoon. It worked. It is holding. Nobody is behind it. And everybody in that room voted for it on Thursday and I voted for it too and I was the only one in that room who could see it was the first of a series, and I did not say so, and I want that said, because the man who can see a series coming and does not say it is the man who ends up explaining it afterwards.**

"**And I do not know the other way. I have no idea how you keep a wet room safe with nine children in it and no shelf, and I have had twenty weeks to have an idea and I have not got one, and the person who has to work it out is me.**

"**And the last part, and it is the part Bel Ordry has asked me to say in a room for four weeks and I have only just got round to it.**

"**A page in a coat is not a system. That is a man. He is thirty-five, he is very good at writing documents nobody in this room can imagine being abused with, and I cannot check him, and a month is up and the clerk has now been made to be suspicious of him in front of nine people and that is the only reason I know it is a month, because I did not notice it and I am the man who noticed things for a living.**

"**So here is the sentence and it is the last one I have got. The man is on a road, four days up a middle passage and then on, with two true sentences in his coat and a form in a courier's hand going the other way, and nobody in this building knows when he gets there and nobody is going to be told. And if this boundary is going to be anything at all, it is going to be the thing that still works on a Friday with a page on a table and a man on a road, and that is a smaller claim than I came down here for and it is the only one I have got left.**"

**And in the pocket of the coat on the anteroom's hook the brass survey rule had said nothing for twelve weeks,** and the wrapped plate was on the shelf above the bench in its knotted bundle with nothing joined to it, and the pot was ten days and had never once gone up, and the plan was in four parts in a coat and it did not leave the building, and the dowel went back into the school store and the store was locked and the key stayed in the room, and a person was still under that lip.
