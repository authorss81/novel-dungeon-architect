# Chapter 209 — What It Cost To Stop It

The sixth day of the fortieth week is a Saturday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the forty-sixth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door, and the nine went down them at exactly the speed they went up them, which had been true since Friday and had not been true on Wednesday. The pump had not run for seven days. It ran at the second bell.

No market came into this building on this day and nobody here went looking for one. The one thing anybody knew about a sixth day arrived in a woman's mouth at the first bell, and what she brought was a fact about a counter, and it was about salvage this time and not about queues.

"**On a sixth day a claim is looked at on a second day, and not on a sixth, because the man who looks at claims is in the yard and stands in it,**" said Nella Bray. "**So a claim that goes in on a Friday is looked at on a Monday, and a claim that goes in on a Monday is looked at on a Tuesday, and a claim that went in on the day this one went in — the day before last, on a Friday — is being looked at today by a man standing in a yard in the sun. And I have not asked him anything and I am not going to, and I would like it entered that a claim on that table has been sitting here for two days and is today somebody else's job and not mine, and that this is the only day in a week when that is true and it is a day of the week and not an instrument.**"

"**Entered, as a fact about counters, and entered that the day of the week is the reason, and that nothing in this room is dated to a sixth day,**" said Dalla Sen.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twenty-ninth reading, and the same as twenty-eighth, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked, and the clerk entered all four as four refusals and not as one, and the count of mornings is twenty-nine and is the same count as the wall's.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell for about as long as a sentence takes to say, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and entered it, and the count of mornings out of mornings is fourteen.

The anteroom's terms were said at the first bell, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk, and they were said differently this morning and the difference was entered. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing, and the clerk entered that she did not ask him to read it on a morning when the pump was about to be started and entered that she had wanted to ask him. The third was six pails a day and it was false and it was said.

The sixth term was the pump, and the pump had been attended and stopped for seven days, and on this morning it was going to be attended and running, and the clerk entered that the sixth term had not been changed in forty weeks and that the thing the sixth term describes had.

Then the room got to the frame, and the room got to it because the pump could not be started until somebody said the rule, and the rule was a piece of chalk and a groove, and there was no other rule.

"**I am going to say the rule out loud because the sixth term says I have to and because nobody in this room is going to recognise it if I only nod,**" said Renn Vale. "**The pump is attended, two people, second bell to fifth. And it is stopped on a rising water line, and the rising water line is the chalk, and the chalk is at the height the water stood at in the eleventh week, and the chalk is on the head of that frame, and the chalk was put there by a woman with a sling on her arm who has not been in this room this week and whom nobody in this room has asked about anything.**"

"**There is a second line,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**There is a second line,**" Renn Vale said, "**and it is a hand's breadth under the chalk, and it is a groove in the stone and not a chalk, and Jo Verrick found it on Thursday afternoon and entered it on Sunday morning and gave the reason for the day late, and it is older than the chalk by a long way and nobody in this room knows who cut it.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything, and the pump stood in the middle of the floor.

Dalla Sen had the book open and was reading the sixth term back, because the sixth term is read back before a pump is started and it always has been, and the sixth term says that the pump is attended and is stopped on a rising water line and is not to be started on any other condition, and she had got as far as the word *not*.

Emrys Callow stood up.

"**Then we are not starting it,**" he said, and stopped, and looked at the floor.

Dalla Sen did not look up from the book.

"**I did not say that,**" said Bel Ordry. "**A clerk said the word *not* inside a clause and a foreman heard it and I heard it and nobody said it. I am going to say it now, because it is mine and it is nine o'clock on a Saturday and the school has been and gone and this is the only hour in a week I have for it.**"

He came down the nine steps, all the way.

"**I am going to say three things and the second of them is the one that matters and I have been carrying it since Monday and I have not said it because I have been waiting to see whether the room would find it, and the room has not found it, and a room that has not found a thing in seven days is not a room that is going to find it.**"

"**Say them,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**One. A groove in a stone that is nine hundred years old is not a rule. Jo said that on Sunday and she was right and I have agreed with her and I am going to agree with her in front of a pump this morning. Two. If we start this pump against the groove then the person whose rule is being kept is not the person who put the rule there, and that is the whole of the argument this room has had about everything for five volumes, and it has arrived at a pump, and I did not think it would get here and it has.**"

"**And the third,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**And the third is the one I do not want to say, and I am going to say it because I have said the other two. We cannot cut a new line on that frame. The rule in this building is that no mark is cut in the stone anywhere in it, and I have said that rule out loud in this room more times than I have said my own name, and it is the only rule in this building that has never once been argued, and if I stand here this morning and we decide that a nine-hundred-year-old groove is good enough to stop a pump, then in four months somebody is going to stand here and ask why a groove was good enough and not a line, and the answer will be that a groove is there already. And a thing that is already there is not a reason. It is a thing that was already there.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have not given it and it is owed.**"

"**The half against is that a man with a shut book under his arm is standing at the end of a trestle in a room where the water is two days deep in a pot and four of us have not had a whole night's sleep since Friday, and the groove is nine hundred years old, and the chalk is five weeks old, and the pump is not a document.**" Bel Ordry put his hand flat on the frame. "**I know. I want it entered that the man with the page in the coat has just argued for a mark cut in stone by a nine-hundred-year-old cutler and that he would do it again in nine hundred years and that he is not a person anybody should take advice from about a pump.**"

"**Then the pump is started,**" said Renn Vale, "**and the rule is both lines, and the clerk enters that both lines are the rule, and that one of them was put there by a person in this building and one of them was not, and that I am the person starting the pump and not the air engineer, and that the air engineer is not in this room and has not been asked and I am not going to ask her in a corridor.**"

"**I am here,**" said Nera Oduya, from the second of her three steps.

"**You are here,**" Renn Vale said, "**and I am not going to ask you in a corridor, and I am also not going to ask you now, and I would like the clerk to enter that I had a person standing two feet away who could have said the sentence and that I did not use her, and the reason is that she has not been asked about this all week and a person who has not been asked does not get asked at the moment it is most convenient for the room.**"

"**Entered, in the keeper's words, and entered that the air engineer was in the room and was not asked, and entered that the clerk did not ask either,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then two people turned the pump, and they were Renn Vale and Emrys Callow, and neither of them asked the other to, and the head came up in about a minute and a half and the sound of it filled the corner of that room where the seam was, and the room had not heard it for seven days and four of the six people off the far end had not heard it for seven nights.

At the fifth bell the pump stopped, because the fifth bell is the fifth bell, and the room had a pump again.

Then the room found out what it had bought, and it took until the sixth bell, and nobody had called it and nobody had arranged it, and the four of them were four different people and none of them was the clerk.

Emrys Callow took the wall first, because it was the quickest.

"**A head of water is on that wall now from the second bell to the fifth, three days in seven, and that wall has five and a half shores and a spreader that came out of this floor and a sixth place with nothing in it, and a brace of a school's own beech pushing on it from the other side, and a bad repair on one face only, and nine children behind that repair. I have been shoring for nine years and I would not stand a head of water on that wall and call it a pump. I am not saying take it off. I am saying somebody with a better eye than mine has to look at that wall before somebody puts a head of water on it again on Wednesday.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the foreman, and entered that he said *before somebody puts a head of water on it again on Wednesday* and not *never*,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then Jo Verrick, from the wall.

"**The bed under the ninth hearth stone is a third of an inch higher than it was in the twenty-ninth week and I gave that figure on Friday in this room and I said I would not write what caused it. It is not the pump. I have checked it twice this morning and it is not the pump.**"

"**Then what is it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I have not got that,**" Jo Verrick said. "**And I am going to say the thing that I think is the true thing and that nobody is going to like. If a room has a bed under a stone that is going up, and there is water in this building, and there is a wall between the two with a bad repair on one face only, then there is a thing I would want to look at that I have not looked at, and I have not looked at it because I have been reading the party wall every morning for twenty-eight mornings and the party wall is not that wall.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The half against is that I have said a great many things I would want to look at in forty weeks and that the reason I have not looked at any of them is that this building has a rule about sending people to places where a person can be killed, and the ninth hearth stone is in a room with nine children in it four mornings in seven, and a woman with a rule on a wall is not a person anybody should be sent into a wall about.**" She took her hand off the string. "**So there is a figure on that bed and there is a reason on that wall and neither of them is going to be looked at this week, and I have said that out loud so that somebody in a year knows that two people in this room knew there was a thing to look at and did not look.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it, and entered that the two of those are a figure and a reason and are not next to each other, and entered that the clerk did not put them next to each other and would like somebody to notice that**," said Dalla Sen.

Then Nella Bray, at the anvil end, with her slate under her arm and the claim squared on the trestle in front of her.

"**A cook gave this room a number on Saturday that was about water and not about people, and I said at the time that it was a flow and that a flow was a third instrument. I said that a flow could be had without anybody in this building becoming a column. That was true on Saturday. It is not true this morning.**"

"**Say why,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Because a flow is a number a person has to decide to keep,**" Nella Bray said, "**and a person decides to keep a number for as long as it is the only number, and the pump is running, and the pot was two days on Friday, and the moment the pump comes back the flow is a number somebody can stop keeping, and I have watched people stop keeping numbers before. The pot is at three. The seam gave one yesterday and nothing this morning. The number came from a kitchen, and the kitchen is going to stop being the instrument the day the pump is worth more than the pot, and the pump is worth more than the pot this morning.**"

"**So you are saying the cook is going to be quiet,**" said Mikka Hale, from the doorway.

"**I am saying the cook is going to be right and quiet,**" said Nella Bray, "**and that there is a difference between those two things and that the difference is about six people who are not this building's.**"

"**I will say the fourth thing then,**" said Mikka Hale, "**and I would like it to be the last, and I am not going to cry about it and I want that entered. Seven days ago I stood in that doorway and told this room that four days of no pump was four days, and I was right, and I have been carrying a number about this building's water in my head all week that nobody could use, and I have been proud of it, and the pump is running this morning and the number is a number of days that have already happened, and there is nothing at the end of it.**"

She did not put the cloth over her arm. It was over her arm. She left it there.

"**And the fourth thing is a person, and it is not me, and I have known it since Friday and I have not said it because I could not think of the sentence.**"

"**Then say the sentence,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The line on that frame is a rule,**" said Mikka Hale, "**and the rule is Leena Orr's, and the rule was hers because she put it there in the eleventh week and she has the right arm in a sling and she takes no weight and she has started that pump every working morning for nineteen weeks out of a thing nobody asked her to do. And we have just started that pump on a second rule that she did not put there, and she is not in this building, and nobody in this room has asked her, and I have been the person who knows what a rule is because I have been the person asking all of you for things all week, and I have not gone and asked her.**"

The room was quiet, and the pump was between strokes.

"**Entered, in the cook's words,**" said Dalla Sen, and her voice was not different at all, "**and entered that the woman who owns a rule is not in this room and has not been asked, and entered that I am a clerk and that a clerk's business is what has happened and not what was going to be asked, and entered that both of those are true and that I have found this week that the second of them has a price.**"

"**It has got a name on it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**It has got a name on it and I did not want it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I want it entered that the rule on that frame has had a name on it since the eleventh week and I have entered it forty times as *the rule* and not as anybody's, and that was correct procedure, and I am not going to say it was correct.**"

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a room which has just paid for something four times over is owed three numbers before the day is over and is not to be told they are a comfort.

"**Four today and three hundred and twenty-nine since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and forty-six and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty-four days and one hundred and fifty-four nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and fifty-five, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have said that in this room forty-five weeks running and I am not going to stop saying it, and a man with a page in a coat stood in this room this morning and said he would argue for a mark cut in stone by a nine-hundred-year-old cutler, and I have entered that, and I would like a person in a year to know that the clerk did not think it was a good argument, and that she entered it anyway, and that she is the clerk.**"

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a pump had been started on a rule with two lines in it and one of the lines belongs to a person who is not in the building, and a board is a column, and a clerk does not walk a column on a morning when a column has just got a name on it against her will.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman rose on Tuesday and named three people he could not ask and did not ask, and he has said since that there is a wall on the common landing that has to be looked at before Wednesday and that is a work, and he has not said the other two yet, and the reason has not been said in a room and a reason that has not been said in a room is not a reason.

The pot is three days and the seam gave one on Friday and nothing this morning, and nobody has measured whether the head coming back is why, and nobody is going to, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for sixteen weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a woman came up nine steps on Wednesday and asked this building to do nothing to a room full of water and went down them on Friday afternoon, and it is not known whether she will come back and no figure is attached to that either.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with a book on it and a guild's claim on it that was somebody else's job today, and a pump that had run, and a wall with a head of water on it three days in seven, and a schoolroom with nine hearth stones in it where a bed had gone up a third of an inch, and a rule on a frame that belonged to a woman with a sling on her arm who was not in the building, and a rule under it that belonged to nobody, and neither of them was going to be rubbed out by anybody.
