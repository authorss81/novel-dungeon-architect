# Batch Summary — Volume 01, Batch 0004

## Outcome

Chapters 31–40 are complete. All ten chapters exist as finished prose scenes, in close third person on Marek Venn, with **one** clearly marked Nera viewpoint shift at the end of Chapter 36 that carries distinct interior knowledge and changes a decision. The six-person opening party is intact. No opening-party member dies. The returning stair is not crossed, not opened, and not repaired, and it is still corded at the last line of the batch.

The batch turns a maintained but limited local room into a limited way of staying. It does not resolve the stair. It ends with a plan in a locked school box, a cost already paid, and a page that nobody has marked, because nothing in it has been agreed to.

## Movement

- **Chapter 31** is the third work-bell review, called on its own terms, and the interim decision is **corrected rather than renewed**. Jo refuses to read a decision that is being read twice and strikes the number nine, which was a test number that had become a capacity. Isel Ferrow refuses the line that made his room the reason for the clinic return watch, and Mikka admits she wrote it. Renn corrects four-in-seven back into a list that is hers. Tams's count is unchanged and unchangeable. Dalla then asks what the room does on the day the pump stops for good, and Marek says he has no answer that does not involve the corded stair.
- **Chapter 32** makes the absence of a way out ordinary. The crew's list cannot be started, the ledger has not moved in nine days, and a resident uses the crew's hands for something the crew did not propose: Ruk Sella's sheared door hinge. Renn then refuses the order of the day and gives the account instead — the lower receiving landing was given away by the school, cut in three, to a wash door, and a wedge-shaped piece of it was afterwards driven back into the socket by hand. The project's material question dies in the same breath: the parapet slab is lien-tainted and the pump gallery is Nera's air space.
- **Chapter 33** is the attended look at the stair's fixed first landing, on the corded near side only. Leena's four minutes set the stop-distance, Nera's grille and cloth set the conditions, and Pell — who cannot go — is given the most important job in the period, which is calling the dead window off the plinth. Marek's fifth null is written on a slate Jo pockets. Inside, the hollow is a **line-block**, not a seat, with a hundred-year running groove and a bright unwetted band above it, and the piece in the gap is a stop. A turn is survived correctly and costs a lamp-stand wedge into the throat.
- **Chapter 34** is craft that will outlast the crew. Isel is trained to be second at the water mark. Ilsa's grit turns out to be a floor with a mouth in it, and the repair — kerb, fall, two-board cover, a bar bent over a form cut from the floor's own broken stone — is paid for with Jo's shutter route, the boundary's grease, two boards released in writing, and the last drawn iron rod, which leaves the air baffle with no pin. Mikka writes the rota of people rather than jobs. Ilsa writes the refusal that it is not the crew's repair.
- **Chapter 35** is the mid-batch reversal. A school-door head drops a finger, a wedge splits, and the Hearthholds choose what stops. Mikka stops the kitchen brace outright. Ilsa takes back the waiting room and the school runs four days in seven. Renn will not stop the door and the landing. Marek proposes that the crew stand the sixth watch and is refused three separate ways, then proposes closing the corridor and is refused by Mikka. The head is one wedge short of what it wants, and Dalla enters the refused proposal in the crew's own column.
- **Chapter 36** begins the plan and kills it four times. The word *counter-support* is wrong because the hollow is a line-block. The bench cannot stand on the same block the answer depends on. The near lip is the edge of the hole over eleven feet of shaft. Oren shows that a lip one stone long is pulled at both ends. Every material line in Tams's four-column tally is *none*. Iven kills the four ways round it. Leena names the shape: a support that has to be stood by a person in the one place no person may stand. A marked Nera section closes the chapter with a private eleven-day count of forty-one turns and a rule she will not break — she will not put an unproven pattern on a page she does not hold — so the plan becomes a plan for stopping.
- **Chapter 37** is the pressure event during attended work, handled correctly by the watches and paid for anyway. Tams crushes two fingers on the stair's lip; the anteroom stays shut for nineteen minutes; and the school corner's new cover lifts two fingers and puts a line of grit along the kerb. Jo's stop record is written on her own board under pressure and then disputed by Sefu, Mikka, Leena, and Nera, four different ways. The void under the school corner is two feet deep in dry grit and is the floor of the old wash room, and it has been breathing into a room with nine children in it. Nera withdraws the plan's second step in public and says the school corner is a mouth and that no plan gets to treat that as a small thing.
- **Chapter 38** is the hard civic chapter. Renn calls the meeting. Mikka refuses to feed stair work. Renn refuses twice, including the access to the woman from the wash rooms, which costs the project its only living witness. Ilsa refuses the school corner as a work site. Sefu reads his boundary paragraph separately and says a watch stood by somebody who is not of the boundary is not a watch. Nera says the shaft is not hers to fix and not the crew's to promise. Iven brings in the guild: a restored lower side is how a route charter arrives, so the restriction protects the crew too. The terms are read one at a time, two of the nine are changed on the spot and one is struck, and Marek's own line is struck by Jo. The page ends with a line nobody can fill.
- **Chapter 39** writes the plan out in the open and has it taken apart line by line. Nera makes Marek take an outcome out of a step and put a limit in. Renn refuses step two and insists the record say *refused*, not *struck*. Nera makes the third step come off the page entirely. Leena strikes the clause that would end the plan with a walking person. Sefu strikes *the way out*. Tams's tally produces the batch's finding: forty-eight crew hours against forty-nine and a half hours of everybody else's, within an hour of each other for the first time, and the honest form of the question is *asked*, not *agreed*.
- **Chapter 40** stops at the cord, on a day the school was not running and in the first hour, which is the only hour the Chapter 38 terms leave anybody. The five posts that mattered are filled by named substitutes and the sixth watch is not a post at all, because the corridor is shut. The stair has not moved. Marek refuses to mark the stone. Renn sees the band, says her mother's piece is not a code, and has her own written term about no lamp on her landing reconciled on the slate in her own hand. Sefu answers the empty line with a threshold, says out loud that the answer will not work while the bell has nobody at it, and the gap is filled by Ruk Sella, who said yes and was not told she had been given anything. The next step is named and it is not the stair: it is the flooded pump-gallery cache and the screw jack in it, and Nera will write an air plan only when somebody says what they are going in there for. The next review is read out, corrected twice, and recorded. Tams gives the tally and finds the crew has gained nothing that belongs to it. The chapter closes at the kitchen line, with the kerb, the plate, and the stair's sound under the conversation.

## Material and labor handoff

- Water ledger untouched: five liters from the collapse plus one separately sealed liter of verified condensation, and the sealed liter did not open. The kitchen's nineteen days are given, unreturned, and unwaived.
- **Wedges: seven sound and two dead sticks.** Nine at the start of the batch — four at the school-door head, three at the waist, two in the basket. One was lost down the stair throat in Chapter 33 during a correctly handled turn, with the cause written twice, once for the person who is not to blame and once for the guild. One of the head's four split along the grain in Chapter 35, leaving **seven sound wedges and two dead sticks**, every sound one spoken for: three at the head, three at the waist, one in the basket. The head wants four and is one short.
- Timber, lath, and bronze are unchanged. The store list is read aloud at every count.
- Mikka has released three boards in writing; Jo released one off her own shutter stock and a paid household's shutter is a fortnight late; Nera released the last drawn iron rod, which was the air baffle's retaining pin, so the baffle has no spare and the boundary gate's lower hinge has no grease.
- The kitchen brace is **stopped, not deferred**. The clinic sill is unfunded. The school runs four days in seven and does not run on the three days the corridor is shut. The school-door head ends the batch one wedge short of what it wants.
- The fallen parapet slab remains *removed for safety, not recovered, not claimed*, and cannot be built on.
- The screw jack, the pry bar, and the last bulk tools remain in the lower pump-gallery cache, in a flooded room, and are now the named next step.

## Character and labor changes

- **Tams Quill loses two fingers of his right hand** and is off the lever for nine days. He refuses to have anybody decide a *while* for him. Oren takes the lever and asks for the frame to be held by a bored person; Mikka takes the frame.
- **Isel Ferrow** goes from a fever in a record to a person with a job and a refusal: he is trained to be second at the water mark and he is the reason the clinic return watch is for a door.
- **Ruk Sella's door** is rehung out of a released board and closes but will not stay shut against the wheel.
- **Renn** gives the account of her mother's repair at the cost of her mother's name in a room with a keeper in it, and then refuses the crew access to the only living witness to it.
- **Ilsa** gives up three school days a week and says the school pays, in her own words, by her own decision.
- **Mikka** stops a repair she has been owed since the twenty-fourth day, and refuses to feed the project.
- Marek's control flaw is corrected four more times by other people, and the last thing he does in the batch is refuse, on his own, to put a mark in a stone that belongs to a person who has refused him.

## Open handoff

The next step is the **lower pump-gallery cache**, not the stair. The next review is the **first work-bell after the school corner floor is opened, traced, and reported to the people who keep it in their own words**, and it is due immediately if the pump stops, if the air cloth moves, if the water reaches the sill, if any watch is empty, if the cord moves, if the seat goes, if the guild's door opens, if anybody proposes reopening the gallery, or if anybody in this boundary writes the word *out* on the stair slate.

The stair's project is a **line and an anchor on the near side**, not a receiver. It has no material, no line, and nowhere to stand. Its second step is refused and its third is unwritten. The stop on a stair day is a **threshold**, not a person. The stair is corded, the guild's door is locked, Form 7-B is unsigned, the plate is unexplained, and the boundary continues to be lived in.

## Relationship and thematic change

The batch's argument is that a limited way of living has become a limited way of staying, and that the difference shows up as arithmetic before it shows up as stone. Tams's finding is the thematic hinge: the crew's labour was never the free thing, it was the small thing, and the big thing is a woman lifting a cover at the second bell and a school that does not run. Marek's answer to that is not a better design. It is a plan he cannot complete, written in somebody else's hand, in a box held by a schoolteacher, next to forty-one years of a school that kept records about itself.

Nera's Chapter 36 shift is a character change rather than a reveal: she holds an unproven pattern on her own slate because she will not put a pattern on a page she does not hold, having learned that habit from another engineer's recorded air plan. The relationship remains slow, professional, and unaccelerated. No power stage, bearing mark, repeatable reading, route change, binding, relay, or automatic solution exists; Marek has five written nulls and says in public that he has never had anything back.
