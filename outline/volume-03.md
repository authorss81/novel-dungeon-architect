# Starting state — written out of what Volume 02 delivered

**The series outline assumed Volume 03 opens on a fragile charter and open hostility. Volume 02 delivered something else, and Volume 03 is written on the delivery.** None of the following is invented; each is in `state/continuity.md` or in Chapters 91–100.

## The instruments

- **A signed charter with no holder against any route.** Butcher's paper, an office's number at the top, **no name anywhere**, eight clauses argued out loud before the chalk, seven signatures. It keeps the way up the middle passage open to the people who live behind it, will not be shut for a delivery, and nobody may be charged for it. It puts **no holder of a route in this Hearthhold into a register kept by the office**, and writes the reason in the document. **The office can neither grant it nor refuse it**; it can receive it, file it, cite it, and take clause four to four other Hearthholds. **Clause nine puts the ninth room, the four treads, the wash rooms, the school corner's mouth and the blank line outside it, and says that is deliberate.**
- **A finished plan in a coat.** One document in four parts, two in the kitchen and two in a school store under a shelf, in Ilsa Norr's hand and then Mikka Hale's, **kept by Bel Ordry in his coat.** Nine rows: a count with no name anywhere; a condition with no number and no day; **a third column with eight of nine lines filled and one empty, which the keeper will not name**; and a **fourth column empty, with no heading, and a reason on the page in Renn Vale's hand with a modal struck out of it and replaced in Nera Oduya's.** No name on the page. A date in the top corner. It does not leave the building.
- **A boundary that can be left alone.** The crew's maintenance obligation is **discharged, out loud, in the room, on a sentence, with the reason.** Nobody left and nobody was asked to.

## The material position

- **A frame a finger and a bit out of true, holding and not fixed.** The anteroom's outer service door, packed with stone and clay because there is no mortar of the right kind in this Hearthhold and **no timber is coming while the boundary holds the charter it has signed.** Shored. The anteroom's rating depends on **a word said to a room and repeated by the clerk and not said to a doorframe.** The busiest room in the building has a second way out that is a rule.
- **A two-jar seam.** Two jars a day, maybe three, out of a lime-mortar joint in the haunch behind the anteroom's own outer service door. Warm under the hand. **The only drinking water this boundary has found.** It is in the pot and it is **entered separately and never added to the pot.**
- **Seven sound wedges and two dead sticks.** The third wedge at the waist is up a quarter of an inch and a bit. The ground at the waist has moved three times and is not measured, by three people, on purpose.
- **A person under a lip**, packed and not held. The post under it has never been built. Jo Verrick's packing is a knee.
- **Four households at the second gate with nothing**, and a keeper who has promised to say so in person at the counter before the first of the month.

## The political position

- **The Cartwright Guilds are competing, not defeated and not wicked.** A **halfpenny sheet** at the middle gate's store, a hundred copies, **timber in the month after next**, the fourth paragraph verbatim, **three other Hearthholds expected to accept, three of which will have timber and will be able to shut a route when a clerk at a counter says so and will think they are being careful**, and this boundary cited in a file. A competent bounded officer, **Harlan Merrow**, went up a road with a recommendation he does not expect to be granted. **The fourth paragraph has been refused three times** and it is a door with a year's price on it.
- **Nobody is hostile to anybody yet.** There is no surface inspector in this building, no Compact order, and no office that has treated an underground resident as a subject. **The first time somebody from the surface asks this boundary a question it cannot answer, nothing will have prepared anyone, because the charter was written for one office and there is more than one office.**

## Where the money, the power and the pressure sit

- **Nera Oduya is a collaborator, not a dependent, and she is still here.** She has refused to certify the stair's air for eighteen weeks, keeps a watch on it, and **still refuses to publish a rate.** She is the only person who may stop the air gate. She asked the volume's last question out loud and refused to answer it.
- **Talla Roke is outside the building**, running a courier route with three named people of her own, and the boundary's contribution to it is **two sentences with no name and no signature.** She is not a rescue. **The spring is owed by nobody.**
- **Iven Tallo has a suspended bond, an apprentice off the roll, and a charter he did not sign**, with the real reason given on a bench to one man and entered nowhere else.
- **Wen Pask is paid** — food, a bed, and hours entered as work at the cook's own rate, **the first wage this Hearthhold has ever paid anybody** — and **thirty-five days and thirty-five nights into a count** in a column with no heading. He is not a carpenter and being kept is not being trained.
- **Tams Quill's board stands at fifty-two and a half for the crew and a hundred and seventeen and a quarter for the boundary, and both reconcile exactly.** The crew's figure is **a quantity of asking, not a quantity of engineering.** It is not going to move by itself.

# Where this departs from `outline/series.md`, and why the departure serves the ending

**The series outline's Volume 03 starting state is: *the public charter is fragile and the relationship between surface regulators and underground residents is openly hostile.* Neither half is true of the delivered Volume 02, and Volume 03 cannot pretend both.**

### Departure 1 — the charter is not fragile; it is untested

**Delivered:** a charter with seven signatures, no name on it, a written reason for having no name, and an office that can neither grant nor refuse it. It has survived a competent counter-draft and a signed refusal and a halfpenny sheet.

**Why this is better and why it serves the ending:** a fragile charter would make Volume 03 a fight to keep a document. **A strong charter with a hole in it is a better volume.** The hole is real and it is not a flaw of the drafting: **clause four says the office may see the plan when the office asks, in a room, to a person. It is written for one office. Volume 03 brings a second institution, the Stonelight Compact, and the charter does not cover it.** The boundary has to write a procedure for a stranger it never wrote one for, and any procedure it writes is a document a stranger can use. That is the toll gate, opening, four days up the road, in a form nobody planned.

**The ending needs this.** `outline/ending.md` requires that essential routes and records be public without being captured, that residents be able to withdraw a dangerous project through a council process, and that a route be a public good across jurisdictions. A charter that is strong at home and silent about everybody else is exactly the shape of the problem Morrowlight has to solve at the end. **Volume 03 should not fix the hole. It should make the hole visible and leave a second sheet with a different body to argue about.**

### Departure 2 — there is no open hostility, and the volume is better for starting without it

**Delivered:** no surface regulator has ever been in this building. The only offices the boundary has met were competent, and two of them conceded and one of them recommended a charter he did not expect to be granted.

**Why this is better:** hostility is a state, and a state cannot escalate. **What escalates is a question.** The first Stonelight inspector is competent, and her office is competent, and both of them are going to be right about a thing. The volume's escalation is therefore not *the surface attacks the underground* but **the boundary discovers that every correct answer it can give is also a description of itself that can be carried off the hill.** That is a quieter, harder, and more legible escalation, and it is the same escalation the ending needs, because the ending is not about defeating hostility. It is about a city that can be inspected and still be answerable to the people in it.

### Departure 3 — the pressure is not "a fragile charter" but "a working one that is cheaper to keep than to extend"

**Delivered:** a boundary with a plan, a charter, a two-jar seam and no timber, which is a boundary that **can** keep itself. The central pressure committed to Volume 03 is that a series of *safe* closures saves structures while trapping people, and that the Hearthholds must decide whether to open dangerous routes. **Those two fit each other exactly, and they fit the delivered starting state much better than they fit a fragile charter**: a boundary that can just about keep itself has a very strong incentive to keep doing the cheap thing, and the cheap thing is a closure.

**Why this serves the ending:** `outline/ending.md`'s final image is a resident crew opening a market gate by hand while another closes a ventilation shutter on schedule. **That is the argument of Volume 03 in advance.** The volume's job is to make the reader understand why closing the shutter is *the correct professional instinct* and why it is *not enough*, so that the ending's two ordinary workers doing two ordinary things land as an achievement instead of a slogan.

### Departure 4 — the volume opens away from the boundary, not deeper into it

**Delivered:** the crew's obligation is discharged, the five are in the building as something other than a debt, and they are **not on the rota and cannot stand a watch.**

**Why this serves the ending:** Marek's flaw is control disguised as responsibility, and `outline/ending.md` requires that he learn to accept that a person who will not carry a load is not a deserter. **A Volume 03 in which the crew drifts outward — one to a counter, one to a second gate, one to a surface line, one to a schoolroom floor, one to a tally board nobody can head — is the volume in which he discovers that the five people he is responsible for have stopped needing him to be in the room.** It is also the volume in which the *boundary* needs the crew, for the first time, in a way it can name, and the crew can say no, and does, and then comes anyway on terms.

### What is *not* departed from

The central pressure, the four major locations, the four major factions, the midpoint reversal, the escalation, the climax, the concrete resolution, the next-volume question, and the power/relationship change are all delivered as committed. **The departures are to the starting state and to the order in which things happen. They do not touch the volume's destination.**

# Major locations

**All four are working locations, used for work, not toured. None is a set piece and none is a spectacle.**

### 1. The flood-prone school

**It is this boundary's own school, and it is flood-prone because of the boundary's own success.** The school corner is at the school-door end of the corridor, next to the anteroom, and it has **a shut, warm mouth in it that is going into the throat rather than out of it and has never been certified.** The anteroom's haunch has a lime-mortar seam in it with four threads of water in it, and that seam is being drunk from. **Nine children are sitting next to the only water this Hearthhold has.** The volume's first movement is that the school has to move, and the school has nowhere to move to except **down the corridor, past the dip and the waist, to the kitchen-line end** — which puts nine children and a schoolteacher onto a fill that has moved three times and is not measured, on purpose, by three people.

**The school is the volume's consent problem in its most ordinary form.** It runs three days in seven. It has run one day in a worse room. It has lost days. It has a count column with no name in it. **It is the thing a document can be a claim about.**

### 2. The sealed archive

**Not a treasure room and not a revelation. A record room at Hearthhold Middle**, sealed on the outside by a guild inventory and on the inside by the Middle's own route keeper, holding administrative records of rooms. **It is sealed by other people, which is the point.** The boundary is allowed to send **one question, in a room, to a person**, and the answer it gets is a fact about a room and not a cause. That fact is the volume's only concrete advance on *why do the Lattice's rooms remember domestic routines*, and it is a fact a clerk could have had in nine hundred years.

**The archive may not answer the central mystery, may not contain the Civic Loom's diagrams, may not mention the One-Seat, and may not be where Alden is.** Volume 06 is *The Wardens' Split* and Volume 10 is *The Archive of Rooms*; those volumes own the deep records and the One-Seat. **Volume 03's archive is an administrative filing room, and the horror in it is administrative.**

### 3. The market route

**The road between this boundary and Hearthhold Middle, and it is the volume's toll gate.** A market route is where a public route is used by people who do not live on it. The gate question is the one Nera Oduya named in Chapter 100: *a halfpenny sheet, a clay packing that shrinks, a word that has to be said in a room, and a frame that goes a finger a week — that is how a door becomes a toll gate.* **Not a gate with a man at it. A gate with a rule at it.**

The market route's conflict is not smuggling and not crime. It is that the rules for using it were written for people who live here, and the people who come to it are not the people the rules were written for, and **the first toll on that route is a form.** The market route is where Talla Roke's courier sheet meets the boundary's charter and where a *mark on a paper* becomes a claim about a person.

### 4. The surface inspection line above the Cinder Steps

**A line of chalked stations and a permit on the surface, above the sector where Volume 01 happened.** A Stonelight Compact route inspector works it. She is competent, she is bounded, she is not a villain, and **she is not the Cartwright office, so clause four of the charter does not cover her and there is no procedure.**

This is the location where the volume's second half of the departure pays out. It is also where **the stair becomes visible to a stranger for the first time** — not entered, not walked, not opened. Form 9-C stands unattended and the stair is not a route under it. **Nobody goes down the four treads and nobody will be.**

# Midpoint reversal — Chapters 123–126

**Committed:** *Marek seals a leaking room to save its structure and accidentally cuts off the only ventilation and exit for a group of residents.*

**What is delivered, and it is the bible's own worked example.** `bible/power-system.md`, under *Route change*, gives the failure case in one line: *a sealed door saves a leaking room from water but traps six people and cuts off the only ventilation shaft.* Volume 03 spends twenty-five chapters making that sentence expensive.

**The shape of it:**

1. **The room is leaking and the leak is going where it should not.** A room the boundary depends on — not the anteroom, the anteroom is too familiar; **the room the school is in, or the room the seam's water is being carried through.** The water is real, the structure is real, and there is **no timber, no mortar of the right kind, and no second pair of hands**, so the available actions are: lose the room, or close it.
2. **Marek chooses to close it, and he is right.** He says out loud what the alternative is and how many people are in the other room, and he is a competent engineer and the calculation is not wrong. He names the number of people at risk and it is higher than the number behind the door. **He does not decide this in a hurry and nobody accuses him of deciding it in a hurry, and that is what makes it a reversal and not a mistake.**
3. **He does not have the power to do it alone and he is not asked to.** The door is closed by the person who keeps the room, on his recommendation, and the person who keeps the room **is not a person who is going to be blamed for it later.** This is the working practice and the volume's argument in one mechanic: *any change to a room people use is written by the person who will keep it.* The reversal is not that he does something unauthorised. **The reversal is that the system worked exactly as designed and produced a trapped room.**
4. **The room holds. The structure is saved. The ventilation and the only exit are gone.** And **the boundary's own plan, in its own first column, has a count for that room, and the count does not say when anybody is inside it.** The anteroom's rating depends on a word said to a page. The sealed room's rating depends on a word that is not going to be said, because the person in the room cannot reach the page.
5. **The cost is people, and it is counted honestly.** Not a death by ambush. **A counted number of people who were behind a door that a room said was safe**, and at least one of them is a child, and Mero Senn's rule — *a plan without a named injured person is not a plan* — is the volume's instrument. **Mero Senn appears in this volume.** He is owed since Volume 01 and his arrival is the reversal's aftermath, not a rescue.

**The reversal's public nature is the power change.** `outline/series.md` requires that his **first public failure is not hidden.** It is therefore read out in a room, in his own words, with the number in it, and **the boundary does not forgive him and does not blame him**, and the plan gets a line in the third column in the hand of the person who kept the room, and the fourth column stays empty with its reason.

# The Marker stage — owed here and nowhere else

`bible/power-system.md` is the authority. **Marker: place a limited temporary bearing mark, with a visible target and bodily strain.**

## What it is

A short, deliberate inscription — **chalk, grease, or a carved anchorstone groove** — that tells a rule-line which way to carry a **limited** force. **One mark has one direction and one stated limit.** Its duration is until the load changes beyond the mark's condition, the material fails, or a stronger pattern overwrites it.

## What it is not, and this volume must not cross the line

- **It is not a route change.** A route change alters a door, shaft, stair, vent or boundary so that people, air or water move differently, and it requires a bearing plan, an air plan, an exit and a receiving space. **A mark tells a seam where to put a limited force. It does not decide where anybody goes.** No chapter in Volume 03 may have a bearing mark be the reason a door opens or a stair is used.
- **It is not a brace.** A brace is a physical support joined to a bearing mark. **Volume 03 has no brace.** Jo Verrick shoring the anteroom frame is stone against stone, lime and a wedge, and it is not a brace and it is not a mark and it is holding and not fixed.
- **It is not a binding, a relay, or a district-scale act.** Those come in Volumes 06, 07 and beyond.
- **It is not a cure and it is not a solution.** It cannot repair a missing support, purify air, or create a door where the shell cannot carry one.

## Where it arrives, and out of what

**Chapter 112, in Batch 0002, on the anteroom's outer service door frame.** It arrives out of the work, because the work has run out of ordinary means:

- **There is no timber.** No sound timber, no lath, no bronze, and **none is coming while the boundary holds the charter it has signed.**
- **There is no mortar of the right kind.** The packing is stone in clay, and clay shrinks as it dries, and the frame is a finger and a bit out of true after eleven days, and it is going a finger a week in the cold.
- **There are seven sound wedges and two dead sticks, one of the seven is in the frame, and a different one is in the waist.**
- **And Marek Venn's right hand will not close.** The left hand closed properly the morning of the fourth day of the nineteenth week and did not close that night, and it has carried every honest piece of work in this building for four months, and in Chapter 112 it is the hand that is used and it is the hand that burns.

**It is not granted, requested, or asked for.** It is arrived at by a man who has said in Chapter 99 that anybody who comes and asks him to read a boundary will be told no, and who then has to admit in a room that he is out of ordinary answers and that there is one thing left that is not a reading.

## What it costs — all four costs, on the page

1. **A visible mark that can be targeted.** Someone outside the building sees it and asks what it is. **The answer is a condition and a limit and no name**, and the asking is by a competent person doing her job, and the mark is now a thing a stranger holds a description of. **The boundary's own standing rule is that a document about a room travels, and this is a mark on a stone, and it is worse, because a document is asked for and a mark is simply seen.**
2. **Bodyly strain.** Burning in the hand. The hand does not close that night and does not close the next morning either. The anteroom's count arrives as a sound again, and this time he knows what it is, and knowing does not stop it. **A load echo in the body**, which is injury and diagnostic information and not a meter.
3. **The obligation to state the mark's limit.** From `bible/power-system.md`: the cost of the stage includes **the obligation to state the mark's limit.** That means a person has to say out loud, in a room, in words, what condition stops this thing, and **the room's own doctrine says a condition must not be a date with a number on it, because a condition you can satisfy is a date with a number on it.** So the limit is written as a condition by the person who will keep it, and it is falsifiable, and it will be met.

# Volume climax — Chapters 144–147

**Committed:** *he designs a multi-room evacuation network and stays behind to open a secondary route while Nera and Talla move people.*
