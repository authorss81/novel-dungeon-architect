# Chapter 154 — The Reaction Goes On The Schoolroom Wall

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the thirtieth week, a Thursday, and the school did not run, and a man read four sentences out of a book in an anteroom and those four sentences were the whole of what a brace needs, and the room found out that this Hearthhold has three of the four.**

**It goes on the wall the schoolroom leans on, because the other place in this building that could take the push is the lowest point in it, and nobody is to be sent anywhere to make that true, and the two available answers are both bad and one of them is a wall nine hundred years old with a cut in its floor.**

Nera Oduya said the thing about the air before the man on the step could, and she said it from the second step, and she had been up there since the first bell with the school corner's mouth at her back.

"**The water went off that fill on Tuesday afternoon and it has not been back and it is not coming back today, and I have been watching it since Tuesday and I am not going to say what I think it means, because I do not know and I have a name for not knowing and it is not *the water is fine*.**"

"**What I will say is the part that is mine. The air at the low end of that corridor goes down the channel before the water does, and that has been true for thirty weeks, and if the water is not in the channel then the air is not being pushed, and if the air is not being pushed then the thing that has been keeping the ninth hearth stone's bed at a hand and a half below its stone is not the water.**"

"**And the ninth hearth stone is propped on a strip of crate board and two flags and that is not a repair. It is a fortnight that became a month that became ten weeks of a hole not getting worse, and I would like somebody in this room to say that out loud, and I am not going to be the somebody because I have said it four times and it has got no better saying it a fifth.**"

It went unspoken. Then Mikka Hale said it, from the kitchen-line, in one sentence, in the voice she uses for the fire.

"**A hole under one of nine stones that nine children sit on, and a fire in the corner of that room that has not been lit in ten weeks, and the two are not the same thing and the second one is mine and the first one is not.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The rest of it is that if the water is not doing it then the nine are doing it, and they are nine and they are in there three mornings in seven, and I have been the woman who will not light a fire in that room for ten weeks and I am not going to be the woman who says the nine are the reason a stone is low. I am going to keep not lighting the fire and I am going to say the thing I have not said, which is that I do not know what to do and I have not known since Tuesday.**"

That sat in the room for a while, and then Marek Venn stood up off the anteroom bench, which he has not done to speak in a month, and read four sentences out of Jo Verrick's copy of a survey in a stiff cover, and said whose they were.

"**These are not mine. They are out of a book a survey camp wrote in the twenty-second week and they are four sentences long and they are in here because a man I have never met wrote them about this building and a clerk read them out in this room, and I have carried the book up nine steps this morning.**"

"**A brace is a physical support joined to a bearing mark. That is the first sentence and it is a definition and I am not going to improve on it.**"

"**It needs joinwood, timber, stone, cable, or another tested support. That is the second. It needs a counter-bearing. That is the third. And it needs time to let the load settle. That is the fourth.**"

"**That is the whole of it and there is nothing in those four sentences about anybody's name, and I have written them on a board in my life and I have never once had a use for them, and I have had a use for them since Monday.**"

"**And this Hearthhold has got the first one and half of the second. We have stone. We have clay. We have two beech lengths in a school store and seven sound wedges and one dead stick, and none of that is joinwood, and a wedge is not joinwood, and a wedge in a door is the only reason that door does what it does.**"

"**We have got a mark, and I am coming to that, and it is the fourth thing and it is the one I cannot do.**"

"**We have not got time. Nobody in this building has got time. We have a pot at ten days and a seam that gave four threads this week where it gave eight in the nineteenth and a floor that nine children sit on. Time is the one thing in those four sentences that this building cannot buy and cannot beg and cannot argue a woman out of, and it is the one that is going to decide this, and not the wood.**"

"**Say the third one,**" said Dalla Sen. "**Say the counter-bearing, because you have gone round it twice and everybody in this room knows what you are doing.**"

"**Two places in this building can take a push,**" he said. "**That is not an opinion. That is what a counter-bearing is. It is somewhere the push can go when it comes out the other side of the thing you are holding, and if there is nowhere for it to go then the thing you are holding is not being held, it is being stored, and stored is a word for a different failure.**"

"**And I have been in every room of this building that a person can be in, and there are two.**"

He said the first one and Dalla Sen read it out of her own book before he finished saying it, and she read it out because it is hers and not his, and her voice did not change and the room knew what it meant.

"**Somebody stands at the low end of the corridor,**" she said, "**and the page has been blank since the fifteenth week and there are three conditions on it and the third of them says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and the floor is not to be approached, and nobody has been asked whether the conditions are met, and nobody is going to be, and that has been the rule for thirty weeks and I have never once seen anybody in this building argue with it including the man who is talking.**"

"**A brace that takes its reaction at the lowest point in a building is a brace that puts a person there,**" he said. "**Not necessarily a person. A person. Because the only way a brace reacts at the low end is by a body, a jack, or a stack of something a person carried and set up and then went and stood next to. And a person is a post. And a post is what a blank page becomes when somebody fills it in.**"

"**And I said in a room ten days ago that I am not a post and no page is going to get my name on it, and I am not going to have myself be the counter-bearing to a thing I cannot see the limit of, and there is a second reason and the second reason is that I cannot go down there again. I said that in a room in the twenty-ninth week and I do not know why and I am not going to find out, and I am not going to stand at the bottom of the lowest point in this building and pretend a fortnight has changed it.**"

"**So there is one place left, and I am going to say it, and then four people in this room are going to tell me what it is, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said the one place before anybody else did and that I did not have to.**"

"**The party wall. The one the schoolroom leans on.**"

Nobody said anything for the length of two strokes of the pump, and then Jo Verrick put the lamp down on the anteroom table, where the wet had been on it a month ago and was not on it now, and said the thing that made the brace possible and the reason nobody had come back in a fortnight in the same breath.

"**A bad repair on a sound wall.**"

"**That is what the survey said. Fourth page. A frame that is a bad repair on a sound wall, in their words, and they were right, and the frame has been a finger and a bit out of true for a month and it is a finger and three-quarters today and I have measured it and I am not going to say so out loud except that I have, because I am not going to hold that back either.**"

"**And the wall behind the frame is the party wall, and the party wall is this building's spine, and the schoolroom is on the other side of it, and nine children sit on nine hearth stones on the other side of it, and the room that leans on it has leaned on it since before there was a word for a school in it.**"

"**And the same sheet, fourth page, the last line, and I want it read out in this room because I have read it four times in a month and I am tired of that page and I would like a room to be tired of it with me.**"

Dalla Sen read it out of the book, and it was a line in a surveyor's own hand, and it was a good fact and a bad fact in the same sentence, and the man who put them there had said so and said he would do it again.

"**Where a sector is found sound, no further crew is put in it until it is found otherwise.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Jo Verrick. "**Enter that a man told this room in the twenty-second week that this sector was stable and that he was right, and that a stable sector gets no crew, and that a wall that is a bad repair on a sound wall is the single reason a brace can go into this building at all, and that it is also the single reason there has been nobody up that passage for a fortnight.**"

"**Those are the same sentence. I have known that since the twenty-second week and I have not said it to a room, and I am saying it now because this room has been given a piece of wood and a surveyor's sentence in the same week and somebody in here is going to try and keep them apart and they will not come apart.**"

Then Iven Tallo said the thing about the mark, and he said it as a carpenter and then as something else, and it was the fourth of the four sentences.

"**You want a bearing mark and you do not have one and I have told you twice I would not put one on a jamb and I am not going to change that.**"

"**So I went up nine steps this morning and looked at the lintel over that school corner's mouth, because it is the only dressed stone in this building with a mark cut in it, and there are four of them and a groove under them, and I have looked at them three times in a month and I am a carpenter and I have a mason's opinion and the mason's opinion is worth nothing and I am going to give it anyway because nobody else in this building has one.**"

"**Four marks and a groove. One of the four is two strokes, and Renn read one of the four as two strokes a month ago and said the fifth stroke is a stroke and not a stop, and you read that stroke as a stop or as the middle of a letter with no letter, and I have read the second stroke of the pair as the beginning of a cut that never got finished, and that is three readings and I do not think one of us is wrong, and I do not think any of us has got it.**"

"**The groove is not damaged. That is the thing I want in a room. Everything else up there has been handled by nine hundred years of hands and air and somebody's boot, and the groove has not, and a man who has spent his life on wood and stone will tell you that the one undamaged mark on a wall is the one that was cut to be read, and not the one that was cut to be a picture.**"

"**And here is what I can say and it is the whole of what I have.** Anchorstone holds a mark. That is true and it is a sentence in a book and it has been in this building since the twenty-second week and one sentence long. It holds a mark. **It does not say what the mark is for and it does not say when the mark stops and it has not said either of those things to any of the three of us who have looked at it in a month.**"

"**So the limit is not coming out of that stone,**" said Marek Venn.

"**No. And I am a carpenter and I am telling you that about a stone, and I am telling you that I would rather build a thing that does not need a limit from that stone, and I do not think I am going to get that, and I am not going to let anybody in this room pretend that my opinion about a groove makes it safer.**"

Renn Vale said one thing from the landing before the anteroom moved on, and she said it in the flat voice she uses for a term, and it was about herself and not about the wall.

"**I have been on that landing for thirty-one weeks and I have shut a corridor three days in seven and opened it twice on days the arrangement shuts it, and I have never once had to decide anything about a wall. I am going to have to decide something about a wall in this building and I would like a room to know that I have not practised it.**"

Then Tams Quill said it, and he said it in a room with nine people in it and he said it once and Dalla Sen entered it and he has not improved on it since.

"**The column is not headed. Ninth batch, and this is the reason, and I gave it on Thursday because I said I would give it on Thursday and Dalla has that in her hand.**"

"**A heading over that column is how a room finds out what the person in it is for.**"

"**That is the ninth one and the eight before it were about words, and about a hearth stone, and about a wage, and about a heading is a place to keep the person who works under it, and every one of those was a reason I could give a room and this one is the first one that is not about me at all.**"

"**Eleven of the sixteen entries on that board are nulls. A null is a reading that found nothing, and a null is a thing somebody in this building was paid to go and look for and did not find, and I have said for a month that under a heading they stop being eleven nothings and become eleven findings, and a finding is a thing a person is responsible for. That is the seventh reason and it is still true and it is not this one.**"

"**This is the ninth reason. There are rooms in this building that have never been asked what the person in them is for. The anteroom. The landing. The corridor. The store that a man keeps four days up a road. And this one.**"

"**A heading says a thing is for a job. And the moment a heading says that, a room that has never been asked what the person in it is for becomes a room where the person in it can be asked what they are for by somebody who has read the heading. And that somebody does not have to be anybody in particular. It can be anybody. That is what a heading is for.**"

"**I am not going to put a word over this column. And I am not going to give a tenth reason, and if the day comes when the reason costs somebody something then it is a piece of chalk and I have said that.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in his words, and enter that it is the ninth, and enter that he gave it on the fourth day of the thirtieth week, and enter that the count of reasons has gone up and the count of columns that have a heading on them has not.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the board was walked, and the reason she gave for not reading the figures was that she had found out before lunch what a brace is.**

"**Three today and a hundred and seventy-seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and ninety-four and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks. The crew are at sixty-six and a half.**"

"**I entered those and I did not read them, and I am not going to give you dignity as the reason, because I gave that reason in the twenty-ninth week and a man from another office read it in my book and did not say a kind thing about it.**"

"**The real reason is that at the first bell this morning a man stood up off a bench and read four sentences out of a survey and at the fourth bell a woman said the two places a brace can push are the lowest point in this building and a wall nine hundred years old, and I have spent thirty weeks being a clerk who writes down the arithmetic of a building, and this afternoon the arithmetic of this building had a shape I had not seen in it before, and I am not going to read a number out loud in the same breath as that.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two and the seam gave one on Tuesday and gave two today and I am not going to say anything about what that means because Jo has said she does not know and a cook has said she does not know and a man on the step has said he is not going to say which of two prices he would pay. There are three not-knowings in this book today and I have entered all three and I have not put a reason beside any of them.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-nine days and eighty-nine nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be ninety, and I have said in five weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and nobody has thanked me for the fifth week either.**"

"**The board was walked because a man gave a reason in a room and a board is the thing that a reason like that belongs in, and Tams read both columns and named no day and did not improve on the seventh reason because the ninth is bigger and he has said so.**"

---

**On the fifth day of this week a man of forty-nine is going to come down the middle passage, and a carpenter has told him to bring a keeper, and a room in this building has found out that it does not have a counter-bearing and has one.**

**There is a wall at the kitchen-line end of a corridor with a cut in its floor made with stone and no bar, a sill packed in clay that is the only thing holding it up, a strip of crate board under a stone, and nine children who are not to be told that the water has stopped coming.**

**On the lintel over that room's mouth there are four marks and one groove, and three people have read them three ways this month, and the only undamaged thing on that stone is the one that was cut to be read, and it has not said anybody the limit yet.**
