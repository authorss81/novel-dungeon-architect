# Chapter Summaries

## Chapter 1 — The Inspection

Marek refuses to sign Public Works Form 7-B after finding a mineral pressure seam absent from the East Spur survey. The real tunnel collapse sends all six opening-party members below; Leena dislocates her right shoulder while pulling Pell clear. The party loses most standard gear, and Marek chooses rescue and inventory over completing the paperwork.

## Chapter 2 — The Room Below

Marek counts all six survivors, reduces Leena's right shoulder, and orders two of the eight recovered liters opened for the first watch, leaving six clean liters. The group builds a six-person, two-hour lean-to with one exit after Marek admits that a brief pressure impression is relative and unreliable. Iven discovers a Cartwright inspection route mark on a sealed service door.

## Chapter 3 — The Cartwright's Claim

The group spends one liter over a half-day watch, leaving five liters from the collapse. Iven explains that the Cartwright seal is limited by survey date, occupancy, and conditions, while the posted one-operator rule makes the route a legal and political claim. Marek records a public refusal to use the door because its vent is shared with the occupied lean-to. Water begins moving uphill through a mineral pressure seam.

## Chapter 4 — Water Against the Grade

The group identifies a reciprocating sump pump with a shattered bronze gland and missing drive-dog key. Marek uses the wall as a receiving support and sacrifices the last clean cloth and damaged hand-tool kit to wedge the flood gate, explicitly calling it a delay rather than a repair. A brief load impression remains unreliable. A higher dry room with six pads and a blocked ventilation grille is exposed as the water rises.

## Chapter 5 — A Room That Looks Safe

Marek tests the apparent school refuge at six occupants, one litter, and up to 120 kilograms of gear. Bearing, breath, drainage, escape, and change all fail. During a controlled breach, the screw jack kicks and traps Marek's right hand, causing permanent fine-control loss. The crew abandons bulk supplies and opens a receiving path into the domestic room. Marek can no longer keep a turning pencil line steady enough to begin copying the plan, so the next job requires another pair of hands.

## Chapter 6 — The Kitchen That Was Never a Vault

The group recognizes domestic use rather than treasure storage, verifies and seals one liter of condensation water, and finds an evacuation map. With Iven holding the maintenance sheet flat, Marek makes the batch's first sustained map-copying adaptation: he draws slowly with his left hand and leaves the uneven line visible. They borrow two panels from the Hearthhold case and formally disclaim a claim to it. Oren and Tams begin ordinary rest and litter rotations without waiting for orders, and the map points toward a hidden plate.

## Chapter 7 — The Empty Symbol

Marek examines an old gray-green plate bearing an empty central seat inside many support lines. The brass rule reacts with heat, vibration, doubled sound, and a new hairline crack; Marek admits he cannot map the result. The plate is carefully removed and wrapped as evidence. Sefu Aran, an elected Hearthhold boundary route keeper, answers the air-keeper signal but withholds admission.

## Chapter 8 — The Stair That Refuses

The group studies a continuous returning-stair mechanism: a movable slab crosses a visible throat under pressure, with a rope and counterweight moving it. A dead pack does not trigger the return, but a walking person does. Pell's left ankle twists when a step settles. Marek refuses to send Leena's litter onto the unresolved stair and chooses the physically continuous service bypass toward the pump and air gate.

## Chapter 9 — The First Short Route

Nera Oduya rejects Marek's recorded Chapter 5 air plan because one shaft serves supply and exhaust; Sefu separately controls civic admission. Nera supplies a split gland seal and drive-dog key. Iven and Oren install them, the crew runs 20 counted no-load strokes and a five-minute draw-down, and the sump drops fifteen centimeters with a dry gland, unchanged air cloth, and dry flood gate. The delivery check chatters, so the pump is attended and temporary. The group reaches the Hearthhold anteroom without crossing into the deeper homes. A brief Nera viewpoint shift closes the chapter as Renn Vale, Dalla Sen, Jo Verrick, and Mikka Hale volunteer for a later room test whose exact terms remain unwritten.

## Chapter 10 — Who Owns the Door?

A Cartwright courier brings an outdated clearance seal, an old inspection route charter, a salvage lien, an insurance notice, and Dael Harrow's temporary route-custody order. The instruments remain distinct, and the order locks only the guild-controlled door and equipment. Iven lays the wrapped plate on a work cloth, then signs a bounded maintenance sheet rather than a custody transfer. With Renn Vale, Dalla Sen, Jo Verrick, and Mikka Hale participating as volunteers, a twelve-occupant anteroom test (eleven adults and fourteen-year-old Pell), one stretcher, and 120 kilograms of gear passes conditionally for thirty minutes. Only after the evidence is recorded do Nera and Sefu set a working rating of ten people including one stretcher for no more than two hours under named stop and evacuation conditions. Marek signs a limited non-claim receipt. Sefu permits attended anteroom use but withholds admission beyond the gate, leaving a conditional Hearthhold route rather than a public solution.

## Chapter 11 — The Room That Holds the Terms

The pump’s uneven delivery check and Leena’s need for a dry recovery place turn the successful anteroom test into an ordinary maintenance problem. Sefu, Nera, and the four test residents separate air approval, civic admission, and resident consent; the courier tries to turn continuing work into custody. Dalla creates a route record with named work, limits, and stop authority. After a fresh ten-person working interval begins, the residents request a named maintenance shift and a correctable record before deeper admission.

## Chapter 12 — The Work That Keeps Water Down

Jo Verrick takes a trial pump shift under Iven’s instruction. Oren and Tams carry the frame, Pell calls the water mark on the marked stone below, and Nera corrects Marek’s attempt to close the outer service door while the well is open. Jo refuses to let the frame be raised on a packed foot, so Marek revises the plan in public. They clean sediment from the delivery check without raising the pump frame, leave the bulk tools recorded in the lower cache, and finish a resident-maintained shift. Jo’s deferred tool-route work becomes a named cost: one half-day of ordinary labor per cleaning until payment or rotation is decided.

## Chapter 13 — A Charter Is Not a Signature

The old inspection charter is shown not to cover the service shaft or Hearthhold gate. Sefu explains room-by-room refusal, Nera limits air certification, and Iven distinguishes the guild’s frozen door and bond from local pump work. Marek wants a clean agreement; residents insist on consent, withdrawal, labor cost, and the right to stop. Jo refuses the language of beneficiaries and claimants outright; Mikka refuses to let meals make the crew beneficiaries; Iven refuses authorship of the old craft. The resulting maintenance draft is useful but deliberately incomplete.

## Chapter 14 — The Hearthhold Beyond the Gate

A named work request opens a limited look at the common kitchen, clinic/school corner, and boundary workbench. Sefu sets two concrete limits — Iven does not sit at the Hearthhold table while his name is in the Marrowgate hearing, and Pell keeps off the low clinic return — and Mikka's objection that six outsiders do not become six residents is allowed to stand. Marek tries to turn the kitchen into a load diagram until Leena tells him he is calculating who is allowed to be a person and Mikka names a safe room as one where the pot does not block the clinic door. Jo has Iven explain and help make a new air-baffle retaining pin while she keeps the measurement. The crew gains one work period of work-area access, not citizenship or ownership.

## Chapter 15 — The Guild Line Is Closed

The courier leaves with the original guild instruments and closes the surface incline. Iven receives a suspended-bond receipt and explains his bounded maintenance work to Dalla and the residents, accepting that it may be reported. The courier also reminds the room that Form 7-B is still unsigned in Marrowgate and that the record names who answers for the collapse, keeping the crew's surface liability open. The crew inventories local stock, records the locked guild pressure dial against the Hearthholds' own air gauges, and decides not to raid the stores or pretend the locked door is open. A costly retreat toward the flooding Cinder Steps becomes a recorded possibility if local maintenance fails.

## Chapter 16 — The Workday at the Boundary

A workday without a crisis turns the route into routine labor, and Marek states that he wants to learn what a normal day looks like before anything is breaking. The residents and crew share food, keep the five-liter collapse supply and the one-liter reserve separate from Hearthhold water — with the kitchen's provision covering named work periods — repair tools, mend a basket, and keep ordinary schedules. Jo's own shutter route goes long, the pump interval closes short of target, and the real reason is written instead of a flattering one. Pell rejects being protected from every useful task and takes a tool-sorting and level-reading role. Leena corrects the work sheet instead of remaining only a patient. A seven-task work board appears, with a name for each task that comes off when the work changes, changing the outsiders' place without granting them a free residence.

## Chapter 17 — When the Air Path Changes

A wet mineral seam and returning-stair pressure cycle reverse the anteroom air while a fevered clinic resident, Isel Ferrow, cannot be evacuated. Nera orders the room cleared to the upper landing; Marek's load observation and her air plan complement each other, and a tempting bypass shortcut is rejected for two different physical reasons. Five standing watches become a new maintenance obligation — air valve, civic wheel, upper landing, clinic return, and the work-bell relay at the threshold — held separate from the three standing obligations attached to the old return route itself. The pump is limited to short attended intervals and the bypass hatch stays latched.

## Chapter 18 — The Stair in Someone’s House

The crew returns to the fixed first landing of the returning stair without crossing its slab. Marek names the three available choices aloud — map it, warn the rooms, close it — and chooses all three in order, with Sefu enforcing the closure. Renn supplies a local school/infirmary history that conflicts with the guild record without answering the missing receiver. Pell’s splinted ankle and Leena’s sling make a test crossing impossible or unjustified. The stair is black-corded, its visible mechanism and local history are recorded as testimony, and it is restricted pending repair, resident maintenance terms, and a safe evacuation plan.

## Chapter 19 — The Work Sheet Other Hands Could Read

Marek, Leena, Iven, Nera, Sefu, Dalla, Jo, Renn, Mikka, Oren, Tams, and Pell turn scattered notes into a legible interim work sheet, arguing it line by line rather than filing it. Iven talks the pump sequence aloud at the grate so Dalla can write it in her own hand, which is how the craft stops being his private property. Jo makes Nera separate "water at the sill" into a condition nobody may argue about, so no one has to prove they were right before they move; Sefu writes the consent, stop, and withdrawal conditions in the order a person leaving would find them. Leena's named clinic care period is staged on the page as care, not residence. Dalla records labor and payment uncertainty, and Mikka reports that Isel's fever broke. The sheet states that no pump runs overnight without a named watch, that withdrawal stops new work without trapping occupants, and that it is not a permanent charter. Jo then refuses to let anyone mark it as agreed while the delivery seat is still unopened, so the maintainers read their own sections back, Sefu and Nera sign only their own conditions, and the review line is drafted and left unmarked. The chapter ends on a legible draft that nobody has agreed to.

## Chapter 20 — A Route That Must Be Kept

The conditional route faces a stair-pressure turn and a more serious pump failure: the delivery seat is scored, the check has taken a set, and no spare bronze seat has been released. The group rejects forced deeper admission, considers a costly retreat toward the flooding Cinder Steps, and chooses a limited continuation through short attended intervals. Nera forces a written condition for an unfilled watch — an empty watch is a stopped shift, not a shorter one — and Jo catches the word *route* still serving as the subject of Marek's decision sentence after he crossed it out of the heading in Chapter 19, with Dalla copying the correction onto the pump sheet so the two records cannot drift. The drafted review line is copied beside the agreement. This is the batch's only signature event: Sefu reads the sheet aloud, Dalla records the reading time, and only then do the marks get made. The guild line remains closed, the stair remains restricted, the air gate remains conditional, and the six outsiders plus named Hearthhold maintainers accept the maintenance obligation and the second-work-bell review. The route is kept, not owned or made permanent.

## Chapter 21 — The Next Work-Bell

A complete stair-pressure turn empties the anteroom to the landing in four minutes with no instruction given, which the crew reads as evidence about people rather than about the stair; the service bypass hatch stays latched while Iven twice reaches for its lever. The named review falls due at the second work-bell. Water, air, labor, and consent are reviewed through a pump interval and a ledger rather than an argument. Marek proposes a larger room; Jo names the shutter-route cost of every pump cleaning, Iven asks who stands the lever in nine months, and Sefu states that a room a neighborhood must keep is not a room the first builder owns. Marek writes a second line recording a larger room for ordinary residents as *proposed, not agreed*, and Renn goes to fetch Ilsa Norr.

## Chapter 22 — A Room the Hearthholds Want

In a counted work period the crew walks the lower corridor, which turns out to be a working neck rather than a hallway. Mikka, Ilsa Norr, Jo Verrick, and Sefu each describe one real failure of it: a clinic that fills a corridor with basins, a school corner with nowhere to send nine children, a shutter repair with four households and nowhere to wait, and a stretcher route that is the same floor as the escape. Ilsa refuses the register boxes and refuses the gallery as a night room; Ruk Sella accepts daytime family use and refuses nights. Iven identifies the hearth corner as a hundred-year-old cook's decision, not a defect. Marek starts drawing a load diagram, Leena makes him say it in words, and Nera refuses to certify the air of a room nobody has entered. The chapter ends with two survey work periods and Nera's written line that the gallery's air is unknown until it has been made to fail.

## Chapter 23 — The Upper Gallery

The survey of the old school gallery reveals twenty-two paces of dry floor on a timber deck, six joists spanning the nine feet of the room's width, four posts standing in a kerb on top of the corridor's later infill wall, four iron hooks, three register boxes, painted arrows, and a hundred years of worn walking paths. Pell finds the hollow eleventh tread of the fixed stair and reads the floor paths without crossing the deck; Leena insists the stair is both a throat and an escape. Nera proves with a warmed hearth-stone that a standing person closes the low vents under the board they stand on, so a full room would be a sealed floor with one unknown shaft. Marek and Iven disagree about a bolted scarf in the middle of a moving floor; the longest sound length in the stores is six feet, so a nine-foot joist is two lengths and a scarf, which makes the splice unavoidable and the deck unbuildable honestly. The declared design is two levels with the corridor as the shared throat, and Dalla names four costs that must be paid before any full-room test is discussed.

## Chapter 24 — Before the Test

Preparation proves ordinary and difficult. Tams's arithmetic reduces the declared forty minutes to about thirty-four of pump, because the scored seat forces a six-minute rest with the water rising. The plan is written line by line and every line is broken by somebody standing behind it: Leena adds the litter's four minutes each way, Nera makes Dalla write *may not be used* beside the anteroom and states that nobody overrules a stop in the moment, and Jo makes her write that rule down in her own hand. Iven translates the craft into plumb lines, wedges, and drain covers and gives the authorship away. Dalla records labor in columns including an *owed* column. Mikka forces the test to stop for an actual pot and teaches that a declared occupancy is a photograph. The Hearthholds release **four** pieces of their standing timber and the whole lath bundle to the project, deferring the kitchen brace and the clinic sill, and Sefu insists it be recorded as a Hearthhold decision rather than a contribution; Dalla then enters the remaining **two as a held reserve, not a release**, on the ruling that a reserve is for the next thing already broken and not for a thing the crew has decided to build. Ilsa refuses to put any child on an untested floor and offers instead to call the class movement pattern. Renn refuses in advance to be told the gallery is safe.

## Chapter 25 — When the Gallery Loads

The Volume 01 midpoint reversal. The empty gallery passes an empty inspection in front of witnesses, then fails at minute twenty-six when a teacher calls an ordinary second class movement and thirty people walk thirty steps in the same direction. A post foot comes up out of its kerb, a parapet course falls eleven feet into the corridor and takes the school-door lintel with it, and the door's clear width drops from four feet to an unmeasured *about* two and a half, chalked up an hour later as two feet eight. A stair pressure turn removes the remaining margin, the water reaches the sill, and Nera personally authorizes an attended pump start under a stopped test. The crew evacuates the deck in single file at Ilsa's pace and shores the post feet, with Oren levering the first length home on his forearm and saying nothing about it. Dalla then opens the untouched timber reserve herself under a head that is still creeping and enters the word *reserve* and the time, at Jo's insistence that the record distinguish a reserve from a release. Everyone lives. The splice held; the unspliced joists beside the post line did not, which is the sentence the record keeps. Marek closes the gallery before anyone asks him to, and the Hearthholds' entire standing timber is gone. He twice refuses to read the empty-seat plate as a message.

## Chapter 26 — The Shared Corridor

The work is baskets. Every basket comes out of the corridor's dip, Oren finds that the cracked lintel dropped and turned so the jamb is now carrying a load it was never built for, Tams walks stone sacks in the pattern of a hurrying person, and the drained channel is cleared by hand under the fallen slab and given a lid that is explicitly not a repair. A new escape path is chalked on the kitchen side and taught person by person, because people remember a person and not a chalk line. Nera and Marek argue publicly about whether the corridor can have an occupancy or only a condition; he proposes a number and she refuses it, and Leena turns her refusal into a better plan: the corridor may be used only while the pump lever is moving. The sketch carries named limits, a sixth watch at the waist, and a new cost — Jo's shutter route closed for the season and the boundary bench unusable.

## Chapter 27 — Who Pays for the Room

Marek proposes one fund and Nera puts the dyed cloth on his column and dismantles it: a fund has no holder, no standing, and no authority, and she is not available to be bought by it. Dalla and Mikka settle the meals in Mikka's own words as given, not waived, and not a wage, on the grounds that a person who is owed cannot also be fed as a favor. Renn refuses the sixth watch on three days of seven, which means the corridor does not open on those days, and Sefu states that nobody in this building has the standing to bind a stranger to a maintenance obligation. Two pages are written: what everyone has spent, and what nobody can pay for from here, ending with the bronze seat, the missing timber, Iven's unpaid craft, the meals, the sealed liter, the unsigned form, and the only continuous way down, which is restricted. Jo writes her own loss in her own words and insists it not be softened.

## Chapter 28 — The Empty Upper Wing

Nine deck boards come up over the failed post line under Jo's direction and Iven's, revealing the open kerb and a grey furry growth at three bays that Marek's record deliberately does not name, because a record that says *dry rot* has promised the next reader more than the man who wrote it knew. The crew debates what to keep: Iven wants the shoring lengths left standing and marked, Oren says take them out, and Dalla insists the removal be recorded as removed for safety rather than recovered, so the guild's salvage lien cannot later call it stock. Marek then proposes keeping a bench, four feet of floor, and the hooks so the room is not simply lost — and Jo and Ilsa refuse him, because a maintained four feet becomes a watch becomes a shift becomes a room, and because a stranger has no standing to decide what a schoolroom is for. Marek withdraws it. The register boxes go to the school store under Renn's own hand, twelve sound boards reopen Jo's shutter route, the stair head is barricaded at the ninth tread with a slate saying what the room was not, four dates, and the word *no*, and Tams's stock count ends the chapter: no sound timber over six feet, no bronze seat, nine wedges, no lath.

## Chapter 29 — A Full Room, Again

The revised test wets the corridor with sump water on purpose, uses a fouled clinic litter that needs four bearers, and declares nine people including one litter for the pump's interval with six watches filled and the kitchen line as the only exit. It passes eleven minutes, then sediment tears at the delivery check and forces a proper forty-minute cleaning — Jo's fourth, paid out of a route that is already closed — and her stop now carries the one-sentence reason the plan requires. Partway through the second interval the stair's pressure grinds under the channel, Nera stops the test on a hand she opens before she trusts it, and the channel's water is found running **uphill toward the school end** when it has fallen to the pump gallery every day for six weeks. Nera's finding is that the corridor exhales while the pump runs and inhales from the stair when it stops. The test resumes, the outer edge of the boards comes three-eighths of an inch over the fixed chalk line at the waist and goes back, and the result is a bounded pass that is explicitly not a rating because it lasts only as long as a scored bronze seat. A brief marked Nera viewpoint shift closes the chapter: she realizes the one place she certified as safe to stand people is standing on a shaft she cannot see into, and writes it on the valve board before the shift ends.

## Chapter 30 — The Second Threshold

Nera's valve-board line is read first and it takes the word *room* out of everyone's mouth. The Hearthholds and the six-person crew accept an interim local decision: the anteroom and service bypass continue conditionally; the lower corridor takes nine including one litter only while the pump lever moves with six watches filled and the kitchen line as the way out; the school door is relief and not escape; the gallery is closed and barricaded; the returning stair, the guild lock, the plate, and the stair are all named and untouched. Renn takes the sixth watch on four days of seven and names the three she cannot, and says out loud that her refusal in Chapter 27 has not been withdrawn — what changed is that the woman in her rooms who was not walking has gone to the clinic return on Isel's arrangement, so for four days in seven she has nobody in a bed to choose against a stranger's wet floor. Ilsa accepts that her school corner becomes a waiting room on the other three days, at the school's cost and by her own decision. Leena gets four minutes written into the record as the real number. Iven states that he is not a defector and cannot be paid. The change is that Jo Verrick is now handed the work board, and any change to a room people use is written by the person who will keep it. The chapter closes on an ordinary afternoon: the pump's interval, Renn at the waist and the landing standing empty, the meal queue, a child asking about a chalk figure, the slate on the barricade, the plate under its cloth, and Marek picking up a wedge instead of a plan.

## Chapter 31 — The Third Work-Bell

A stair-pressure turn takes the morning and the third work-bell review gets the tired afternoon, and the interim page is not renewed. Jo refuses to read it, then refuses to renew the number nine, which was a test number and has become a capacity; the count is now whatever the people in the corridor are on the day. Isel Ferrow, who has been a name in the record as a fever since the seventeenth day, refuses the line that made his room the reason for the clinic return watch, and Mikka admits she wrote it and asks that her name be on the striking; the watch is for the door, for the door. Renn corrects "four days in seven" back into a list that is hers, and Ilsa's school corner stays a school corner on the days it is not a waiting room. Tams's count is unchanged: no sound timber over six feet, no bronze seat, no lath, nine wedges — four at the school-door head, three at the waist, two in the basket, the basket being the two you reach for when something happens. Dalla then asks the question the batch rests on — what does the room do on the day the pump stops for good — and Marek says plainly that he has no answer that does not involve the corded stair, which stays corded while they look for one.

## Chapter 32 — What the Route Costs Without One

The crew's four-line list cannot be started: no timber, no account, no hand. Tams reads the ledger and the two columns are the same length they were nine days ago, because nothing has been paid. Then a resident uses the crew's hands for something the crew did not propose: Ruk Sella's door hinge has sheared on the frame that came down with the gallery lintel, and the door hangs. Jo rehangs it out of a sound board released in writing by Mikka, and the door will close but will not stay shut against the wheel. Renn then refuses the order of the day, refuses the school corner's floor, and gives the account instead, at the cost of her mother's name in a room with a keeper in it. Ilsa opens a register box she has kept shut since the gallery removal, and the household returns say that in the ninth week of the long cold year the school gave away the lower landing stone, cut in three, to the wash door, on R. Vale senior's account and no survey — and that afterwards Renn's mother and two other women drove a wedge-shaped piece back into the socket under the lower receiving landing and called it a repair. The material question dies at once: the fallen parapet slab cannot be built on without becoming stock under the guild's salvage lien, and the pump gallery is an air space Nera maintains and nobody may sort stone in.

## Chapter 33 — The Lower Side of the Stair

A named attended look at the fixed first landing, near side only, on the corded side of the throat. Leena sets the stop-distance at four minutes because a litter cannot go down three steps, and the litter stops at the service bend. Nera's condition is that the stair-side grille stays shut and that the work runs on the cloth and not on a number. Pell is not sent and is given the most important job in the period, which is to call the dead window off the plinth, because he can hear the wheel coming about four seconds before the floor does anything. Marek touches the fixed rail and gets a fifth explicit null, written down on a slate Jo pockets without being asked. Inside, the hollow is a bearing and not a seat, and it holds two wears: a rounded running groove a hundred years old in the bottom, and a bright four-inch band of unwetted stone on the front face above it. Iven reads the guild's word *receiver* as a misreading: the lower side was a line-block for a counter-line, the counter-line was taken out in three sections, and the piece a woman wedged in has been working as a stop — and something has been moving the line recently and not gently. A turn comes; the party is out in fifty-one seconds, the cord holds, the hatch stays latched, and a lamp-stand wedge goes over the step lip into the throat. The store is at eight, and the cause is written twice: once for the person who is not to blame, once for the guild.

## Chapter 34 — Work That Outlives Us

Isel asks to be taught the water mark and Jo says no, and then agrees to the boring version: he calls the number out loud while she checks him, and in nine days he is the second pair of eyes rather than the first. Ilsa then says the school corner floor has grit in it, Pell reads the fall as wrong, and the repair becomes a dressed kerb, a fall, a two-board lift-out cover, and a bar bent over a form cut from the floor's own broken stone. The cost is named before the work starts: a day and a half of Jo's shutter route, the whole of the boundary's grease, one board off Jo's shutter stock, one board Mikka releases as her third, and one length of drawn iron rod that Nera releases herself, which leaves the air baffle with no retaining pin. Nera's own method comes out of Iven's hands and she has to stop talking. Under the far end they find a two-finger gap under the wall plate into a void that is not the channel and is under the old wash room — and that is where the grit has been coming up for years. Mikka then writes the practice that outlives the crew: a named rota of people, not jobs, for the water mark and the cover, taught by name, with Isel not first at anything for a month and Ilsa holding the floor, the cover and the word. Ilsa's last line is the refusal: the repair is not on the crew's list, because the crew will not be here when the bar rusts.

## Chapter 35 — When the Wedges Run Out

The school-door head drops a finger and one of its four wedges splits into two sticks, leaving seven sound and two dead sticks. Tams reads out where the seven are and what each is holding, including three in a hand of washed-out fill at the waist, and admits he has been carrying one wedge in his head for a fortnight and saying two. Pell reads the tally back to the room from the bench. The Hearthholds then choose what stops. Mikka stops the kitchen brace outright — not deferred — and says why in her own words: the flue wall has never moved and the brace is gone, and she chooses the wall and takes on the watching. Ilsa takes back the waiting room she volunteered in Chapter 30, keeps the principle, and states the difference: on the three days in seven the corridor is shut, the school does not run, and the school pays. Renn will not stop the door and the landing, because a boundary that lets go of a door stops being this boundary. Marek then proposes that the crew stand the sixth watch on Renn's three days, which is the right instinct and fails three ways: Sefu refuses on standing, Renn refuses on owning — she will not have a stranger in the wet so that her own refusal becomes smaller — and Jo refuses because a rota with six strangers in it is the same instrument as a charter. Marek's second proposal, to close the corridor for good, is refused by Mikka, who says three wedges buy a floor for a household with a burning child. The decision leaves the head one wedge short of what it wants, the waist packed in fill that is gone, the clinic sill unfunded, and the kitchen brace struck, and Dalla enters six hours of Marek's refused decent proposal in the crew's own column.

## Chapter 36 — A Counter-Support for the Receiver

Marek brings a receiving support to a session that Dalla insists is a session and not a review, and four people take it apart. Iven kills the word: there is no landing down there to receive, only a line-block. Leena kills the location: a bench on the near landing pushes on the same block the answer depends on. Nera kills the fallback: the near lip is the edge of the hole over eleven feet of shaft, and air is what she can see. Oren demonstrates with a hand line and a stone that a lip one stone long is pulled at both ends, and says he would put his shoulder under it. Renn, Mikka and Ilsa each write their own terms, and Renn's say that the cord is re-tied by Sefu and her and not by the crew. Tams's tally is four columns — labor, material, air, time — and every material line is *none*: no post, no line, no permitted dead weight, and a far wall that cannot be lit at the angle required. Iven kills the four ways round it one at a time. Leena closes the morning: a support that has to be stood by a person, in the one place no person may stand. A marked Nera section follows: she has counted forty-one turns for eleven days on a slate she has not shown anybody, and thirty-four of them came between one and nine minutes after the lever came off. It is not proof and she will not put an unproven pattern on a page she does not hold — the habit she learned from another engineer's recorded air plan — so the plan has to become a plan about stopping, and every step of it has to end with the near side as it was found.

## Chapter 37 — The Turn in the Middle of the Work

Step one is a sounding with Nera's draw cord and a stone, and it sets nothing. The line catches on the bright band and comes up with a jar, and Tams takes the jar on his right, the belay hand, and cannot make a fist. Renn Vale walks the shore every morning — three steps to the service bend, the back of her hand on the foot, her thumb beside the bright band and not on it, because her mother told her once not to put a thumb on a thing that is working — and Jo Verrick has been measuring the near landing's lip every morning for a fortnight on purpose, with a string, a chalk and a bent flat iron, and did not tell the room, which is the exact thing that killed Oren Sill. The reading is staged and objected to: Leena Orr wants the method before the number, and Ilsa Norr makes Jo give a **range** — a finger and three-quarters to two inches — because Jo cannot tell how much of the quarter-inch is the stone and how much is her own string. Tams reads the stores and says the sentence that has not moved: a post under that lip wants nine foot and a bundle, this building has four foot eleven and three lengths that are the head of a shore, and there is five foot nine on a bottom shelf under six feet of water with an open inventory line. Sefu Aran asks whether anybody has named this a work, says a work is three things — a person at the end of it, a date, and something different at the finish — and that a complaint is honest but does not let a person go into a flooded room. Renn refuses, and then says why she changed her mind: **if nobody names it, the next time the slab goes, whoever is under that lip is a volunteer.** Sefu names it in his own words and says it can be struck out loud by whoever he names it to: *a shore under the near landing's lip, so that the route can be used on a day when the bearing hand is Jo Verrick and Jo Verrick is on the lever.* The receiver is the keeper. Nera takes the first gallery card off the valve board and reads line nine aloud — *a method for a room that one person is going into **once*** — and says the word is there on purpose, and that she will not hand over a fifth card until the room has decided what the word is for, because the first time somebody pretended a thing like that was a formality a man died under a lip with a figure on a board that everybody had agreed with. She stops Marek in four words at the end, and he says *somewhere else*, and the shutting takes about two seconds and is the first honest thing he does all week.

## Chapter 52 — What Once Means Now

The word *once* goes to the room in Sefu's hand. It takes an hour and a half and nine people, and four of the nine say it wrong on purpose so that the fifth can say it right — which Jo Verrick arranged and took no credit for and Dalla Sen wrote down anyway, because a room that has learned how to be argued with is worth a line. The answer is **Renn Vale's**, arrived at because she had been listening to how people said *once* rather than what they meant: a card is not a permit for a room, it is a permit for a **work**, and it is **spent when the work is finished or called off**, and it does not survive for anybody. Nera writes the **fourth document** line by line while four people object, keeps the Chapter 42 first line — *the work the cache is for is a proposal before this boundary and is not agreed by anybody in this boundary* — even though the work is now named, on the grounds that the work being named and the air being hers have to be in the same line where somebody can trip over them. She writes **spent** on it and nothing else, and Dalla makes her say it twice because the term may not run out quietly. **Jo Verrick names the price before anybody asks:** she is not opening the housing, so the seat is not touched, but the delivery check is open for the whole draw, and a check open for four intervals chatters and packs, and the price of five foot nine **is the check** — and she wants it written in her own hand with a time and not as *noted*, and Dalla refuses both *noted* and *attention*. Tams puts the hand's date on the board: a month from the day in the seventh week is the eleventh week, and it is not this week and not the week after, and nobody is to be kind to him about it. Line seven is the person, and nobody says Jo's name, and Jo says it herself, and adds that she has been the only bearing hand in the building since the Thursday and that if she is going to be the hand on that lip she is going to be the hand on that lever too, and both are on the same day. **Renn Vale says the two cannot be done.** Jo says then the work cannot be done either, and asks somebody else to carry the sentence, and Sefu Aran does.

## Chapter 53 — The Draw

The first chapter in the batch that turns on a person doing a job. The well opens on the second interval of the third morning. Nera releases the **eight-foot draw cord for the fifth time** and writes the release and the reason: the bay's working depth is six feet, there is one ladder in that well, it is a person wide, and there are eleven feet under it. Six watches filled and named; the outer service door open a shoulder's width; the stair-side grille shut; the bypass hatch latched; Mikka Hale at the water mark because Ruk Sella is at the bell and a person doing two things is doing neither. Jo goes down, and when Nera asks her — as an air engineer and not as a plan — what is in the rest of the room, Jo reports **a tiled floor under the silt, in a pattern, because silt does not lay itself in a pattern**, and **three rows of peg holes in the wall behind the shelf**, at a spacing wrong for tools, which is a spacing for cords and coats and aprons and pans, and a shelf somebody bolted over a wall of pegs because they needed the shelf more. Ilsa Norr insists it go in **the witness's words and not the clerk's**, and Jo reads it back before she comes up. At the second interval the check stops chattering and goes flat, and Jo comes up in a minute and forty with nothing, and Nera declares the card spent — and Dalla Sen reads the card's own line back instead of rewriting it, because **the draw failed and the work did not**, and by the air engineer's own term a card is spent when the work ends. Nera writes the number **three** on it and says the card dies when the work dies. The length comes up on the fourth interval out of about a hand and a half of water, along the ladder's stile, hauled by **Tams Quill and Leena Orr** — arranged over half a day, argued over twice, and Jo's reason for the arrangement is a sentence about the seventh week and a man who died four days later. Then twenty minutes at the bench, and **Sefu Aran receives it in the register, item by item, condition as found, sound, no decay, no split, no saw marks that are not old**, and reads the line that says the crew moved it and own none of it, and Nera closes her own inventory line and asks for the number to stay on the end of the timber, because the next person who opens that wall will want to know whose it was. At half past the fifth bell a courier comes down nine steps with a satchel, a folded paper and a leather case, and does not shout, and a boy on a bench at the top of the nine steps looks up about four seconds later and says *that is my sister* without moving, and Dalla writes the fact down with a time on it, and the courier looks up at the top of the steps, puts her hand flat on the satchel strap, says *Pell*, and nothing else.

## Chapter 54 — A Shore Under the Lip

Iven Tallo says *this is not a post* before anybody cuts, and prices the material out loud: no sound wedge for the head, because Renn has already refused the school-door head its fourth and the refusal stands, so the shore will be **packed**, and the packing is a man and a knee, and Tams reads Oren Sill's method out of the *carried* column longhand in his left hand because he is frightened of losing it. The head is cut as a bearing in the anteroom — set, proud at the top, hollow at the bottom — and the foot wants a reach of **four foot two off the stone**, which Iven insists is a different datum from the older shore's four foot seven off the wall, so that nobody round here reuses a number. Renn refuses in advance, in her own hand and in the anteroom, to be on the landing while the lip is packed, and will not be asked. Marek goes last, stands on the common landing, puts his palm on the dressed stone over the lip, waits, and gets nothing — stone, cold, and eleven feet of shaft — and writes **null** for the **seventh** time and Jo pockets the slate without being asked. The day's board is the first one in that room with a bearer, a plinth reader, a string, and a bell, and finding the fourth takes six minutes and two refusals, because a person doing two things is doing neither and nobody from outside the boundary may stand a watch. **Bel Ordry** takes the bell, asks what a bell is for before he answers, and repeats the whole of it back. The **woman from the wash rooms** takes the string, having been asked by Renn at the top of three steps on the morning with the asking and the answer in Renn's hand with a time on it. Pell asks, in front of the room, that nobody build a plan on him: *if anybody treats what I say as a stop then I have taught this room to be afraid of me.* The crew is on the near landing. Pell calls the turn **eight seconds** early; they are off in fifty-one seconds, which is the best figure in the record for a party with a head of timber in its hands and not the best on the stair, which is still thirty-eight; Isel Ferrow does not leave the lip because a bearer is there for the whole of a crossing. Jo says one word — *packed* — and then, off the floor, out loud in a full room, that **a finger and a half is not a fix and the man is still under the lip**, and the tally that night puts the anteroom's four foot eleven into the packing and leaves a two foot ten and a handful in Jo's basket, and ends on the post, which wants nine foot and a bundle, and which is not built, and which is the tenth item.

## Chapter 55 — A Courier's Order

Talla Roke has carried a written order for thirty-one days and has not opened it under anybody's roof, because a courier who opens an order in somebody else's house has had it in somebody else's house. She asks to be asked. Sefu asks her to open it in front of four people and promises the original back before anybody argues. The order is from the route-council clerk of Hearthhold Middle, witnessed, and it is **exactly the paper Iven Tallo asked for in Chapter 12**: the parapet slab and the bulk public-works stores, to the Middle water-lock, received by a named keeper, moved by a courier's own hands, costs carried by the Middle council. It has a clerk's mark and not a name, and Talla has thought about the mark every day of the thirty-one. **Corva Nye** comes down eleven minutes behind her, a Cartwright route-custody clerk from the Marrowgate clearance office, present as an **inventory witness** because the salvage lien requires one, and she asks to have that said first, and Sefu enters her in the ledger on the clerk's own authority as not a person of the boundary, no watch, no rota. Mikka feeds her and says out loud that the pot is not a wage. Then Iven says the thing the whole of Volume 02 is about: the order is good and the order is the danger, because the moment that slab carries a load in a Hearthhold work it is material in use under a guild lien, and a claim on the boundary's material is a claim on the boundary. Talla says what she wants it for — Middle's water lock is dry, the fourth gate's families have been carrying water up four steps since the cold, she has two brothers on that list and a sister of eleven — and that she has been looking for a cheaper way for thirty-one days and found three and all of them worse. **Marek gets four words into offering the crew's hours and Jo Verrick stops him**: *you do not have to. You have never once had to. That is the entire problem and it has been the entire problem for ten weeks.* Nera gives the answer nobody had asked for: the first thing anybody will take is not the stone, **it is the fact that there is a name in this building**, and she is not having that conversation this week because she has a check that hisses and a bed to cut. Corva Nye asks the only question her form allows — *who maintains the route* — and Sefu reads his paragraph separately as he always does, and she writes the answer in the margin of her own form in her own hand, and says her office will read it as a route with nobody on it, and asks for that fact to go into the report in her own margin because a form with no box for a keeper is a form that fills with owners. The chapter ends with the recognition entered as a fact about two people in the same building, and with Talla Roke asking which of them is the keeper.

## Chapter 56 — The Reckoning

Tams Quill reads the two columns together for the first time and the room finds the second one harder. The crew's **thirty-eight and a half has not gone up** — it went down, because he is not on the lever, not allowed a bar, and because a man who was on the lever for three weeks is under a stone and is not on the board anywhere — and the boundary's has gone from **forty-six to fifty-one**. He reads the items without rounding them: a day and a half of Jo's shutter route, gone and not recovered; the half-day for the Chapter 50 closure; two days of Ilsa's school; nineteen days of meals, which are not hours and which he is putting in anyway; Mikka's pot twice; a watched flue wall; two hours of the clerk's evenings; and four hours of Renn's own, which she keeps in her head, and which he has now put on a board — and she says *it stays*. The finding is the same and one item longer: **the half that gets counted is the half that is ours.** Sefu gets the leaving sentence read and says the obligation is open in the direction that they are leaving and not in the direction that the work is done. And then **Ilsa Norr asks the question nobody has asked in nine weeks** — *who keeps the Hearthhold rooms* — and explains why it has not been asked, which is that every one of them has been assuming somebody is already doing it, and the walls are nobody's job, and if she had not said so out loud and paid for it there would be a hole under a school and it would be nobody's fault. Nobody answers it. Corva Nye puts the form on the bench flat, does not read it out, and shows the room the shape of what it has: a line for the holder of a route, a line for the charter, a line for the date it was inspected, a line for the keeper of a *workshop*, a line for the office, a line for the guild — **and no line for a woman who teaches nine children and pays for a floor out of her own hand and shuts a door because she decides to.** She writes that in her margin in her own ink and Dalla makes the clerk read it back. Corva then asks properly to see the nine register boxes, and **Ilsa refuses again, in her own words, in front of the same people**, and says what is in the box without opening it — attendance rolls, a fuel book, a lintel that is not a lintel, four inches of copper, ground maps, and one plan rolled on a dowel with a tape round it, on its own box, never opened — and gives the reason: everything else is a thing somebody did and finished, and *that plan is a decision about what this building is*, and she will not open it in the week after a man died under a stair, and if it is a house she has to be the woman who found out. She says yes in three weeks with a name and a date, and one term is that the crew will not be in the room. Talla Roke then says the thing that makes the question more expensive: in eleven Hearthholds in four years she has never seen a vault, and every plan she has carried off the middle gate's survey shelf is drawn like a house, with doors drawn on hinges, and nobody has ever told her why. Nera says she does not know, and says the honest half — she has not opened that plan either, and if Ilsa unrolls it in three weeks she will be the only person in the room who cannot be trusted with it, because she will want it to be a house. Dalla writes the question down with the week and the room and the asker, and no answer under it, and **no word *pending***.

## Chapter 57 — Reading It by Ear

The check does not get worse, and that is the thing Jo says out loud, because a room decides a hiss is the new normal and the new normal is fine. It is a hole with a shape. She gives the two-note vocabulary — a hiss that stays a hiss, and a hiss that gets **thinner**, and thinner is the seat — and asks that it be written in a room that can be frightened and not with *noted* on it. The school runs, because the school always runs, and Ilsa says so in the flat voice and Mikka says so the same way, and a day lost is a day the nine children do not get back, and the corridor is open and the waist is a live post, and **Renn Vale takes it and says out loud what she is and is not before she takes it**. The pump comes off **four minutes early, on the nineteenth stroke**, and this time everybody is off, because the terms were read at the school door, the bearer was named on the day's board, the plinth was read by Ruk Sella, the bell was rung by Bel Ordry, and **the woman from the wash rooms had been asked in advance and had said yes**. Nobody dies. The anteroom clears in a minute and a quarter on foot and the litter sets the number and has not come, and Sefu records that the system worked and the pump did not, which are two different sentences and only one of them is a reason to be pleased. She shuts the route at the top of three steps, on her own hand, for a reason nobody would have thought of: *Jo's hiss has gone thin and I am not a pump hand and I do not know what a thin hiss is, and that is exactly why I am shutting it*, and she says it out loud instead of writing it because she can do both and the harder one is better. It costs a day of Ilsa's school and a pump interval, and the corridor stays shut for the rest of the week, and Renn says that out loud so that nobody can say afterwards a watch quietly stopped. Then **Isel Ferrow puts a bar in Tams Quill's left hand and asks twice and Tams refuses it in front of the room**, and the reason is the best sentence in the batch: when the interval came off early, the first thing that went was the hiss, and a man at a frame with a bar in his hand does not hear a hiss. The tally gives **fifty-eight and thirty-eight and a half**, and Tams reads the same finding again with a shut in the *held* column and the check on the list. And Nera claims her own cost at the end and does not let anybody else carry it: she took the stair's air out of the certification list, a watch is the cheapest thing there is, she chose it, and **a watch is not a smaller certificate — it is a debt that gets paid in somebody's day** — and she will not change it, because the alternative is publishing a number she cannot stand behind and a man going under a lip because of it.

## Chapter 58 — Eleven Feet Down

**The one marked viewpoint shift in the batch, into Nera Oduya.** It opens on the fact that she was **asked** — by Renn Vale, at the top of three steps, in the flat voice, with the day and the hours and the post named, and the right to refuse given and the promise not to ask again this month. Dalla Sen turns the yes into a second question, because the air valve watch does not go to a lip, and Nera's answer is to take the board and the cloth with her, shut the valve, and declare it out loud before she goes: **it is the first time in eleven weeks that the watch has moved and it is not to be done again without the air engineer saying so in writing.** Bel Ordry goes with her as the bearer, because a bearer at the near lip is a person of this boundary. She stands on the fixed first landing with her left boot on the stone and her right hand on the rail, four feet from the rail over the throat, not going near it, and the chapter gives the reader the thing nobody in the room has: **her private count of the turns, forty-one before the shore and more since, of which thirty-four came between one and nine minutes after the lever came off, said once out loud in the thirty-sixth day and refused three times since**, and the two unexplained marks on her board, and the fact that she is the only person in this building who can only say *there is nobody down there* in eleven weeks. At the second minute of the nineteen strokes, from eleven feet down, she hears **a flat, dry, wooden knock with a slide on the end of it** — a bar in a staple, a hand leaving a door frame — and an air push past her with a shape to it, out of the throat's north-east cheek at about the height of her knee. She does not move, and she counts four seconds of breath. Then the turn comes on the second minute of the six, and she says the word and she and Bel Ordry are up the three steps in under a minute, and the terms are the only reason that was a sentence and not a scramble. She tells the room two things and then gives **four conditions**: the post's work is scoped to what can be seen from the fixed first landing and **nobody goes under that stone**; the four treads are **not walked this month**; the throat is **sounded, not entered**, daily at the second bell, with a bar, by a person, and the answer written down whatever it is, **including nothing**; and the review is **brought forward** to the first work-bell after a sound from the throat, a thin hiss, or anybody writing the word *out* on the slate. She says plainly that she is not going down, not publishing a rate, not taking the watch off, and not certifying anything, and that the reason for all four is one reason: she has never certified a room she has not stood in, and she learned that in a place where a room nobody stood in killed thirty people. Renn asks the practical question — *what would a door be doing down there* — and Nera says: where people walk in and out. Talla Roke connects a latch, a door drawn on hinges on a plan, and a plan rolled on a dowel, and says she has been in the building a week and has counted four things nobody has opened and they are all about the same subject, and asks the clerk to write down who wants the boxes and who wants the door, because those are two different people. And in the anteroom on the Wednesday, Marek stands at the bench with the tally board and the plate in his coat pocket, puts his hand flat on the bench either side of it, waits, **gets nothing, and does not write *null*, because there was nothing to write it on and he knew what it would say.**

## Chapter 59 — The Order Withdrawn

Talla Roke withdraws the order in the anteroom, in her own hand, at the fifth bell, after the tally, and asks for it in her words and not the clerk's and for it read back. She withdraws somebody else's order, on a clerk's mark, that she believes was made in good faith for a better reason than hers. The reason is Iven's, given a week earlier: the moment the slab carries a load in a Hearthhold work it is material in use under a guild lien, and **a claim on the boundary's material is a claim on the boundary**; and her own reason is simpler — *a courier's whole trade is that a thing claimed by nobody can be carried by anybody, and the moment I write my order into a boundary's book I put a second lock on the same door.* She refuses to have it recorded as a refusal, and asks the clerk to record the distinction she learned in eleven days: **a refusal is the clerk's and stands and can be appealed; a withdrawal is the courier's and can be made again by anybody with a mark.** Dalla records both, in the clerk's hand as well as the courier's, because in a year somebody will want to know whether this boundary ever got anybody to change their mind, and the answer is yes, once, and it was not about a stone. Then Talla says the cost herself before anybody can be gentle with her and asks somebody else to say it out loud and write it, and **Mikka Hale says it: four households in the second gate carrying water up four sets of steps this winter.** Talla asks for the crew's hours — a named work, a person asking, a clerk to write it down — for the second gate's inlet, in the spring, and Marek says there are **two springs** and promises neither, and then admits to a room that he has been thinking about it at two in the morning on his own with nobody's name on it, and Sefu Aran makes him say it twice and put a time on it, and says the correct answer is to write it in a room because a man who will admit a two-o'clock plan in a room is worth ten men who have not got one. **Sefu holds that the first spring is not the crew's hours and not this year: it is a conversation between the keeper and the air engineer about a named hand-over, and it happens whether or not anybody is leaving, and Nera Oduya is not going to be impressed by a man who has been talking to a courier.** Corva Nye asks, properly and in front of the keeper and Renn, to sit at the top of nine steps and watch a clerk write a number. **Renn refuses**, gives her reasons, says it is both a condition and a refusal on purpose, and adds that if the office wants to know whether the boundary can keep the route then the office can come in the cold, and be asked, and may be asked not, and carry water, and nobody writes a report. The report goes up on foot. And **Iven Tallo says the correct response to a guild page is nothing** — no second page, no seventh line on the slate, because the day the slate gets a line added to answer a clerk is the day it stops being a thing the boundary keeps and becomes a document the boundary defends — and that the boundary's own page was the answer and **the guild's page is the first nail**, and that the wall is four habits and a rope and a clerk who says no, and that it will cost somebody something not written down anywhere. Then Jo Verrick, at the frame, says the seat goes before the cold, names the three things she does not have, and says the man who holds it cannot be Tams Quill and cannot be Marek Venn, because **a seat you cannot feel is a seat you cannot tell has gone wrong.**

## Chapter 60 — A Threshold That Is Not the Last One

Renn Vale writes the shore onto her slate and argues the room into a **seventh term**, which says in her own hand that the lip is not held but packed, that it goes a finger and a half with a man under it and went two inches before, and that **the next time that slab turns somebody will be under that lip with a knee on a piece of wood and their whole weight on it, and it will be Jo Verrick** — and that the room is writing down that it knows, which is not the same as writing down a plan. Talla Roke asks for the ledger to be read to her in full, because she is going to walk out of here and would rather know what this boundary has from the book than from a person, and it is read, item by item, and it is read as the entries that are **new since the fourth of the month** and not the whole of it: the pump and the seat and the check read by ear, with a two-hand correction Jo Verrick insists on — *I did not write and the seat is going; hers is a finding and mine is a rule* — the four air cards and the word *spent* and the reason the room worked, the recovered length received by the keeper with a time on it, the material that does not exist, the route's seven terms, the closure with a name and a date, **the first entry in nine weeks with a stranger's name in it** (Corva Nye, entered on the clerk's own authority, and Renn's refusal of her, both halves), the withdrawal and the refusal/withdrawal distinction and the four households, **the question asked in the tenth week by Ilsa Norr and not answered with no word *pending* on it**, the register boxes refused with a date three weeks out and the crew not in the room, and the throat sounded with a bar and the answer written down including nothing, and the two knocks kept separate in two hands, and a correction entered in the clerk's own hand because a half-day of shutter route had been read out as though it belonged to two closures instead of one. Then **Sefu's closure clause fires for the first time**: the route has been used for **work**, not air and not its own testing, and it goes on the clerk's page with the names of the people who opened and shut it, the reason, and the date — and the room hears what that sentence sounds like, and Mikka says *well* and Ilsa says *about time*, and Renn goes down the three steps and puts her hand on a shore that is not hers and says nothing about that. The maintenance obligation has not ended, and Marek says why out loud: a woman who will not stand on that stair and will not be on that landing, a woman who has a dead mother in a hole and will not be asked, and himself, which makes three of the four who could say it, and a boundary Renn has only just started believing she is allowed to have. Talla goes out at first light with a satchel, a drawing of the second gate's inlet and a copy of her own withdrawal, and has a short conversation with her brother at the top of nine steps about a hand that will open in the eleventh week, and he says *you have a hand* and she says nobody in this room says anything to anybody in a way they did not ask to hear first. On the last morning **Bel Ordry sounds the throat with a bar and writes down *nothing*** and says out loud that that is a real thing he has written down, and the review line is named and read out before it is written and taken apart twice. The unresolved list gains its eleventh item, which is the seat and the check; the post is the tenth: **a post under the near landing's lip, which wants nine foot and a bundle, which is not built, and which is the reason there is a woman under a stone at all.** And the two questions carry on, and the plate did not go anywhere because nobody went anywhere, and the brass rule said nothing again, and a key turns in three weeks, and the chapter ends on a shore that is **not enough**, and that everybody in the room knows is not enough, and that the page says so in Renn Vale's hand with a date on it.
