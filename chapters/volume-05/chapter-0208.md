# Chapter 208 — One Line And Three Rooms

The fifth day of the fortieth week is a Friday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the forty-fifth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door, and four of them came down them at the same speed they went up them, which had not been true on Wednesday and which nobody in the anteroom mentioned and all of them had noticed. The pump had not run for six days.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twenty-eighth reading, and the same as twenty-seventh, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right. She gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked, and the clerk entered all four as four refusals and not as one, and the count of mornings is twenty-eight and is the same count as the wall's.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell for about as long as a sentence takes to say, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and entered it, and the count of mornings out of mornings is fifteen.

The anteroom's terms were said at the first bell, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The third of the six was six pails a day and it was false and it was said. The pump was attended, and stopped, and had been for six days.

Then Jo Verrick did the thing that the rest of the day was made of, and she did it before she had eaten anything and before the terms had been said, and Dalla Sen entered the order of it afterwards because she did not want to interrupt it.

"**I have got a figure and I am going to give it to the room before anything else happens today, and I am giving it in the space where a finding goes, and I am giving the half against it in the same breath, and I have been sitting on this figure for three days and I am not sitting on it any longer and I would like the clerk to enter that I decided that this morning at about the sixth bell and not on Wednesday.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it, and entered that she was not asked,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The bed under the ninth hearth stone in the room at the kitchen-line end is a third of an inch higher than it was in the twenty-ninth week. I have read that floor sixteen times since the twenty-ninth week and I have got a figure for it and it is a third of an inch, and I have said in this room that I would not write what caused it, and I am not going to write what caused it.**"

"**Then give the half against,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The half against is the bad repair,**" said Jo Verrick. "**The lime repair on that wall is on one face only, because it was put on the face that gets looked at, and the skin has let go on the face it was put on, which means it is going to let go on the face it was not put on, and the face it was not put on is the face nine children lean against at the ninth bell four mornings in seven.**"

The room did not fill in after it.

"**And I am going to say the part that is mine and not the wall's,**" Jo Verrick said, "**because I have been in this building forty weeks as a woman who reads walls and I have never once said a figure out loud in this room and I have been right about every one of them, and I am saying one now, and I am saying it on a Friday, and I would like a clerk to enter that a wall got a number out of me this week and that nobody in this room asked it a question first.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the space where a reason goes is empty beside it and that the clerk did not fill it.**"

"**And the ninth stone is the ninth,**" said Tams Quill, from the end of the bench. "**I have said that in this building before and I am not going to say it again and it is not joined to anything.**"

Then the room got to the water, and it got there in the way it has got to everything this week, which is that somebody said a thing that had been standing in the room for two days.

"**A line is a line,**" Iven Tallo said. "**I said that on Thursday at the ninth bell into a room that was not listening, and I have had a night and a morning to think about whether I meant it, and I meant it, and I am going to finish the sentence and I am going to do it in about a minute and then I am going to go back to the anvil.**"

"**Go on,**" said Renn Vale.

"**On Tuesday I said there were two waters and a piece of this building in the middle of them, and on Wednesday a woman came up nine steps and put a third in the room, and a third one is not a pot. A pot has a lid on it and it is a thing you make in a room. A gallery at the bottom of a passage off the second gate has no lid on it and it is nine hundred years old and nobody in this building has been in it. So there are two waters and a pot, and a pot does not sit on a line. A line goes through rooms.**"

"**Say how many rooms,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I do not know,**" Iven Tallo said. "**That is the whole of my answer and I would like it entered that a carpenter has declined to give a number about a thing he cannot see, and that he has counted four rooms in a wall twice in his life and got four both times and how he counts them is his and it is a carpenter's answer and not a survey.**"

Then Nella Bray put the guild's claim out on the trestle and squared it and read two lines of it out loud in a clerk's voice, which she can do and which nobody in that room had ever heard her do before.

"**The claim describes a gallery at the lower end of a passage off the second gate, with rooms off it, and it gives a floor count and it gives nothing about depth,**" she said, "**and it is a claim over salvage and the salvage is water and timber and metal and salt-crust, and there is one line in it I am not going to read out because I struck it out on Monday and it stays struck.**"

"**What floor,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**It does not say,**" said Nella Bray. "**It says *lower*. And I have been at a counter for nineteen years and I will tell you what that word is on a form: *lower* means the writer has not been and is describing it to somebody who has.**"

The anteroom went quiet for about as long as four strokes of a pump that is not running, and there were no strokes, and the quiet was the whole of the time there was.

Then the third room was named, and it was named by a man who had been in the room since the sixth bell and had not spoken, and what he said was not a discovery.

"**The warehouse,**" said Bel Ordry. "**It is on the claim. It is four days up the middle passage. It is the only building on that piece of paper with a name on it that anybody in this room has ever heard of, and I know the name of it because I have read a file this year that has a district on it and a line in it about stores standing on a sill. And nobody in this room has ever been inside it and I am not going to say one word about what is in it.**"

"**Then how do you know it is on a line,**" said Nella Bray.

"**I do not know it is on a line,**" Bel Ordry said. "**I know a warehouse stands on a sill. And I know that water in a gallery goes somewhere, and I know it does not stop at a wall, and I know that a thing that is stopped by a wall and a thing that is stopped by a sill are the same kind of stopped. That is a shape, and it is not a survey, and I am the man in this building most likely to be able to do a survey and I am not doing one because I would have to leave.**"

"**Then say the shape,**" said Renn Vale, "**because a foreman will not put it in a book and a clerk will not put it in a book and a woman from a counter has just read two lines off a guild's form and stopped. Who is going to say it.**"

"**Nobody,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I am going to say that, and I am going to say why, and then somebody is going to say it anyway, because that is what rooms do.**"

"**Say it anyway,**" said Bel Ordry.

So Mara Quell, who was nine years old and had come down the nine steps at the fourth bell and was sitting on the bench at the anvil end because the bench at the kitchen-line end was taken, said it.

"**It is all the same water,**" she said.

Nine people in that anteroom did not move, and the clerk entered that nobody moved, and entered that a child of nine had said in the fourth bell of a shut-then-open Friday what four adults had spent two days assembling out of a woman's sentence, a guild's form, a foreman's refusal, a line of nothing in a wall and a figure about a bed under a stone.

"**Say the last part,**" said Bel Ordry, and his voice was not steady and he did not pretend it was.

"**And it is all the same water and it is not anywhere anybody put it,**" Mara Quell said, "**and you are all talking about which room it is in.**"

Nobody told her she was wrong, and nobody told her she was right, and Dalla Sen entered the whole of it in the space where a finding goes and put the word *guess* in the space where a reason goes and said out loud, twice, that she was entering a guess in a findings column on purpose and that it was the second time in forty weeks and that she was going to keep doing it.

"**Three rooms,**" she said. "**A schoolroom with a hearth stone in it, a pump station with a stopped pump in it, and a warehouse four days up a middle passage that a claim says is there. One line. A guess with a place at each end, and a rule on the line, and not one word of the rule, and I am entering that there is a rule on the line as a guess and not as a finding, and I am entering that no figure of anything is attached to any of the three, and I am entering that the clerk was not asked for any of it.**"

Then somebody put the line in front of Tams Quill, which is the only way it has ever been put in front of him, and it took a woman with a slate and no instrument at all.

"**A nineteenth,**" said Nella Bray, and she said it as though she had brought it in with the claim. "**A line. In the unheaded column, because there is no word for it, and because a line is a thing this building has found and the other eighteen are things this building has.**"

The room looked at the end of the bench, and the man at the end of the bench had the piece of chalk he has not used and did not pick it up.

"**No,**" said Tams Quill, "**and I am going to say why, and it is the eighth time I have said no to this board and it is not a variation of the seven, and I would like the clerk to have it in my words and not in hers.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the man who said it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that it is the eighth and that he said the number himself before she wrote it.**"

"**A line is not a thing,**" Tams Quill said. "**A line is where two things are. It is not a thing and it does not do anything and it cannot be maintained by anybody, and if it goes on that board then this board is not a list of what this building has found, it is a list of what this building believes, and a list of what a building believes is a plan, and a plan has a corner in it with a day in the corner.**"

He looked at the end of the bench and not at anybody in the room.

"**And the difference between a line on that board and a plan is not a heading. It is a corner. That is all it is, and it is a day of the week and a month and somebody's hand, and I have refused seven things on that board and every one of them was a thing, and this is the first one that is not a thing, and I would like it entered that a man who reads numbers for a living has spent eight weeks refusing things and has never once been asked to refuse a shape.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the man who said it,**" said Dalla Sen, and she did not write the number, "**and entered that the unheaded column has nothing over the top of it this morning and will have nothing over the top of it this evening, and entered that no heading was proposed by anybody and none is wanted.**"

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell with his shut book under his forearm, and he had the shape. He had had it since about the eighth bell on Wednesday and he had not said it, and he was the only person in that room who could have put a bearing on it and a direction and a limit, and he was asked, in effect, by a child of nine who had got there first.

He did not say it.

"**I am going to say why I am not saying it, and then I am going to go and stand somewhere else,**" he said, "**and the reason is not the seat, and it is not humility, and I want it written down that it is not humility, because a physician called it a seat on Thursday and it is not a seat and I have been let off and I do not think I have been.**"

"**Then say the reason,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**A guess made by four people who are not me is more use to this room than a shape made by the man who wants to be the man who found out,**" Marek Venn said. "**That is the whole of it. I have been in this building forty weeks waiting to be the person who says the true thing in a room and gets believed, and on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week a carpenter asked four words in this room and got nothing, and I knew the answer for two of the four and I gave a reason for not saying it and I have not given the reason since. This morning a child of nine has said it better than I would have said it and I am not going to improve on her in a room, and if the shape in my head is right then two people in this room are going to be in a gallery in a fortnight and they will find out, and if it is wrong then I would rather they found that out in a room with no name on it in it than from a man at a trestle who has been wrong in this building four times.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that the man who found out declined to find out, and entered that he gave a reason that was about other people and not about himself, and entered that the clerk noticed that the last three reasons he has given were all about other people and is not going to do anything about that and would like a person in a year to notice it,**" said Dalla Sen.

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went in and were not read, and the reason went in beside them: a room which has entered a guess in a findings column on purpose does not read a total over it, because a total read out loud is a clerk telling a room the guess is dealt with.

Two today and three hundred and twenty-five since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and forty-two and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that on its own line, in the ordinary hand, and was not read, and no reason was given, and the line has no heading over it and is not going to get one. Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty-three days and one hundred and fifty-three nights, forty-five weeks of a clerk saying out loud that she is going to keep writing it down. It is not going to be one hundred and fifty-four.

The board was walked, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven of them not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it. Tams Quill gave the eighth reason for the refusal and she did not ask him for a ninth and he did not offer one.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman rose on Tuesday and named three people he could not ask and did not ask, and there is now a shape in a clerk's book with a guess in it, and a crew asked to go and look at a guess is a crew asked to be right about a guess.

The seam gave one this morning and the pot is three days, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for sixteen weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a child of nine is sitting on a bench at the anvil end of an anteroom and has no idea what she has done.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with a book on it with a guess in it and the word guess beside it, and a pump that had not run for six days, and three rooms on a line where there was one, and a board with eighteen things on it and a line that was not one of them, and a man at the end of a trestle who had the shape and had let a girl of nine say it first.
