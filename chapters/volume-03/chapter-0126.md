# Chapter 126 — Eleven

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the twenty-fifth week, and the school did not run, and the room at the kitchen-line end of the corridor was opened at the second bell by four people, and a count was read out in a corridor in the order this room does things in, and one of the numbers in it had a name on it and one of the numbers in it was eleven.**

**The word was said first, and nobody was in the room to say it.**

Renn Vale stood on the corridor side of that door with the pump running behind her and said, in a room, in front of eleven people at the kitchen line and the fourth step and the school door, the sentence that is the second way out of it.

"**The room is going to be opened. The rule is that the word is said by the person who is going to be in the room and the clerk repeats it. There is nobody in that room. There has been nobody in that room since Friday's fifth bell. So the rule cannot be kept this morning, and I am saying it out loud before anybody starts, and Dalla is going to say it, and I am going to ask her to repeat it after me, and neither of those two things is a second way out and both of them are what this building has instead.**"

"**Word,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Door,**" said Dalla Sen, and then, in her own words, because the clerk is the clerk, "**and I am entering that the rule could not be kept this morning, that I said the word and repeated it, that nobody was in the room, and that the reason nobody was in the room is a condition with no number and no day on it that has not been met, and I am not putting those two sentences next to each other and I do not know what I am doing and I am not going to find out in a corridor.**"

---

**The condition had not been met, and both of the people whose condition it was said so in a room, and the door was opened anyway, and the argument for opening it was made by a schoolteacher and it was four words long.**

"**Read it out,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and read the strike, and I want the original and not the copy.**

Renn Vale read the condition out of her own hand off her own slate, in a room, word for word, and the clerk read her out against the copy, and the two of them are the same and it took four minutes and it is the way this room checks anything.

"**It is opened when the clay under that sill has stopped shrinking, and the person who decides that is the person who laid it, and the person who laid it is the man who held the stones and the man who beat the clay, and they have to be in a room together and both of them have to say it.**"

"**Say it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The clay has not stopped,**" said Iven Tallo. "**I laid it on the Thursday with a piece of the pump's bed-plate and I have put my thumb in the joint at the kitchen-line end every morning since, which is four mornings, and on the first two it went in about the depth of a nail and on the last two it has gone in about half that, and it is still going in.**

"**I am not saying the condition is not met to be difficult. I am saying it because a condition that is met when the man who wrote it wants it met is a condition with a name on it and we do not have one of those.**"

"**Then the condition is not met,**" said Marek Venn, "**and I said so on Monday in a room, and I laid the stones on Thursday and I have put my thumb in that joint every morning since, and it is the same answer and mine is the same answer, and I am not going to be asked to say anything else about it, because the next thing I say about it will be a reason and I do not have one that is not a wish.**"

"**Then the door stays,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The door does not stay,**" said Ilsa Norr.

She was standing in the corridor in a room's width of nothing between the school door and the kitchen line and she had not come down from the fourth step, and she said it in the tone she uses when a thing has been settled in her since the second bell on the first day of the week and she is only now letting it out of her mouth.

"**Read the fourth condition off Renn's slate out loud in a room, and read it the way it is written, and then say the difference between it and this.**

"**I will say the difference,**" said Ilsa Norr. "**In three words. The condition is about a room. This is about a child. A room can wait. I am not going to be the woman who decides that for nine weeks, and I am not going to let a slate decide it for me, and a condition is not a warrant.**

"**And I am not saying the slate is wrong. I am saying a slate is a thing a room agrees to in a room, and the agreeing happened on the Thursday when the room was not full of anybody, and this morning the room is full of one person and she is nine years old.**"

---

**They counted them out in a corridor, at both ends, in the way this room counts, and it was eleven, and one of the eleven could not put weight on a foot.**

They came out at the strokes and at a walk because a sill nine inches deep packed in stone and clay by two people will take a boot's weight for about four minutes and not one minute more, and Renn Vale said so before anybody asked and Dalla Sen entered that she had said it before anybody asked.

"**Ten out,**" said Dalla Sen, at the school door.

"**Ten,**" said Bel Ordry, at the kitchen line, and then, because that is the way this room counts, he read the number back and made a person read it back after him, and the corridor was empty and the lamp was in the room and the ninth hearth stone had a hole in the bed of it at its low side that was the width of three fingers, which is one more than it was on Thursday.

"**Eleven,**" said Dalla Sen, at the school door. "**Ten walked. Eleven came.**"

"**Say the eleventh,**" said Ilsa Norr, "**and say it however you have to say it, and say the number before the name, because the number is the fact and the name is a person and I am not going to have the person go into a page without the number in front of it.**"

"**Eleven. The eleventh is Orla Kern, nine years old, the fourth of my nine, and she came out of that room on her own two feet and she got to the fourth step and she sat down on it, and she has not said anything, and she is not saying anything now, and I am going to look at her foot because I have been not looking at it since the third bell and I am not going to do that again.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the eleven was read out before the name and that the name is Orla Kern and that she is nine and that she is the fourth of nine, and entered that the room held eleven and the page says eleven and the two numbers are the same number and I am entering that they are the same number and I am not entering anything else about them.**

"**And entered that a boundary in which a room holds a number that matches the number on its own page is a boundary in which a man can be right and everybody in the room can check him, and that this happened on the first day of the twenty-fifth week and that I do not know what to do with it and that is my entry and not his.**"

---

**Orla Kern's foot was under the ninth hearth stone, and the ninth hearth stone had gone over about a hand's width at its low side and down at the front, and the bed of it under the lime was a hole.**

Jo Verrick was on her knees in it with a lamp and her rule and she had the stone lifted on two flags and propped, and she was not crying and she was not speaking for about a minute, which for Jo Verrick is a quarter of an hour.

"**Say it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**The lime is gone.**" She put her thumb in the bed of it where the stone had been. "**I put my thumb to the second joint of my forefinger in under the bed of this stone on Monday and I said I would not put a cat on it, and it is a hand and a half down, and the stone has gone over into the room and down at the front, and there is a hole under it the width of my hand and I can put my arm in up to the elbow.**

"**And here is what I am going to say and I am going to say it once. That stone has been bedded on lime since it was laid and the lime under it has been wet and dried and wet again since the first day of the twentieth week because a floor of that room is warm under nine children and the water came up under that floor for six weeks and this morning it did not come up at all because two men packed a sill in clay.

"**And the lime that let go was not the water's. The water went away. The lime that let go was nine children sitting on a warm stone for five weeks and a cold hearth that would not be lit and a room with no light in it in the morning except the light off nine stones, and a stone moves a hair at a time and then one day it moves its own weight.

"**I said on Monday that I would not put a cat on that bed. I said it in a room with nine people in it and it is in the book in my hand. And then four people packed a sill on Thursday and nobody went back to that stone, and this morning a child is sitting on the step with her foot under it.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in Jo Verrick's words and not shortened, and entered that it was said by the woman who said it would happen, and entered that the room did not act on it, and entered that on the Thursday a packing went into that sill with two men laying it and nobody went back to that stone, and entered that I am the clerk and I read the whole of the first day's entry out at the sixth bell and none of this one, because this one was not written until this morning and I did not know it on the Thursday, and I am entering that I did not know it and that I am not going to be let off with that.**"

"**Nobody is going to let you off with it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I did not ask, and I am entering that I answered for a room.**"

---

**Mero Senn's rule was then in this building for the first time without him, because four people had been doing his work for twenty-five weeks without knowing it, and a schoolteacher said his sentence in a corridor and did not know whose it was.**

Ilsa Norr crouched on the fourth step with a child's ankle in her hands and did not look up, and said:

"**A plan without a named injured person is not a plan.**

"**And a plan with an acceptable casualty is not automatically acceptable. And I have just had the second half of that said to me by my own head in a corridor and I am not going to be told it by a stranger, and I am not going to be told it by a friend, and this boundary does not have a physician in it.**

"**Write it in a clerk's hand and put it next to the count of eleven and the name, and put it next to the sill and the hearth stone and the word eleven children said in a corridor on Friday, and then somebody in this building is going to have to say what a plan is for, and I would like that somebody to be somebody who is not me and not the cook and not the man who recommended it.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the sentence was said in this corridor by the schoolteacher and that she said she did not know whose it was, and I know whose it is, and I am not going to say, and I am entering that I know and am not saying and that this is the first thing I have entered this week that I have any business not saying.**

"**And I am entering that four people in this building have been doing that man's work since the first week of the twentieth week without knowing it, and they are not going to be told by him, because he is not here, and one of them has just said his rule out loud in a corridor and thinks she invented it.**"

---

**The door was opened at the fourth bell, and the reason it was opened was a child on a step, and the reason the reason was allowed was that a woman with a slate said the condition was hers and lifted it.**

"**Say it properly,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I want it in the form a person in a year can hold.**

"**The condition is mine and I am lifting it,**" said Renn Vale, standing on the landing, "**and it has not been met, and I am lifting it on a child's foot, and I am not going to be blamed for it, because the rule says the person who writes a change to a room is not to blame for it, and I wrote the condition and I am the one who keeps it and that is the whole of the protection the rule gives me and I am going to take it.**

"**And I am not going to be comfortable. I have been in this building twenty-five weeks and I have spent five of them being careful and I would like somebody to write down that being careful was not enough and that the word for what I am doing now is not careful.

"**And I want the two people who laid the stone to be in this room and I want them to say that the joint is in that door and not in the wall, so that a person in a year knows that the repair was in the sill and that the sill is in the thing that opens.**"

"**It is in the sill and the sill is in the door,**" said Iven Tallo. "**And I laid it and I am not taking it out and I am not going to be asked to. And I want it entered that I am the man who put a packing in a door and that the same door has a child's foot under a stone in it this morning, and that those are two facts about one afternoon and they do not argue with each other, and that I am not going to have anybody in this room use one of them to get rid of the other.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered all of it, and I am going to read out the count of the boundary's own hours at the sixth bell and the count in the column with nothing over the top of it, and the crew are at fifty-six and a half and have not moved since Thursday for the reason in my hand, and the boundary is at two hundred and eleven and a quarter, and that is four today and ninety-four since the keeper signed, and it adds by the day the work was done, and both columns reconcile and they have reconciled for twelve weeks.**

"**And a man of twenty-two is sixty-one days and sixty-one nights into a count that is his own, and he is at the fourth step with a tally in his hand, and he has put his hand on that hearth stone twice today and did not say anything about it either time, and I have entered that he did and that he said nothing and that I did not ask him, and that is the second day running I have not asked him, and I am not going to say why and I am not going to be asked.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked, because it is walked when a thing has happened that a room will want to argue about, and the thing that had happened that day was a child on a step.**

"**Fourteen entries would be the wrong number and I am going to say why I have not put it up,**" said Tams Quill, with the chalk in his left hand and his right one open and flat in his lap, "**and the reason is not the one anybody in this room is expecting, and I have got three reasons and I have not chosen, and I told you on Thursday that I would say the reason when I had chosen it and not one day before, and it is not Thursday.**

"**Thirteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark. Nine in the ordinary column and four in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim is a claim and its condition is not met. The mark is on this board and is not a null and is not a reading and I gave the reason for it and the reason holds.

"**And the thing that happened this morning at the kitchen-line end of this corridor is not going on this board. I have been standing here a quarter of an hour with the chalk and I have put it in my pocket twice and I am telling the room why: a hearth stone and a child's foot are not a thing that happened to a boundary, they are a thing that happened to a person, and this board is a record of what has happened to this boundary, and the day this board starts being a record of what has happened to the people in it is the day it stops being worth walking, and I will not be the man who changes that on a Monday in the same hour as a child.**"

That was a different reason from any he had given. Dalla Sen entered it, and entered that it was not one of the three he had, and entered that the count had said on Thursday that he had three reasons and had not chosen between them, and that on the Monday of a week later he had given a fourth without choosing, and that the clerk was entering the four of them and was not going to say which was which.

"**Then the column is still not headed,**" she said.

"**The column is still not headed,**" said Tams Quill, "**and I am still not going to say the reason, and I have said that in front of a room that has had four of them this week and I would like it entered that the count has now given four and has not chosen and is not going to, and that is the whole of what is known about it.**

"**And I want somebody to notice that a man who has four reasons and will not choose is not the same man as a man who has none, and that I said on Thursday I would say it when I had chosen, and that is still true, and that I have got a week, and that this week was the week I was going to do it in and I have not done it, and that I would like the room to understand that the week getting away from me is the reason and not one of the four.**"

The room let it stand. The anteroom above them was exactly the room it had been for twenty-five weeks — ten including one litter for no more than two hours, a packed frame, a head a finger and a bit out of true, and a second way out that was a word.

**And at the kitchen-line end of the corridor, a room that had been closed over a weekend with eleven people in it stood open with a packed sill and a cold hearth and nine hearth stones, one of which had a hand's width of daylight under its low end, and a child of nine was sitting on the fourth step with her foot across the clerk's knee and a strip of a crate board bound to it with a piece of an old apron, and the boundary had a name and a number and both of them were correct, and the plan in four parts in a coat had no row for that room on it and was not going to get one this week.**
