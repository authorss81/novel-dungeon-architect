# Chapter 15 — The Guild Line Is Closed

The courier left through the inspection incline while the rest of them watched from the anteroom.

He had one key and a written count of the papers he carried. The old blue seal went into his inner coat. The lien, insurance notice, and Dael Harrow’s custody order went into the satchel. Before he turned the wheel, he took out a narrow strip of guild paper and placed it beside Iven’s maintenance sheet.

“Receipt for the suspended bond,” he said. “Your witness privilege is frozen until the custody hearing. Any Cartwright inspection that depends on your name is invalid until the guild restores it. This is not the route order. It is the cost of signing the other sheet.”

Iven read the strip without touching it.

“The suspension is separate from the custody order,” the courier said. “One question is whether the guild may hold the route. The other is what happens to a survey record that never got closed. Form 7-B is still sitting unsigned in a Marrowgate office, and it will be sitting there after the hearing. Whoever eventually signs it will be certifying a partial closure no one has tested.”

“That is Marrowgate’s paper,” Marek said. “Not ours to settle from this room.”

“It is also the paper that names who answers for the collapse,” the courier said. “You are below without a signed certificate. That is not a guild matter, but it is a record, and records travel.”

“Can you still inspect guild work?” Dalla asked.

“Not with the guild behind the inspection. I can look at timber, show a joint, and say what I did. The guild does not have to accept my answer.”

“Will they send someone else?”

“When the surface door opens, if it opens, they can appoint another witness. Dael does not have to use me.”

The courier’s expression stayed professionally blank. “The surface door will remain closed. The pressure dial, route lamp, upper-catch chain, and guild tool case are frozen in place. The order does not touch the Hearthhold pump or the residents’ stock.”

Iven looked at Nera. “He is saying the guild cannot reach the pump.”

“I am saying the order cannot reach the pump,” Nera replied. “The guild can still refuse us a spare seal, a record, or a rescue crew. Air does not become local because the water is.”

Sefu stood beside the civic wheel. “And the boundary does not become guild territory because the inspection door is locked. Nera controls the air mechanism. I control admission at this boundary. The order controls neither.”

The courier turned the wheel. From the anteroom, the sound of metal teeth engaging came through the thick door, followed by a heavy bar dropping on the far side. The surface route was physically closed. No one could use it to send for help, fetch a part, or appeal Dael’s order without first reopening the guild-controlled door.

The courier called through the metal, “The hearing will note that the repair continued after the order.”

“Then note that the pump continued,” Iven said.

The footsteps went up the incline and faded.

For a while, nobody spoke. The anteroom had always seemed small because it had too many people in it. Now it felt small because the door beyond it had become an absolute fact. A paper could be argued with. A locked door only needed a key.

Iven picked up the narrow receipt. His thumb rubbed across the guild seal until the edge of the paper began to bend.

Marek knew the look. It was the expression of a man discovering that a signature had weight after he had already put it down.

“Explain it to them,” Dalla said. “Not the guild language. The work.”

Iven set the receipt beside the pump record. “The custody order asked for a route witness. I refused that and signed the maintenance sheet instead. I identified the split gland, the missing drive-dog key, the retained delivery check, the frame foot, and the temporary timber lever. I fitted the seal and key with Oren and Tams carrying the frame. Nera kept the air chamber isolated and set the test. Jo cleaned the delivery seat under my instruction. The pump drew flooded water for a witnessed draw-down. Its delivery check chatters, so it is attended.”

“Did you claim the route?” Dalla asked.

“No. Did I claim the pump? No. Did I claim that the Hearthholds owe the guild for keeping it running? No.”

“Could Dael use your name to say you accepted custody?”

“He can try. The guild can say the work helped the route. That is true. It cannot turn that fact into permission from the people who maintain the air, gate, or rooms.”

Iven’s voice stayed level, but his hand kept bending the receipt. Marek recognized the effort it cost to explain a choice to the people who might report it.

Dalla took the paper from him before it creased. “I will record the sequence. If the guild asks, you have told me what you did and what you refused.”

“Report me if the work is wrong,” Iven said.

Jo looked up from the pump grate. “If you hide a mistake, that is a different report.”

“There was no hidden mistake.”

“There is a crooked retaining bolt,” she said. “You showed me. That is not hidden.”

Iven’s mouth twisted. “It is a poor bolt.”

“It is a fact.”

Marek looked at the locked incline. The guild order had not opened a door, ordered air, or changed the anteroom’s bearing. It had made the cost of failure more distant and more personal. If the pump failed, no guild rescue would arrive. If the gland needed replacing, the local stock might not contain the right seal. If a record disappeared, the only account would be the one written by the people standing in the room.

He could still make a route plan that used the surface line for rescue. He could still pretend the legal order was a delay rather than a constraint. The locked door made the lie inconvenient, not false.

“Write down what we have locally,” he said. “Not what the guild might release.”

Dalla opened the maintenance ledger. Nera listed the air cloth, baffle, valve, and stop conditions. Jo named the daily tools and the new shutter pin. Tams counted the three short wedges and the two repair pieces. Iven added the installed gland and key, the lever, and the condition of the lower cache.

“The spare seal is not in the gallery,” Nera said. “The next full seal is in the Hearthhold stores, if the stores choose to release it. The drive-dog key is the only one we have.”

“And the pressure dial in the guild case?” Marek asked.

“Locked,” Nera said. “The Hearthholds have their own air gauges. They are not better, but they are ours to read.”

Iven looked toward the surface door. “Then the route cannot depend on a guild part being released after the hearing.”

“Correct,” Nera said.

“Or on the inspection door opening for a rescue crew,” Sefu added. “If the pump fails, we do not count on a surface rescue. We use the stop conditions and decide whether the crew withdraws or the boundary closes.”

Marek looked at the service bypass. It was physically continuous, but behind it the Cinder Steps were flooding. A retreat would mean carrying Leena’s litter, helping Pell over uneven stone, and leaving behind whatever the local shift could not move. It would be a costly route, not an escape.

“Write that as a cost,” he said. “If the guild refuses future parts, records, or rescue, we do not raid the stores and we do not call the locked door open. We use the local stock until it is safe to continue. If the pump cannot be kept safely, Sefu can close the boundary and Nera can stop the air. We decide then whether the crew withdraws toward the Cinder Steps.”

“Or the Hearthholds keep the route for their own work,” Jo said.

“Or that.”

Sefu nodded. “The decision cannot depend on the guild order being harmless. It depends on what we can do without it.”

Marek had expected the legal order to produce a fight. Instead, it produced a list. The order was a pressure that changed the route’s future; it was not the force holding the pump in place.

Iven picked up his guild badge from the bench. The blue cord was stained with pump oil. He looked at it for a moment, then put it into his pocket rather than laying it on the work sheet.

“I am not leaving the guild today,” he said. “I am refusing to let the guild put this repair under its custody. Those are different things.”

Dalla wrote both sentences beneath the record. “Then the report can use them.”

At the next pump interval, the delivery check chattered. Tams began the lever strokes, Oren took the relief, and Jo watched the water mark. Nera kept the air cloth visible from the gate. Sefu watched the threshold. Marek listened to the pump, the locked incline, and the people who would have to keep the water down if no one else came.

The local shift continued.
