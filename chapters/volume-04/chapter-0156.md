# Chapter 156 — The Air Engineer Asks The Engineer

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the thirty-first week, a Monday, and the school did not run and lost nothing, and the woman who keeps the air asked a man who keeps nothing for three hours of a work, in a room, with the terms out loud, and named the three she was not going to ask and the reason for each before she asked him anything, and she said the cost of the asking out loud before anybody else could say it after her.**

**The air at the low end of a corridor goes down the channel before the water does; the water has been off the fill since the second day of this week; the page that says somebody stands there is blank; she is not going to send anybody; and she cannot be at a valve and under a floor at the same time.**

Nera Oduya had the valve board in her own hands at the first bell and she had a piece of paper in her own writing on it, which is what an air engineer's sheet is in a building with no paper in it, and she read it out in a room with nine people in it and she read it as an instruction and not as an opinion.

"**I am going to ask a man in this room for a piece of work and I am going to do it in this building's own practice, and I am going to say the practice out loud first, because I have taken it four times off people and I have never once offered it, and a person who only ever takes a practice is not a person who believes in it.**"

"**The terms are said out loud. A person is named who can be told no, and that person is Renn Vale and she is on the landing and she can hear me. And the three who are not going to be asked are named with the reason for each before I ask anybody anything.**"

"**Iven Tallo. He has been asked four times in thirty weeks and he has four hours in the crew's column and he came down four days of road in a fortnight and I am not going to ask him twice in a month. The reason is not that he is tired. The reason is that a man who has just stood in a store is the only person in this building who has touched this beech, and I am asking a different man to look at a different thing and I am not going to have the two of them in a room together on a Monday.**"

"**Pell Roke. He is fourteen and the reason is not his age. The reason is that he reads numbers and I am not asking for a number. On Wednesday he is going to read fourteen lines out of a store's book and I have watched that man hold a column of figures in a room and make it be true, and I am not going to stand at the other end of a figure in a room with him.**"

"**Tams Quill. His right hand is open and flat and it is not a working hand and that has not changed and I am not going to pretend it has. The reason is that he is the man who reads the board and I have decided in the last half hour that this particular work is going to be entered somewhere, and I would like the man who enters it to be a person who is not the man who is doing it.**"

Then she said the half that was against herself, and she said it standing, and nobody in that anteroom interrupted her.

"**Here is why I am asking, and it is not that I need hands, because I have got Renn and I have got Jo and I have got a pump that two people can work, and none of those three is what I need.**"

"**I need somebody to go and stand at the low end of that corridor and look at the air there. From above. Not from inside it. Not in the channel. From the floor, at the end, at the frame, and look at what is coming off it, and come back and tell a room what it is, and do not tell me first.**"

"**The water went off that fill on Tuesday afternoon and it has not been back. The air at that end goes down the channel before the water does, and that has been true for thirty weeks, and I have had my eye on it every day of them, and I have no figure for it and I have not published a rate in thirty weeks and I am not going to publish one now.**"

"**What has changed is that for four weeks something has been pushing the air at that end, and the pushing was the water, and the pushing has stopped, and I do not know what is happening at the low end of this building now and I have not been there and I am not going to be.**"

"**And the reason I am not going is not that I am frightened of the channel, and I want that said in a room. It is that I have spent thirty weeks being the only person in this building who may turn a wheel, and if I go down to that floor then for as long as I am on it I am a person standing at the low end of a corridor, and a person standing there is a post, and a post is what a blank page becomes when somebody fills it in.**"

"**So I am not going. And the man who is not going to go is the man who does not keep a room, does not stand a watch, is not on a rota, is not one of the five, and is not a resident of this Hearthhold by anything except that he is standing in it.**"

Dalla Sen had the book open and said what she was going to do before she did it.

"**I am entering that the terms were said out loud, that the three not asked were named with the reason for each before the asking, and that a person was named who can be told no, and I am entering that the practice in this book is four askings old and this is the fifth and it is the first one that has not been run by me.**"

Then Marek Venn said the thing that was the actual cost, and he said it out loud before she had to, because he had been on the step with his left hand open on his knee and he had heard the whole of it.

"**Say the second half of it, Nera, because I have known what you were about to do for about a minute and so has everybody else in this room and I am not going to let you get to it late.**"

"**I was not going to get to it late. I was going to get to it at the end, and you are not the clerk, and Dalla is the clerk.**"

"**Then say it, and Dalla can enter it, and I would rather it were said than entered.**"

"**Fine.**" She turned round from the valve board. "**The cost. The asking puts a shape on you that you do not currently have. I have been the only person in this building who may ask the air for something for thirty weeks and I have not asked anybody once, and this morning I asked a man, and the shape of a man somebody has asked for something is a standing, and a standing is the first half of a post.**"

"**I have thought about that for a day and a night and I am doing it anyway, and I would like it in your clerk's book in those words and not in better ones, and I would like the word *standing* in it and not the word *help*, because it is not help and I have heard it called help twice this month by people who meant something else.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in her own words, with the word she wanted and not the word I would have used. And entered that she said the cost out loud before anybody else could say it after her, and entered that a man on the step asked her to say it early, and entered that I am not going to improve on her sentence with a reason, because she has been doing this in this building for thirty weeks and I am not the person who gets to explain her to a book.**"

"**Say yes or no,**" said Nera Oduya, **"and say it in a room and not to me.**"

"**Yes.**" He did not get up off the bench. "**And I am going to say three things about it and one of them is against me.**"

"**One. I have been standing in this building for thirty weeks telling rooms that nobody is a post while its condition is not happening, and I have said the word *post* in this anteroom more times than any other word I have, and I have said it about myself, and I said ten days ago that no page was going to get my name on it. And this morning the shape of me changed and I did not have to do anything dishonest for it to change.**"

"**Two. I have been asking this room for a month what it is going to do about a corridor with a hole in it, and the answer I have wanted has been *somebody stands there*, and the answer that is actually available is *somebody goes and looks and comes back*. Those are not the same and I have been pretending for a month that they are, because the second one is slower and does not look like anything.**"

"**Three, and this is the one against me. I will go. And the reason I will go is not that you are the air authority and I am not the air authority and you have asked the man with nothing. It is that I want to go.**"

"**Enter that last one,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that I entered it, and enter that a man who is not a post has just said in a room that he wants to be asked, and that is the first entry of that shape in this book in thirty weeks, and that I am not going to put a reason beside it.**"

Then the clerk had to decide something she had been putting off since the twenty-eighth week, and she did it in a room and gave the reason out loud, and the reason was the same one from a fortnight ago and she said so.

"**Where it goes. A work asked for in a room with the terms out loud is hours, and hours go in a column, and there are two columns on this board and they are not the same column and I have been clerk for thirty weeks and I know which is which.**"

"**It is not the crew's. The crew are five and he is one of the five and he has not been asked as one of the five, and putting it in that column would hand the five of them an hour and take it off him and I am not going to do that to a man who has said no post with his whole body for a month.**"

"**It is the boundary's. And the reason is the reason I gave in the twenty-eighth week and I am going to give it in the same words so that anybody in a year can see it has not moved: a man is not an hour. A man's hour is a thing a person can be given and taken back, and there is no version of this in which anybody in this room gives that man an hour today.**"

"**And the second reason is new and it is this. She is not the crew. She has never once asked the five of them for anything in thirty weeks and she has said in this room that she is not going to start. If the boundary's column is where a work goes when nobody in this building owns it, then this is what that column is for, and I have been putting other things in it for a month without knowing what it was.**"

"**Enter that, and enter that I have been putting things in that column for a month without knowing what it was, and enter that a woman with a valve board just told me what it is.**"

Then the question came back, and nobody had asked for it, and it had been sitting in a clerk's book for ten days in the shape of a question and not in the shape of a finding.

Dalla Sen read it out, and read it as she had written it, and said that.

"**You said on the fourth day of last week that you were not a post and that no page was going to get your name on it, and I entered it, and I read it out in this room on the Tuesday, and nobody argued with it. And I asked you one thing at the seventh bell of the fifth day of the twenty-ninth week and I did not ask you twice, and it is in the book as a question and not as a finding, and it is this: what are you going to do when the weather turns?**"

"**I am going to go and look at the air at the low end of that corridor on Wednesday morning with a lamp in my left hand,**" he said, "**and stand at the frame and look at what is coming off it, and come back up nine steps and say in a room what it is.**"

"**And after that?**"

"**After that I do not know,**" he said. "**And I am not going to pretend to this room that I have worked it out in a fortnight because a woman asked me a question about a post.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**the first half and not the second half, and I am entering the reason for that, and the reason is that the second half is the question and the question has not been answered and there is no version of this in which a clerk answers a man's question for him by writing one down in his own book. So the first half is a work and it is in the boundary's column with a reason. And the second half is a question, and it stays a question, and I am not going to enter a reason in the space where one would go, and I have had a fortnight of wanting to.**"

Then Nera Oduya said the thing about her own condition, and she said it to a room and not to a man, and she said it before anybody could ask her.

"**I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since the fourth day of the eleventh week in a room, and I am going to say the half of it that is about the condition and not the half of it that is the theory, because the theory is not owed to anybody in this room and no room may do the asking.**"

"**My condition is that I will say it when this boundary is keeping itself. And I have been looking for that for eighteen weeks and telling this room every week that it has not been met, and I have been getting more accurate and more annoying about it, and Bel has been getting more accurate and more annoying back, and I want that on the page in both our hands because we have both been enjoying it.**"

"**And here is what has happened in the last three days. This boundary now keeps a brace it has not built. It keeps a store four days up a road that it does not own and that a man can stop. It uses a road it does not control, on which a piece of paper is going to come back on a board, and a schedule is going to be a thing it has to be in rather than a thing it can refuse.**"

"**That is keeping itself. I have been waiting twenty weeks for something and that is something, and I am not going to stand in a room and say that my condition is met, because it is not, and I have never been met, and I do not think I am ever going to be met in the way I said it would be.**"

"**Because keeping itself and being asked to open a road while something is going wrong four days up a passage are not the same sentence, and the one where the second half happens is the one that kills somebody, and I have been asked for nothing for thirty weeks and I am not going to start by being asked to open a gate.**"

"**So I am setting a second condition, and it is a condition and not a number, and there is no day on it, and I am not asking anybody to agree to it or to argue with it and I am not asking anybody in a room anything at all.**"

"**Here it is, and I am saying it out loud so that it is in the book before anybody needs it. I will not take it back while it is being watched.**"

"**Say that in the book in your own words and say it again,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say why you are saying it twice.**"

"**Because the first one will be a sentence and the second one will be a fact, and this boundary has spent thirty weeks learning that a sentence can be lifted off a page and a fact cannot, and I would like a room to hear me say the same thing twice and to be given the reason for it by the woman who asks for a figure read against an original.**"

"**And nobody is to ask me what the condition is about,**" said Nera Oduya, from the second step. "**Not you, not him, not the woman who was going to be glad about something in the twenty-ninth week. There is a reason and it is mine and I am not handing it over because a boundary has been good to me.**"

Nobody asked. Dalla Sen entered the two readings and entered that they were the same, and entered that nobody in the room had asked, and entered that she had noticed she wanted to and had not.

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the count was read, and the board was not walked, and the reason she gave for two of those three was a man who had said yes to something.**

"**Three today and a hundred and eighty-two since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and ninety-nine and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks. The crew are at sixty-six and a half.**"

"**I entered those and I did not read them, and I am not going to say the reason is dignity, because I have used that word three times in a month and I have been wrong about it twice.**"

"**The reason is that a woman asked a man in this room for three hours of a work at the first bell and a man said yes in a room and said afterwards that he wanted to be asked, and I put that in the book in his words with no reason beside it, and I am not going to read a number out loud in the same breath as that. It is the same rule I have been using all month and I have finally got a reason for it that is not a mood.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is not on the fill and nobody has told me where it went. The count. Wen Pask is ninety-one days and ninety-one nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be ninety-two, and I have said in six weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and I have written it in six weeks running and nobody has thanked me in six weeks running.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is the count's and not mine, and here it is in his words because he said it out loud at the sixth bell and I am entering it as he gave it: a man has just been asked to do a thing in this building and I am not going to put a column of what other people found next to it in a room in the hour somebody asked him.**"

**On the second day of this week a post is going to come up the middle passage with Iven Tallo's name on it, and a name on a paper is the whole of the difficulty, and the guild's fourth paragraph is coming back for the fourth time and this time it pays in coin.**

**On the third day of this week the nine go in for the twenty-fifth morning and a boy of fourteen reads fourteen lines out of a store's book, and a man with a lamp in his left hand is going to stand at the frame at the low end of a corridor and look at what is coming off it, and he is going to come back up nine steps and say it in a room.**

**An air engineer has set a second condition on something she has carried for twenty weeks, and said it out loud twice, and nobody asked her what it was about, and it is in a clerk's book in her own hand and not on a page.**
