## Immediate obligation for Volume 02, Batch 0002

- **OT-41 (partly discharged, partly open).** **A post under the near landing's lip.** The work was **named by the boundary** — Sefu Aran, keeper, in the tenth week: *a shore under the near landing's lip, so that the route can be used on a day when the bearing hand is Jo Verrick and Jo Verrick is on the lever.* The fifth descent of the flooded gallery happened on a **fourth air document**, Jo Verrick went down, the **five foot nine came up** and was **received by the keeper with a time on it**, and it is now the head of a **second shore on the common landing**. The lip's movement came down from a range of *a finger and three-quarters to two inches* to **about a finger and a half**. **Still open:** the **post** is not built, wants nine foot and a bundle, and is the **tenth** item on the unresolved list (the seat and the check are the eleventh); the shore is **packed and not wedged**, and Renn Vale's seventh term on the slate says in her own hand that the lip is not held, that the next time the slab turns somebody will be under it, and that it will be Jo Verrick.
- **OT-47 (new, and it is the batch's own named next step):** **The delivery check is a hiss and there is no spare.** The draw of Chapter 53 stopped the check chattering; it is now flat and continuous and is read by ear with a two-note vocabulary — a hiss that stays a hiss, and a hiss that gets *thinner*, and thinner means the seat. **Jo Verrick has said out loud that the seat goes before the cold**, that there is no bronze in this building, that the replacement is hardwood, that hardwood is a third of the life, and that she does not have the three things she needs: a length of hardwood, a man who can cut it, and a man who can hold it. **She has also said out loud that the man who holds it cannot be Tams Quill and cannot be Marek Venn**, on the grounds that a seat you cannot feel is a seat you cannot tell has gone wrong. The estimate stands at **two cleanings or fewer**, unmoved in eleven weeks. **The hiss went thin on the tenth-week Thursday, did not come back to a full note, and the seat is now going rather than going in the cold.**
- **OT-48 (new):** **The word *once* has been decided and a card has been spent.** Nera Oduya's first gallery card still carries *once*. The room agreed, in Renn Vale's words and on Dalla Sen's page, that **a card is a permit for a work and is spent when the work is finished or called off**, not when a trip fails; the fourth document carried the word **spent** and was used three times. The card is now spent in both books. **Any further descent of that gallery needs a fifth card, and nobody has written one, and the fourth card's own line says the next one will be a different method because it was written for a machine that was chattering.**
- **OT-49 (new, and it is the volume's question, now asked out loud):** ***Who keeps the Hearthhold rooms.*** Asked by **Ilsa Norr** in the anteroom in the tenth week, in front of the room, and **not answered**. Corva Nye, a Cartwright route-custody clerk, put the form on the bench and showed that it has a line for the holder of a route, a line for the charter, a line for the date it was inspected, a line for the keeper of a *workshop*, and **no line for a woman who teaches nine children and pays for a floor out of her own hand and shuts a door because she decides to.** The clerk wrote that in the margin of her own form in her own hand: *a form with no place for a keeper has no place to write a keeper, and my office will read that as a route with nobody on it.* **Dalla Sen recorded what a person reading it in a year will need: the week, the room, the asker, the sentence about the dowel, and the fact that a clerk from an office was present — because in a year there will be a clerk from an office and this time he will know what to ask for.**
- **OT-50 (new):** **The nine register boxes and the plan on a dowel.** The boxes are in a locked box in the school store under Ilsa Norr's key, and they hold attendance rolls, a fuel book, a lintel that is not a lintel, four inches of copper, ground maps of the surface, and **one plan rolled on a dowel with a tape round it**, on its own box, never opened. Corva Nye asked properly and in front of the room to see them, and **Ilsa Norr refused again, in her own words, in front of the same people, and gave the reason**: everything else in the box is a thing somebody did and finished, and *that plan is a decision about what this building is*, and she will not make it in the week after a man died under a stair. **It is agreed out loud, recorded with a date, and the key turns in three weeks. The terms will be said by the person who keeps the key, and the crew will not be in the room.** Nera Oduya has said she will be in the room and will be the only person in it who cannot be trusted with what is on it.
- **OT-51 (new):** **The knock in the throat.** In Chapter 58, at the second minute of the nineteen strokes, from about eleven feet down, Nera Oduya heard **a flat, dry, wooden knock with a slide on the end of it** — a bar in a staple, a hand leaving a door frame — and an air push out of the **north-east cheek of the throat at about knee height**, which is where air comes out of a doorway and not out of a wall. **Her four conditions**, given in the room and on the page: (1) the post's work is scoped to what can be seen from the fixed first landing and **nobody goes under that stone**; (2) the **four treads are not walked this month**; (3) the throat is **sounded, not entered**, once a day at the second bell, with a bar, by a person, and the answer written down whatever it is, **including nothing**; (4) the **review is brought forward** to the first work-bell after a sound from the throat, a thin hiss, or anybody writing the word *out* on the slate. **There are two knocks and they are not the same knock.** The first is the one Nera heard at the lip on the Sunday morning at the second minute of the nineteen strokes. The second is the one **Bel Ordry's bar found against the north-east cheek** on the third morning of the daily sounding. They are recorded separately, in separate hands, and Bel Ordry said so out loud and the clerk read both entries back. **Nobody has been down there. Nothing was identified.**
- **OT-52 (new):** **A guild page exists and it is the beginning of a wall's life being numbered.** Corva Nye's report went up on foot with a form and a margin. The margin names the stair. **Iven Tallo's assessment is that the boundary's own page was the answer and the guild's page is the first nail**, and that the correct response is **nothing** — no second page, no seventh line on the route's slate, because the day the slate gets a line added to answer a clerk is the day it stops being a thing the boundary keeps and becomes a document the boundary defends. **He also says the wall is four habits and a rope and a clerk who says no, and that it will cost somebody something that is not written down anywhere, and that this is the beginning of that.** **The claim is not resolved and must not be resolved in the next batch.**
- **OT-53 (new):** **Two springs.** Sefu Aran has held that the first spring conversation is between the keeper and the air engineer about a **named air hand-over**, that it happens **whether or not anybody is leaving**, and that it should happen in a month when everybody knows which spring it is. Talla Roke asked for the crew's hours, by name, for a named work, in the spring, for the second gate's inlet. Marek Venn has said out loud that there are two springs, has promised neither, and has admitted to a room, twice, on Sefu's instruction, that he has been planning a private two-o'clock solution with nobody's name on it. **Talla Roke is gone and her cost is on the page: four households in the second gate carrying water up four sets of steps this winter.**
- **OT-54 (new):** **Renn Vale has refused a Cartwright observer, and refused to withdraw either half of the answer.** Corva Nye asked properly, in the anteroom, in front of the keeper and Renn, to sit at the top of nine steps with a slate and watch a clerk write a number. Renn said no, gave reasons — a second opinion over a crossing where a woman with a mother and a room is doing the string, and a person who is going to write a report is not the same as a person who is only there — and added that if the office wants to know whether the boundary can keep the route then the office can come in the cold, and be asked, and may be asked not, and carry water, and nobody writes a report. **Both halves are on the page on purpose and neither is withdrawn.**
