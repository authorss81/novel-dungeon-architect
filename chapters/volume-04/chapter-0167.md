# Chapter 167 — The Condition Is Met

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the thirty-third week, a Tuesday, and the school did not run and lost nothing, and a condition with no number and no day on it was found to be met, and a second person read it out against the original word for word and compared the two, and a room admitted it out loud, and nothing had been stood under the brace.**

**A room's rating is a word said to a page. The word is *door*. A brace now goes across the head of one of the four doors in that anteroom, and a man who keeps the page said out loud that nobody in this building has checked whether the word is about the same thing it was about last week, and a clerk repeated it, and a room found out what it now means.**

Mero Senn came down the nine steps at the first bell with his bag and set it against the wall in the worst room in the building, which is the anteroom, and said what he was before he was asked, and then said the thing he had come for, which was that he had read the wall nine times and it had read nothing twice and he did not know what that was.

"**I am not going to tell the room what I think it is. I have been in this building eleven days and I have learned that a man in this room who tells a room what he thinks has four of the room with him by the end of the sentence.**"

"**Say what you have instead of a conclusion,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I have a shape.**" He had his own hand flat on the wall, not touching the string. "**Eight readings at a quarter of an inch, and then nothing, and then nothing. A wall that has been finding out what it weighs does not stop finding out because a piece of beech has been cut. It either finds out and stops, or something is holding it, and nothing is holding it, and I have looked.**"

"**Say the sentence that is under that,**" said Marek Venn.

"**The sentence is that a wall can stop moving for a week and a wall can stop moving for good, and there is no instrument in this building that tells those two apart, and the physician's instrument is a piece of string and a piece of chalk and a woman with a folding rule who has said out loud that she does not want to make a number.**"

"**Then say what you want,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing.

"**I want the condition met or not met, and I want it met in a room, out loud, by the two people who have to say so, and I want it met before a person is under that thing.**"

The schoolteacher was already in the doorway. She had come down four steps on a shut Tuesday, which she does not do, and she stood with her back to the wall of the room she keeps, on the other side of the party wall, in the part of the anteroom where the lamplight does not reach.

"**Ask us,**" she said. "**Both of us. And read the thing out first, because I wrote it a fortnight ago and I have not looked at it since and I have a right to hear what it says.**"

Dalla Sen read it out of her own book, word for word, and then the schoolteacher said whether that was what she had said in the room on the fourth day of the thirty-first week, and then Renn Vale read it a second time against the original, and the two were the same.

"**Compare it and say whether it is the same,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in front of everybody, because that is the practice and because the two of you are about to say a thing out loud on the strength of it.**"

Renn Vale compared them, and the clerk read the original one more time, and the two of them were the same, and the schoolteacher said that they were the same, and neither of them improved on it.

"**Entered, and entered that the condition is on a brace and not on a room, and that it has no number and no day on it, and that it has been read out and compared by a second person. This is the third time this month a thing in this building has been put against an original, and I am not going to number them, because the last clerk who numbered them got it right and I have discovered I would rather get it right than be the one who said so.**"

Then Bel Ordry said the thing that made it a real morning, and he said it standing, with the plan in his coat and his hand flat on the stair rail.

"**The anteroom's terms are a count, a word, a pump, and two people who can stop it. The word is *door*, and the word is said in a room and repeated by the clerk, and the room's rating is restored when that happens, and I have said in this room for thirty-two weeks that a page in a coat is not a system and that I am the only person who has to say it.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have said the thirty-two weeks and you have not said the thing you came to say.**"

"**I have not said that a word can change what it is about.**"

There were a lot of people in that anteroom. The pump got two strokes in and nobody used them.

"**There are four doors out of this room. There have been four for thirty-two weeks. The word has meant the outer service door for thirty-two weeks, because that is the one a room empties through, and that is the door a body goes out of, and that is why the word is a second way out and not a decoration.**"

"**And now a brace is going across the head of that door,**" he said, "**and a brace does not make a door into a different door. It is still the same door. It opens the same way. But it is now a door with a thing over its head, and the thing over its head came out of a school store on a school door that has no wedge in it, and a room that says the word in six weeks is going to hear the brace in it and not only the door, and nobody has asked whether those are the same sound.**"

"**Say it as an objection and not as a discovery, because I keep the book and I know which of those two I am writing.**"

"**As an objection.**"

"**Then somebody has to object to it properly, and the person who has to object is the person who keeps the room, and she is standing in a doorway, and I am not going to ask her to do a thing in a doorway that she should do at a bench.**"

Nobody asked her. The clerk wrote for about twenty seconds and then stopped and said what she had written.

Dalla Sen read out what she had written without stopping to look at the page.

"**The anteroom's word is *door*, and it has been the same word for thirty-two weeks, and a brace is going across the head of the door the word is about. Nobody has checked whether the word is now about the same thing. The man who keeps the page raised it and the room has not answered it.**"

"**And where does that entry go,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**On a page in a coat. The fourth column stays empty. It is empty because a modal was struck out of it and replaced in a better hand, and that has been true for thirty-two weeks, and nothing that has happened this morning changes it.**"

"**Read the terms anyway,**" said Renn Vale, "**because a room that stops saying the word stops having a rating, and a room that has a brace over a door and no rating is a room that has decided it is fine without saying so.**"

"**The anteroom. Ten including one litter, no more than two hours. The outer service door. The pump attended. The air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it. And the word.**"

"**Door,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Say it again and mean it,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because the practice is that the word is said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and both of those have to happen, and if a room only says it out of habit then a habit is a rating and a habit can be kept by a person who is not in the room.**"

"**Door,**" said Bel Ordry, and the pump was running, and Jo Verrick was at the wall, and a boy was on the bottom of the nine steps, and nobody was under the brace.

Then Mero Senn put his refusal on the table, and he put it on the table because nobody had asked him to and because the anteroom was the worst room in the building and he had run out of smaller ones.

"**I have refused in writing to certify that schoolroom. Eleven days in this building and that is the only thing I have put anywhere, and it is in a clerk's book and not on a page, and I said arguable was worth more than safe in a building like this and that I was not going to be the man who says it is worth more than safe anywhere else.**"

"**Say what it is about now, because it is not about the same thing it was about on Thursday,**" said Marek Venn.

"**It is about a room that has a brace over it.**" He said it to the wall. "**A fortnight ago I refused to certify a room with a strip of crate board under a stone and a sill packed in clay. Today that room has a thing across the other side of its wall that pushes four tons into it through a beech shim on a stone bed, and the thing is sound, and I have said so, and the refusal has not changed and the reason for it has.**"

"**Say the reason for the reason,**" said Dalla Sen, and her hand had stopped on the chalk.

"**Because a fortnight ago I could name what was wrong with that room. A floor, a sill, a stone, a strip of board. I could point at a thing and say *this*. Today there is a thing on the other side of the wall, and the thing is four tons, and I cannot point at the thing it will hurt until somebody stands in the room and looks.**"

"**Then you want it stood in,**" said Ilsa Norr, from the doorway.

"**I want it stood in by somebody who is not me and not him, and I want it after the condition is met and not before, and I want the condition met in a room, out loud, the way you have just done it.**"

"**Then say that the two of us still agree,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because that is what the condition is. Not that it is safe. That the two of you still agree that the wall can take it. Say it in that shape and not in any other.**"

Mero Senn looked at the wall with the string on it, and then at the doorway where the schoolteacher was standing, and he did not take a long time about it, which is a thing he is careful about and which she noticed.

"**I still agree that the wall can take it. And I want it said that I have said that with a refusal to certify standing next to it, and that the two things are not cancelling.**"

Ilsa Norr did not answer immediately, and the room gave her the whole of the time it took, which is a thing it had learned from a woman on a landing.

"**I still agree that the wall can take it,**" she said. "**And I want three things said in the same breath, and I am not going to be talked out of any of them and I would like them written where I can see them.**"

"**Say them.**"

"**One: I have never liked that condition and I have said so in this room and I am not going to pretend I have come round to it. It is nearly free. A condition that costs nothing is a condition a person in a bad month decides is not worth saying.**"

"**Two.**"

"**Two: if it comes out, nine children are going to think the woman who keeps their room is frightened, and they will be right, and I will be wrong, and I am not going to be able to stand in that doorway and explain the difference. I said that a fortnight ago and nothing this morning has made it less true.**"

"**Three.**"

"**Three: he is right that I am glad the wall read nothing. I am the person whose agreement holds that thing up and I am glad, and I said on Monday in this room that I am the last person in this building who should be glad about a wall, and I have not stopped being glad, and I am saying it out loud today because the condition is nearly free and this is the one thing I can make it cost.**"

Then the clerk read the condition out one more time, and the two people compared it again, and the room was told that it was met, and nobody improved on the shape of it, and nothing was stood under.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The condition is met. It is met on the second day of the thirty-third week, in a room, out loud, by the two people it names, with a physician's written refusal to certify standing next to it, and with three things said by the woman who keeps the room that the clerk has entered in the space where a reason goes and not in a column.**"

"**And entered that it is the first condition in this building with no number and no day on it that has ever been found to be met, and that the finding is not safe, and that nobody in this room is entitled to read the word met as the word fine.**"

"**And entered that a man with a figure in a private book stood in a room today and did not say the figure, and that I have not asked him for it and am not going to, and that I have known for three weeks that his book has two walls in it.**"

"**And entered that the two people talking about a wall this morning were a physician and a schoolteacher, and that the man in the room with a number in his pocket was not the man the room asked, and that I would like a person in a year to understand that as a change and not as a loss.**"

Marek Venn did not say anything for about a minute.

He was aware, in that minute, of being the only man in that anteroom who wanted to be asked. Wanting it was the reason he had got a standing in the first place. A woman three steps off had handed it to him out loud and had named the cost before she did it, and she had set a limit on the air, and she was not going to be thanked, and he understood with something that was not quite shame that the reason she would not be thanked is him.

The figure was in the book. It was the only figure he had ever written down about a wall in this building, and he had given it to a woman and not to a room.

For a fortnight he had been waiting for a morning when saying it out loud would be the useful thing. The morning had come on a Tuesday with the pump running, and the room had asked somebody else, and the somebody else had been better at it, and he was going to have to live in a building where that was true for the rest of his life.

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and the board was walked at seventeen entries, and the count was read at one hundred and two, and the column with nothing over the top of it is unheaded for the eleventh time and the man who reads it said nothing about it and nobody asked him.**

"**Three today and two hundred and twelve since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and twenty-nine and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for sixteen weeks. The crew are at seventy-one.**"

"**I read those out because a condition was met in a room this morning and a number that has not moved in nine weeks has come off a seam and a brace has come out of a bench, and a room that stops counting on a morning like this is a room that has decided the two are the same kind of thing.**"

"**The pot is nine. The seam gave two this morning and the count is one hundred and two, and I am entering the two numbers next to each other with nothing between them because a clerk who arranges them is making a rhyme, and I have spent thirty-two weeks refusing to make rhymes.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and two days and one hundred and two nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and three, and I have said in twelve weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and I am writing it, and today is a day with a thing in it and I have not changed my face and I would like it entered that I did not have to work at it.**"

Tams Quill read the board with the chalk in his left hand and the right one flat and open in his lap, and named no day, and did not improve on the tenth reason and did not give an eleventh.

"**Seventeen on it. Eleven of them are nulls. Then one claim, one mark, one row, a plan, a condition and a store. Nine in the ordinary column and eight in the one with nothing over the top of it.**"

"**The condition on that board has been met. The claim's condition has not. Both of those are true this morning and I am not going to confuse them for a room because they are the same shape and one of them is eight weeks old and one of them is two hours old.**"

"**And the column is not headed, and it is the eleventh, and nobody has asked me and I have not been asked all week, and I am not going to start.**"

Dalla Sen read the seventeen entries out, read her own reading back, and entered that he had said nothing about the column, and that she had not asked, and that this was the fourth week in a row in which she had chosen not to ask and had entered the choosing.

The word is *door*, and a brace is going across the head of the door the word is about, and a man who keeps the page has asked in a room whether the word is now about the same thing, and the room has not answered, and the word was said twice anyway and the clerk repeated it.**
