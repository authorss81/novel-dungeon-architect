# Chapter 218 — The Warehouse And The Claim

The first day of the forty-second week is a Monday, the corridor is shut, and the school did not run and lost nothing, and a guild's salvage claim that had been lying squared on a trestle for a fortnight was taken off the trestle at the first bell and put face up in the middle of the table, and the reason it was put in the middle was that a hearing had been owed since the first day of the fortieth week and a hearing is a thing that happens on a day the counter is open.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the thirty-eighth reading, and the same as thirty-seven, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, and the clerk entered it in the same place she entered it on Sunday and entered that it was the same sentence and that the woman had said in advance it would be.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is twenty-three.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it was right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and a hearing was held in this room on the same morning and not one of the six terms was altered by the hearing, and the clerk entered that because somebody in a year is going to wonder. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth term is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The sixth was the pump, and a piece of paper on the trestle described the same pump house from four days away, and the clerk entered both of those and did not join them.

Nine people who had gone down a wet stair on Saturday afternoon came up it at about the seventh bell on Saturday night, and the last two of them came up about four hours after the first seven, and the clerk entered that nobody had watched them come and that the corridor is not a crossing place after the seventh bell on a shut day and that four of them came up a road off a passage in the dark and found their own way, and that this boundary had given them a corner of a cook's floor and a slate with nine names on it and had not given them a lamp and had said in a book on Saturday that it was not going to give them a lamp.

Then Seth Wray came up the nine steps at about the third bell with a coat he had not had on Saturday, and he came to the end of the trestle and did not sit down.

"**I said I would come when this room said and not before,**" he said. "**The room has said. I am here and I have the claim in my hand because it lives in my coat and it is the only paper in this building with a name on it that I did not put there.**"

"**Then read it,**" said Bel Ordry, "**all of it, from the top, and the clerk will follow you on the slate, and I want the whole sheet read because the last time it was in this room a woman read two lines of it and stopped.**"

Nella Bray had her slate and read it in a clerk's voice, which she can do and which she has now done twice in this building.

"**Made out in the ninth week. Sent to a middle gate. Off the board on a Friday. Returned up the middle passage on the Saturday with a seal on the corner of it.**"

"**That is the first four lines and they are about the claim and not about the ground,**" Bel Ordry said. "**Go on.**"

"**One. The ground: a gallery at the lower end of a passage off the second gate, with rooms off it.**"

"**Two. The person responsible for the load, in a clerk's hand, and there is a name in it, and I have read that name four times in three weeks and it is not a name this boundary knows.**"

"**Three. The salvage: water, timber, metal, salt-crust, and anything a person can take out and carry.**"

"**Four.**" Nella Bray stopped.

There was a line of chalk through the fourth line. It was one stroke, about four inches, and it was the cleanest thing on the sheet.

"**The fourth line is struck out,**" said Nella Bray, "**and it was struck out on the first day of the fortieth week before anybody asked, by a person who was in this room, and the reason was not given and has not been given since, and the clerk has entered that it is the fifth time and that nobody in the room said a word about it afterwards and that the not-saying was entered on purpose.**"

Nobody in that anteroom looked at the air engineer, and she was on the second of her three steps, and the clerk entered that nobody had looked and entered that she had noticed.

"**Then read the rest of it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Five. The extent.**" Nella Bray had to stop for a second and find her place, which she had never had to do in nineteen years at a counter. "**Five. The extent: the water stands against the sill of the store at the top of the middle passage, on the north side, and against the sill of the pump house below it, and at the foot of the passage off the second gate. That is the line as it is written. There is no figure on it. It is three places and the word *against* and it is written by somebody who has stood in all three.**"

The anteroom was very quiet.

"**Go on,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Six. The date on which the thing claimed was seen. It is a day and a month and a year and it is a fact about this form and about nothing else in this building, and no other instrument in this room may use it, and I am going to read it and I am going to say out loud that it does not go anywhere.**"

She read it.

"**Then the rest is the office's own hand,**" said Nella Bray, "**and it is four lines long and it is the four lines I read out loud on Saturday about a pumping station that has not been turned over in nine years, and it does not say the word water once.**"

Seth Wray put a hand flat on the table.

"**That is the claim,**" he said. "**I have carried it up four days and left it and I have never read the fifth line out loud to anybody, and I read it on the road in about the second hour because I could not stand carrying a thing I had not looked at, and I have been carrying that fifth line for eleven days and I have not been able to put it down and I have not been able to say why.**"

"**Say why,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Because it is a salvage claim and I have been reading it as one,**" said Seth Wray. "**Water, timber, metal, salt-crust, and a man with a foreman's bond is supposed to come back with a figure of what he took. And that fifth line is not a figure. That fifth line is a man writing down where a water line is in three places at once, and I have been a salvage foreman for twenty-three years and I have never once in my life put that on a form, and I want somebody in this room to understand that I did not write it, because I did not know it, and I have read a great many things off that form in my life without understanding them.**"

"**Then say the half against,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Say the half against that,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The half against is this boundary's and it has been in this room for eight weeks and it does not work, and I would like it entered today that it is still here and that it is still correct and that a hearing has not improved it by one word,**" said Bel Ordry. "**A road a person carries a load along is not a recognised road unless there is a named person responsible for the load. The rule is standing. The office is right about the rule. And a claim over a room full of water is a claim over a thing that cannot be carried, so the second line asks for a person responsible for a load on a ground where there is no load, and a form that cannot be answered correctly is a form that cannot be used, and that argument is correct and it has been correct since the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week, and on Saturday a woman from a counter said her own objection stopped holding the day a piece of timber went out on a cart, and the claim is answered anyway, by somebody competent, in a clerk's hand, four days away.**"

He put his hand flat on the sheet.

"**And now the part I came to say and I would like it said before anybody else says anything,**" Bel Ordry said. "**The fifth line of that form names three places. A schoolroom with a hearth stone in it, a pump house, and a store at the top of the middle passage. I put the third of those three on a line in this room on the fifth day of the fortieth week and I said I did not know it was on a line and that I would have to leave to find out. A child of nine then said the whole of it better than I had, and this clerk entered it as a guess with the word guess in the space where a reason goes.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Nella Bray.

"**The rest of it is that a piece of printed paper came up this passage four days before that and has had three places on it the whole time,**" said Bel Ordry. "**I have been out-forgiven by a number printed again with no line on it and I said in this room that being right about both halves was the worst thing that had happened to me here, and that was a fortnight ago, and I would like it entered that a fortnight is not long enough and that this is worse, and that I have spent forty weeks explaining to rooms that sentences do not travel, and a form has travelled, and it has arrived with a seal on it, and it is correct.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything, and the clerk entered it in the words of the man who said it, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that the fifth line of a guild's form described this boundary's water in three places and that no person in this room had written it and that the person who had written it was at a counter four days away and had filled it in correctly because that is what they are for.

"**Then this boundary's answer,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**It is heard,**" said Bel Ordry. "**It is not granted and it is not refused, and I am going to say why it is not refused on a Monday morning, and I said the same thing in this room a fortnight ago and I am not going to improve it: sending it back is a refusal, and a room is not allowed to refuse without a hearing, and a hearing has now happened, and the hearing has made it worse and not better, and I am going to hold the paper on this table until somebody in this room can say what the third thing is.**"

"**Then say the half against the holding,**" said Mero Senn.

"**The half against the holding is that a claim that is neither granted nor refused is still a claim, and it still has a seal on it, and it still has a name in the second line, and in a year a person at a counter four days away is going to open a file and find that this boundary has had the claim in a room and has done nothing with it, and the doing of nothing is going to look in the file exactly like the doing of something,**" said Bel Ordry. "**I have taught that lesson to three offices for forty weeks. I am now on the receiving end of it and I would like it entered that I am not going to be able to complain.**"

Then Marek Venn put his hand on the shut book and took it away again, and stood up, which he does about twice in a week, and the room turned round.

"**Then there is a question in this room and it has been in it for ten days and I have been the only person holding it,**" he said.

"**Entered, that he is speaking, and that he was not asked,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I am going to ask it, and I am going to ask it of the one man in this room who might be able to answer it, and I am going to say why I have not asked it before, and the reason is the reason I gave in this room ten days ago and I am not going to give a new one.**"

"**The reason is that the answer is a question,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The answer is a question and I had not asked it,**" Marek Venn said. "**The question is this. The hand that filled in the second line of that form is the hand at the counter this boundary has been dealing with for eight weeks, or it is another hand in the same office four days away. It is one of the two. There is no third thing. And I am asking it of you, Mr Wray, because you carried the form up four days and did not ask whose name was on it, and because you said on the fourth day of the fortieth week that a man who does not ask a question on a four-day walk is not a careful man but a man who has decided that what he finds out will be worse than what he does not know, and I would like to know whether that is what you did or whether somebody else did it for you.**"

Seth Wray looked at the form for a while.

"**I did not ask,**" he said. "**And I have thought about it every day since and I have got as far as this. I did not fill in the second line. I have never filled in a second line in my life; there is a clerk for that and there has been a clerk for that since before I was a foreman. The form came off the board with the name in it and a seal on it. If you are asking me whether the hand is the hand at the counter you have been dealing with, then I can tell you that I do not know and that I have been at four counters in nineteen years and I could not tell you the hand of a clerk I saw once.**"

"**Then say what you will do,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I will put the question at that counter,**" said Seth Wray. "**Not by letter. At the counter, out loud, to the person who is standing there. And I will not come up these nine steps with the answer unless this room says, and I will not put it in a coat and carry it down a passage, and if the answer is that the man at the counter struck a name into a form about a room nobody has been in, then that is between that man and whoever is responsible for his file, and it is not a thing for this room to hold.**"

"**And if the answer is that the man at the counter is the one who struck the number through on a market gate in the thirty-ninth week,**" said Bel Ordry, "**then what.**"

"**Then this room will be sitting where it has been sitting since the second week of the thirty-ninth week with two correct reasons and no way to use either of them,**" said Seth Wray, "**and I will say that to you at a counter and not in this room, because it is a thing about an office and not about a house.**"

"**Entered, in a foreman's words, and entered that a question was asked in this room and was not answered in this room, and entered that the man who asked it had it for ten days, and entered that the reason he gives for not asking it is the same reason he gave ten days ago and that the clerk has noticed that he keeps returning to the same reason and has not entered why,**" said Dalla Sen.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a room which has had a claim read whole for the first time in a fortnight is owed three numbers it can verify and a hearing is a thing that is done to numbers, not to feelings.

"**Five today and three hundred and sixty-three since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and eighty and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and sixty-three days and one hundred and sixty-three nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and sixty-four, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for fifty-two weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in fifty-two weeks, and this morning a man read a piece of paper out loud in front of a room and I have entered every word of it, and there is not one of those words that will still be true in a year, and there is that one.**"

The board was walked, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven that are not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it. The claim was the second of the seven and it had been the second of the seven for a fortnight and reading it out loud in a room had not changed where it sat, and Tams Quill said so out loud in five words, and the clerk entered it and entered that he was not asked.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and it is that a foreman has four and a half hours and will ask for them on the open day and has not asked on the two shut days since he was asked whether he would.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for twenty-four weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a guild's claim with a seal on it and a name in it and a date on it and three places on it is squared up on a trestle in this building and is neither granted nor refused.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a sheet of paper lying in the middle of a table with a line of chalk through the fourth of its six lines, and a plate on a shelf above the bench that had not been opened for forty-two weeks, and a brass rule in the pocket of a coat on a hook that had said nothing for thirty-three, and nine men asleep at the end of a cook's room over a floor that has nothing under it yet and a lamp standing cold on a shelf.
