# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline

## Batch identity

- **Batch:** 0001
- **Chapter range:** 1–10
- **POV:** Close third person on Marek Venn, with the first brief viewpoint shift to Nera Oduya only after she appears at the Hearthhold air shaft.
- **Batch goal:** Establish Marek's ordinary work, pull him and a small crew into the Lattice, prove that every room is a structural problem, create one meaningful mini-victory and one serious failure, and plant the first physical and political clues about a centralized route authority before the volume moves toward the Hearthholds.
- **Starting condition:** Marek is an ordinary Marrowgate civil engineer during a routine service-tunnel inspection. He has no reliable loadcraft, no fantasy system, and only a damaged brass survey rule connected to his missing father.
- **Starting relationships:** Marek trusts his measurements more than the official account of the tunnel; Leena Orr is a fellow public-works technician; Iven Tallo is a practical Cartwright carpenter; Pell Roke is a fourteen-year-old courier's younger brother. Nera Oduya and the Hearthholds are not yet part of Marek's group.
- **Batch midpoint:** Chapter 5's first refuge passes an empty inspection but fails a full-room test. Marek must dismantle his own strongest work to save the people inside it, proving that visual stability is not refuge safety.
- **Batch climax:** Chapter 9 opens a short controlled route toward a Hearthhold air shaft, fits the retrieved gland seal to the damaged sump pump, completes a witnessed draw-down test, and introduces Nera's first confrontation: she rejects the recorded air plan of Marek's Chapter 5 refuge. It is a first local victory, not an escape from the Cinder Steps. Chapter 10 is the aftermath: a guild courier freezes a guild-controlled door, and a Hearthhold full-room test sets narrow conditions for continued access.
- **Required character changes:** Marek becomes responsible for a route rather than only a rescue; Iven shifts from guild worker to reluctant craft ally; Nera enters as a skeptical technical equal; Leena and Pell make the cost of every design personal.
- **Required power changes:** Ordinary engineering → brief, unreliable impressions of load shifts and a clearer understanding of the five Room Checks. No bearing mark, named power stage, repeatable reading, route change, or automatic solution is gained in this batch.
- **Required world reveals:** Room Checks; Lattice rule-lines; faultforms as unresolved room functions; domestic rooms in the old refuge; the Hearthhold boundary is nearby; an old empty-seat symbol; a clearance system that treats central authorization as a safety tool.
- **Open questions after the batch:** Who built the Lattice, why does Alden's rule respond to it, who owns the route, what is the empty single-seat symbol, and can the group reach the Hearthholds before the Cinder Steps close?

## Locked opening baseline

- **Opening party:** six people are present before the collapse and all six must be accounted for in Chapter 2: Marek Venn, Leena Orr, Iven Tallo, fourteen-year-old junior route messenger Pell Roke, surface laborer Oren Sill, and surface laborer Tams Quill. Do not add an unnamed seventh person or a missing crewmate.
- **Equipment and water:** the shift carries twenty-four liters of water in four six-liter skins, a twenty-meter rescue line, two hand lamps, a survey case, timber wedges, grease pencil, chalk, Marek's folding rule, and Alden's brass rule. The collapse leaves the party with eight liters, one frayed nine-meter line, one cracked hand lamp, a folding rule, a grease-pencil stub, the damaged chalk case, a surviving screw jack, one pry bar, two short timber uprights, and the brass rule; the small hand-tool kit is damaged but recoverable. Most standard gear is lost. The ration ledger is eight liters recovered, six after Chapter 2, five after Chapter 3, and one additional liter of verified condensation reserve in Chapter 6; the Chapter 9 pump draws from the flooded sump, not the drinking supply.
- **Injuries and later cost:** Leena dislocates her right shoulder in Chapter 1 while pulling Pell clear. Pell twists his left ankle on a settling stair in Chapter 8. Oren remains alive through this batch and is established as a competent second bearing/support hand whose later Hearthhold-crossing death must arise from an insufficient route capacity, not an unexplained disappearance.

Sefu Aran, an elected Hearthhold boundary route keeper first heard in Chapter 7, independently controls civic admission and may veto opening the route. Neither may speak for every Hearthhold.
- **Legal instruments:** a Cartwright clearance seal certifies a route only for its survey date, occupancy, and conditions; a route charter governs use and maintenance; a salvage lien claims materials rather than a route or Hearthhold property; and a temporary route-custody order can lock a guild-controlled door or freeze guild-funded equipment but cannot manufacture resident consent or air clearance.

## Chapter 0001 — The Inspection

- **POV:** Marek
- **Location/time:** Marrowgate Public Works dry service tunnel, late night before the opening collapse.
- **Immediate goal:** Complete a routine inspection of the East Spur, document the lining, and get the six-person shift out before the changeover makes him look like a man who cannot finish a job.
- **Resistance:** The tunnel does not match the surface survey. Marek's shift supervisor pressures him to sign Public Works Form 7-B, a partial-closure certificate for the unused North Relief Branch; signing would leave that branch's unresolved maintenance burden unassigned and permit the East Spur to reopen under a one-shift clearance. Marek's father's brass rule taps a rhythm against his palm.
- **Information revealed:** The East lining has a mineral pressure seam absent from the public plans. The Lattice is not named yet; only a real structural anomaly and a small physical warning are visible.
- **Action or decision:** Marek refuses to sign Form 7-B, orders all six people to stop, marks the wall with grease pencil, and moves them toward the inspected escape shaft. The tunnel gives way before the warning becomes a full explanation. While ceiling stone pins the group, Leena pulls Pell clear and dislocates her right shoulder; Oren and Tams keep Iven from being crushed and help Marek reach Leena.
- **Emotional change:** Marek's refusal prevents a worse lie but cannot prevent the disaster; the responsibility he tried to control becomes a public failure, and accepting Leena's injury matters more to him than finishing the form.
- **Power or resource change:** The collapse destroys most standard gear. The six recover eight liters of water, one frayed nine-meter rescue line, one cracked hand lamp, Marek's folding rule and grease-pencil stub, the damaged chalk case, a surviving screw jack, one pry bar, two short timber uprights, a damaged but recoverable hand-tool kit, and Alden's brass rule. Form 7-B remains unsigned.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The collapse is a real engineering failure amplified by an older pressure system, not a dream, simulation, or convenient summoning. All six opening-party members survive the initial fall, and Leena's right-shoulder injury begins on-page.
- **Ending type:** Action reversal.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Marek wakes below the tunnel with Leena unable to use her right arm, no surface radio, and a chamber that should not be able to hold their weight.

## Chapter 0002 — The Room Below

- **POV:** Marek
- **Location/time:** The first Lattice chamber, several hours after the collapse, dawn filtered through a broken ceiling shaft.
- **Immediate goal:** Count all six living, reduce Leena's dislocated right shoulder, ration the eight liters of recovered water, and find breathable air and a second exit before the group exhausts itself.
- **Resistance:** Pell is frightened, Iven wants to break through the nearest wall, the room's water line is rising, and the group cannot agree on which cracks are active. Leena cannot take weight through her right arm and objects to being treated as dead weight.
- **Information revealed:** Floor seams settle by millimeters and clearances shift as bodies, the broken shaft, and rising water alter the load; the room does not teleport or redesign itself. Marek gets a brief, unreliable sense that the floor's weight is being carried by something behind the wall.
- **Action or decision:** He names all six aloud, splints and slings Leena's shoulder, assigns Iven and Oren to shore the active wall, and uses the recovered rope and chalk to mark a route. He builds a temporary lean-to for exactly six people, including Leena's litter, with one exit and a two-hour rest limit; it is a rest shelter, not a certified refuge. He chooses the slower room over a faster, unventilated passage.
- **Emotional change:** Marek's first useful decision buys trust, but it also reveals how little authority he has over people who are not his crew. Leena makes him state the shelter's time and occupancy limits aloud instead of calling it safe.
- **Power or resource change:** No named power stage. The group retains the frayed nine-meter line and gains a temporary shelter and shared work rhythm while using two liters of water, leaving six clean liters.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The first lean-to is ordinary engineering plus observation, not a magical construction spell. The opening roster is exactly six, Leena's right shoulder is injured, and there is no missing crewmate.
- **Ending type:** Earned discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Iven recognizes a Cartwright clearance mark on a sealed service door and says the group has entered licensed guild territory without a valid route.

## Chapter 0003 — The Cartwright's Claim

- **POV:** Marek
- **Location/time:** The first Lattice chamber and a sealed transit door, morning.
- **Immediate goal:** Ration six clean liters, keep Leena still, prevent panic, and decide whether to use the sealed service door as a shortcut.
- **Resistance:** Iven's guild identity makes the route claimable and the crew a potential liability; Pell wants the door opened immediately; Leena needs water and cannot be moved far. The posted rule says one licensed operator may close or reopen a route for everyone, an early political version of the empty-seat idea.
- **Information revealed:** The clearance mark is valid for a surveyed route, date, and occupancy, not for the room's changed condition now. The door opens onto a licensed inspection incline that still reaches the surface, so a physical courier could reach the Cinder Steps if the guild controls the outer wheel. That makes the door a legal claim, a possible message route, and a possible trap rather than a free shortcut.
- **Action or decision:** Marek refuses the shortest group route because it shares a vent with the occupied lean-to. He gives Iven the survey notes, writes the refusal and its reason where everyone can see it, and leaves the guild door mechanically known but unusable without supervised testing.
- **Emotional change:** Iven's respect hardens into guarded trust, while the crew sees that a correct answer can still cost them a route. Pell resents a door that remains closed because someone owns a mark on it.
- **Power or resource change:** The group gains Iven's practical guild knowledge and a known surface-linked inspection line, but spends half a day and one liter rationing air and water, leaving five clean liters.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** Guild law and physical safety are separate systems. A legal clearance seal cannot guarantee a safe room, and the door's surface connection must be physical rather than an unexplained communication line.
- **Ending type:** Decision.
- **Next-chapter pull:** Water begins moving uphill through an ordinary mineral pressure seam, revealing that the floor and drainage are part of one connected load path.

## Chapter 0004 — Water Against the Grade

- **POV:** Marek
- **Location/time:** The chamber's lower pump recess, late afternoon.
- **Immediate goal:** Keep the rising water away from the lean-to and find a way to move Leena to higher ground.
- **Resistance:** Water follows hydrostatic pressure in an ordinary mineral seam rather than the visible grade. The group finds a reciprocating sump pump bolted to a stable plinth, but its bronze piston gland is shattered and the drive-dog key is missing, so it cannot hold prime; the hinged flood gate can only isolate the pump bay while the water rises. Iven's first shoring attempt makes a wall crack.
- **Information revealed:** Rule-lines carry force, air, heat, or civic function, not water. This water travels through a wet mineral pressure seam coupled to a shared foundation, so a room can look dry while the load beneath it is already full.
- **Action or decision:** Marek reads the shifting stress by touch and sight, but Leena's right shoulder rules her out as a jack operator. Iven takes the timber line, Oren takes the second chock line, and Tams watches the mineral seam. Marek uses the wall as the receiving support and sacrifices the last clean cloth and the damaged hand-tool kit to wedge the flood gate closed; the surviving screw jack, pry bar, and two short uprights remain available for emergency shoring. He calls the gate a delay, not a pump repair, and admits that he cannot explain his sense with certainty.
- **Emotional change:** Leena calls him out for making the temporary gate sound like a solution. Marek admits the limit aloud, Oren refuses to let him take the jack alone, and the group changes the plan before the water does.
- **Power or resource change:** A brief, unreliable impression of load shifts; the group loses clean cloth and the damaged hand-tool kit but retains the screw jack, pry bar, and two short uprights, and cannot treat the first chamber as a permanent shelter. The failed pump remains a named mechanical problem: replace the gland, restore the missing drive dog, and prove the pump holds prime.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** A load reading is relative and fallible; it is not a map, prophecy, guarantee, or repeatable skill. Water itself uses ordinary pressure physics, while Marek's brief impression tells him only where relative stress is changing.
- **Ending type:** Costly discovery.
- **Next-chapter pull:** A drained recess reveals an old room sized for six sleepers, but its only air return runs toward a sealed wall and the pump gate is only holding temporarily.

## Chapter 0005 — A Room That Looks Safe

- **POV:** Marek
- **Location/time:** The apparent refuge chamber, dusk after the flood gate is wedged.
- **Immediate goal:** Certify the old room for a declared occupancy of six people, including Leena on a litter, with up to 120 kilograms of gear, water, and pump parts for a four-hour rest. The room's six floor pads and narrow landing define that test load; it is not being sold as a larger shelter.
- **Resistance:** Two massive piers, a dry-looking floor, a lockable door, and fitted shelves look sufficient. In fact, both piers bear onto the same hollow lintel, the sole intake and exhaust vent share a shaft, the floor drain reverses grade when the gallery lip settles, the inward-opening door leaves no room to turn a loaded litter, and ordinary crowding sends people toward the same weak landing.
- **Information revealed:** The room's old use was a six-person school refuge. It retains civic proportions but has lost the maintenance that kept its load, air, drainage, and escape paths independent.
- **Action or decision:** Marek names and runs all five Room Checks at the declared load. With all six present, Leena on the litter, Iven and Oren moving it, Tams placing dead loads, Pell marking escape time, and smoke, chalk, rope, and test water used in sequence, **bearing** fails when the two piers settle through the shared lintel; **breath** fails when the smoke returns through the only shaft; **drainage** fails when test water pools beside the litter after the floor settles; **escape** fails when the loaded litter jams the inward door at full occupancy; and **change** fails when people move toward that door and the landing lip sheds dust.
- **Action or decision:** Marek orders a controlled breach, not an improvised smash. Iven and Oren shore the lintel into the stable side pier, while Tams and Marek remove only the non-bearing masonry infill; the receiving load is the adjacent domestic room's outer jamb. When a pressure shift kicks the jack while Marek is holding it seated, stone traps his **right hand**, and Iven frees him only after the counter-supports take the weight. The group splits across the new opening and abandons the bulk supplies that made the false refuge look valuable.
- **Emotional change:** Iven loses confidence in Marek's judgment; Pell believes the room has betrayed them; Marek accepts that a stable structure can still be a lethal design and that his own confidence helped hide the flaw. Leena tells him plainly that keeping the room would not have been caution; it would have been choosing who died first.
- **Power or resource change:** No bearing mark. The false refuge fails all five checks at six-person occupancy, Marek's right hand loses permanent fine control, and the crew loses bulk supplies while gaining the first clear field version of the Room Checks. His left hand remains usable but is not yet strained.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** The five checks are bearing, breath, drainage, escape, and change. Two visible supports are not independent if they share a failing lintel. The controlled opening has two receiving paths, and the right-hand injury has an on-page cause and remains present in every later chapter.
- **Ending type:** Midpoint reversal.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The shored opening exposes a domestic room whose old floor plan points toward a service shaft, while Marek can no longer hold a fine line steady enough to copy it.

## Chapter 0008 — The Stair That Refuses

- **POV:** Marek
- **Location/time:** The stairwell beyond the maintenance room, dawn.
- **Immediate goal:** Use the copied evacuation route before the pressure pulse closes the first chamber.
- **Resistance:** The stair physically returns walkers to the first landing without teleporting them: an old movable landing slab and its counterweight run around a visible stone throat, and a pressure-fed rule-line drives the slab whenever human weight crosses. Pell's left ankle twists when a step settles during the first test, making the injured litter party slower; Iven's guild rules demand a licensed guide, and none is present.
- **Information revealed:** This was a school-and-infirmary evacuation stair. Walking patients once used the upper flight while a stretcher line balanced the lower flight across the moving landing. When the lower receiver collapsed, the stretcher side never completed its return load, so the stair now repeats the unfinished separation. The rope stays attached and broken edges pass in view, proving the motion is a continuous physical route cycle rather than teleportation.
- **Action or decision:** Marek maps the slab, throat, and counterweight with colored chalk, timed rope pulls, dead loads, and changes in group weight. Iven and Oren establish a temporary counter-support; Leena can call survey marks but cannot take the load because of her right shoulder. Marek orders withdrawal when the route begins taking Leena's litter load. No unmanned pack repeats the return, so the trigger is specifically the old walking/stretcher imbalance rather than any body at all.
- **Emotional change:** The group stops treating the dungeon as a monster den and starts treating it as a damaged building whose rule can be studied, though not trusted. Marek accepts that a usable clue is not a usable exit, and Pell refuses to hide an injury to avoid being left behind.
- **Power or resource change:** A better map of one stair, Pell's injured left ankle, a strained **left** hand from compensating for Marek's right-hand injury, and a wasted half-day of air; no faultform is resolved.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** A faultform requires a function, load condition, ordinary history, and a response beyond simple violence. The returning stair has a physical mechanism and a missing lower-route counter-support, but the larger crisis is not solved.
- **Ending type:** Discovery and reversal.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The copied diagram points to an old service shaft that bypasses the moving landing, reaches the failed pump from the Hearthhold side, and ends at an air gate operated by Sefu.

## Chapter 0010 — Who Owns the Door?

- **POV:** Marek
- **Location/time:** The forward chamber at the Hearthhold air gate, midday.
- **Immediate goal:** Complete a Hearthhold full-room occupancy test, keep Leena and Pell stable, and decide whether the group may use the air-gate anteroom while the next pressure pulse approaches.
- **Resistance:** Nera requires a witnessed air test; Sefu can independently veto admission; Pell's left ankle and Leena's right shoulder limit movement; a Cartwright courier arrives by the licensed inspection incline identified in Chapter 3. The courier carries Dael Harrow's temporary route-custody order, an old clearance seal that does not cover the current six-person condition, a salvage lien over the dismantled case and recovered materials, and a notice that rescue insurance is forfeited if the guild line is used outside its stated survey. The order locks the guild-controlled surface door and freezes guild-financed equipment; it cannot open the Hearthhold gate, order the air, or establish physical ownership.
- **Information revealed:** Nera has technical authority over the air mechanism and test; Sefu has the Hearthholds' local civic veto over admission; Iven has a guild bond that makes him the only person who can formally witness the old route. - **Action or decision:** Marek chooses the live maintenance test over Dael's demand. Iven must sign either as the Cartwright witness that would validate Dael's custody order or as a temporary craft maintainer who accepts responsibility for the timber, pump, and air-gate work; he signs the second and risks his guild standing. Nera runs the test with Sefu counting residents, while Marek checks the bearing and escape conditions. If airflow reverses or water crosses the sill, everyone evacuates to the upper service landing.
- **Action or decision:** Marek has Iven write the local maintenance sheet while Leena calls corrections and he states the limits aloud, because his right hand cannot hold a fine line reliably. He signs a **non-claim receipt** that relinquishes only any priority claim to the dismantled Hearthhold case and unrecovered Cinder Steps material under Dael's notice; he does not surrender the public-works tools, the brass rule, the route, or Hearthhold consent. The empty-seat plate stays under a work cloth while the argument is recorded.

Nera's first respect is earned by his refusal to call a closed room safe; Sefu's cooperation is narrower and more important because it comes with a veto. Iven's choice costs him a guild privilege before he has chosen a side in the larger conflict.
- **Power or resource change:** The group has not yet reached the Hearthholds. Marek has only unreliable impressions, no formal stage gain, and a right hand that will need support for delicate work. The route is conditionally open in practice, unlicensed in guild law, and dependent on Nera's air crew and Sefu's civic consent.
- **Continuity fact to preserve:** No crew member dies in this batch; Oren Sill remains alive but is already visible as a trained support and stretcher hand for the later Hearthhold crossing. The first confirmed permanent cost is Marek's right-hand fine-control loss, not a formal power upgrade.
- **Ending type:** Cost and decision.
- **Next-chapter pull:** The Hearthholds must decide whether to admit the group beyond the conditional test, the Cartwright Guilds will contest the route, and Marek must learn that a public shelter requires a maintenance charter no outsider can write alone.
