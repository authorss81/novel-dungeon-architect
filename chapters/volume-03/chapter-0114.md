# Chapter 114 — The First Of The Month

*He came four days up the middle passage and on past a gate, and the whole of what he had with him was two sentences and a piece of paper in a clerk's hand that had gone the other way and arrived before him. There is a board on the wall of the counter room at the second gate with the days of the week chalked on it and one of them is chalked up, and nobody in this room looked at it, and the reason nobody looked is that the week belongs to a building four days back down the passage and the first of the month belongs to the road, and the two do not meet anywhere on a road. Four days back down that passage a man of twenty-two is forty-nine days and forty-nine nights into a count that is his own, because a clerk writes him up by one every day of his life and it is not a register and it dates nothing.*

---

Eight feet by eleven, with a counter down one side, a stove in the corner and a window that is a slot: that is the counter room at the second gate, and it is the only room on this road with a stove in it that is not somebody's house. There were four people in it when he came in and two of them work there.

Wenna Dray had been at that counter for eleven years, and she looked at a man coming off the passage with a coat full of road on it and said, before he had got his breath, "**You are the Hearthhold Middle's neighbour. Stand there and do not lean on the counter, everybody leans on that counter and it is not built for it.**"

"**I am the elected keeper of a boundary,**" he said, "**four days back down the middle passage, and I have come to say a true thing to four households, and I am not going to put it on your counter and I am not going to put it in your book.**"

"**Everybody wants that last part,**" said Wenna Dray. "**Sit down. I have got a survey in the room next door that I am running and the man in the hut has been in it since the lever came off and he has water of his own and does not need mine, and I have about an hour. Do you want me in the room when you say it?**"

He had not thought about that, and he thought about it for the length of a breath.

"**Yes,**" he said. "**If you are writing it down I would rather you were not, and if you are not writing it down I would rather you were.**

"**And I know what I have just asked for and I am not going to explain it twice, so here it is once. I have spent five months teaching a boundary not to put a person in a book, and I have got a charter with no name on it and a plan with no name on it, and there is a woman four days back down that passage who reads a sheet out in a room every fifth bell for nobody's benefit except that a thing heard once gets repeated wrong by the fourth person who hears it. I know all of that. I am still not having it written.**"

Wenna Dray put the pen down on the counter, which she had not picked up, and said: "**Then it is not written, and I am going to say the rest of it once and then we are going into the room next door and you are going to say your thing and I am going to stand at the back with my hands empty. And I am going to remember it, because I have been at this counter eleven years and there has not been a keeper come down that passage in any of them, and because a thing that is not written is a thing that four people heard and nobody has, and in a year you are going to be asked whether you came, and there is going to be nothing to point at.**"

"**Then there will be nothing to point at,**" he said, "**and that is the true thing about it, and I would rather have that than have it written.**"

"**Right,**" she said. "**Then let us go and do it.**"

---

**They were not summoned, because there is nothing here that can summon anybody. The four households live on the near side of a water-lock that a competent office has closed for the examination of a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood, and they were in their own rooms, and the keeper went to four doors.**

Two brothers, Ansel and Peth Dunmow, thirty-one and twenty-six, in a room with a dry floor and two sets of steps up to a landing. Their sister Hessa Dunmow, twenty-four, in the room next door, with a second set of steps and a door that does not shut above the latch.

Then there was the fourth house, which is Marda Skell, sixty-one, who has not walked to a counter since before the cold closed, and who was in a room at the end of the passage and who did not come to the counter and could not have come to the counter.

**The keeper of a boundary said it in each of the three rooms,** and then he said it a fourth time standing in Marda Skell's doorway, which is a doorway and not a room, and it took about nine minutes in all, and he had practised it and it came out the way it had been practised, and that was the part of the day he would think about afterwards.

"**Four days back down the middle passage there is a boundary with a pump and a kitchen and nine children in a room at the far end of a corridor, and its drinking water is two jars a day out of a joint in the wall of its own busiest room, and maybe three, and the cook enters them separately and they never go in the pot.**

"**And it has been using them. It is using them now. It has not sent you a jar and it is not going to send you a jar, and nobody in that boundary is going to be sorry about that in a month, and I have come here to tell you to their faces, because I am the only one who can say it and I said in a room that I would say it at the first of the month and not one day before, and a thing said early is a thing that gets repeated wrong on a road.**

"**You have your own water and you have had it since the cold closed and you carried it up four sets of steps yourself, and you did not ask us for any of it, and you were not supposed to ask and you would have been refused and it would have been the correct thing to refuse you.**

"**And both of those are true at the same time. And one of them was our choice.**"

Nobody filled the space after it, in any of the three rooms, and the fourth time he said it in a doorway to a woman of sixty-one who was sitting down and who had known what he was going to say before he got there, because people at a counter who are sixty-one and cannot walk to a counter have heard their own arithmetic before a man comes down a passage with it.

Marda Skell said: "**You have told us the true thing. Say the other half of it.**"

"**There is no other half of it.**"

"**There is always the other half of it. I have been sixty-one for a long time and men come down passages with one half.**"

He told her the other half, which is that the boundary that has two jars a day and nine children in a corridor has no second pair of hands and no timber and no mortar of the right kind, and that it looked after itself while four people carried water up four sets of steps, and that the true sentence is that the looking after was the correct thing to do with what it had and that the four sets of steps were the correct thing to do with what they had, and that neither of those cancels the other and he was not going to stand in a doorway pretending that it did.

Then she said the thing that was the whole of his week.

"**Will it be open again.**"

"**I do not know. It is not our door. I have asked nothing of anybody up that road because I have had nothing to ask with and I have not been able to find anybody who would answer me, and I have not sent anybody to ask, because there is nobody in my Hearthhold I could stand at a counter and send and there is nobody outside it who has any reason to be sent by us.**"

"**Then you came a long way to tell us a thing we had already worked out.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**And you said in advance that you would come, and somebody in that building read your two sentences out loud every day while you were gone, and one of them is a woman who does the sums, and I would like you to notice that I know about the woman who does the sums. There is a route that goes through and it is not yours and it is not a guild's, and a woman on it told a man in a room about your boundary and she said the thing that she was told.**"

He had not known that. Standing in a doorway, he found out that the second sentence had gone further than the road.

"**Who told her,**" he said.

"**Do not ask me that,**" said Marda Skell. "**If I tell you then you will go and look for the person who told me, and I did not come down those four steps and tell a stranger in a doorway that so that a keeper could go and find a woman. She came through. That is all I have got and that is all I am going to have.**"

"**Then I will not ask,**" he said, "**and I will tell you that the two sentences in that room are read at the fifth bell every day, and that the reading is not for you and was never for you, and that it is for the fourth person in that room who has not been there for all of it, and I have never been able to explain that sentence to anybody who was not in the room.**"

---

**And the last hour of it was in the counter room next door, and it was not about the four households at all, and it is the part of the day the boundary four days back down the passage is going to have to live with.**

**The water-lock's survey was going on,** and the man in the hut had his own water and did not need the counter's, and the butt and the hand-pump that the office provides at the counter was out on the flagstones with a lid on it and a clerk's mark on the lid.

"**That is the day of the survey only,**" said Wenna Dray. "**And the survey is two days and it may be three, and the sheet said the day of the survey, and the sheet is not wrong, it is just about a day.**"

"**Who wrote the sheet.**"

"**A clerk in the office, and it is a good sheet. I would have written it the same way and I have argued with that office about nine things and won six of them.**" She said it flatly and without heat, and she was not on anybody's side. "**And I will tell you the thing nobody up that road is going to tell you, because you came and asked and I am not going to make a man come a second time. When that butt is gone, the people on the near side of that lock are drinking out of their own four sets of steps for as long as the lock is shut, and the lock is shut for a sill, and a sill is a thing that takes a long time and a cold month to look at properly, and nobody is going to be cruel about it and nobody is going to hurry, and at the end of it the water is still a step away and down a slope, exactly as it was before anybody surveyed it.**"

"**That is not a fault.**"

"**No. It is not a fault. It is what a correct survey is.**" She shut the lid on the butt. "**You have come down here to find out whether anybody did anything wrong and the answer is that nobody did anything wrong, and I want you to take that back up that passage and not to soften it, because the softening is what people do, and then in three months somebody will say you checked it.**"

"**I will take it back and I will not soften it,**" he said, "**and I have a piece of paper in a clerk's hand that went up that passage before me and it is a copy of the two sentences and it is unsigned and there is no ring on it, and you are welcome to look at it and I would rather you did.**"

She looked at it, and put it down, and said: "**Then I will tell you what it is worth at a counter. It is worth that a woman at a gate four days down a road can read it. It is worth nothing to the four people whose water it is about, because the four people cannot read and one of them cannot stand up, and a thing that has to be read to you is a thing that has to be brought to you, and your boundary could not bring it, and it sent paper, and paper is not a man at a counter.**

"**That is not a thing I am saying to be unkind. That is the whole of what I know about paper, and I have been at this counter eleven years.**"

"**And you have a book,**" he said.

"**I have a book. And I am not going to put your two sentences in it, because you have asked me not to, and because a thing a man will not have written down is his to hold and mine to remember, and I would rather be trusted with a thing in my head than given a piece of paper with a routing mark on it. And I am going to write down one line, in my own hand, that a keeper of a boundary came to this counter on the first of the month and said a true thing to four households and would not have it written, and that line is mine and it is not his and it is not theirs, and you may take it or leave it.**"

"**Write it,**" he said.

He went out onto the flagstones afterwards, with the four of them — Ansel and Peth and Hessa, and two neighbours carrying Marda Skell, because she said she would come to the counter if it was being asked about and it was not — and he stood there in a doorway and found out that **the promise had been kept and that keeping it had cost exactly what the clerk's book had said it would cost, and that the only difference was that he had said it.**

**The road went down four days to a middle gate and then on.** There was nothing on him to carry back that he had not come with, and there was no way to send anything, and the two jars a day were two jars a day, and the four sets of steps were four sets of steps, and both of those were true at the same time and one of them was his boundary's choice and he had said so to a woman of sixty-one in a doorway and she had said *yes, I know* and had not been unkind about it.
