**The spoken measure of elapsed weeks is a separate register and it was held where Batch 0004 left it.** Tams Quill says *fifteen weeks* in Chapter 94 and is not corrected; other voices say *eighteen* and *nineteen* for the same building, which is the drift Batch 0004 already had and which is **not** an inconsistency in the book but a property of the register. **A later batch may not "correct" it, and may not use a spoken count to date an event.**

**Wen Pask's count runs one a day: twenty-three on the fifth day of the seventeenth week, twenty-six on the first day of the eighteenth, thirty on its fifth, and thirty-seven on the fifth day of the nineteenth.** It is in the column with no heading and in nothing else, and it is **thirty-seven days and thirty-seven nights** at the volume's close.

**No day number is attached to any in-batch event, and the highest value in the day-number register remains the forty-sixth day.** The only date on any object remains the date in the top corner of the plan, and it is still the plan's whole distinction from the dowel.

## Relationship, thematic, and power change

**The Reader stage is granted here, once, and it is a stage and not a solution.** It identifies **a likely failure in one room** — the anteroom, and then a **fingerprint of stone** — with **fatigue and sensory bleed**: a headache behind the left eye with a floor to it, a left hand that clamped and shook and did not close that night, the count of a room arriving as a sound under the pump's check, and the count still audible in a room he was not in. **The cost is not erased by anything in this batch**: the shoring is done by two other people, the frame is still out of true, the left hand is still not right, and the man himself says that it did not save anybody and was worth an afternoon. **The risk of treating a first impression as fact is the scene's centre**: he says it as a fact before he has decided anything, an air engineer makes him say how he knows, and the count enters it as **a claim, not a reading**, with a condition. **There is no bearing mark, no route change, no binding, no relay, and no brace**, and Marek Venn refuses in advance to read a boundary. **His permanent fine-control loss in the right hand is not recovered.** The brass survey rule is taken out of the coat's pocket by the clerk, because his right hand could not open it, and it lies on a table saying nothing for the tenth week, and nobody looks at it.

**Nera is a collaborator and not a dependent, and the change is legible in three scenes rather than in one.** She is asked for a piece of **work** and not consulted for a decision. **The room does without her for four hours in front of the reader and says so in front of the reader**, and she entered the arrangement and states its price, which is that a room which can do without the person stops asking, and that this is the price and she paid it and is not going to be kept another four months by a boundary that needs her. She is **wrong** — about the direction of the school corner's air, and about the anteroom's danger, which is the word and not the stone — and she is wrong in public and is not consoled. She **refuses to sign the charter** and gives a structural reason and nobody asks her to. She **strikes the woman's covers out of a legal document** because nobody had asked the woman. She **refuses to hand the air board to a man** and puts it on a page instead. And at the volume's close she is the person who asks the next volume's question out loud and **refuses to answer it**, which is the last exercise of an authority nobody delegated to her.

**Talla begins organising a courier route outside the guild system, and it is hers.** It is a sheet in her own hand with three other names under hers, addressed to nobody in this building, saying that a paper with their mark is theirs and a paper without it came from a counter. **The boundary's contribution is a sentence, and then two, and neither is a page and neither is signed by anybody.** **She is not a rescue, she is not in the building, she is not offering the boy anything, and the spring is not owed by anybody and is not paid off as a gift** — it is named at the volume's close as owed by nobody and asked for by nobody, and the charter's road is not a spring.

**The volume's question is now two questions and one refusal.** *Why do the Lattice's rooms remember domestic routines* has been held by the air engineer since the fourth day of the eleventh week, is worse than not knowing, and is **said aloud in a room in Chapter 100 and not answered**, on the condition *when this boundary is keeping itself.* *What happens when a door becomes a toll gate* is answered for this boundary in a charter and answered for the world by nothing: a halfpenny sheet, three Hearthholds that will have timber, a frame that goes a finger a week, and a page in a coat. **Marek Venn's last act in Volume 02 is to tell the keeper that a page in a coat is not a system, that is a man, and that the road being open is a fact and not a system** — which is the series' central flaw, named by its owner, one volume early and in a room.

**The stair is still not a holder and the stair is not in any work and the plan is not a route, and nobody goes down the four treads and the eighth term stands.** The anteroom's row has a third column and does not have the word *person* in it, and the ninth room has no row and a charter clause that says so on purpose, and both women who refused it still refuse it.
