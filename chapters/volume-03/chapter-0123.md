# Chapter 123 — The Number On Each Side

**The third day of the twenty-fourth week was a Wednesday and the corridor was open, and the school ran in the room at the kitchen-line end for the eleventh morning, because the arrangement says it runs on the three open days and a wet floor and a lamp are not a closure, and the crossing took nine minutes and fifty seconds in two parties and a fifth at a walk, with Renn Vale first over and last back and Isel Ferrow on the fifth, and nobody put a hand on the fill at the waist, and the third wedge there is up a quarter of an inch and a bit and was not touched.**

At the first bell, in the anteroom, with the room at its number and the pump running, Marek Venn said the thing he had been carrying since the second bell of the first day of the week, and he said it standing up, and he had written it out on the back of a day-board and Dalla Sen had read it out for him first because that is what happens in this room.

"**I am not going to keep it. I have had it since the first day of this week and I have been not keeping it since the first day of this week, and I am going to say that before I say it, because a man who says he has been thinking about a thing sounds braver than a man who has been arguing with himself, and it is the second one.**

"**I recommend that the room at the kitchen-line end be shut.**

"**Not because it is a good room. It is the worst room in this building and I have said so. I recommend it because the floor in it is dished toward a sill, and the sill is the lowest thing in that room, and the water is coming up under that floor now and it will find that sill, and a sill is a thing you pack, and there is one pack in this Hearthhold that works and it is the one that was last used on that sill nine hundred years ago.**

"**And packing that sill means putting stone in clay in a joint nine inches deep, and clay shrinks as it dries, and the room has to be shut while it does that, and shut means shut, and the number of people in that room when it is shut is eleven and the number is not going down, and I have looked since the first day of this week.**

"**That is the recommendation. It is a bad recommendation on a good argument. I am not going to soften either half and I am not going to be asked to.**"

Then he said the two numbers, in a room, in the order the room does things in, and Dalla Sen had the chalk up for both.

"**Eleven behind the door. Nine of them are between six and eleven years old and one of them is their teacher and one of them is the cook, because the hearth corner in that room is the cook's job and the cook is in it.**

"**And fourteen on the other side of the low end of this corridor at the fifth bell on a Friday, going to the kitchen line, crossing four times a day, with a bearer at a lip that is packed and not held, over eleven feet of shaft, in the six minutes after the lever comes off when the water stands two foot eight wide there.**

"**The higher number is on the other side of the door. I have said it twice now and I am not going to say it a third time, and I want the clerk to enter that I said it twice on purpose, because a number that gets said once in a room is a number somebody can agree with and a number that gets said twice in a room is a number everybody in that room has to be holding by the end of it.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and read out twice, and the eleven is in the clerk's hand and the fourteen is in the clerk's hand and they are a long way from each other on the page and I am not putting a line between them, because there is no line between them that is not a kind of arithmetic, and this room has been getting its arithmetic into trouble since the first week of the twentieth.**

"**And entered that the man said both numbers twice on purpose, and I did not know that was on purpose when I wrote the first one down and I would not have written it if I had known, and he has now told me why, and I am glad, and I am also going to say that a man who tells the clerk what she is for is doing a thing this boundary has not had before and I have not decided yet whether I like it.**"

---

**The air engineer opposed it, in public, in a room with the pump running. She was right, nobody consoled her, and she said out loud that she knew who was going to lose and it was not her.**

"**No.**"

One word, from three steps, and the room stopped, because in twenty-four weeks she had said no to a man four times and every one of those four had been wrong.

"**You are going to be right about the water,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and I am going to be right about the air, and one of those two is going to lose this week and it is not going to be me, and I would like the room to hear me say that now, in a room, out loud, in my own words, and I would like the clerk to enter it.**

"**Because here is what I have got and I have said four of it twice already and I have not got a fifth. That door is nine inches deep and eleven feet long and it is the only way air gets into that room and the only way it gets out, and a door shut is a wall, and a room with a wall on one side of it and a floor that is dished toward that wall is a room that is a box with nine people in it, and in this cold, tonight and tomorrow, that box is going to be a box that nine people breathe in and nobody measures.

"**I have no figure for it. I have said that three times and I am not going to invent one this week, and the reason I have no figure is that there is no figure to take, because the only number anybody in this Hearthhold has ever put against that door is a number on a page that is a permission and not a measurement, and I have said that in this room twice and I am saying it now as the reason I cannot do my job in that room tomorrow, and that is not modesty, that is the actual state of the work.**"

"**Then stop the door,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I cannot stop the door,**" said Nera Oduya. "**That is the sentence I said on the first day of this week in four lines and I am not going to say it again, and if somebody in this room cannot tell the difference between the air I keep and the room people shut then I would rather be asked the question now than in a month.**"

"**Say what the difference is, in words, in a room,**" said Marek Venn, "**and I will repeat it, and the room will take it from me the way it took the mark's limit.**

"**You are not going to repeat it and you know you are not going to repeat it, which is why you offered.**"

"**I am going to try,**" he said.

"**Then try it and be wrong about it in front of everybody, which is a thing you have done twice and I have watched both times and it is the only reason I still work with you.**"

He said it, and he got it wrong, and Dalla Sen read it back and struck out four words of it at his own request and put back three that she had found in the book in Renn Vale's hand, from the twentieth week, and then she read out what was left, which was: *the air engineer keeps the air and the woman who keeps the room's terms keeps the room, and they can be stopped by each other, and neither of them is a name.*

"**That is Renn Vale's sentence and not yours,**" said Nera Oduya.

"**That is Renn Vale's sentence and you said it to me and I have said it wrong three times,**" said Marek Venn, "**and I would like the clerk to enter that I offered to repeat a sentence I was going to get wrong, and that she made me say it anyway, and that I would rather have been made.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, and entered all three, and entered that it was the second time in twenty-four weeks that a man in this room had asked to be corrected in public and had been, and that the first time was about a floor.

Then she said the other thing, and it is the sentence the whole of this room is going to be arguing about in a week, and she said it standing up, and she did not say it to the clerk.

"**And now I am going to say the last of it and it is about him and not about the work, and I have thought about whether to say it and I am saying it because I have been right about a thing in this room three times in four weeks and every time I was right about a thing and did not say so, and I am tired of being right quietly.

"**He is going to lose the next one. Not this one. This afternoon he is going to be right about a sill and about a floor and about a corridor, and everybody in this room is going to agree with him and I am not going to agree with him on the air and I will be right about the air, and then on Thursday and Friday he is going to lose it, and he is going to lose it with eleven people behind a door and a number on a page and a child in it, and I am not going to be able to help him and I will not be asked to and I would not answer if I were.

"**That is not a threat and it is not a warning. It is a professional statement by a woman who keeps air about what she thinks is going to happen in a room she is not allowed to make decisions in, and I have made plenty of them about other people's rooms in twenty-four weeks, and I have never once made one out loud before it happened, and I am doing it now and I would like it written in my own words and not improved, because in a month one of you is going to need to know that I said it and when.**"

Dalla Sen read it out twice, and Nera Oduya said yes to the second reading, and nobody in that room said a kind thing to her, and one woman who might have said one did not, and the clerk entered that nobody had.

---

**And then the man who had made the recommendation discovered that he could not carry out a single part of it, and found out in the way this room finds things out, which is by being told in front of people.**

"**Say that back,**" said Bel Ordry. "**You are going to close that room. On what authority.**"

"**I do not have one. That is the whole of what I have and it has been the whole of what I have for twenty-four weeks.**"

"**Say it out loud in a room so that a clerk can write it down, because in a month somebody is going to say that a man with a chalk and a hand that will not close shut a room with nine children in it.**

"**I recommend it and I cannot do it and I am not going to be asked to do it, and the person who writes a change to a room people use is the person who will keep it, and the person who will keep that room is the woman who teaches in it, and the person who keeps the corridor and the terms is the woman on the landing, and neither of them is me and neither of them asked me and I am going to say the other half of that, which is that I would have said it in a room a fortnight ago and I am not saying it a third time.**"

"**Then who shuts it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I am going to shut it,**" said Ilsa Norr.

She had been standing at the fourth step for the whole of it, in the place she stands, and she said it in a room with twelve people in it and did not come down the steps.

"**It is my room. I emptied my own school store in the thirteenth week to give up a room, and I carried nine boxes down nine steps and a half, and the room the school is in is the room I took, and the school runs in it on the three open days, and the arithmetic on that is the arithmetic I have been doing for five weeks and I have not been asked to do it and I am not going to be asked to do it now.**

"**So I will write it and I will shut it, and I will not write a figure on it, and I will not write a day on it, and I will say the reason out loud in a room so that a clerk can have it and so that nobody in a year can say I did it quietly.**

"**And I want the reason to be the true one and not a brave one. The true one is this. I am not shutting that room because it is dangerous. I am shutting it because nine children sit on a hearth stone that has gone soft at its bed, and the low side of that bed has a hole the width of two fingers in it, and the fire in that corner is not lit, and there is no light in that room in the morning that is not the light coming off nine stones nine children are sitting on.

"**I am shutting it because the only room I have ever taught nine children in safely for five weeks has a floor under it that is coming up wet, and I cannot move them anywhere, and I am not going to be the last person in this book to learn a thing about a room from Jo Verrick on a Monday and do nothing about it until Thursday.**"

"**Say the other half,**" said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up, and the room let it stand, which is a thing rooms in this building do about four hundred times a day.

"**The other half is that I am shutting a room with nine children in it and I am not going to be able to say afterwards that I did not know that, and everybody in this room knows it, and we are all going to go on to Thursday and do it, and one day somebody is going to write down that on the first day of the twenty-fourth week a room full of people was shut on purpose by the people who keep it, in a room, with the reason, and the room agreed.**

"**I am writing that down now in my own words and I am handing it to the clerk, and I am not going to argue with anybody about it, and I would like it read out in this room before I write the condition and I would like a person to object to it if they are going to.**"

Renn Vale came down one step.

"**I object to one word and I am going to say which one. Not *shut*. *Shut* is a room that is closed and a shut room can be opened and the difference between a shut room and a sealed room is the difference between a Wednesday and a Sunday, and this boundary has a school in it, and the word on the page is going to be read by somebody at a counter in a year who does not know that.**

"**Write *closed*, and write *with the room in use when it was closed*, and write *eleven*, and write it in the teacher's hand because it is her room, and write the condition under it with a person who can stop it, and write the person who can be stopped, and I will write the condition in my own hand with no number and no day on it, and I am not going to be blamed for that condition, and the reason I am not going to be blamed for it is that the rule says the person who writes a change to a room is the one who keeps it, and I am the one who keeps it, and the rule does not say the person who keeps it gets to be comfortable.**"

---

**And then Bel Ordry began a sentence at the fourth bell and did not finish it, and Renn Vale finished it for him in his own words and made him stop, and nobody in the room understood at the time what any of it had to do with the afternoon.**

"**A room with one door and a shut door has a second way out,**" he began, "**and this boundary's second way out is —**"

"**Stop,**" said Renn Vale. "**Say the rest of that in a room in a month when somebody asks you, and not on a Wednesday with nine children in it.**

"**A room's second way out in this building is a word said in a room by the person who is going to be in the room, and the clerk repeats it, and nobody says it to a doorframe. And I am not going to have that said out loud this week by anybody, and I have said it in this room once, in the twenty-second week, and the clerk wrote it once, and I told the room not to find out about it before the rest of us had, and the rest of us have not.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the keeper of the plan began a sentence and did not finish it, and that the woman who keeps the terms finished it for him and made him stop, and that I did not ask him what the rest of it was, and that I have entered that I did not ask him.**"

Nobody asked him. Marek Venn stood at the foot of the nine steps for a while afterwards and worked out that he had known the whole of that rule since the twentieth week and had described it to a stranger with a permit in front of nine people, and that it had been written down in a stranger's hand, and that a boundary surveyed and found sound is not inspected again when it changes, and that he had worked out the last two of those three things in the last fortnight and had not said any of them to anybody, and that the clerk had not said them either, and that there was no reason she had not said them.

There was no reason. He had not asked her about it. He had never once in twenty-four weeks thought to ask the clerk what she had noticed, because the clerk is the person who writes down what other people have noticed, and he had never in his life wanted to be one of the things she wrote down.

**The pump's check came off. A man of twenty-two was fifty-eight days and fifty-eight nights into a count that was his own, and the corridor was open, and the school was in the worst room in the building on the far side of a fill that has moved three times, and the door of that room was going to be shut on Friday at the fifth bell, and nobody in this building had said so yet, and one woman in it had said every single thing that was going to happen and had been right about the air and had been told by a man in front of twelve people that she was going to be right.**
