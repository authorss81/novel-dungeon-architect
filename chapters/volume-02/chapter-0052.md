# Chapter 52 — What Once Means Now

They put it to the room in the anteroom on the second morning with the card in Sefu Aran's hand, and it took most of a morning and a half, and nine people said something, and four of the nine said it wrong on purpose so that the fifth could say it right.

That last part was Jo Verrick's doing and she took no credit for it and Dalla Sen wrote it in the ledger anyway, in the clerk's own hand, because a room that has learned how to be argued with is worth a line.

Sefu stood at the bench and held the slate up the way Nera had held it, and did not read it out, and made the room do it.

"Line nine," he said. "Read it."

"Nine people in a room," said Mikka Hale, "do not get a vote because nine people are in a room. That is how everything goes wrong in this building and it is how it went wrong in every building I have ever washed a floor in. You get a work, and a person at the end of it, and a date, and something different at the finish. You do not get a vote because a thing is frightening."

"Then the air engineer is the one who says," said Ilsa Norr, "and I would like to know that before we all start."

"No," said Nera.

"Then who."

"The person whose work it is," Nera said. "Which means the keeper, and the keeper is Sefu, and the keeper cannot be the person who says *once* means *once* in a plan for a room with water in it, because the moment he can, every keeper in every Hearthhold gets to open a room whenever he wants a plank. So: not me and not him. The room."

"You are the room," said Leena Orr, from the frame, with the slate on her knees. "You are five people and a clerk and an air engineer, and if you say it then it is a boundary decision and it will be in the ledger as one, and it will be a boundary decision about how to use a room, and that is a different animal from a decision about a piece of timber."

"Which is why I will not have it as a decision either," Nera said. "Not a vote, not a keeper's word, not mine. It is a term. You put a term in and then anybody can read it in a year and argue with it, and that is the only way this building has ever got anything done that was going to last past me."

---

The arguments went on for an hour and a half and Dalla Sen wrote down every one of them, and then read all of them back, and the room took the reading apart twice, and out of that came four words, and the four words were Renn Vale's, and they were hers because she had been listening to how people said *once* and not to what they meant by it.

"You have all been arguing about a number," she said, "and it is not a number. Look at what the card says. It says a method for a room that one person is going into **once**, in the dark, in water, for eleven minutes, **to take four things off a shelf**. She wrote a rule about a job. The job was four things off a shelf. She wrote *once* because a man in the dark in water with an eight-foot cord and no second ladder is not a man who is going to make a habit of it."

"That is one job," said Nera.

"It is one job and you have had three more since and each one of them had its own card and its own morning, and if you look at them—" Nera reached up without taking her eyes off the valve and took down the second document and the third, and set them beside the first, "—then the word *once* is on one of the three and it has never been on the other two, because the second one is a method and the third one is a method, and I wrote *once* on the first one because the first one is the one I was frightened of."

Nera looked at her own three cards for a while.

"That is a better reading of my own work than I gave it," she said. "Say the rest of it."

"The rest of it is that a card is not a permit for a room," Renn said. "It is a permit for a *work*. A card covers a work and it is spent when the work is finished or called off, and then the room is a room with four hours of standing air and no plan, exactly as it was before, and that has to be on the card so that nobody comes in next month with the same slip of paper and says *we did this once already*."

---

Nobody moved for a moment, and then four people spoke at once, which Dalla recorded, because it is the only honest way to write down that a thing was agreed.

Ilsa Norr: "Who spends it. I want a name and not a feeling."

Renn Vale: "The keeper."

Nera: "No. The work does, and the keeper finds out after. A card that is spent by the man who wants to spend it is a card that never is."

Marek Venn, from the wall, quietly, and only the second time that morning that anybody had let him: "The work ends or it is called off. Those are the two. There is no third thing where a card quietly runs out because a person got tired of reading it."

"That is it," Nera said. "And I want it in that order and I want *quietly runs out* in it, because that is the phrase that would have got a man killed in the ninth week, when a plan for stopping was used as a plan for timing."

---

The fourth document took the rest of the morning and it is the shortest of the four and the only one that was written by somebody who had been told no twice.

Nera Oduya wrote it on a slate, standing up, with the three older cards on the bench beside her so that anybody could see what she had done to them, and she read it out as she wrote it, and four people took it apart, and Dalla Sen copied each line as it was agreed and read each line back before the next one was written.

"Method for one work," Nera said. "**Recovery of one length of public-works timber, five foot nine, from the bottom shelf of the lower pump gallery, at the cache mark, under the shelf's inventory number.** Not a rating. Not a certification. It covers the room, the draw, the interval, the turn, the person, and the receiving, and it does not cover the stair and it does not cover the proposal that started it, and I am writing that on the first line because I wrote it on the first line of the first one and it is the only line of the first one I would write again exactly."

"Read the first line of the first one," said Sefu, "and then read yours."

"The work the cache is for is a proposal before this boundary and is not agreed by anybody in this boundary," Nera said. "This plan covers the room, the draw, and the person, and does not cover the proposal." She chalked it. "Same first line. Good. Because the work is *no longer* a proposal. It has a keeper and a date and a person at the end of it, and the moment I take the word *proposal* off that card the air in that room stops being my decision and starts being yours, and I am not having that. So the card says the work is named and the air is mine, and those two facts are in the same line where everybody can trip over them."

"And the spending," said Ilsa Norr.

"And the spending," Nera said. "The card is spent when the length is up and received, or when the work is called off in the room, whichever comes first, and it is spent either way, and it does not survive for anybody, including me, including the next person, including a man from an office who comes with a piece of paper and a good coat."

"Somebody object," said Dalla Sen, because that is what she said, and Jo Verrick said it, and it had become the phrase.

"Third person objected," said Sefu Aran, "on the grounds that the crew's tally and this room's tally are two documents and a person who is owed time might reasonably want to know which book the word *spent* is in."

"Then it is in both," Nera said, "and it is spent in both on the same day, and Tams Quill writes it in his column and Dalla Sen writes it in her line, and if they ever disagree then the work is not finished and it does not get to be spent twice."

Tams, from the bench, with his left hand flat on the board: "That is the first sensible thing anybody has said about my column all week and I am going to be difficult about it for a day and a half and then I will behave."

"Write *spent* on the card," said Nera, "in those words, and nothing else. I do not want a paragraph. I want one word, and I want it in a place where a tired person can find it with a thumb."

---

Then the price, and Jo Verrick gave it before anybody had asked her, standing at the pump frame with her hand on the delivery valve, in the voice she used for numbers.

"Two cleanings or fewer," she said. "That is my estimate and it is in my own hand and it has a time on it and nobody has improved it in nine weeks. I am not touching that housing. If I open that housing for this work then the draw is a second drain in a week and my estimate is finished, and the estimate is the only thing anybody in this building has got that they can plan a winter against, and I am not spending it on five foot nine.

"Which means the housing stays shut. Which means the delivery check is open for the whole draw, because that check is the only thing in the pump that lets a sump go down, and a check that is open for four intervals does not stop chattering. It chatters for four intervals and it packs, and the packing is in the delivery, and the delivery is what I have been listening to since the twenty-ninth day and it is a thing I know by ear the way you know a road."

"Price," said Sefu. "Say the price, not the mechanism."

Jo put her hand flat on the frame.

"The check," she said. "Four intervals of open delivery, and the check is the only sacrificial thing in that pump and there is no spare check in this building and there is no bronze in this building. So the price of five foot nine is the check, and after this week the check is not a thing we look at, it is a thing we listen to, and if it changes its note the draw stops and the work stops and nobody in this room is to be angry with me about it, because that is what I am saying now, in my own voice, before it happens."

Dalla Sen wrote it and read it back and then made her read the second half again, which she did.

"Written, with a time, next to the name, and read back before the ledger closes," Jo said, "because in a month somebody is going to ask whether this room knew what a length of timber cost, and the answer has to be on this page and not in my head. And I want the seat's estimate written next to it, and the seat's estimate is two or fewer, and I have not moved it today and I am not going to."

---

The calendar came up in the middle of the afternoon and it came up because Jo Verrick asked the only practical question left in the room, which was who was going to be on the lever.

Tams Quill put his right hand on the table, palm up, and did not say anything, and the sling was taken off, and the two crushed fingers were opened as far as they went.

They went about as far as a finger and a half. It was the same as it had been in the seventh week, and the gap back to the seventh week was less than the gap had been the week before, by an amount that only somebody who was looking every day would have been able to swear to, and Jo was looking every day, and she said so.

"That is a fortnight of your hand in a fortnight that I have watched every morning," she said, "and it is going the right way and it is going at about half the speed it needs. So: I am not asking. I am telling the room that I am not asking, and I am putting the date on the board so that nobody can be kind to me about it later."

"Which date," said Dalla Sen.

"A month from the day I said it in the anteroom with nine people watching," Tams said, "and that was a day in the seventh week, and a month is four weeks, and four weeks out of the seventh week is in the eleventh week. So it is the eleventh week. It is not this week and it is not the week after. And I am on the tally and I am on the count and I am on the water record and I am the reading-back, and I am not on that lever, and I am not on a bar, and if anybody in this room sees me reach for one of those two things you are to take it off me and I will not be angry, and I will be angry later, in a week, on my own."

"Noted," said Dalla, and then, remembering the word, and remembering who had asked for it to be struck out of her own hand in the ninth week, she said, "Written, with a time on it, and read back. Not *noted*."

"It is the eleventh week," Tams said, to the room at large, and put his sling back on himself, which nobody offered to do. "I am telling everybody now so that in three weeks nobody in this anteroom can say *Tams, you are nearly right* and make me sound like a man being encouraged. I will be right in the eleventh week or I will not be right at all."

---

The person was the last thing, and it was not settled until the fourth bell, and it was settled the way Nera had said it would be, which is to say out loud, in the room, by the person claiming it.

Line seven of the fourth document was, in her hand, the same as it had been on all three of the older ones, and it said: *the person, to be named aloud in this room and agreed, and not to be written before it is said.*

"Iven's condition from the twelfth day," Nera said, "and my own from the first card, and it stands four times now. Who."

Nobody said Jo's name, and Jo did not say it either, and the anteroom sat there being extremely careful, which is a thing that happens in a room where everybody already knows and one person has to go first.

"I am going to say a name and I am going to say why and then I am going to stop talking, and if it is the wrong answer then somebody in this room who is not me has to say so before the fourth bell," Jo Verrick said. "It is me. I have been in that housing five times and I am the only person in this building who has been inside the pump's own rooms with my hands, and the draw needs somebody who knows what a check sounds like and what a sump does when it is coming down, and that is not a job for a boy on a crutch and it is not a job for a woman with a shoulder and it is not a job for a man who went down that ladder the first time and came up in a minute and eleven seconds with a jack he was told twice to leave.

"And I am going to say the other thing, because Sefu named the work with my name in it and nobody in this room is going to be allowed to pretend that is a coincidence. The bearing hand on that lip is me now. It has been me since the Thursday. Oren was the only one before and he is under that stone and not coming up, and I have been the only pair of hands in this boundary that knows how to pack a head to bearing since the day I packed one, and if I am going to be the hand on that lip then I am going to be the hand on that lever too, and both of those are on the same day, and somebody in this room is going to have to tell me the two can be done."

"The two cannot be done," said Renn Vale.

"Then the work cannot be done either," said Jo, "and I have said the price out loud, and it is not my price, it is the check's, and I would like somebody else to carry the sentence."

Which Sefu Aran did, in his own words, and Dalla Sen wrote it in her own hand, and the work stayed named and stayed real and the ledger gained a line that says the two cannot be done and that this is known and was said in the room.

---

They read the card on the valve board out loud at the fifth bell, all of it, and Nera would not let Jo read the sixth line for her because Jo had both hands in the housing's frame by then and had no business standing in a room reading anything, which is the second time in a fortnight that she had refused to let Jo read her out, and the room had noticed, and did not write it down.

Sefu Aran then asked Ruk Sella, at the threshold, in front of four people, and told her what she was being given, which he does every time and has never once shortened.

"It is a bell, and not a watch. You pull it if the cloth moves or if I say the word or if anybody shouts, and then you go up the nine steps and you stand at the top of them, and you do not come down. Six watches are filled on the morning the well opens and I will say all six out loud so that nobody can say afterwards that one of them was a courtesy. Nera at the valve. Me at the wheel. Renn on the upper landing. Mikka at the clinic return. Renn on the wet seam at the waist, four days in seven, and she has said the whole of that out loud before she takes it every time she takes it. Dalla at the bell relay. And you at the bell. Read it back."

"Six watches, and I am the bell, and I do not come down," said Ruk Sella. "And the sixth watch at the waist is not a post that morning because the corridor is shut, and I am going to say that out loud as well, because in the ninth week a watch was not a watch and a page said it was. And I am not to be at the water mark while I am at the bell, and Mikka has the mark, and she has said yes, and I am saying it out loud so that nobody has to go and ask her and wonder."

"That is the practice now," said Sefu. "Say it every time."

The card went onto the valve board beside the three older ones, and the four of them hung there in a row, and Nera Oduya stood and looked at all four of them the way you look at a row of tools you have used.

The first one said *once*. The second one said nothing about number at all, and everybody in the room knew that the second one was the document a man died outside of, and had died inside a plan built on the refusal to write down a number, and Nera had put *once* on the first one and had refused to put it on the second one and everybody in that anteroom understood that she had done that on purpose. The third one said nothing. The fourth said **spent**, and one word, and no paragraph.

Four cards and no line on any of them that said how many times anybody was allowed to go into that room in a month, and the woman who owned all four of them was standing in front of them with her arms folded, and a fact with a date on it is something a boundary can argue with, and a decision is something it has already lost.
