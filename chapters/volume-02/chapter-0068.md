# Chapter 68 — Locally, or by Charter

The argument was had on the common landing, in the gaps, with the school coming through.

That was Sefu Aran's condition and he made it before anybody started, standing at the head of the waist with the school door open behind him and a cord across the top of the three steps.

"Nine children at a walk, an adult at each end, and a bell at the threshold, and between those crossings this landing is yours. So you have got about nine minutes at a time and you have got them until the school is finished and then I want the landing shut and I want the cord tied and I want it tied by Renn and me in that order. Everything that gets said on this landing this morning gets said in the holes, and I am telling you that now so that none of you can complain at the second bell that the room did not have time."

He had said that at the first bell on a Wednesday, and by the fourth bell the argument had been had four times over and had produced two sentences anybody in that building would still be using in a month, and a pot, and a book.

---

Renn Vale went first, and she went first because the waist was in her rota and she had said so out loud on Friday and had not withdrawn it.

"**Locally.** Packed here, by the people whose rooms it is under, in our own week, out of what we have, and the keeping of it in our hands and not on a piece of paper in a file.

"And I am not going to argue that from a position of feeling. I am going to argue it from a document, and the document is four days old, and it is on page four of a file forty days away. A woman came down those nine steps with a case and read us a sentence: *no officer of this or any chartered office shall be required to enter, inspect, maintain, fund, or answer for any condition arising upon it.* **That is the best sentence anybody has ever read out in this room, and it is in my own hand that we said no to putting the fault in the one box on that form that could have put a person in our corner.**

"So: a charter is a way of telling somebody else that this stair is worth having. And I have watched a clerk fill in *none reported* about a lip that moves a quarter of an inch with a person under it, and go down nine steps, and be right to. And the moment this boundary puts a charter on that stair, then this stair is a thing an office can be asked to inspect, and the day it is inspected, somebody from an office will be on this landing, and Renn Vale will be standing at the top of three steps saying no for the fifth time, and she will be a woman in a report."

"And in exchange," said Iven Tallo, "you get a waist that is packed with nothing."

---

He said it from the far side of the landing, sitting down on the step with his back to the wall, and he had been in the room for a week without saying a whole sentence about a fault, and the room had got suspicious of him about it.

"Locally, then, and here is what locally is, and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have and I am not going to be liked for it.

"The waist is washed-out fill. Not stone. Not a floor somebody laid. It is what came out of a flooded dip, and it went in there in the ninth week in four hours because a man packed a corridor with what he had, and the *having* was never in question, and now it has taken a frost and let go twice in three days, and the third wedge is up a quarter inch and the ground under it moved.

"To pack that properly you want a thing removed and a thing beaten in and a thing that holds it, in a hole cut to a bottom, in the dry, by a man who has done it in a shaft with a lamp in his teeth, on a **survey** — because you cannot pack what you have not looked under, and under that fill is an eleven-foot shaft that nobody in this building has ever seen the wall of, and a piece of dressed stone and a line-block at the base of it.

"And there is not one of those five things in this Hearthhold. So what locally *is* — right now, this week, with the wood we do not have — is that somebody stands on it. That is the repair. **The repair is a person.** And a person is a person, and a person is on the rota four days in seven, and the rota is mine, and I have already told this room that the boundary's keeping has become my calendar, and now I am going to say the other half of it, which is that the boundary's *waist* has become my calendar, and my boots, and a woman called Bel Ordry, and there is not one hour of any of it in a guild file, and in four years the question in that file will be *what changed since*, and the answer will be *nothing changed, and a man from above stood on it*.

"So: I am against it. I am against locally. And I want it understood by everybody in this landing that **I said the correct response to a guild page is nothing on the fourth day, and I am now arguing for the guild on the ninth, and both of those are the same sentence and I have not changed my mind about anything.**"

Somebody said *Iven* and he shook his head.

"I have not changed my mind about a page. I have changed my mind about a floor. A page is a document and a floor is a floor, and I have spent nine weeks telling this building that a page is how you end up owned, and I would still say it, and I would still say it to a man with a store of seasoned hardwood standing in this landing. And I would still take the hardwood. **That is the whole of the position and I am not going to be let out of it by a man in a good coat, and if somebody in this room wants to say that a carpenter who argues for a charter after refusing a bond is a hypocrite, they can say it in the next nine minutes while the school is in the corridor.**"

"Iven," said Sefu Aran.

"I said it myself. I do not need it said twice by a keeper."

---

Nera Oduya had not been on the landing for any of it. She came down the three steps with the board under her arm and stood at the top of the stair, which is as far as she has gone since the Sunday in the tenth week, and she said the thing that broke the argument in half and neither side wanted.

"You are both talking about one fault," she said, "and there are two, and a charter will buy you one of them, and it is the one you do not need.

"**The waist is a floor.** It is not a route. It is not in a charter, it is not in a clearance, it is not on anybody's register, and a Cartwright office that put a charter on that stair would send a licensed man with a slate to look at a *route* and would never once look at a floor, because a floor is not a route and there is no money in a floor and there is no form with a box for a floor.

"**The seat is a machine**, and the machine is under this anteroom, and the machine is the pump that holds the dip down, and the dip is under the corridor, and the corridor is the route. If you wanted to make that one clean and payable and arguable, that is the one — a charter on that stair, a holder's name, an office that has been told in writing about a pump with two cleanings on its seat, and in nine months somebody's clerk can say the boundary knew and said nothing. And in nine months it will be a fault in a file, and a fault in a file is a thing a man can be sent to.

"So: a charter buys you the *pump* argument and leaves the *floor* argument exactly where it is, and the floor is the one that is going to put somebody on the ground.

"And the water, which is the only instrument any of us have left in this cold, is going to be under a person, and I have said that I have no person for it, and I have said I would not name one, and I am not going to name one now. So I am going to say the thing that is underneath all of this and then I am going back up the nine steps.

"**You are not choosing between a local repair and a charter. You are choosing which of your two faults you are willing to write down in words.** The one you write down, somebody can hold you to. The one you do not, is yours, alone, on a landing, at five in the morning, forever. And every one of you in this room has understood that for about four seconds and I would like it to stay understood for longer than four seconds."

And she went up the three steps and did not go further, and the school came through, and nine children went past a waist with a risen wedge in it at a walk.

---

It went on in the holes, as Sefu had said it would. Sefu himself brought the keeper's answer and he brought it late and he brought it as a man who had learned it four days ago from a woman with a stick.

"A charter is an admission document," he said. "That is all it is. It says who may be on a route and who answers for it, and the reason those two go together is that a route with a holder is a route somebody can be blamed for, and a route somebody can be blamed for is a route somebody can be asked to fund.

"What it buys you is reach. Material, a licensed man, an inspector, and a door that opens. What it costs is a name on a page, and the name is not a person, it is a **holder** — and a holder is a fiction, and the fiction has to be kept alive by somebody writing to a clerk, and the first clerk who gets tired of writing to a clerk comes down nine steps with a form.

"So: I have spent a week watching a boundary run its water on the shoulders of strangers and its stair on the knees of its own. I am not going to put a holder on that. **I would rather this Hearthhold had a waist that is a person.**"

And Mikka Hale, from the step below, said the thing that ended it.

"A charter does not carry pails," she said.

That got a noise out of the room that was not quite a laugh and was not quite anything else.

"I have got sixteen days," she said, "and it is on my board, and I have got a new book, and I am going to do a thing on Monday that I have never done in eleven weeks of keeping a pot, and I am going to say it out loud first so that nobody can say afterwards that a cook went mad.

"**I am going to write down every favour.** Every time a person carries water up the nine steps for this Hearthhold and is not paid for it, I am going to write the date, the name, and how many pails, in my own hand, in a book of my own, and at the fifth bell on the last day of every week I am going to read the week out in this room, and it is going to be a book of what this Hearthhold costs and who is paying it in their own backs.

"And I want everybody in this room to hear why, because it is not sentiment. **A favour is not a resource. A favour is a loan with no date on it, and this room has spent a fortnight learning that from Bel Ordry, who would not let us thank him for a floor, and from a courier who stood on a bench in the tenth week and told us that a thing given to a boundary is a second lock on the same door.**

"So: this boundary is being kept on favours and on days, and it is being kept by four people in seven and by nine children who do not get a school, and one day somebody is going to stop, and they are not going to tell us, and I am putting it in a book with their name and the date so that when it happens it is a fact and not a mystery."

---

A bell went at the threshold, and Bel Ordry came up the nine steps two at a time and said, "It is the second bell, the school is coming, and there is a thing at the throat."

"**What** kind of thing," said Nera Oduya, from the top of the three steps, and she was moving before she had finished the sentence.

"The same tick. Three of them, with a slide on the third, and I want to say before anybody asks me that the bar was cold and so was the wall and it ticked in the other two places too, so it is the same as Wednesday and I cannot tell you what it is any better than I could on Wednesday."

"Then it goes in the review," Nera said, "and the review is the first work-bell after a sound from the throat, and I called that line in the tenth week and I have not moved it, and it is due in about an hour, and I want the landing cleared for a school and then I want this anteroom."

And the landing cleared, and nine children came through at a walk, and the argument did not resume, because the argument was not going to be resolved, and everyone in that anteroom knew it, and nobody had said so, and the person who said it was Marek Venn, from the bench at the side, with his slate under his arm, and he said it into a room that had gone quiet for twenty seconds.

"There is no version of this week where somebody is not standing on that waist," he said. "Locally, it is a person. By charter, it is a person and a form and a name and a person after that. I have been sitting on this bench for an hour listening to four people who all know that, and not one of us has said it, and I would like it said, because in about four years the only record of this week is going to be a number in a ledger and a line in a clerk's book, and neither of those says that there was a choice on this landing and that the choice was a person."

"Yes," said Renn Vale, from the top of three steps. "Say it in the book, and put the date on it, and put in that the choice was not between a floor and a charter. **The choice was between a floor with a name on it and a floor without, and this room has not chosen either, and it is going to find out what that costs on Monday morning when Jo Verrick opens a housing with a borrowed chisel.**"

And it was not resolved, and it was not written down as resolved, and Dalla Sen refused *pending* a second time in eleven weeks, and the review was called at the first work-bell after the second bell, and the room went in.

And in the anteroom, on the frame, at the end of the day, Jo Verrick's price was written in her own hand with the time on it, and it was this: *housing open, pump out, dip up, corridor shut, school shut, pot unfilled. One day. That is the price of the look and I am going to pay it in the morning and I am not going to be argued out of it in the morning.*
