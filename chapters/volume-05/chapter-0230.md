# Chapter 230 — What This Room Has

The sixth day of the forty-third week is a Saturday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the fifty-fifth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and a man who has been at nineteen counters in his life said out loud that the corridor at the middle gate shuts at the fourth bell on a sixth day because there is nothing to count, and the clerk entered that nothing in this room is dated to a sixth day, and entered that it is the second sixth day in a fortnight on which a fact about a counter has been said out loud in this anteroom and neither of the two has touched an instrument, and that she noticed the second one and entered the noticing.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the fiftieth reading, and the same as forty-nine, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, in the same words for the thirteenth time, and the clerk entered it and entered that thirteen mornings is a practice and that a practice is a thing this room now has and is not going to be asked about, and entered that the fifty readings of that wall and the thirteen mornings of one sentence are the same number of mornings, and that the clerk has not joined them.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is thirty-five.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it is right, and the cook was in the room and did not leave it, which is the fifth morning running, and the clerk entered that she has stopped writing the word *practice* beside it and that a clerk who has stopped writing a word beside a thing because the thing has become a habit is a clerk who has just let a column grow without a heading over it, and that she entered that instead.

Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The sixth was the pump, and it ran, and a stalled channel is under this floor and a decision about a body in it is in a clerk's book and a watch that has nobody's name on it is in a keeper's mouth, and none of the three is in the sixth of the six terms, and the sixth of the six terms has not been changed in forty-two weeks.

The pump was started, two people turning it, and the fourth bell came and went, and the nine children came down the nine steps and went out at the far door, and nobody in the room counted them and everybody in the room knew.

Then Dalla Sen shut the book, which she has done three times in five volumes, and opened it again, and did not turn it round, and said the thing she had come in with.

"**I have entered things in this book for forty-two weeks,**" she said, "**and I have not once entered what this room has. I have entered what it owes, four times, in four hands. I have entered what it found, eighteen times, on a board. I have entered what it will not do, nine times, in a man's own mouth. I have entered what it has decided, four times this week, and two of those were decisions not to do anything. Not one entry in this book has a list of what is in the room.**"

"**Then say why,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**Because a list of what a room has is a column,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and a column has a heading over it, and this boundary has refused six headings in five volumes and has had reasons given for every one of the six and has not accepted a single one of them. So I am not making one. I am going to read it out loud, once, in this room, and I am not going to write it down, and if anybody in this room wants a copy of it they can write it down themselves, and the difference between my reading it and their writing it is the whole of what I have been doing for forty-two weeks and it is the first time I have said it that plainly.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" Dalla Sen said, to herself, and nobody laughed.

"**The half against is that a list nobody has written down is a list this room will have to make again in six months, in a bad month, with worse people in the room, and that is the ordinary way a thing like this gets lost,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and that I am doing it anyway, and that the reason is that the two of those are not the same loss. Entered in the space where a reason goes.**"

She turned round and looked at the room.

"**Ten.**" She said it flatly and she did not put her hand near the book. "**Nine people who are not this building's are alive and are at the far end of a cook's room this morning and one of them is limping less than he was on Monday and one of them has a job to go back to in a fortnight and a report he cannot write. Six other people who are not this building's are in two rooms off the far end with a plan on this list for the first time since anybody wrote it down. A pump that has run for nineteen weeks on a rule with two lines in it, one of which is a woman's and one of which is nine hundred years old. A fall that goes down at the same rate for its whole length from a cistern through a gallery to this building, and nobody knows how clear the third of it is.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**", from the top of the nine steps.

"**A chamber with a floor that does not dip and a course of stone round it at the height of a woman's knee and four fathoms of water in it, and two people in this room who do not agree about the word for it and one of them has now agreed. A room of shelves with water damage in it and eleven spines on a tier and a leaf in my coat that I am not going to read in a room. A drain nine days old in the book and a body in it two days old and a decision one day old not to break it.**"

Nobody in that anteroom moved, and the pump made a stroke and then made another.

"**A door at the bottom of a wet stair that is a door and not a way out and goes across in four minutes and came back in four minutes on Monday and nine people came up it. Four vents that a woman shut and a woman opened and nobody is going to pretend that was a plan. A stove that has not been lit for thirty-six weeks. A pot at three days and a seam at four threads that has given nothing for eighteen mornings. Four households at a second gate with nothing. A clerk's book with a name in it against a loss.**"

"**And the things that are not in it,**", and she did not look up.

"**And the things that are not in it,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and I am going to say those out loud too because a list of what a room has that does not say what it has not got is a salesman's list. Nobody in this room knows how many people are in this building. Nobody knows how much water is in the chamber four days away — the air engineer has the figure of pails and has refused it three times in a room and the room refused the figure with the clerk's book, and that is two refusals of the same number in a fortnight and neither of them is mine to join. Nobody knows who watches a heavy thing in a channel four days away. Nobody knows what is on the fourth line of a guild's claim, or who wrote the second line, or what the three things on that claim's fifth line have to do with a fall that a woman read off a wall.**"

She shut the book and put her hand flat on it.

"**That is the whole of it and I have read it out loud once and I am not going to read it again this week, and if a person in a year finds this page and finds nothing on it they are to understand that there was a list and it was not written down on purpose and the reason is in the space where a reason goes and I have entered that the reason is the refusal of a heading and that the refusal is five volumes old and has a name in it that is a building's, not a man's.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything for about as long as a pump takes to make five strokes, and the pump was between strokes and then it was not, and the pump went on.

Then a child of nine came down the nine steps from the room at the kitchen-line end with her shoes in her hand and came and sat on the bench at the anvil end, which is the bench, and put her shoes on, and nobody had told her to do anything, and she had been in the room since the fourth bell and the clerk entered that she was in the room and had not been asked to leave it and would not be.

"**Say what you want to say,**" said Bel Ordry, "**or do not say it, and I will take either and I have said that sentence in this room before today and it is the only sentence I have ever said in this room that I would say again.**"

Mara Quell put one shoe on.

"**Nine of us go to a school,**" she said, "**and there are nine at that school who are not us, and they have not had water since before the cold closed, and we have known that for about two weeks, and I have not said anything about it in here because I do not know where you keep the thing you say things into.**"

Nothing came out of anybody in that room.

"**So somebody told somebody,**" Mara Quell said, "**and it was not you, because you have not said anything, and it was not me. It is the ones who go out at the far door. Nine of us go out at the far door and we talk. That is all it is. Somebody said *there is water in that building* and we asked *whose water* and somebody said *we do not know, they have not told anybody*, and that is a funny thing to say about a building and we have been saying it for a week and a bit and I did not think anything of it until this morning when the room at the kitchen-line end had nine children in it and every one of them was telling somebody else that there is water here and does not know who has it.**"

She put the other shoe on.

"**My brother has not had water for two days,**" she said, "**and he is eight, and he asked me this morning whether the school with the beech table had got it, and I said no, and he said then how does the other one have it, and I did not have an answer, and I am not going to say anything about that to anybody in here because it is not mine to fix. I just wanted to say it in a room, and I am saying it in a room, and I have said it, and now you can do what you want with it.**"

She got up and went out at the far door.

"**Do not follow her,**" said Mikka Hale, from the kitchen-line doorway, and nobody had got up, and the clerk entered that nobody had got up.

Then the elected keeper came down the four steps he comes down to, which is the third time in five volumes and the first time in a fortnight, and he stood there and did not come the rest of the way, and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him why.

"**I am going to say one thing and it is the only thing I have,**" said Sefu Aran, "**and I have had it since Wednesday and I have not said it because a woman at a counter said on Tuesday that a fact is out of a room by the following day, and I have been waiting four days to find out whether she was right, and she was right, and I was asked at the second gate yesterday afternoon by a woman at the bottom of the steps who is not one of the four and who I do not know.**"

The room did the thing it does about four times in a week, which was nothing, for about two seconds.

"**She asked me whether this building had got water,**" Sefu Aran said. "**And I am the elected keeper of this boundary and my instrument is admission and a crossing, and a question like that is a crossing, and I could have answered it at that gate in about four seconds. I did not answer it. I said that it was not mine to answer and that a clerk would have to, and she said that a clerk at nine steps above me had better have an answer by Monday, and I said that I would put the question in the room, and I have put it in the room by standing up on a step and saying it, and it is about two days late and I have made it late myself.**"

"**Then say the rest of it,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**I am not going down those four flights of steps this week,**" Sefu Aran said. "**And I am going to say why, and the reason is not that I have nothing to say this time. I went down them on the seventh day of the forty-first week and I said the answer to the faces of four people and it did not fix anything, and a clerk wrote that down before I had finished and wrote down that the reason was not a mystery and had never been a mystery. I have read that entry nine times. I am not going to do the same walk again with the same face on so that a room in here feels better about itself, and I am not going to stand at the bottom of a stair and be the answer to a second question of the same shape, because a man who answers the same question twice for the same people is not a keeper, he is a habit with a face on.**"

"**Entered, in the keeper's words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that this is the second promise of his this volume and it is not a payment of the first and it is not a refusal of the first, and entered that a man has been two days late with a question because he would not answer it at a gate, and entered that the two of those things have nothing to do with each other and I am not going to put them next to each other,**" said Dalla Sen, and she wrote that last part in a different ink, which she has done twice in five volumes.

"**Then say what you are going to do instead,**", and he did not look up from the stair.

"**Nothing,**" Sefu Aran said, "**and it is the first time I have said that word in this room about myself and I would like it entered that I have said it and that nobody asked me to and that I went up four steps today to say it.**"

"**Entered, and entered that the keeper of this boundary has no instrument for a question about water and has said so in a room, and entered that his instrument is admission and a crossing and that a question about whether this building has got water is neither of those, and entered that I have not written the two next to each other because I have been asked three times in a month to and I said no three times and I am saying it a fourth time standing next to the man who said it,**", and her voice was not different at all.

Then the anteroom was quiet for a long time, and Dalla Sen did not write any of it down, and the clerk entered that she did not write any of it down, and entered the reason, which is that a list of what a room has and a list of what a room is short of were read out loud in the same hour by two people, one of them nine years old, and that putting the second of those into a clerk's book on the same page as the first of those would be a join, and that this clerk has refused four joins in a volume and is refusing a fifth, and that a room which refuses a join is a room that has learned something, and that she is not going to learn it twice.

"**Then I am going to say the whole of the position out loud,**" said Bel Ordry, from the top of the nine steps, and he had not come down all morning and he came down four of them, "**because a clerk has read a list out loud and a child has read a list out loud and this room now has two lists and they are about different things, and a room with two lists is a room that has to be asked a question, and I would like to be the one who asks it and to be in this room when it is asked.**"

He stopped on the fourth step.

"**What this room has, this morning, is nine men, six men, a pump, a rule, a chamber, a ruin, a door, a body in a channel, a decision about the body, a watch with nobody's name on it, a sentence said on Friday by a man with no post, and a list a clerk read out loud and did not write down. What this room is short of is a way of turning any of the twelve into the thirteenth.**"

"**That is a good line and I am not going to improve it,**", from the middle of the floor.

"**It is not a line, it is a position,**" Bel Ordry said, "**and I am going to hand it to the clerk to be entered as a position and not as a finding, because a finding is something this room has checked and I have checked none of the twelve.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the man who said it, as a position, and entered that it is the first position anybody has entered in this book in five volumes, and entered that a position is a thing a person stands at and is not a thing a room has decided, and entered that a man with a page in a coat said that out loud as well and that a clerk is tired of being the only person in a room who distinguishes the two,**", with her hand near the book.

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell and had heard a clerk read a list of what this room has and a child of nine read a list of what this room has not got, and he had done the thing he does about four mornings in ten, and what came up at about the fifth bell was the last thing he had in him this fortnight and it was not a structure.

The list was right. That was the thing. Every item on it was true and he could have put a figure on three of them and had deliberately not, and the figures would not have helped, and the clerk had read the whole of it out loud and had not written one word of it down, and the room had not been frightened by it, and had not refused it, and had sat with it.

And the item he would have added was the one that was not a have.

There is a room four days away with four fathoms of water in it and a floor that does not dip and a course of stone set round it at the height of a woman's knee, and a woman said on the third day of the forty-first week that a chamber is a room with an institution round it, and a man said on Friday that a gallery that is drained is a room people can work in, and between those two sentences there is a thing this building has never once said out loud, which is that the nine-hundred-year-old men who cut that course at one height all the way round a room were not storing water.

They were working in it. A course at one height all the way round a room is not a mark on a wall. It is a bench. Somebody cut a bench round a room at the height a person sits at, and cut a floor that does not dip so that a person could stand on it, and cut the walls in courses that are level, and then put four fathoms of water in the room and let the salt come up on the walls and went away.

He had known it for two days. He had worked it out on Thursday night with a shut book on his knee and had looked at it for about an hour and had not said it, and the reason he had not said it was not the groove and was not the offer and was not a rule. It was that it is the only sentence he has ever had in this building that is not a figure, and that if he said it out loud then Bel Ordry would have to write it down, and Dalla Sen would have to put it in a column, and Nera Oduya would have to have an opinion about it, and Renn Vale would have to keep it, and Mero Senn would have to stop it, and a room that has a bench in it has a keeper of a bench, and this building has refused six posts and a man at a page and a foreman and an air engineer and a cook in five volumes and it does not need a seventh thing to be the one that broke it.

And it is not a have. It is a sentence about a room that has not been drained. It is a thing this room does not yet own, in a room this building cannot reach, about people who are not this building's and have been dead for nine hundred years, and putting it in a clerk's list of what this room has would be a lie, because a room does not have a sentence. A room has stone.

He put his hand flat on the book and left it there, and he did not say it, and he knew what he was doing and he knew exactly what it was going to cost, and the cost was that a sentence in his head is a room of one and he is the only person in it and he is not going to be in it for very long.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a clerk has read a list of what this room has out loud and has written none of it down, and a child of nine has read a second list out loud, and neither of those is in a book, and a room that is holding two spoken things and no written things is owed the two numbers that are in every book in this building, and is owed the one number that is in none of them, and the clerk is going to give all three and then she is going to close the book for the week.

"**Seven today and four hundred and twenty-one since the keeper signed. The boundary is at five hundred and thirty-eight and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and seventy-five days and one hundred and seventy-five nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and seventy-six, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for sixty-four weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in sixty-four weeks, and this morning I read a list out loud in a room and wrote none of it down, and that is the first time those two things have happened in the same week in forty-two weeks of this book, and I do not know what to make of it and I am not going to guess in a room.**"

The board was walked, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven that are not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it and had nothing over the top of it at the first bell. Tams Quill was not asked about it and did not offer, and the clerk entered both of those, and entered that she is not going to ask, and entered that a man whose right hand is open, flat and not a working hand was in this room for a whole morning while a clerk read out what this building has and did not ask him whether any of it was right, and that this is the first time a clerk has read a list in a room and not asked a man with a flat hand whether the list is right, and that she noticed, and that she entered the noticing and not the reason.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman said in a room on Friday that if this building builds a workshop the first man who asks to keep it has asked for a post, and that he is going to be the one who says no, and that he said it before he was asked and has still not been asked, and the clerk entered that she wrote both of those and that a man who has refused nothing is a man who has not been made a refusal, and that she does not know which of those two a clerk is supposed to write down.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning for the eighteenth morning running, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it and nobody explained it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for thirty-six weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a child of nine has said out loud in this room that a child of eight at a second gate has not had water for two days, and a clerk has entered that and not written it down, and the pot is at three days, and a pump is running, and there is a seam that has given nothing for eighteen mornings, and neither of those two facts has moved and neither of them is going to be fixed by anything that happened in this room this week.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a book that was shut for the week, and nine steps of a stair with a room at the top of it where nine children had been taught that morning and would be taught on the third day of next week, and a far door with nobody at it, and a bench at the anvil end with a child's size on it, and a door at the bottom of a wet stair that is a door and is shut, and a channel under this floor with a heavy thing in it that nobody is watching because nobody may be named to watch it, and a man at the end of a trestle with a sentence in his head about a bench that he did not say and is not going to say this week, and a room that has, for the first time in forty-two weeks, been told what it has, and has not been asked a single question about it, and is going to be asked one, and the question is already at the bottom of four sets of steps, and it is there because nine children go out of a far door and talk.
