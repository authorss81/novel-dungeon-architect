# Chapter 217 — Four Sets Of Steps

The seventh day of the forty-first week is a Sunday, and a seventh day is not a crossing day, so nobody came up the nine steps that morning and nobody went down them. The school did not run and lost nothing. The pump ran at the second bell and stopped at the fifth, because a seventh day is not a shut day and is not an open day and the pump has never known the difference, and at the seventh bell on the seventh day of the fortieth week the clerk entered that nobody had thought of it until it had happened and that she was not going to make a rule about it now.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the thirty-seventh reading, and the same as thirty-six, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right. Then she did not give the four refusals, and the room noticed inside of about four seconds that she had not given them, and nobody said anything about it for a while.

"**I said on the seventh day of the fortieth week that I was going to give a reason instead,**" she said. "**And I have had a week to think of one and I have not had a week to think of one, because a reason is not a thing you think of, it is a thing you have been not saying for thirty-seven mornings. So here it is, and it is one sentence, and I am not going to give you four.**"

"**Say the sentence,**" said Dalla Sen, with her hand near the book.

"**A wall is not mine,**" said Jo Verrick.

Nobody in the anteroom improved on it and nobody asked her to.

"**That is the whole of it and it is not the whole of it, and the rest of it is this,**" she said. "**If I put a number on that join, then in about six months somebody in this room is going to ask me whether the number is in my head or in the wall. And I am going to have to answer that question in front of you. And I am not going to have done that. So I have refused four things a day for thirty-seven mornings, and every one of the four was true, and none of the four was the reason, and the reason is that I would rather be tiresome for a year than be a person in this room who cannot say where a number of hers came from.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the reason is about her and not about a wall, and entered that this is the first reason about a wall that anybody has given in this room, and entered that the clerk did not ask for a second one and would not have accepted one.**"

"**And I am not going to say I will never measure it,**" Jo Verrick said. "**I have said that about a great many things in this room and I would like somebody in a year to notice that I have said it about a great many things.**"

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is twenty-two.

The terms were said at the second bell, which on a shut day is her bell and on a seventh day is the only hour this room will accept them at, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it was right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and on a seventh day it is said like everything else in here, and it did not change. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing, and a keeper went down four sets of steps an hour later and the clerk entered that the fifth term had held all morning. And the sixth was the pump, and it ran, and it has run on a seventh day since a Sunday in the fortieth week, and the phrase this room uses about it is unchanged and the pump is unchanged and both of those are now wrong in the same direction.

Then the elected keeper came down the nine steps, all of them, at about the third bell, which he has not done in five volumes, and he did not come down to the trestle. He came down to the fourth step and stopped.

"**I am going down to the second gate,**" he said, "**and I am going down because of something I said in the twenty-first week, and I have carried it for twenty weeks and it is the only thing I have ever promised anybody in this building that I did not do the same week.**"

"**Then say what you are going to say,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I am going to say the answer to their faces,**" said Sefu Aran. "**The answer is me. It is not a list. It is not good — I said in the twenty-second week that it would not be good and it is not, and I have had twenty weeks to improve it and I have not improved it, because it is not the kind of thing that gets better. They are owed everything. Nothing has been given to them. This boundary looked after itself for two volumes while four people carried water up four sets of steps, and the clerk wrote that down and wrote down the number of the times and wrote down that it was true, and none of that is a thing I can stand at the bottom of a stair and undo.**"

"**Then take the clerk,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because a thing said at that gate is a thing that this boundary has to be able to point at afterwards.**"

Dalla Sen had the book shut and then she had it open.

"**I am going,**" she said, "**and I am going to enter what he says and I am not going to enter what they say back, and I would like the room to hear the difference between those two things before the fifth bell, because a keeper went down to that gate on the sixth day of the thirty-ninth week and came back up and declined to report what she had been told, and everybody in this room thought she was right, and I am going down and I am reporting one half of it and not the other half, and if that is a different thing then somebody who is in this room and is not me is welcome to say so at the bottom of the steps.**"

"**It is a different thing and I can see why,**" said Renn Vale. "**You are the clerk of this boundary and he is the keeper of it and he is answering for the boundary and what he says is the boundary's. What they say back is theirs and I was not the keeper of them, I was a woman who went down and said a term she had broken, and I had no standing to enter one syllable of it. Go and enter him and leave them out of your book and I will tell anybody who asks that you did it on purpose.**"

"**Entered, in the keeper's words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the reason for the half of it is standing and not taste.**"

Then Marek Venn got up off the stool at the end of the trestle, which he does not do, and came down the nine steps with them, and stopped on the second one.

"**I am coming as far as the landing,**" he said, "**and I am not coming further, and I would like the reason said out loud now rather than at the top of the steps where it sounds better.**"

"**Say the reason,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**My knee does not bend past about half,**" Marek Venn said. "**Four sets of stairs is about nine hundred and forty feet of rise if you count the landings, and I have been down four sets of steps twice in nine weeks and both times a man with a sling came behind me with a rope and neither time was it mentioned in a book. If I go down those steps this afternoon, then four people who have had nothing since the cold closed are going to watch a man who cannot manage a stair come down a stair in front of them, and they are going to be comforted by it, and it is going to cost them about an hour of their own afternoon, and they will not get an hour of this building's water out of it.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Dalla Sen, and her voice was not different at all.

"**That is the whole of the reason and it is the first reason I have given anybody in this building in five volumes that is about me,**" Marek Venn said, "**and the four before it were about other people and the clerk has entered that four times, and I would like her to enter that the fifth one is about me and that I have noticed the difference, and that I have not decided yet what the difference means.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he said the number himself before she wrote it,**" said Dalla Sen, and she wrote it, and nobody in that anteroom thanked him for it, and the clerk entered that nobody had.

Then the keeper and the clerk went down, and the room was a room with fewer people in it than it had at the first bell, and the four terms that are said at the fourth bell on a day when nine people went down a wet stair were not said, because the fourth bell is when the nine children come down nine steps and there were no nine children, and Renn Vale said so and the clerk entered that she had said so.

Marek Venn went and stood on the landing at the foot of the nine steps with his hand on the rail, and the four sets of steps went down from the landing in four flights with a turn on each, and he could hear the whole of it, and about halfway down the second flight somebody's voice stopped and started again.

What he could not do was work out what it was about, and he stood on that landing for the better part of an hour with a hand on a rail he did not need, and he understood that this was the shape of the forty weeks he has been in this building. He had a shut book under his arm that nobody had asked him for, and a shape in his head that he had given away on a Tuesday to two other people, and a stage he could not use, and a woman from a second gate who had said a sentence at the end of that trestle on a Wednesday a fortnight ago that two other people in this room had said before her in their own words, and he was standing on a landing listening to a conversation he could not hear, and there was nothing in the world he could add to it, and he had been told there was nothing he could add to it twice that week by two different people and both of them had been right.

At about the fifth bell the clerk came back up the nine steps with the book under her arm, and she was not wet and she was not cold, and she did not sit down.

"**Entered,**" she said. "**In his words, all of it, the whole of what he said, in the order he said it, and I have entered that he did not say a list and that he was asked whether he was going to and that he said no, and I have entered the two volumes and the water and the four people carrying it, and I have entered that he said he had carried it twenty weeks and that I have believed him, and that believing him is not a finding.**"

"**Say the finding,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The finding is that it did not fix anything,**" said Dalla Sen. "**A promise from the twenty-first week has been paid to the faces of four people, and the four people have nothing, and this boundary has not given them one pail since the sixth day of the thirty-ninth week, and the reason is not a mystery and has never been a mystery and the clerk has been writing it in a book for two volumes. I am entering that in the space where a finding goes and I am entering it now, before anybody has said anything else about this morning, because I want it on the page before anybody in this room starts being kind about the rest of it.**"

"**And what they said to him,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**No,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And I have said why I would not enter it before I went, and I am not going to give the other half of the reason now, and the reason I will not give it now is that I have not got it written down anywhere and I have not got a slate, and I am a clerk and I do not carry things in my head that are going to come back out in a room, and that is the whole of it.**"

Renn Vale nodded at her and did not say anything, which is the closest the two of them have come in five volumes.

Then a woman came up the nine steps, and it took her about a minute and a half, and she did not stop on the top one, and she had been up four sets of stairs a fortnight ago and had refused a chair and given the reason out loud before anybody could have made a thing of it.

"**I was at the bottom of the steps,**" said Petra Wend, "**and I heard the whole of it, because the second flight has a wall on one side and nothing on the other, and I am not going to say I did not hear it, and I am also not going to say what I thought of it, because what I thought of it is mine.**"

"**Then say the thing you have come up for,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I have come up to say one thing and I would like it said out loud in a room and not at a gate,**" Petra Wend said. "**This building has now gone and done a great deal to a room that I asked it to do nothing to. I know that a woman came down those steps and looked at a chamber, and I know that nine men went into it on a piece of paper, and I know about the water because everybody at my steps knows about the water, because it is under the floor of the room my brother's child sleeps in and it has been for a fortnight.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything.

"**I am not going to say thank you,**" Petra Wend said, "**and I am not going to say it was wrong, and I have thought about which of the two I was going to say for a whole flight of stairs and I have decided on neither. What I am going to say is this, and I would like the clerk to write it down and not improve it: the four households at the bottom of these steps have nothing, and the reason is not that anybody has been preventing it, and the reason is not that there is a mystery in a gallery, and the reason is that a boundary decided a long time ago that it would not have a post and has been very pleased with itself about it for two volumes. That is all. That is the whole of my reason and I have carried it up four sets of stairs and I would like it said in a room.**"

Dalla Sen wrote for about as long as it takes to say a sentence, and she entered it in the woman's own words, and she entered that she was not asked, and she entered that the woman at the bottom of the steps had heard a keeper pay a twenty-week promise and had said nothing about it and had come up the stairs to say that instead.

"**And the last thing,**" Petra Wend said, "**which is small and which I am going to say anyway. I asked this building a fortnight ago not to do anything to a room full of water. And this morning a woman at the bottom of my steps said the answer is him, and it is a true thing, and it did not help, and I am telling you it did not help not to make anybody feel better about it. I am telling you because it is what happened.**"

Then she went back down the nine steps and down the four flights, and nobody in that anteroom thanked her, and the clerk entered that nobody had, and entered that the woman had not asked for anything on her way out, and that the whole of what she had come up for was a sentence she had to say in a room rather than at a gate.

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither was read, and the reason went in beside them, and the reason was that a morning on which a keeper went down four sets of steps and paid something that had been owed for twenty weeks is a morning on which a clerk is not going to stand up in front of a room and read a total.

One today and three hundred and fifty-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and seventy-five and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that on its own line, in the ordinary hand, and was not read, and the reason was given, and it was that the clerk is the woman who wrote down that it did not fix anything, and she is not going to read a number over that.

Wen Pask is one hundred and sixty-two days and one hundred and sixty-two nights, fifty-one weeks of a clerk saying out loud that she is going to keep writing it down. It is not going to be one hundred and sixty-three.

Then the clerk entered a thing that has nothing to do with any of the above and that she had worked out on the Friday, on the stair, alone, and gave the reason for it in the space where a reason goes.

A head of water has been on the other side of the party wall from the second bell to the fifth, three days in seven, and this building has been saying *three days in seven* for four volumes, and the three are the first day, the second day and the fourth day, which are the three days of the week the corridor is shut, and a shut day is a day on which nobody carries anything up nine steps and the pump is the only thing running and the haunch behind that frame has nothing to relieve it.

And on the seventh day of the fortieth week it was on for three hours on a Sunday, which is not a shut day and is not an open day, and it has been on every seventh day since, and so it is four days in seven now, and the standing phrase says three.

"I am entering that the phrase and the wall disagree and that I am not changing the phrase," said Dalla Sen. "A phrase is not a rule. I have sat in this room for forty weeks and told it that over and over and I have been right every time, and if I change a phrase because a wall has changed then the first thing this room learns is that a phrase can be edited. So the phrase stays at three days in seven, and the wall says four, and both of those are in this book, and a person in a year can have the argument."

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a clerk has entered a promise paid, a finding that it fixed nothing, a refusal to report half a conversation and a phrase that disagrees with a wall, and a board is a column, and a clerk does not walk one on a Sunday.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and it is that a foreman said he will not ask for his four and a half hours before the open days, and there is no open day in a week of this one's beginning.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for twenty-three weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody and were told so to their faces this afternoon, and three of them were at the top of a clerk's book and one of them was not.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a landing below it with a rail on it, and a room at the kitchen-line end with nine strangers asleep in it, and a passage at the bottom of this building with nine people in it and a lamp, and a rail on a landing that a man had put his hand on for an hour because he could not go down a stair and would not stand at the top of one and say so.
