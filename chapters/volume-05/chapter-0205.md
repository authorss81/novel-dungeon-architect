# Chapter 205 — The Terms Before The Asking

The second day of the fortieth week is a Tuesday, and the corridor is shut, and the anteroom was nine steps and one pump that was not running and a floor that was colder at the kitchen-line end than at the top of it. The school did not run and lost nothing. The salvage claim was on the trestle where it had been left on Monday and nobody had turned it over, and there is a thing about a piece of paper lying face up in a room for a day, which is that everybody who comes into the room reads it, and by the fourth bell on Tuesday there were three people in this building who had read a guild's second line and could not say whose name was on it.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell and did not look at the frame, because she had said on Sunday that she was going to leave it alone for a week and she was a person who said things and was expected to do them. The party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twenty-fifth reading, and the same as twenty-fourth, and the string was where the string was, and she put her thumb under it out of habit and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words and the clerk entered them as four refusals and not as one.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the second bell and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and entered it, and the count of mornings out of mornings is twelve.

The anteroom's terms were said at the second bell, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The third was six pails a day and it was false and it was said. The pump was attended, and stopped, and had been for three days.

Then nobody sat down for about ten minutes, and then Emrys Callow stood up, which he does not do in that room.

"**I have been in this building eight weeks,**" he said, "**and I have brought five men up nine steps on the first day of a week and set their packs down in a row along that bench, and every morning since then I have come down those steps and looked at that row and the row has been there, and I have not asked, and they have not asked, and neither of us has said one word about it in eight weeks.**"

"**Then say it now,**" said Renn Vale.

"**No,**" said Emrys Callow. "**That is what a man says when he has decided to say it and I have not decided anything. What I am going to say is why I am standing up, and then I am going to ask, and the asking is not part of the standing up.**"

Dalla Sen put her hand near the book.

"**A foreman does not ask a crew for a work in a room where the work is not settled,**" Emrys Callow said. "**I have said that out loud in this building in the thirty-fifth week and it was in the book the same afternoon and I have not disagreed with it since. There is no work to ask for. A pump has stopped and a seam has given two and a pot is at two days and a guild has left a piece of paper on that table, and I have got five men who are extremely good at shoring and holding and reading a wall, and there is not one hour of that in this building's week that I could honestly ask them for.**"

"**Then why are you standing up,**" said Bel Ordry, from the top of the nine steps.

"**Because the fifth morning of this week I went past the second gate,**" Emrys Callow said, "**on my own, not on anybody's word, and there are four households at the bottom of those steps and they have had nothing since the cold closed, and I have known that since the twenty-first week of this building because a clerk entered it, and I went past the gate and I did not stop and I have not been able to account for that and I have had a fortnight and a half to account for it.**"

"**Entered, in a foreman's words, and entered that he was not asked,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The half against that is not mine, it is hers, and I am putting it in the room because she put hers in the room on Monday and this room does not know what to do with a fact it has been given and I am not leaving it lying there,**" Emrys Callow said, and he did not look at Renn Vale while he said it.

"**I said on Friday that what a person says to a keeper at a gate is not the keeper's to enter, and I meant it, and I am not withdrawing it,**" Renn Vale said.

"**I know you are not,**" said Emrys Callow. "**I am not asking you to enter it. I am asking this room to hear that a keeper went down four sets of steps and said a true thing, and that a foreman went past the same gate on Saturday and did not stop, and that the two of those are the same kind of thing going the other way, and I would like a clerk to enter that I said it.**"

Dalla Sen entered it, and entered that two people had said it and neither of them had asked the other.

Then the room waited, and it was Emrys Callow who broke the wait, and he did it the way he has done it twice before in this building, which is by saying the terms first and asking second, and which is why this boundary has never once had to argue with a man about what he was being asked for.

"**Terms, out loud, and then the asking, and the asking is in this room and not outside it.**"

"**Four and a half hours,**" he said. "**There is water in this building and it is not coming out of a pipe and it is not coming up a pail, and there is a head of water somewhere in a haunch behind that frame that nobody has ever looked at because there was a pump on top of it, and it is not a shoring job and it is not a bracing job and I do not know yet what it is.**"

"**Then what is the four and a half hours,**" said Iven Tallo, from the anvil, without looking up.

"**Reading it,**" Emrys Callow said. "**A wall that is wet behind a pump and a floor a man sleeps on with his head six feet from it, and a joint that is weeping into a corner and nobody knows where to, and two jars of lime water in a trestle this morning. That is not shoring. That is two men with a string and a lamp and a pot of water to see which of nine things is happening, and it is a thing four and a half hours buys and it is a thing two days buys properly and we have got neither.**"

"**The split,**" said Dalla Sen, "**before anybody names it.**"

"**I have named it,**" said Emrys Callow. "**It is the same split as the fourth asking and the sixth and there is no new split, and the split is two of mine, two of yours, and half of the fifth man who reads numbers and has never once been asked for anything in a room in eight weeks, and I am naming the half because if I do not name it then somebody will offer it and it will be a favour.**"

Tams Quill looked up from the end of the bench with the piece of chalk he has not used.

"**I will take the half,**" he said, "**and I will say the thing that goes with it, because I have not been asked for anything in eight weeks and I have said a reason out loud twice and I am not going to be given a third one for free.**"

"**Say whatever you like,**" said Emrys Callow, and did not take his eyes off the middle of the room, "**and it does not change the half and you may not give it back after.**"

"**I have been on the rota for eight weeks,**" Tams Quill said, "**and I have read eighteen numbers out of a stranger's sheet and a wall at a quarter of an inch every morning for twenty-four mornings and I have walked a board with nineteen things on it that are not rooms, and I have not been asked for a single hour of work in eight weeks because this room has nothing it can hand a man who reads numbers, and that has been restful, and I am going to say the word I have been avoiding, and it is not restful, it is a room that has decided it has nothing to give anybody and has made its not-having into a policy and put the policy in a book.**"

Nobody in the room said anything.

"**That is not my eighth reason for anything and I want that understood and I want it in the book in these words,**" he said. "**It is not a reason for the column. It has nothing to do with the column. It is the reason I have been sitting at the end of that bench for eight weeks with my right hand open and flat and everybody in this room has decided that is a thing about a hand.**"

He put the hand on the table, palm up, and did not close it, and did not try to.

"**It is a thing about a hand because a hand that cannot close is the reason I was not useful in the first eight weeks, and then a room got used to me not being useful, and then a room got used to me being here, and there is a difference between those two and it took me eight weeks to work out which one I was in, and the answer is the second one, and the second one is a man sitting in a room being paid out of a column with nothing over the top of it for sitting in the room.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the man who said it, and it is not a reason for the column and he said so before he said it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the crew's column is a quantity of asking and a man who is being paid for sitting in a room is not being paid for asking, and entered that a clerk has written that down and does not know what to do with it and is going to leave it where it is until somebody who is not a clerk does something about it.**"

"**You are going to leave it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I am going to leave it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I would like somebody to write down that I said I would leave it and that I am the clerk.**"

Then Emrys Callow named the three who could not be asked, and he named them before the asking, and he gave a reason for each, and none of the three reasons was one this building had used before.

"**Leena Orr,**" he said, "**and the reason is not the reason from the last three askings, and I am going to say the new one and I would like the clerk to have it exactly. Her trade is reading a thing that is already built, and everything I have asked anybody for in this building has been a thing that is not built yet, and this one is worse than that, because this one is a thing that was built nine hundred years ago and has been standing there with nobody watching it and now it is wet. She is the only person in this building who could tell me what a wall is doing and she can only tell me by putting a rule on it, and the folding rule is in a belt on the fourth of the fortieth week and has been on a belt for twenty-five mornings, and I am not asking the woman who has just told this room four refusals about one wall to come and put her rule on a second one in the same week.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the foreman, and it is a new reason and it is not the reason from the last three askings,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Pell Roke,**" Emrys Callow said, "**and the reason is the same shape as every other time and I am sorry about it and I am going to say it anyway because a reason that has not changed is still a reason. The work is holding and the clerk is not going to ask him to watch instead, and a boy with his ear against a stone at the top of nine steps is not going down nine steps into a wet corner on a Tuesday, and I have not asked him and I am not going to and nobody in this room is going to suggest it.**"

"**Entered, and entered that the clerk did not ask and did not intend to,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**And the third,**" said Emrys Callow, "**and this one is new and it is not about a body and it is the only one of the nine of them that is not about a body, and I would like the room to notice that before I say it.**"

He stopped, and the room waited, and it was a waiting that went on slightly too long and everybody in it heard it.

"**Nera Oduya,**" he said.

The air engineer did not move off the second of her three steps.

"**She cannot be asked, and the reason is the water, and the reason is going to be strange to say out loud in a room and I am going to say it out loud in a room anyway because that is what this room does and I have stopped minding.**"

"**Say it,**" said Nera Oduya, in her own voice, and she had not asked and the room noticed that she had not asked either.

"**There is no pump in this building this morning, and there will not be one this afternoon,**" Emrys Callow said, "**and every pail of water that comes into this boundary today comes in through nine steps, by four or five men, up a corridor that is shut on a Tuesday, and that is the whole of the water. If the air engineer is in a room reading a wall from the eleventh hour to the third, then for four hours this building has a keeper and a clerk and a cook and no air authority, and the last time a room in this building was four hours without an air authority, six people were asleep in two rooms off the far end of an anteroom with a door that latches, and I have not slept a whole night since the thirty-fifth week about that and I am not going to explain it in a room.**"

"**Then the half is the wrong half,**" said Nella Bray, from the anvil end, "**and you know it is, because you have just said the air engineer cannot be in a wet corner and then asked a man who reads numbers to stand in one for four hours.**"

"**It is not a wet corner,**" Emrys Callow said. "**It is a room with a wall in it and a floor with a hole in the corner and nine things to look at. The air engineer would be on a step.**"

"**Then ask Tams Quill for a step,**" said Nella Bray.

"**Tams Quill does not stand on a step and look at a wall,**" Emrys Callow said. "**He stands on a step and looks at a floor and reads a number off it, and I would rather have the number. And I have said the reason and I have said it out loud and I am going to ask now, and if anybody in this room thinks the split is wrong they can say so after, and not before, because I have been doing this in this building since the thirty-second week and I have never once asked for a work and then found out what the work was.**"

"**Then say the asking,**" said Dalla Sen, and did not put her hand near the book.

"**Emrys Callow, nine years, shoring, gave up a cleared-risk post-holder's bond in this district, and five men. Four and a half hours, today, on the third and fifth of this week and the open day after, with the terms as I have given them. I will not carry for a man whose name is on two lines of a form, and I am not going to start on a Tuesday, and my five are not going to be on any column, and the hours go on the line they are already on and nobody adds them up. And Renn Vale is the person who can be told no, and I am telling her now, in front of everybody, and she has not said yes and I am not going to ask her in a corridor.**"

"**I have not said yes,**" said Renn Vale, from the middle of the floor.

"**I have not asked you,**" Emrys Callow said.

Renn Vale agreed with him.

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle with his shut book under his forearm and he understood the whole of it about four minutes before it happened, which is the thing that has happened to him in this room more often than anybody knows.

He watched a man hold a room by the shape of his own asking for eight weeks, and he watched a room give him the asking anyway without being asked, and he understood that a boundary which cannot ask is a boundary that gets asked, and that this is the second time that has happened in this building and that the first time was the material in the thirty-first week, and that the price of the timber was two lines on a form and the price of this is going to be something else and he did not know what it was yet.

"**I am going to say the half against and I am not going to say it kindly,**" Nera Oduya said, from her second step, and the room turned round.

"**Four and a half hours of a crew with a stopped pump is four and a half hours of people carrying water, and this room has just asked five men to do a thing that a woman in this building is paid for, and it did it in a room, and it said the terms out loud, and that is exactly how this room does everything and it is the reason I have watched it for five volumes and the reason I have never once been able to say a bad word about it.**"

"**Then say the bad word,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**There is no bad word,**" said Nera Oduya, and did not move off the second of her three steps, and had the dyed cloth folded in her hand and not out. "**I have four numbers and not a fifth this morning and the fifth stays in my mouth. My first condition is not met and my second condition is not met and nobody has asked me about either of them and nobody is going to be asked by me this morning. I am not going anywhere. I have not said any of those five things to make anybody feel better about the sixth.**"

Nobody asked her.

And then somebody asked, out loud, without arranging it, from the kitchen-line doorway.

"**How much water does this building use in a day,**" said Mikka Hale. "**That is all. I said a number out loud on Saturday that was about water and not about people and I would like somebody who is competent to say whether the number I said is right, and if it is not right then I have said a number in a room that is wrong and I would like that entered.**"

"**Entered, and entered that she was not asked and asked,**" said Dalla Sen.

Nobody answered her, and Emrys Callow did not ask his crew for anything that morning, and the anteroom went on being the room it is.

Nothing crossed the corridor all day and the anteroom did not get emptier and did not get fuller, and the light came off the wall where five and a half shores stand the way it does on a Tuesday in the fortieth week, and the two rows of packs along the bench stayed a row of packs and were not a row of tools, and the man who reads numbers had his hand palm up on the table for about a minute and a half and then put it in his lap.

At the sixth bell Dalla Sen read the day's figure and the total out loud and then read the count, and gave the reason for reading all three, and the reason was that a room which has been told a thing about itself that it has been carrying for eight weeks is owed three true numbers before the day is over.

"**Five today and three hundred and fifteen since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and thirty-two and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty days and one hundred and fifty nights,**" she said, and stopped, and looked at the slate for a moment, and then went on, and the room noticed both halves of that and not one person in it said anything.

"**It is a number of days and not a number of anything, it is not going to be one hundred and fifty-one, and I have been promising this room for forty-five weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in forty-five weeks and I am not going to stop, and it is a hundred and fifty this morning and I read it out and I am not going to say anything else about it and a person in a year is welcome to check that nothing else was said.**"

Bel Ordry, who had not moved from the top of the nine steps, put one hand on the frame of the top step.

"**I am going to say one sentence about that number and then I am going to go and stand somewhere else,**" he said, "**and it is not in the same four words as anything else I have said this week and I have had a week to think about how to say it. That number is a number of days. It is not a milestone, it is not a birthday, it is not a foundation, and it does not belong to anybody in this room, including the man it is for, and if it is ever read out again in a room by a person who means something by it, then that person has found a thing in a column that was not put there for them.**"

Nobody thanked him either, and the clerk entered that nobody had thanked him, because that is the standing practice and because it is the first time in this building that a refusal and a thank-you have been declined in the same week by two different men for two different reasons.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a man stood up and did not ask and then a man stood up and did, and a board is a column, and a clerk does not walk a column in the same hour as either of those.

The pot is two days and the seam gave nothing on Monday and nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for sixteen weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a keeper has been down those steps twice and has not reported what she was told.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with a book on it and a guild's claim on it with a line of chalk through its fourth line, and a pump that had not run for three days, and a row of five packs along a bench belonging to five men, and at the end of the row a gap of about a foot where a sixth pack had stood eight weeks ago and had not come back. Nobody had been asked for anything. A hundred and fifty was on a slate in a clerk's hand and two men had declined to make anything of it, and a woman in a doorway had asked a question that nobody in the room could answer, and a man who reads numbers had put his hand palm up on a table and said the room had made its not-having into a policy.
