- **Chapter range:** 201–250

# Where this departs from `outline/series.md`, and why the departure serves the ending

### Departure 1 — the drainage rule is found before the sump ox, so the ox is a receipt and not a revelation

**Committed:** *a sump ox reveals the flooding is caused by a failed drainage rule, not a monster's appetite.*

**Delivered:** Chapter 219 finds the rule — a fact about a rule, from a person, out loud, in a room — and **the sump ox arrives in Chapter 227, eight chapters later, after the midpoint.** In between: the reservoir chamber, the submerged archive, the maintenance crew, Nera's ventilation plan, the pressure door, the controlled loss, and a batch of chapters in which a room already knows the water is a rule and is arguing about what to do with it.

**Why this is better:** the series' version makes the monster the teacher. **This version makes the room the teacher and the monster the receipt**, and the difference is the whole reason the monster has to exist at all: *a room that already knew* is a room that has to act anyway, because there is a school on the line and a pump station on the line and a warehouse on the line, and knowing is not draining. **And `bible/world.md` is the authority: a sump ox is a pressure-supported form occupying a stalled drainage route, and breaking it does not repair the flood.** The escalation therefore has a mechanical truth to deliver, not a taxonomic one.

**How it serves the ending:** the ending needs a city where residents can withdraw a dangerous project through a council process that protects neighboring districts. **A room that learns a fact and then argues for nineteen weeks about what to do with a fact is the council, demonstrated on a small scale, before the council exists.**

### Departure 2 — the residents are right about the water and wrong about the keeping, and the difference is the volume's civic argument

**Committed:** *residents who want the water kept for drinking rather than drained into the surface.*

**Delivered, in full and unsoftened.** Four households at a second gate have had nothing for two volumes, and a person from that gate comes up the nine steps in Batch 0001 and asks this boundary for the water. **The boundary cannot give it and cannot refuse it, and the honest answer from the clerk is that keeping water is a post and this building has decided it will not have one.** And the person at the bottom of the four sets of steps says the sentence the volume is built on: **water is not a thing anybody keeps. It is a thing that goes somewhere. The question is where it goes when it goes.** They are right about the water and wrong about the keeping, and the book does not correct them and does not let this boundary accept it either.

**Why this is better:** the series' version has residents as a pressure group to be balanced against engineers. **This version has them as the only people in the book asking the right question**, and it puts it to a room that has spent thirty-nine weeks refusing to be drawn by questions.

**How it serves the ending:** the ending's world is a city where essential routes and records are public and private stewardship is permitted but not exclusive. **A keeper who is a person and not a post is that principle, and the person at the second gate is the first person outside this building to hold one.**

### Departure 3 — the romance begins inside the public argument, not beside it

**Committed:** *Nera becomes an equal political planner, and their romance begins as a private respect for one another's competence.*

**Delivered at Chapters 240–242 and nowhere else.** The climax is a cooperative pump-and-brace effort, and its moral half is a public disagreement between the air engineer and the man with no post about who had the right to choose the risk. **Both are right. He is right that a person cannot be spent as a counter-support. She is right that a plan which protects nobody is not a plan, and she is right that a room which will not choose cannot be helped by anybody who can.** **The respect is stated in private, once, in one sentence, between two people, at the end of a chapter about a public argument, and it is not a scene that is only the two of them and it is not a scene about looking at each other.**

**Why this is better:** a romance that begins in a private scene in the middle of a volume is a reward for reading. **A romance that begins inside the volume's central public argument is a consequence of it, and it is slower, and the reader has to do the work.**

**How it serves the ending:** `outline/ending.md` requires that Marek obey Nera's technical stop order during the final crisis and that Nera trust him to refuse the seat without closing her routes. **They cannot do that unless they have disagreed in public, in a room with people in it, and both been right, and neither gone.**

### Departure 4 — the volume opens on this building's own instrument, not on a body in a gallery

**Delivered:** the pump. **The pump runs attended, two people, second bell to fifth, and the last of the six terms says it is stopped on a rising water line chalked on the head of its own frame by a woman with a sling on her arm.** The line has not moved in nineteen weeks and the pump has run against it every working morning for nineteen weeks and nobody in that room has looked at it since the week it went on there. **On an ordinary Friday it stops about nine minutes past the second bell, of its own accord, and there is a second mark on the frame a hand's breadth under the chalk which is not chalk and which the woman who reads the frame saw at two o'clock the day before and did not report for a day.** The building then has to re-make six pails a day, in two crossings, and it cannot, because from Chapter 195 nobody in it can say how many people are in the anteroom.

**Why this is better:** a volume about water that opens with a monster in a gallery is a volume about a dungeon. **A volume that opens with a chalk mark moving on a pump frame is a volume about a public works boundary that has just discovered the cost of its own last refusal**, and the flood is four chapters away and is already the volume's fault.

**How it serves the ending:** the ending's *Room Checks* are a field discipline and the whole final movement is a city running them without a wizard. **A book that starts a volume by running one Room Check — drainage — against its own rule gets there honestly.**

### What is *not* departed from

The central pressure, the four major locations, the four major factions, the midpoint reversal, the escalation, the climax, the concrete resolution, the next-volume question, and the power/relationship change are all delivered as committed. **The departures are to the order in which things are found, to the shape of the opposition, and to the starting state. They do not touch the volume's destination.**
