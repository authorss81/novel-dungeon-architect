# Character State — End of Volume 02, Batch 0002

## Opening party

Six people came out of the Cinder Steps. **Five are here at the end of Volume 02, Batch 0002**, and all five appear in Chapters 61–70.

### Marek Venn

- **Position:** the Hearthhold air-gate anteroom and its lower level, the common landing, and — by named work period and named terms — the lower corridor, the school corner, and the stair's service bend, fixed first landing, and near landing.
- **Body:** Right hand permanently lost for fine control from the Chapter 5 jack crush; left hand usable and strained. **Seven written nulls** and nothing usable. In Chapter 63 he reaches for a reading in the cold, gets nothing, and **refuses to write *null*** — a null in the cold is not the same instrument as a null in the warm, and a column that has a lie in it is worse than an honest one. Tams Quill opens **a column with no heading** with a one in it, the temperature, and the reason (*a fault in the reader and not a fault in the building*), and says a null in the cold is worth more than a null in the warm and he wants it counted twice. **The count stays at seven.**
- **Mind:** Caught once in this batch and self-corrected once. In Chapter 64 he admits in his own words that he **filled one of the three boxes on a Cartwright route-disposition form in his head in four seconds with no name in it**, that nobody saw him do it, and that it is the same four seconds he has been doing since the seventh week, and he asks for the rule to go in the book with a time on it: *a man who is stopped once is a careful man and a man who is stopped twice is a man with a rule, and I have only got the first one so far.* In Chapter 69 he **does not take the chalk out of his pocket** over a quarter inch in a pump housing, because the figure is inside Jo Verrick's number and a figure said in a full room becomes his in nine seconds. In Chapter 70 he **puts a slate down blank side up and leaves it on the bench**, because the only thing he had to record belonged to a woman at a frame. His interior life appears in six of the batch's ten chapters, against three in Batch 0001.
- **What he has worked out, and it is not insight:** that he is the man who asks a question out loud in a corridor and is willing to be told no. The batch's last line is him doing it to Jo Verrick, about a post that Nera Oduya has deliberately left blank.
- **Immediate choice:** the leaving is unchanged. **The spring conversation has happened** — the second day of the twelfth week, the fourth bell, one hour, Sefu Aran and Nera Oduya with the five of them and the clerk, and the leaving not mentioned once — and it produced one thing that is not in the ledger: a name Nera Oduya said at the top of three steps, which the person has not been told yet. Talla Roke's second gate is a second spring and is still unpromised. The maintenance obligation's end condition has not been met.

### Leena Orr

- **Position:** litter in the anteroom, the lower corridor, and the service bend; limited clinic care access, not residence. The litter does not go past the service bend for anything on the returning stair.
- **Body:** Right shoulder dislocated in Chapter 1 and reduced in Chapter 2; slinged, no weight through the right arm, **not currently dislocated**. Four bearers and four minutes through a two foot eight inch gap on a wet floor.
- **New in this batch, and it is the batch's human cost:** with the waist shut on three days in seven, **her litter is on the anteroom's side of the closure and the clinic is on the other.** Mikka Hale said it out loud with the reason — *I cannot stop a bleed up nine steps in a corridor two foot eight wide with a litter in it* — and then said, and asked for it in the book in her own words with the time on it, **I asked for it.**
- **Mind:** Unchanged and still the structural critic, and used rather than displayed. **She chalked the water line on the pump frame** — the line the draw is stopped on, because the check is unusable in the cold — and the line has not moved, because *a chalk line is a reference and does not move*. Her chalk marks off a step are copied onto a slate by Marek because she does not go down three steps.
- **Immediate need:** shoulder recovery, a dry place, and a route to the clinic that does not depend on a piece of ground that has moved.

### Iven Tallo

- **Position:** anteroom, lower corridor, common landing, the fixed east stair, the service bend, and the stair's near landing inside the cord.
- **Body:** uninjured; exhausted. Thirty-six. **Twenty-nine hours of uncompensated instruction** as last counted, plus two days of shore work he has asked Dalla Sen to keep off the front of the ledger. He prices work out loud as a matter of principle and in this batch he priced two things: the search (*the best wood in a building is almost never the wood anybody can have*) and the waist (*what locally is, right now, is that somebody stands on it*).
- **Role and the turn:** **Sabra Kest told him in front of the room, before he could find it in a file, that his suspended bond can be reinstated on a single document.** He refused, and the reason is the best argument in the batch: *a licensed man who signs a route does not stop being the man who signed it when he leaves the room, and there are five of us who are leaving, and the only way to make me leave is to take my licence.* He then read out the *carried* column's method applied to a pump bed and said **a bed is not a shore — a bed is packed and then it is finished, and a finished thing is a thing that can be taken** — which is why the best timber in the Hearthhold cannot be cut. And in Chapter 68 he **argues for the charter** that the reinstated bond would have come from, and says out loud, before anyone else can, that he said the correct response to a guild page is nothing on the fourth day of the batch and is arguing for the guild on the ninth, and that both are the same sentence and he has not changed his mind about anything.
- **Immediate tension:** the first person in this building to be offered the thing he actually wants by an honest official in front of witnesses, and to have said no to it in a room, and then to have argued for the thing the offer was made of. That is a debt and the next batch should collect it.

### Pell Roke

- **Position:** the anteroom bench, the top of the nine steps, the stair head, on his crutch. Never on a step.
- **Body:** fourteen; left ankle sprained in Chapter 8, splinted, supported. Cannot run, take unsafe loads, cross the gallery deck, or take three steps and a lip.
- **Mind:** Three things this batch, all of them his. He is the plinth reader and calls the turn **eight seconds** early, and on the first crossing in the cold he said afterward that he had called it eight seconds early and **he would not be told he had been late or early by anybody who was not standing where he was sitting.** He **corrected the clerk's word *boy* to *fourteen*** from the bench, unprompted, and Dalla Sen struck it and read the correction back. And he asked that the crossing count be recorded properly: **six is a photograph of six cold mornings, it is not six of anything else, and nobody is to use it** — and the clerk read the line out twice without being asked, which is the first time in twelve weeks she has put a boy's words into the air without him asking.
- **Family:** **Talla Roke is his older sister and it is in the prose** (Chapter 53, entered in the ledger in Chapter 60). **She has left the building. He is not mentioned in connection with her anywhere in Batch 0002. It may not be re-dramatised as a discovery. It is known.** His name is still in the margin of the plinth rota in Tams Quill's hand as a debt and not a duty.

### Tams Quill

- **Position:** the tally board, the count, the water record, the reading-back, the column with no heading, and a right hand that opens.
- **Body:** **The hand opened in the eleventh week, in Chapter 61, and it is a scene.** The gap that had been a finger and a half is the width of two fingers; the little finger still does not come all the way down; the hand opens flat and stays open for as long as he asks, and he asked for twenty-two seconds because somebody in the room would have counted otherwise, and he says that is not the twenty-second second. **It is still off the lever, the bar, the chisel and the packing, and nobody let it near the housing** — and the refusal was not his.
- **Role:** He reads the stores, finds that the boundary has eleven inches of hardwood in it and every inch belongs to somebody, and asks for the search to be recorded as three days and not as hours. He opens **a column with no heading** and leaves it with no heading on purpose, because a day is not an hour and a pair of shoulders is neither, and because if he gives it a name it becomes a unit and then something a room can be praised in. He finds that **the packing which went into the shore's head and the packing the waist wanted are the same wood, six weeks apart, on the same page of his own column**, reads both lines out loud, and says *that is mine* in front of a room and asks for his name under it. He refuses to write Marek's cold null in the ordinary column, puts it in the new one with the temperature, and says **a room that only writes down the half it likes is the guild, and it is not going to be the guild.** He puts the seat's cleaning in **held, not spent**, because it went into a thing and the thing is still there. He is the man who says **the half that gets counted is the half that is ours**, and who adds that the crew's column went up twice in a fortnight and not one of the hours was work anybody in the boundary needed done.
- **Visibility:** named in every chapter of the batch.

### Oren Sill

- **Position:** dead.
- **Death:** Chapter 48, at the near landing's lip, bearing, with four people behind him and the stair turning. **Not recovered. Not converted. Not named as a living man anywhere in Chapters 61–70.**
- **How he is used in this batch:** only as a method. Tams carries his sentences about packing a face in a *carried* column in his own left hand, written longhand because he was frightened of losing it, and reads them out **once in ten chapters**, in Chapter 62, at the moment the question of the pump's bed is decided — because the method is about bearing faces, and a bed is not a shore, and a bed is *finished*, and a finished thing is a thing that can be taken. **Jo Verrick says out loud that she learned the set face off a man who is under a stone eleven feet down.** Iven Tallo says, in Chapter 68, that the local repair of the waist is the thing that killed him, done on purpose.

## Nera Oduya

- **Position:** the valve, the stair-side grille, the clinic return channel, the school corner's floor, and the stair's new air. She has not gone below the top of the three steps in this batch, and has said that the Sunday in the tenth week was the first time and the last time without her in writing.
- **Authority:** technical authority over airflow, air testing, and safe operation of the air gate. Unchanged, independent, and never folded into a resident's consent or a keeper's veto. She **still refuses to certify the stair's air**, keeps a watch on it, and **still refuses to publish a rate.**
- **The batch's work:** she discovers that **the shaft is not a chimney in the cold** — the dyed cloth sank, lay down against the wall of the throat, and came back slowly — and says out loud that everything she taught the room about that stair was a warm-weather lesson taught in September, and that **the terms were written for the worst case and the worst case is now a six minutes in which nothing moves because nothing is moving.** She puts the blank on the review page — somebody at the low end of the corridor in the six minutes — and **refuses to name a person and says she will not**, because naming one in a week when the water came up is how a person ends up named in a worse week. She records the throat's first ambiguous answer and says out loud that **her own refusal to publish a rate and her own method are standing on opposite sides of the same wall**, and that for one morning in the twelfth week the instrument stopped being an instrument. She **names a person for the air valve watch hand-over**, in her own hand, at the top of three steps, in a room, and the person does not know yet, and she goes and stands at their door on the third day. She writes the **hardwood inventory** — twelve lines, thirteen with the want — and refuses to put a name in the wrong column, because there is no column for a person who has a thing.
- **Relationship:** no intimacy, no recognition scene, no accelerated beat. **There is no viewpoint shift in this batch.** Their closest moment is not between them: it is a question Marek asks Jo Verrick about a post Nera has left blank.

## Sefu Aran

- **Position:** the civic wheel, the deeper gate, the threshold, the cord, the black cord's hook, and the boundary's stores.
- **Role:** He **names the delivery-seat work and strikes half of his own naming aloud**, because a keeper who strikes half his own sentence and does not say so is a keeper who has learned to write. He **asks the crew for hours by name** for the search — eleven words that turn out to be the whole of what Marek Venn has to learn — and he says that the **correct response to a guild page is nothing and that the correct answer to a boundary's own scarcity is a shortage of reach, not a shortage of planks.** He **schedules the spring conversation** with a date, an hour, a room and two people, and says in front of the clerk that it is not about the crew, that it happens whether or not anybody is leaving, and that he will not let a keeper's wet-seam position become the building's water timetable and its school timetable. He **flags the anteroom over its rating** when fourteen people are in it and sends six of them down the nine steps to listen. He says the day a keeper starts saying no to paper is the day a keeper starts keeping a file, and he will not be the one who says it: **the clerk will, and he will stand next to her when she does.**

## Hearthhold boundary maintainers

- **Renn Vale:** keeper of the shore, the road, and the lead line. In this batch she **feels the ground move through the sole of a boot** and says that is the whole of the instrument anybody has built for that waist; she **says out loud that a wedge in a fill does not come up because it is tired** and that telling you is the whole of its office; she writes an **eighth term** — if it turns out to be a door then it is a door in a building I keep and nobody goes down it, and that decision is made now, in the cold, so that in the summer nobody can say *we did not know*; she **refuses to name the wash rooms on a list** because the one person who could answer has a mother in the same hole; she **refuses to put her own seventh term in the one free-text field on a Cartwright form**, reads it out to a stranger first, and is thanked for it by a woman with a stick; she **refuses to have the woman from the wash rooms' eleven words copied into a slate**, because it is hers and it is not policy; and she says the sentence the batch ends on: **then we do it four days in seven** — the corridor open on the days she holds the wet seam, shut on the three she does not — **calls it a calendar and not a rota, puts it in the book in her own words, and says it is the worst thing anybody in that building has said out loud.** Her sixth-watch position is four days in seven and unchanged; her stair-watch refusal is a wider refusal of the same thing and is **not withdrawn**.
- **Dalla Sen:** repair ledger, route record, work-bell relay, cost witness, and the person who asks who checks the tally. This batch she **writes the fact that four people offered before she writes any of the names**, and puts the *boy*/*fourteen* correction in her own hand; she **enters that a post was offered to nobody**; she **chalked an empty chair for a Cartwright assessor and did not use it**; she **records Mikka Hale's request that her own cost be read at the fifth bell on a schedule**; she **refuses *pending* a second time in eleven weeks**; and she is stopped by Jo Verrick over the word in a failure entry and changes it in her own hand.
- **Jo Verrick:** trial pump maintainer with **five paid cleanings paid for and an estimate of one**, moved in her own hand for the first time in eleven weeks, and **no check** — a hiss on one note in the cold, and the two-note vocabulary withdrawn. In this batch she **refuses Tams Quill the seat in front of the room**, prices the work line by line before she starts and names *I do not have the wood* as line nine, **opens the housing and holds the seat up into a lamp in front of nine people at her own request**, says that in four years somebody will ask her whether she looked, **refuses to write zero** because zero is a decision, **applies her own ninth-week rule against herself** — a cart is not a person and pails are not a cart — and asks for both sentences on one line, and names the price of a look she did not need.
- **Mikka Hale:** kitchen, clinic return watch, pump frame, meals, and now **the water**. She counts the water for the first time in eleven weeks and says it out loud, and asks for a column with no heading because it is a pair of shoulders. Her number goes **nineteen, sixteen, fifteen** and she asks for it to be read at the fifth bell every day so that she is not the only person who knows. She says the thing about **Leena Orr's litter and the shut waist**, and then says *I asked for it* and asks for it in the book with the time on it. And she announces **a book of favours in her own hand** — every date, name and pails, read out at the fifth bell every week — because a favour is a loan with no date on it, and a boundary whose water runs on favours is a boundary that cannot keep a charter.
- **Ilsa Norr (nine children and herself):** she makes Jo's lip reading a range in the cold, refuses the school store's two beech lengths **and then says she would have said yes if the shelf had been sound**, **corrects her own ownership from *Ilsa Norr's* to *the school, held by Ilsa Norr***, says a day of school is a day the nine children do not get back, and then says the batch's hardest civic sentence: **the school used to run when she said it ran, and it now runs on Renn Vale's days and not on hers**, and asks for it in the book in her words and for her own cost to be read at the fifth bell on a schedule. The **register boxes and the plan on a dowel are still refused, the key turns in the thirteenth week, and the crew will not be in the room.**
- **Isel Ferrow:** the first reader of a stair in the Hearthhold. In this batch he **feels the waist go with Renn Vale** and is there for the whole of it, **takes the near lip for six crossings a week** and asks for the word *asked* to be written next to his name, **does not leave the lip when the turn comes**, and **stands at the pump frame from the second bell to the fifth with his hands empty, losing shutter-route work that pays him**, and says so out loud in front of Dalla Sen.
- **Ruk Sella:** plinth reader in this batch, and the instrument that **failed in the cold** — she felt the turn late, said so in her own words, and Dalla Sen made her say it twice, and the record says a cold thing under a cold hand does not move the way a warm thing does.
- **Bel Ordry:** of the two rooms above the clinic return, thirty-five, has held nothing in twelve weeks. In this batch he **offers the only piece of sound hardwood in his rooms — his mother's floor — and refuses to be thanked for it**, in a full room, in the best argument in the book about why a gift is a lock; he **takes the threshold bell he did not volunteer for**, says plainly that a bell is the only part of that stair that has to be *heard*, and **names his cost as a month of his mother's door instead of a day**; and his bar at the second bell produces the **first ambiguous entry in the record**, which he refuses to write as a knock and refuses to write as nothing.
- **The woman from the wash rooms:** **she is asked before she is needed.** She says **no** to twenty-eight crossings a week, out loud, in front of everyone, and the reason is the covers at the second bell and the fifth and a mother under a slab. Then she does the arithmetic herself, standing on the step, and finds a **six-crossing yes that costs her nothing she had**, and says she is not taking yesterday back, she is adding to it, and that this is a woman who has been here a month. Later she comes up **on a day she is not needed** to say the condition she has been carrying: the reason she shut the route — *I am not a pump hand and I do not know what a thin hiss is* — is now the condition she personally stands in six times a week, and there is no thin and no full any more and nothing to teach her and nobody to ask. And she says her **eleven words a second time**, in her own hand, brought written out because she did not want to do it in front of them, and asks for them to stay hers.

## Dael Harrow, the Cartwright line, and the guild claim

Dael Harrow is still not present. His route-custody order is unchanged and still cannot open the Hearthhold gate, order air, create consent, or establish ownership. **The guild has now answered in writing, and the answer is a form with three boxes and a person to fill one, and nobody filled one in.**

**Corva Nye** (Cartwright route-custody clerk) is gone; her margin is in a file. **Sabra Kest** (Cartwright route-custody assessor) is new in this batch, walked forty days with the reply, entered herself in the ledger on her own authority with a name and a number, read the reply out standing up, let a woman at a frame interrupt her, and **put the form flat on the bench because a form held up is a performance.** She said the thing nobody had said in this book yet — that **nobody has to be the bad one; there just has to be a form, a deadline, and a person in the room who knows what the box is for, and after that it is only arithmetic.** She offered Iven Tallo a reinstated bond honestly and in front of witnesses. She **did not** ask to observe a crossing, having read Renn Vale's refusal of Corva Nye in the file. She was asked whether a known condition would go in the one free-text field, and was refused, and wrote **none reported** about a fault she had been told about, and said she would be asked about it and would give the same answer again. **She is a competent, sincere, bounded official who did her job properly and went away. She is not Dael Harrow and she is not Cassian Rook and she must not be turned into one.**

**The claim is not resolved.** **Form 9-C is returned as *unattended*,** which means no officer of any chartered office may be required to enter, inspect, maintain, fund, or answer for any condition arising on that stair. **Renn Vale has said out loud that this is simultaneously the best answer anybody in that building could have wanted and the reason nobody is ever going to help them,** and the batch has now paid for that position twice: once in cold water and once in an unrepaired floor.

## Talla Roke

- **Age:** twenty-two. Courier, salvage picker, and route scout out of the middle gate. **Gone.** She went out at first light at the end of Batch 0001 with a satchel, a drawing of the second gate's inlet, and a copy of her own withdrawal in her own hand. **She is not in this batch and her cost is still on the page: four households in the second gate carrying water up four sets of steps this winter.** And the batch has now shown the rest of it: **her withdrawal cost the middle the timber that would have fixed its own water-lock, which is the same timber as the seat, and the middle's water-lock is dry, and the timber is four days away and cannot be got.**

## The stair, the plate, and the evidence

The stair is **restricted, corded, and in service on its near side only**, with two timber shores on the common landing, the newer one packed and **not wedged**, and a lip that now ranges **a finger and a half to a finger and three-quarters** in the cold — worse than after the shore, and it is given as a range because in the cold a quarter inch is inside the noise. **Four treads go down behind the rail on the far side and nobody has been on them and they are not walked this month**, and Tams Quill read that term out among the seven and noted it is the only one of Nera's four conditions that nobody in the room has ever thought was about safety. The far wall of the throat is eleven feet down and has never been seen. **A wooden knock with a slide on the end of it came out of it in the tenth week, and in the twelfth week Bel Ordry's cold bar found three ticks with a slide on the third in the same place, and the same tick in two other places on the same cold wall.** Three events, three hands, none of them classified, and nobody has been down there. The **wrapped empty-seat plate** lies in a knotted bundle of work cloth, unexplained, unidentified, unused, unclaimed, and **unopened**, and it may not be connected to the plan on a dowel or to anything in the throat. The **brass survey rule** is in the pocket of the coat on its hook and has said nothing for six weeks, and Marek has not put his hand on it once in a fortnight, and there is nobody left in the building who would be able to tell him whether that was restraint or luck.

## Relationship and power guardrails

- No harem structure and no instant romance. Nera remains an independent air engineer and civic adult. **No viewpoint shift in the batch, recorded as an author decision.** No intimacy, no recognition scene, and no acceleration.
- **No formal loadcraft stage, bearing mark, repeatable reading, route change, binding, relay, or automatic repair has been granted.** Seven nulls, all written down, all giving nothing usable, plus one null refused and put in a column with no heading.
- The five Room Checks and the declared-occupancy discipline remain the growth framework. **The anteroom's rating is unchanged for the third volume running: the route changes the escape and it does not change the occupancy**, and it was flagged aloud this batch when fourteen people were in the room and six of them went down the nine steps to listen.
- The working practice — the person who will keep a room writes the change to it — produced nine new instruments in this batch in nine hands, **and not one of them is a document anybody outside this building can hold.**
