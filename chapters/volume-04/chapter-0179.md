# Chapter 179 — The Last Thing A Room Does

**Shut on the seventh day of the thirty-fourth week, a Sunday, and shut by the standing arrangement alone, because the seventh day is not a crossing day, and the school did not run and lost nothing. At the third bell a lime repair in a party wall came away along the line where bad work meets sound stone, and behind it there was a space nobody in this building has ever been in, and nine children were not in this building, and the clerk entered all four of those facts in that order and did not say the word for the thing until the fourth bell.**

**The room worked out what the void was doing and could not work out what it was doing it about, and said both of those things out loud, and a man said the reason it is not being killed in his own mouth with the load in it, and nobody went in.**

**And a woman who keeps a room wrote one line in her own hand in the third column of a page in a coat, and it is the one line in that column that is about a wall.**

The seventh day is a day on which nothing in this building crosses anything. The nine steps are the only stairs anybody uses, the pump runs from the second bell to the fifth, and the rest of the building is the sound of water finding its level.

Jo Verrick read the party wall at the first bell with a piece of chalk and a piece of string, the way she has read it every morning for as long as she has been reading it. The string was where the string was.

Then she read it again, because the number was not the number.

"**Say what you have,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I have got a line.**" Jo Verrick did not put the chalk down and did not step back from the wall, which is not her way. "**Under the string, along the join, there is a line of nothing about two fingers wide, and it runs from the height of my knee to about the height of the string, and on Thursday morning that was lime.**"

"**Say how you know it was lime on Thursday,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Because I put my thumb in it every morning to find out whether the string was still tight, and it has been lime for as long as I have been reading it, and it is not lime now.**" She took her hand off the wall. "**Nothing has gone into that gap and nothing is going to.**"

Nobody moved for about four seconds, and the pump got a stroke in.

"**Now go back to the string and give it to me again, and take your time,**" said Dalla Sen.

Jo Verrick went back to the string. She set the tape under the chalk, read down to the chalk on the flags, and did it twice. Then she read the eleven readings out of her own book against where the string stood on Thursday morning, without hurrying, and Dalla Sen read them out of the clerk's book at the same time in the same order, and the two were the same in all eleven.

"**Entered that eleven readings were read against the original and against the book and that they were the same, and that neither the original nor Jo Verrick's copy of it has been altered, and that the wall this morning is not one of the eleven,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Say what is in the line, then, and I am asking you as the person who has read that wall eleven times,**" said Renn Vale.

"**I have got air coming out of it.**" Jo Verrick's voice had not changed and her hand had. "**Cold, and moving, and not much, and it moves in a rhythm, and the rhythm is not the pump and the rhythm is not the water. It comes about four times in the time it takes me to say this and it stops for a count and it comes again. And it is coming out of a space and not out of a crack, because a crack does that. This is a gap with a floor to it.**"

"**Say the half against your own reading,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because you have made a very large claim on a string and a thumb.**"

"**The half against it is that I have read a wall eleven times and a wall does not have a floor, and that a rhythm in a gap is a rhythm I can hear and not one I can measure, and that there is a folding rule on this belt and the belt is staying where it is.**" She put the tape away. "**And I would like somebody to notice that the reason I cannot measure it is that in thirty-four weeks I have only ever been given walls.**"

Sefu Aran had been standing at the end of the trestle since the first bell doing nothing at all, and he said one word.

"**Say that again,**" he said.

"**Say which,**" said Jo Verrick.

"**The four in the time it takes to say this.**"

"**Four in that, and it stops for a count, and it comes again.**"

Sefu Aran crossed the anteroom in three steps and put his ear against the party wall about a foot above the flags, and he stayed there for a long time, long enough that the pump got two strokes in, and then he came back and stood where he had been.

"**That is not the pump and that is not the water,**" he said. "**I am going to say one thing about it and then stop, because I am the only person in this room whose whole job is a question about whether a place is a room, and this is not the morning and this is not the corridor.**"

"**Say the one thing,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The sound is somebody making a decision.**" He put his hands flat on the table. "**Not a person making a decision. A decision being made, over and over, the same decision, the way a thing is decided when there is nobody in the room to decide it. And I do not know what the decision is, and I am not going to stand here and guess at it, and I would like it entered that I was the second person in this room to hear it and that I did not find it out and that I heard a person find it out.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that the second person to hear it was the elected keeper and that he did not find it out,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Now say the part that matters, and I am asking both of you,**" said Renn Vale, "**because Jo has got a space and Sefu has got a sound, and a room that puts those two together in one sentence is going to get a monster out of it by Friday.**"

Nobody put them together.

Iven Tallo came over from the anvil end, stood in front of the line, did not touch it, and looked at it the way he looked at a joint. Then he got the one lamp in that room, set it on the flags a foot off the wall, and did not light it.

"**Say what the join is,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it as a builder and not as a man who has heard a thing breathe.**"

"**The join is where the lime repair stops and the old stone starts.**" He pointed at it without touching it. "**There is a survey's finding in this building's book that this wall is a bad repair on a sound wall, and it is nine hundred years old in its stone and about two hundred in its mortar. The stone is one of the best I have ever put my hand on. The mortar is not work. It is something put there quickly by somebody who wanted the wall to stop being a problem.**"

"**And what is it doing now,**" said Marek Venn.

"**It is coming away along the join, in a line, at the rate of about a hand's width a day, and neither I nor the clerk has measured the rate of it and neither of us is starting today.**" He straightened up. "**And I can tell you the shape of what it is doing and I cannot tell you what it is doing it about, and I want a room to hold on to that, because the shape is the easy half and the half I do not have is the half that matters.**"

"**Say the shape,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**It is closing.**" Iven Tallo did not move his hand. "**Not opening. It has been going the other way since Thursday, whatever the join is doing, and the line under the string is getting wider, and that is a thing that is being shut as much as a thing that is being opened, and the sound in the wall is a thing being shut. Four times, and a count, and again.**"

Nobody said anything. Marek Venn felt the thing he has felt in empty rooms since the twenty-eighth week come up the inside of his left forearm, in the place the channel frame marked it. It was the same sensation he had got in a corridor on a Tuesday in a bad month, and in an anteroom on a Saturday with a crate board under a stone, and it was not an instrument and had never been one. It told him a direction and no cause.

He did not put his hand on the wall.

"**Say what you are not doing,**" said Dalla Sen, "**before somebody else does it for you.**"

"**My hand stays off it,**" he said. "**And the reason is that I have read this wall eleven times with a string and a thumb and it is not going to be me who is the one man in this building who puts a hand on a thing that is repeating a decision, because that is the one thing I know about this that is not in anybody's book and it is in mine.**"

"**Entered, and entered that a man said no to a reading out loud, and entered that the reason was in his own book and not in a guild notice, and entered that I have no reason to put in the space where a reason goes and I am leaving it empty,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then the air engineer came down four steps, all of them, which she does not do for anything that is not about air, and she stood in front of the line and listened to it four times, and then said a word and would not say it twice.

"**Say the word,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and then say whether it is a finding.**"

"**It is a word I have read eight years in other people's cellars and it is the word for a thing that is happening because a room is finishing something it never finished.**" Nera Oduya did not point at it and did not go near it. "**And it is not a finding, and it gets said once in this room, and twice would make it a thing this room knows instead of a word a woman carried in from somewhere else.**"

"**Entered that a word was said once by the air engineer and that she declined to say it twice, and entered that the word is not a finding and that I am writing it once in the space where a thing this room does not know goes, and entered that a word is not a name and this room is not going to give it one this week.**"

"**Then say your work,**" said Renn Vale, "**because there is a room nine children are taught in on the other side of that wall and they are not in this building today, and a room with a gap in its wall is a room that has to be looked at before it is used, and I am the person who says when this corridor is open.**"

"**And I certify the air of it,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and I stood in that room on Friday morning with nine children in it, for the first time in eight weeks. I will not tell you the gap has changed the air, because I do not know, and I will tell you equally that it has not.**" She turned round. "**So I will say the two things I can say. The air moves out of that gap and it is cold, and a room with a draught out of a wall in it has an air that is not the air I certified on Thursday, and the limit I set a fortnight ago has no number and no day on it. If the air does not stay what it is, the brace comes out before the air is argued about.**"

"**And the brace is on the other wall,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The brace is on the other wall, and the air in a room on this side of a wall is not the air in a room on the other side of it, and I have been wrong about exactly that once this month in front of a room and nobody consoled me.**" She went up the steps. "**So I am certifying nothing and refusing nothing, and I will be in the corridor at the fifth bell and on every open day, and a room that wants a number out of me this week has to wait for one that is not on a page.**"

"**Entered: that the air authority has declined to certify and has declined to refuse, and has said in a room that the limit she set a fortnight ago applies to a wall the brace is not in, and that she named her own error out loud before anybody could name it, and that nobody consoled her,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then Ilsa Norr came up the nine steps from the kitchen-line end and said the sentence she had been carrying since the first bell, and it was not about the fire.

"**I have been in that room since the first bell with my hand on nine hearth stones in order, and I knelt at the ninth one longer than the other eight took together, and I came out at the second bell, because I do not stay alone in a room when I do not like the sound of it.**" She put her slate down on the sill, which is not where she puts it. "**I am telling this room that the room is not to be used, and I am saying nothing whatever about the room being shut, and I want both of those entered, and I want the difference between them explained to me by somebody.**"

"**Say the difference,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**A shut room is a room a person cannot get into. A room that is not to be used is a room I will not take nine children into.**" Renn Vale came down the four steps. "**A shut room is a decision anybody can undo with a key. The other is a decision I have to keep making, and there is a woman in this Hearthhold whose whole job is keeping it, and she has not asked anybody to help her with it.**"

"**Entered, in her own words, both sentences, and that the difference was given by the woman who keeps the corridor and not by the clerk, and that I have asked this room nine times this month to be told a thing and this morning it gave me one I did not ask for,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Now say the last of it,**" said Marek Venn, "**and say it before somebody else asks you, because I have been in this room for two hours saying nothing and I have got three facts and one of them is the reason nobody in this building is going to put a bar into that gap, and I would like it entered in my own words.**"

"**Then say it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**One. Nobody in this building cut anything, and there is no tool mark in that join, and the line is in lime that has gone soft and out along a join two hundred years old.**" He was not looking at the wall. "**Two. The brace across the head of the outer service door pushes on a wall on this side, and that wall is standing up out of the same nine hundred years of stone, and nobody in this room has ever been on the far side of it.**"

"**And three,**" said Renn Vale.

"**And three, and this is the one I want in the book. Whatever that void is doing, it is the only thing in that wall doing anything at all with the force that is coming through that wall, and the force is coming through that wall whether we take the void away or not.**" He put his knuckles on the trestle, the hand that will not close. "**So a bar in that gap is not a repair. A bar in that gap is a removal. And a removal puts the closing's share of that wall back into a repair in lime on the side nine children lean against, and this building found out a fortnight ago what happens when a piece of wood is taken out of a wall on a Thursday morning with a woman standing in the room.**"

"**Say the half against that, and say it as a carpenter, and do not give me a reason about loads I cannot check,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The half against it is that I have been in this building thirty-four weeks and I have never seen a thing do that, and I would not believe it if a man in a book told me, and I am telling you I do not believe it and I am not going to put a bar in there.**" Iven Tallo put the lamp out again without having lit it. "**A thing I do not believe that a man in a book can check is still a thing I will not take a bar to. Enter that as my trade and not as a virtue.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that a man has given his disbelief as a reason and not given it as a fear, and entered that the two are different and that the clerk knows which one she has got,**" said Dalla Sen.

Mero Senn said the sentence, once, and then did the thing he does.

"**Say the name,**" he said, and Ilsa Norr said it before anybody else could.

"**The nine children,**" said Mero Senn. "**Who are not in this building, and whose not being in this building is the only reason this morning has gone the way it has gone, and who will be in it on the next open day. A plan that depends on a day of the week for the safety of nine children is not a plan.**"

Nobody sent anybody anywhere. The review page at the low end was blank and stayed blank with its three conditions on it, and the third of them said that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true. The floor was not approached, no name went on it, and no row was asked for.

Ilsa Norr wrote one line in the third column of the page in the coat, in the hand of the person who keeps the row, on the kitchen-line room, and Dalla Sen read it out and entered it and entered that nobody had improved on it.

"**The join on the kitchen-line side opened to about the width of two fingers on the seventh day. The room is not to be used. I am not saying the room is shut.**"

The seam gave two on Sunday. The cook said the arithmetic out loud the way she does and would not say the word, and the four households at a second gate still had nothing, and no figure was attached to that gate by anybody.

At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total in the book and left them both unsaid, and the count was read at one hundred and fourteen.

"**Three today and two hundred and forty-two since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and fifty-nine and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile. The counter is at seventeen.**"

"**I entered those and did not read them, and the reason is that there is a line of nothing in a party wall in this room. A clerk does not read a total over a thing like that, because a total is what makes a room feel it has found out where it stands, and this room has not.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one, and there has been no sixth asking, because a piece of wall happened and a piece of wall is not a work anybody can be asked five people for. The pot is nine days and the seam gave two on Sunday, and those are not the same quantity, and I am entering both.**"

"**And the count. Wen Pask is one hundred and fourteen days and one hundred and fourteen nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and fifteen, and I have said in twenty-three weeks running that I am going to keep writing it.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, because a word was said once in this room today and refused a second time, and a board is a column, and eighteen entries read out loud this morning would have given the room something to count while it was working out what it had.**"

**On the seventh day of a week, with nothing crossing anything, a lime repair came away along the line where bad work meets sound stone in the party wall, and a woman who reads that wall with a string read it against the original and against the book, and the eleven readings were the same and the wall this morning is not one of them.**

**Air came out of the gap four times in the time it takes to say a sentence, and stopped for a count, and came again, and nobody put a tape in it and nobody put a hand on it.**

**The room worked out the shape of what the void was doing and could not work out what it was doing it about, and said both out loud. A carpenter said he does not believe the reason and will not put a bar in there anyway, and entered it as a trade and not as a virtue.**

**A man said the void is the only thing in that wall doing anything with the force coming through that wall, and the force is coming through that wall whether the void is there or not, and so a bar in the gap would not be a repair and would be a removal.**

**A woman who keeps a room wrote one line in her own hand and said the room is not to be used and is not shut, and a woman who keeps a corridor gave her the difference, and an air engineer certified nothing and refused nothing.**

**And nine children were not in this building, and a physician said that a plan that depends on a day of the week for the safety of nine children is not a plan.**
