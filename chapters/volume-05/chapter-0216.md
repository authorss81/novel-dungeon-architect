# Chapter 216 — What The Plan Does To Nine People

The sixth day of the forty-first week is a Saturday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the forty-ninth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door. No market came into that building on this day and nobody in it went looking for one. The one thing anybody in that anteroom knew about a sixth day arrived in a woman's mouth at the first bell, and what she brought was a fact about a counter and not about anything in the room.

At the anvil end Nella Bray had her slate out from under her arm and looked at it rather than at anybody. "**At the middle gate the counter is shut at the fourth bell on a sixth day. Not because anybody is resting. Because there is nothing to count. So a thing that goes in on a sixth day before the fourth bell is looked at on the second day and not on the sixth, and a thing that goes in after the fourth bell sits until the first day. I have put that in a room for six weeks and I have not been asked about it once, and I am putting it in a room this morning because a man in this room is owed a hearing and a hearing in a room has to happen on a day the counter is open, and I would like somebody in a year to be able to tell from a clerk's book why a hearing happened on a Monday and not a Saturday.**"

"**Entered, as a fact about counters, and entered that nothing in this room is dated to a sixth day,**" said Dalla Sen.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the thirty-sixth reading, and the same as thirty-five, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked her, and the count of mornings is thirty-six and is the same count as the wall's, and the folding rule is in a belt and has been on a belt for thirty-six mornings.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is twenty-one.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it was right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and it is going to be unchanged on the day a man in this room finds out how much water there is, which is a sentence the clerk entered and the cook did not ask her to enter. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The sixth was the pump, and it ran, and at the fifth bell nine people went down a wet stair in front of it and nobody in that room called the connection.

The pump was started, and at about the third bell nine people stood up off the floor of the room at the kitchen-line end, one after another, in the way of people who had agreed something at the first bell and had not yet said so.

Seth Wray came to the end of the trestle and did not put his hands on it.

"**We are going back down this afternoon,**" he said. "**Not tonight. This afternoon, because the light is the same at the third bell as at the seventh and the cold is not.**"

"**Say the reasons,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Four,**" said Seth Wray. "**One. There is a pumping station on that line with a windlass on it and it has not been turned over in nine years and the chamber is filling, and a pump that has not been turned over in nine years is a pump with a body of water on the wrong side of it, and I am not going to stand at the top of a stair and tell a room that the thing that is filling has a pump on it and nobody has looked at the pump.**"

"**Say the rest of them,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Two. The paper that sent us said pumps. We are nine people who turn pumps over. We have been here three days and we have not turned one over, and I have to write a report, and a man who has been in a room for three days and has not turned over a pump has nothing to put in a report except the word *waited*, and a crew that writes *waited* is a crew that gets sent somewhere else next time.**"

"**That is a reason about you,**" said Nella Bray.

"**It is a reason about me and it is a true one and I am not going to pretend it is a better one than it is,**" Seth Wray said. "**Three. If there is a person on the far side of that door when it shuts, then whoever is on that side is going to be holding a line in a wet room with a door closed behind him, and I would like it to be three of my nine who chose to be there, and not a room full of people who were sent.**"

"**Say the fourth,**" said Dalla Sen.

Seth Wray did not say the fourth for a while.

"**Four is that eight of us have been in that room three days and one of us has not slept more than an hour at a time and none of us has been asked for anything and all nine of us have been fed, and there is a woman in this room who has a slate with our names on it and a man with a counter-clerk's hand who wrote down what we are owed, and that is more than nine people have had off this building in three days,**" he said, "**and I would like it entered that I have said that out loud in a room and that I am going back down anyway, and that the two of those are not connected and that I have looked at them from both ends this morning.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything for about four strokes.

"**Then the arithmetic,**" said Nera Oduya, and she was standing and she had a piece of chalk and no cloth, "**and I am going to do it out loud because I did it on the back of a slate in the second bell yesterday and I would rather this room heard it from me than found it in three weeks.**"

"**Do it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Nine people,**" said Nera Oduya. "**One door. Four minutes to shut it. And a length of time that I cannot name, and that is the whole of my plan and the whole of what is wrong with my plan, and I am going to say it in three pieces and the third piece is the one that matters.**"

"**Say the first two,**" said Renn Vale.

"**First. That door has to be shut for as long as it takes to turn a pump over, and that pump has not been turned over in nine years, and nobody in this building or in this room knows how long a turn over takes, and I am not going to put a figure on it to make my own plan tidy, and I have had four chances this week to put a figure on it and I have not.**"

"**Second,**" she said. "**The reason my plan is efficient is this, and I have not said it out loud until now and I am saying it now because nine people are going to stand on the far side of the door. Everything the chamber pushes out has to go somewhere. With four vents shut and two open, what it pushes goes up the shaft that serves this anteroom and the room at the kitchen-line end, and that is only true if the far side of that door is closed. The far side of that door is not this building's while the door is shut. That is the whole of the mechanism. That is why four shut and two opened works. My plan is efficient because for the length of a pump turn-over there is a passage at the bottom of this building that nobody is responsible for.**"

She put the chalk down.

"**There. I have said it. I have been saying a plan for two days and I have not said the sentence under it, and I have had it since the second bell yesterday and I did not say it yesterday because yesterday I did not know nine people were going to volunteer, and I am saying it now because they have.**"

"**Say the third piece,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**If they go back down to do my work, then my plan's cost is nine people,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and I am saying that now, in a room, before the fourth bell, and I am not going to be the one who decides it, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said it and that I did not decide it and that I am not going to.**"

"**Entered, in her own words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the plan has a cost attached to it by the person who wrote it, and that the cost is a number and not a name, and that the number is nine, and that a number of people is not a column and is not a rota and is not anybody's hour.**"

Then Emrys Callow stood up at the anvil end with his cup in his hand.

"**I have got a crew and four and a half hours and the first of my open days is this morning,**" he said, "**and I am not going to ask for them to go down there, and I am going to say why in two sentences and I would like it entered that I said it before anybody asked me whether I was going to.**"

"**Say the two sentences,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**One. A man going into that water on my ticket is a man whose name is on my asking, and this room has spent three volumes refusing to put a name on a thing, and I am not going to do it with four and a half hours.**"

"**And two,**" said Renn Vale.

"**And two is that they have not asked me,**" Emrys Callow said. "**They have not asked this room for anything. They have got five things written down on a slate and a corner of a cook's floor and a stove somebody is going to go and look at, and they have not asked, and I have been in nine years of crews and I have never in my life been in a room where nobody asked me for anything and I went back down anyway on my own. I am not going to be the man who makes them ask.**"

"**Then what this room is going to do,**" said Bel Ordry, "**is going to be said out loud by somebody, and I would like it to be said out loud by the clerk, because a clerk is the only person in this room whose whole trade is saying what a room did.**"

"**Then I will say it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I will say both halves and I am not going to improve either of them, and this goes in the space where a finding goes and not in the space where a reason goes, because it is a finding.**"

"**This room is not going to stop nine people going down a wet stair this afternoon. This room is not going to help them go either. It is not going to give them a lamp, a line, a bell, a time and a name to shout. It is not going to put one of its five men on that line for four and a half hours, and a foreman has declined to be asked, and a keeper could have been asked and was not, and a man at the end of a trestle could have been asked and was not. Those are two decisions and I am entering them as two decisions and not as one thing.**"

"**Why not help them,**" said Iven Tallo.

"**Because helping them is a post,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The day this building puts a line and a bell and a name on nine people going down a passage, that line is a rota, and the rota is a column, and the column has a heading over it, and a heading over it is a thing a bad month can be pointed at. There is a woman at the bottom of four sets of steps in this district who has been owed water for two volumes and who was told in a room by this clerk that this boundary will not have a post. If I write nine names and a bell on a slate, then the first person I have to say no to at that gate is her, and I will say no to her with a line and a bell in my hand, and that is a different sentence and I will not be able to un-say it.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything, and Bel Ordry came down the rest of the nine steps for the first time in a fortnight and stood at the trestle.

"**Then the half against that, and it is mine and it is not the clerk's,**" he said. "**A boundary that will not help nine people go into a wet room is not a boundary that is refusing a post. It is a boundary that has decided that the worst thing that can happen to a person is a rescue, and it is going to find out what the second worst thing is on a Saturday afternoon with a good plan in the room and nobody in the water yet.**"

"**The second worst thing is at the fourth bell,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I have entered that, and I have entered that I do not know what it is, and that is the first entry in this clerk's book that is the word *unknown* and it is not going to get a reason beside it.**"

At about the fifth bell nine people went down the nine steps and along the passage and down a set of wet stairs, and nobody in that anteroom watched them go past the kitchen-line door, and one of the nine had a lamp and one of them had a length of cord and none of them had a bell.

Marek Venn sat at the end of the trestle with his shut book under his forearm and did the thing he does about four mornings in ten, which is to listen to two people describe the same fact in two instruments and find that the two instruments agree.

They agreed. A frame across the head of that passage would take the pressure off the head of the door, and a door that is not under pressure takes a windlass and one person instead of a windlass and two. He could put one up in a day with material that does not exist, and he could name the failure point of it in a room before he stood it on, and he could then be one of the people who watched it for nineteen weeks, and there would be no mark cut in the stone anywhere in this building, and it would be a physical support joined to a bearing mark and nothing else, and it would not save anybody by itself.

And he understood, in about the time it takes the pump to make four strokes, why he was not going to say it, and it was not the rule about marks, and it was not the missing beech, and it was not that nobody had asked him.

It was that a woman had stood at the end of a trestle an hour ago and said *the cost is a person and not a room*, in her own mouth, before anybody had asked her for it, in a room that has spent forty weeks refusing to be drawn by questions — and if he stood up now and offered to take the pressure off that door, then this room would hear an offer, and an offer is what a room hears instead of a cost, and the cost was the only new thing in the room all day.

He had built his whole trade on being the man who answers a cost with a thing he can make. He had never once in nine weeks in this building noticed that there is a second kind of man, who hears a cost and lets it stand, and who is not brave and is not careful and is not the man at the end of the trestle.

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither was read, and the reason went in beside them: a room which has entered the word *unknown* in a findings column for the first time in forty weeks is not going to be told a total over it.

Four today and three hundred and fifty-seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and seventy-four and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that and was read out loud, and the reason was given, and the reason was that a clerk who has entered *unknown* in a book this morning is going to stand in front of a room at the sixth bell and say one number she has been saying for fifty weeks and it is the only number in this building that nobody has ever asked her to justify.

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and sixty-one days and one hundred and sixty-one nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and sixty-two, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for fifty weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in fifty weeks.**"

The board was walked, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven that are not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it. Tams Quill was not asked about it and did not offer, and the clerk entered both of those.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman had four and a half hours available on an open morning and declined to ask for them, and the clerk entered the declining and the two sentences and did not improve either of them.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning, and nobody measured it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for twenty-two weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and nine people went down a wet stair in the afternoon without a bell and a plan is in this room whose cost has a number on it and the number is nine.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a room at the kitchen-line end with nobody in it but a cook's corner and a slate, and a passage at the bottom of this building with a lamp in it, and a man at the end of a trestle who had worked out how to take a pressure off a door and had let it stand, and who could not afterwards have said for the life of him whether that had been principle.
