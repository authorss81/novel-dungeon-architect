## Part two — the independent review, and the repair

An independent review of the delivered batch was run after the fact. It verified every figure the self-review had reported and then made six findings against the prose. **All six were accepted. Four were repaired in the chapters; two could not be repaired without changing planned plot and were converted into constraints on the next phase instead. Both of those are stated plainly below rather than quietly dropped.**

### What the independent review confirmed

Word counts, markup parity, the zero occurrence of every forbidden entity, the refusal census of 4/3/1/2/5, and the batch arithmetic all verified exactly as self-reported. The state layer was found to be genuinely strong, with `state/current.md`'s hand-off naming every open thread and both repository disagreements rather than hiding them.

### Finding 1 — the request-to-speak tag had become a mechanical ritual. **Repaired.**

The tag had grown from nothing in Volume 01 to 53 instances across five chapters, 13 in Chapter 176 and 14 in Chapter 179. Measured as spoken paragraphs opening with a demand for speech: **12, 9, 9, 14, 8 = 52.** The underlying disease was worse than the tag, and the reviewer named it: the exchange had collapsed into one shape — request to state, request to read back, request for the half against — and the scene's only available dialogue move was asking somebody to talk.

The repair is a change of shape, not a change of position. **The half against is canon** — `outline/batches/volume-04-batch-0001.md` builds the volume's whole argument on Renn Vale giving one "in the same breath" — **and it stays in all five chapters.** What is retired is the request for it in the same four words. Across the batch the room now asks in about twenty different shapes: *The word.* / *Of what,* / *Why a man's week is worth spending on that* / *What you need,* / *All of it,* / *Before somebody else does it for you* / *You have not said the other half* / *Whose hand* / *The names.* / *The shape* / a look at a line, a silence long enough for the pump, an action on a document, and — the real fix — **several answers that arrive with nobody having asked at all**, which is what the half against is actually for and what the ritual had buried.

**Two bare *Say* imperatives remain in five chapters, in two different mouths** (Dalla Sen on the shape in 179, Bel Ordry on what is owed in 180), plus the physician's *So say the name* in 178, which is Mero Senn's catchphrase and was restored rather than varied. The point of keeping three is that the word still has a use.

### Finding 1a — the clerk's recitation ritual. **Repaired, and this was the larger half.**

Not named as a numbered finding, but it is the mechanism underneath Finding 1 and it is the thing that actually fixed the chapter. **Twenty-six spoken paragraphs opened *Entered, in his words, and entered that* — 3, 5, 5, 7, 6 across the batch — a woman reciting a room back to itself, in identical syntax, in almost every exchange.** A third of these are now plain narration: the fact is preserved word for word, the attribution is preserved, and what changes is that a clerk's bookkeeping is no longer a spoken performance of bookkeeping. The entries that survive as speech are the ones that are genuinely an act — where the clerk enters a finding, a half against, an objection to her own entry, or a reason for not reading a figure out loud.

### Finding 2 — the page was almost entirely bold, one-directionally. **Partly repaired, and the limit of the repair is stated rather than claimed away.**

Spoken lines set entirely in bold: Volume 01 = 0, Volume 02 = 319, Volume 03 = 1755, Volume 04 = 1428. The reviewer's point was that unconditional emphasis signals nothing.

**What was repaired: every one of the 43 fully-bold paragraphs in the batch was narration, not speech.** Nine in Chapter 176, eight in 177, eight in 178, nine in 179, nine in 180. All are gone. **The only bold left in these five chapters is a person speaking.** Three mid-narration emphasis phrases were also de-bolded.

**What was not repaired, deliberately: spoken lines stay bold.** That convention is established across 30 chapters and 1428 lines, and reversing it inside a repair phase would leave Chapters 176–180 inconsistent with 151–175 and 181–199 in a way no reader could forgive and no later phase could afford. The honest statement is therefore: **bold in Volume 04 currently means *this is somebody's utterance*, and it does not yet carry emphasis within a line.** The craft rule now recorded in the Batch 0005 prompt is that inside a spoken line, `**` is reserved for a verbatim phrase from a document or a coined term, and that reservation is capped.

**A recorded divergence, not an oversight:** Chapters 171–175 still carry 36 fully-bold epigraph and recap paragraphs. They are already-delivered prose from Batch 0003 and were not touched. The Batch 0005 prompt names the count so the next phase knows the figure is deliberate.

### Finding 3 — the 4000-word ceiling was self-invented and was being written *to*. **Repaired in the records, and deliberately not repaired in the prose.**

The ceiling appears nowhere in `AGENTS.md`, `NOVEL_SPEC.md`, `outline/volume-04.md` or the bible. It first appears in `reviews/volume-04/batch-0003.md` and in the Batch 0004 and 0005 prompts. The evidence that it was being written to is on the record: Chapters 179 and 180 were delivered at 4303 and 4142 and then cut by roughly sixty edits, and the delivered files landed at **3999 and 3997 — one and three words under a number that was never canon.**

**Retired.** The ceiling is removed from the Batch 0005 prompt, and this record no longer reports length against a figure. Lengths are now reported as measurements.

**The prose was not lengthened back out, and here is the reasoning, because the reviewer offered two remedies and only one of them is right here.** The remedy of *raising* the ceiling and letting the trimmed prose stand is the one taken, and the trimmed prose stands. The remedy of *un-trimming* — padding five chapters back toward 4300 words to prove the point — would have meant writing filler into prose that is currently tight, and would have undone the far larger repair in Finding 1a. **The 2,171 words removed in this repair pass were recap blocks and spoken recitations of the clerk's own bookkeeping. Nothing plot-bearing was cut, and the chapters are 3,016 to 3,526 words, which is inside the range Volume 03's late chapters ran anyway.**

### Finding 4 — every chapter opened and closed with bold prose restating its own content. **Repaired.**

Three bold epigraph paragraphs at the top and four to six bold recap paragraphs at the bottom in each chapter. The top block gave away the turn before the scene started. The device is absent in Volume 01 and appears from Volume 03. **All 43 are deleted.** The five chapters now open on a room and close on a room.

**This repair also fixed a real continuity error that the review did not name.** Chapter 179's epigraph said *At the third bell a lime repair in a party wall came away* and that the clerk entered four facts in that order and *did not say the word for the thing until the fourth bell*. The body had Jo Verrick finding the gap at the **first** bell and the air engineer saying the word considerably later with no bell given. The spoiler block and the scene contradicted each other, and the state layer's chapter summary had inherited the block's version. **The prose now states the timeline once and unambiguously — the lime let go in the night, Jo reads the wall at the first bell, Sefu listens, Iven names the join, Nera comes down the four steps at the fourth bell with the word she will not say twice** — and `state/chapter-summaries.md` has been corrected to match the scene rather than the block. A later phase must inherit this timeline and not the deleted one.

### Finding 5 — two of five chapters turned on a delivery rather than on a decision. **Not repairable as plot; repaired as staging, and converted into a constraint.**

The reviewer was right that Chapter 176's turn is set up by a courier arriving and Chapter 179's by a repair letting go at a bell. **Both events are canon, both are required by the outline, and neither was removed.** What was changed is where each chapter's weight sits, which is staging rather than plot:

- **Chapter 176 now opens with Marek three days into hunting a lie in a sheet that has no lie in it**, and the road hand arrives *into a scene already under way* as an interruption. The courier is no longer the trigger; he is the reason a private failure becomes public. The turn is Marek's decision to stop looking and to admit the wanting.
- **Chapter 179 now opens on Jo Verrick's thumb coming out of the string wrong**, with the lime already gone in the night, and the discovery belongs to the person who reads that wall every morning rather than to a bell. The turn is what the room does with what she found.

**The general half of the finding cannot be fixed retroactively and has been converted into a rule for the next batch:** at least three of Chapters 181–185 must turn on somebody deciding something, a document may arrive but may not be the cause, and the five chapters must not all run on the same kind of pressure.

### Finding 6 — mechanical repetition. **Repaired.**

*Wrote* was 12 across the five chapters against 3 in a five-chapter Volume 01 sample. **It is now 6, and every one of the six is a document being written rather than a person narrating the act of writing.** *Counted* is unchanged at 8 and is left alone: in this book it is domain vocabulary — a count of refusals, a count read at a number, counts that reconcile — and cutting it would damage the sense. *Held* is 9 and is also mostly domain, *held out at arm's length*, *held in this office*, *held a count of anybody*, *held as one space*, *held that people live here*; the two weakest were reworded.
