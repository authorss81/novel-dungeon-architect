# Chapter 173 — The Fifth Term

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the thirty-fourth week, a Monday, and the school did not run and lost nothing, and the anteroom's fifth term was said out loud in a room with both of the people who can keep it in it for the first time since the middle of the twenty-sixth week, and a second person read it against the book and the two were the same.**

**And a woman on a landing asked out loud, in front of him, the question nobody had wanted to ask a man who comes back into a building that has been getting on without him, and four people answered it in four shapes and none of the four was an answer.**

On a Monday this building is one engine and no traffic, and the man who had come up the steps on Saturday and walked down them on Sunday came up them again at the first bell and stood in the anteroom where a clerk could see him, which is a thing he had not had to do on either of the first two days and which Dalla Sen entered without being asked.

"**Say what that is,**" she said.

"**It is a man who has been in this building two days standing where he can be seen,**" said Sefu Aran. "**And it is not humility and I would like it not to be written as humility. It is that a room where a man can be seen is a different room from a room where he cannot, and I have read enough of your books to know that this boundary has been getting on without both kinds.**"

"**Entered, and entered that he asked for it not to be called humility,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a clerk who thinks it is humility is a clerk who would like it to be.**"

Then she read the finding out of the book, word for word, and gave it to the room the way she gives everything, which is by reading it and then asking somebody who was not in the room to check whether she had improved on it.

"**The anteroom's fifth term. The air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it. Since the middle of the twenty-sixth week that term has not been able to be kept, and it was kept on Saturday morning, out loud, with a person in the room, and this is a finding and not an accusation, and it is about a room and not about a man.**"

Renn Vale read it against the book. It was the same, and the clerk entered that it was the same, and entered that she had read it out and had not improved on it and had asked for the check anyway.

"**Say the part that is hard,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because you have read a finding out of a book and a room is going to say *so what*, and I am asking it before anybody can.**"

"**Then say what you do know,**" said Mero Senn, from the doorway, where he had been standing since the first bell with his bag against the wall.

"**The so what is this. This room has had a rating restored on a count and a word and a pump and a door, and one of the six terms that rating rests on could not be true. The other four were true the whole time.**"

"**And I entered it on Saturday and told nobody until this morning, and I have been this boundary's clerk since the nineteenth week, and I did not notice that a term of six was false. Which means I was not reading them.**" She put the chalk down on the anvil end of the trestle. "**A term nobody is reading is not a term. It is a line.**"

Nobody improved on that. The pump got four strokes in and a woman who keeps a room said from the doorway that a line is a thing a body can be written on in an office four days up a road and never once read out loud in the room it is about, and then she said she had not been going to say that and had said it, and nobody asked her which office.

Marek Venn had a book open on his forearm and no line in it, and he found that the thing he had wanted all morning was for a person to ask the keeper something hard. He wanted it the way he had wanted a figure off a frame for a month: as a thing that would put a room on one side of an answer and a man on the other.

That was a want and not a reason, and he knew the difference, and it had cost him two burns and a channel and a knee to learn it. He shut the book.

Then Renn Vale asked the question, and she asked it in the flat voice and she asked it in front of him, which is the part that made it cost her.

"**I am going to ask a question and I am going to ask it in this room and in front of you, and I am going to say why I am doing that first. A question asked in a room can be answered in a room. A question asked in a book is a question a person finds out about in a year, and this book has more than one question in it that has been found out about in a year.**"

"**Say it,**" said Sefu Aran.

"**What does a boundary owe a man who comes back into one that has been getting on without him?**" She did not put the slate down this time. "**And I am not asking it because I want an answer. I am asking it because it has been sitting in this room since Saturday and every person in it has been careful, and being careful is what this building does instead of asking.**"

The answers came in the order they came, and none of them was an answer.

Bel Ordry answered from the step, and he answered it as a man with a page and not as a man with a feeling.

"**Nothing. A boundary does not owe a man anything, and a clerk's book does not owe a man anything, and the reason this building has been easy to live in for thirty-four weeks is that nothing in it owes anybody anything. The day something in here owes somebody something is the day this starts being a master and a servant, and I have watched a man run a crew for eleven years to find out what that is like, and I am not going to help it happen from the other end.**"

Dalla Sen answered second and she did not answer, and she said so.

"**I am not going to answer that, and I want it entered that I was asked a question in a room and declined it, because the answer I have is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper is not a debt and it is not nothing either. A clerk who says *nothing* is doing the thing Bel has just done, only in a different direction and for worse reasons, because I have read the books.**"

Nera Oduya answered third and she came down two steps to do it, which she does not do for anything that is not about air.

"**I am not answering either, and I am answering in the third person, which is a thing I have never done in a room. This boundary has an air authority in it and that authority is me, and an authority that has been here thirty-four weeks has been kept honest by two people refusing to be grateful to it. That is the whole of what I have to say about a man coming back, and it is a thing about me and not about him.**" She stopped. "**And I would like it recorded that I said that, and that I have no idea whether it is true, and that a room is allowed to be suspicious of its own air engineer once.**"

The fourth answer came from the man on the bottom of the nine steps, who had come up as far as the anteroom floor and no further, and who had a slate he had not been given.

"**I am not owed anything,**" said Pell Roke. "**And I am not going to say what I want, because I have got a crutch and a number I read out loud, and that is the whole of what I am for and I have been told so by a clerk. And I am the only person in this room who was here every day of the seven weeks and I have not been asked to say a word about that either, and I noticed.**"

The room looked at the clerk, and Dalla Sen looked at the slate.

"**Entered, in his words,**" she said, "**and entered that nobody asked him and that he noticed, and entered that the two of those are the same fact and I am putting them next to each other.**"

Then the engineer asked it and nobody answered him, because a boy had got there first, and Marek Venn found that he had been the man in the room for thirty-four weeks who was most prepared to say something and had been beaten to it by a fourteen-year-old on a crutch, and that this was the correct order and he would have got it wrong.

Sefu Aran took it last, and he took it standing, and he did not have the benefit of a week.

"**I am not going to answer that in a room,**" he said, "**and I am going to say why, and the reason is the only thing I have got that is worth anything this week, so I am going to spend it carefully. I have been in a room in this building that I did not build. I do not know what this boundary owes me, and I do not know what I would want if I did know. A man who has been away and comes back and is handed an answer to that in his second morning is a man who has been given a post by the people who did the work.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" said Renn Vale, "**because you did not do it and I do it for everybody in this room.**"

"**The half against it is that somebody has to be able to say it, and nobody is, and a boundary that cannot say what it owes a man is a boundary that pays him instead, in something that is not money and is not a wage and is a favour, and I would rather be owed nothing than be paid in a favour by four people who cannot afford one.**"

"**Entered, and entered that four people answered a question in four shapes and one of them was a boy, and that the fifth person to be asked was the person the question was about. I did not ask him first because I wanted to see whether the room would get to him on its own, and that was a clerk's test and not a clerk's right. The room got to him anyway, and I do not know what to do with that.**"

At the fourth bell the anteroom's terms were said. The count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and the pump was attended, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the word was said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and then the fifth was said, and both of the people it names were in the room and one of them had said it.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am entering that this is the second morning it has been kept and that the first was a Saturday with nine people in the room and this one is a Monday with the same nine. A term is not improved by being said twice, and I have written down twice anyway, because a book that says *it was said* is a different instrument from a book that says *it was kept*.**"

At the sixth bell the clerk stood up off the step with the book and read the day's number and the total, and read both of them out loud, and did not walk the board, and the count was read at one hundred and eight.

"**One today and two hundred and twenty-seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and forty-four and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile. The counter is seventeen this week and stays seventeen, because a counter is a thing a clerk stops looking at on purpose.**"

"**I read those out because a condition in this building has been false for seven weeks and is true this morning, and a room that has been keeping itself on a number deserves to hear where the number is.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one. The pot is nine days. And I entered this morning that a term of six was false for seven weeks and that I did not notice, and that does not get softened by saying the room did not notice either, because the room is not a person and does not get a clerk's share of a thing a clerk did.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and eight days and one hundred and eight nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and nine, and I have said in seventeen weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and I am writing it. Nobody has thanked me and I have stopped keeping track of who does not.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is that I have put two findings in that book this morning and a board is a column with a heading on it, and a heading does not get held up in a room where the heading has just been proved to be one of the things that can be false.**"

A brace of a school's own beech stood over the head of the outer service door with a beech shim in a nine-hundred-year-old hollow carrying four tons on its edges. The term that said about two years was in a book in four hands, and the frame was a finger and two-thirds out of true. A clerk had entered that a number which has only been the same twice four minutes apart is not a number to put in a column, and nobody had improved on that entry since.

**On the first day of the thirty-fourth week the anteroom's fifth term was said out loud in a room with both of the people it names in it, and a second person read it against the book and the two were the same.**

**A clerk entered that for seven weeks this room's rating had rested on one term of six that could not be true, and said that a term nobody is reading is not a term. It is a line.**

**A woman on a landing asked out loud what a boundary owes a man who comes back into one that has been getting on without him, and four people answered it in four shapes, and a boy of fourteen got there before any of them and said he was not owed anything and that he had noticed nobody had asked him either.**

**And a man who had been away said he was not going to be handed an answer in his second morning, on the grounds that a man who is given an answer by the people who did the work has been given a post by them.**

**A woman on a landing said the half against it for him, which is that a boundary that cannot say what it owes a man will pay him in a favour instead, and that four people in this anteroom cannot afford one.**
