# Chapter 38 — What the Hearthholds Will Not Carry

It took nine days to get everybody in one room, and four of the nine were spent getting them to agree to be in it.

Renn called it, from the landing, on a Thursday morning, and she said why before anybody could ask, which was that a meeting about a stair that six of the six had arranged would be a meeting about a stair the six of the six had decided, and she was not going to let a thing she had not agreed to be argued about in a room she had been asked to attend.

"Sit down or stand up, I do not mind," she said. "But everybody in this boundary who has a reason to have an opinion about a stair says it today, in this room, out loud, including the ones who are going to refuse. Including me. I want it before there is a plan, because afterwards a refusal looks like an obstacle and now it is just a fact."

Mikka went first because Mikka had been thinking about it for four days and had gotten tired of it.

"I am not feeding it," she said.

The room waited, because nobody knew what *it* was.

"I am not feeding the stair work," Mikka said. "Not a work party. Not a morning pot for six people who are going down to a hole. On stair days the crew eats what the kitchen eats and when the kitchen eats, and that is not me being unkind, that is me keeping a line I drew in the twenty-seventh day."

"You said you would not make yourself a creditor of six people who cannot leave," Marek said.

"I said I would not make myself a creditor. Feeding a work party for four days is not a creditor. It is a wage with a pot on it. And the moment I put a pot in front of a man who is working on a stair, that pot is in his arithmetic, and he will not be able to see the arithmetic any more, and neither will I." She put her hands on the shelf. "If the stair work needs food, then it needs somebody else to bring it, and I will hand it over at the kitchen line and I will not carry it in."

"Then we eat less," Tams said, cheerfully, and got a look from both of them.

"You will eat less," Mikka said, "and you will say so in your own column, and it will not be my line and it will not be softened into a hardship by anybody who is not the one going without."

"Renn," Jo said. "Your rooms."

Renn had been standing since she started.

"My rooms are on the same air as a pressure shaft," she said, "and my four people and I sleep in that, and I have not been asked about it in three weeks. So here is what I will not carry."

She did not sit, and her voice got quieter rather than louder, which in this room was the sign that she meant every word of it.

"I will not carry your plan past my door. No lamp, no stand, no bench on my landing. My four people do not move out of a room because there is work at the end of it. And nobody of mine counts as a worker for anything. If one of mine ends up carrying for you, that is a thing I will stop."

"And you will not stand a watch on a stair day," Jo said, writing it.

"I will not stand a watch on a stair day. Renn said in the twenty-seventh day she would not be the sixth. Renn said in the thirtieth day she would take it four days in seven because the woman in her rooms was not walking any more, and she said out loud that her refusal had not been withdrawn." She looked round the room. "You are all very good at hearing the version of a refusal that is convenient. It is a refusal of the *wet seam at the waist*. It is not a permission to be the boundary's stair person, and I am saying that now while there are nine days of nothing in front of us, because after a plan exists I will sound like an obstacle and I am not an obstacle. I am a door."

Nobody argued. Dalla wrote: *Renn Vale will not stand a watch on a stair day. In her words, and her words are not a withdrawal of the twenty-seventh refusal and are a wider refusal of the same thing.*

And then she refused the second thing, and it was the one that cost.

"You are not going to the woman from the wash rooms," she said. "You will not go to my mother's neighbour with a lamp and a slate and a question about my mother's repair at my mother's table. That is hers to offer and she has not offered, and she lifts your cover at the second bell every day of her life and has earned the right to be asked by a person, not by a survey."

"Renn—"

"No. You had a question on Tuesday and you have had four days to find another way to ask it and you have not, because there is no other way." Her jaw was set. "The bright band on that block has been there since my mother and nobody has ever seen it move. If somebody is pulling a line through that hollow lately, that is a fact in a building I live in, and it will stay a fact in a building I live in, and it will not become a fact in a record with a date and a name on it because six strangers wanted a plan. I know what that costs you. I do not care."

That was the outright refusal, and it came from the person the crew had come to rely on most, and it cost them the only living witness to the hand repair, and there was no way round it, and Marek sat down.

Ilsa was quick and blunt and refused two things in eleven words, and Jo wrote both.

"The school corner is not a work site, and the school does not suspend again, and I am not losing days to a hole somebody else is digging."

Then Sefu, who had not spoken for an hour, and who stood at the wheel the entire time because standing at the wheel was how he worked, and who said the boundary paragraph separately, and had it marked separately, and would have walked out of the room if anybody had read it inside the rest.

"I am going to say my part and I am going to say it on its own, and Jo is to read it on its own, and nothing I say is to be argued with this afternoon, because everything else in this room can be argued and this is the part that is simply mine."

Dalla read it. It said, in the plainest words Sefu had:

*This is a boundary. A boundary is the line between the people who live on this side of it and everything else. I hold the gate. I can refuse admission and I have never yet used it to shut a person out who had a reason to be here, and I will use it today if the answer to the question on that page is that six people from above are going to work on a mechanism in these rooms. The work in these rooms is a work of this Hearthhold. Its consent is given room by room, by the people who keep the rooms, and it is revoked the same way. I cannot give it and I cannot withhold it on anybody's behalf. I have no standing to bind a stranger to a maintenance obligation, and I will say that again in a year if I have to. If a stair begins to be worked on, the six people who are doing the working are not tenants, not neighbours, and not residents, and no hour they spend here is an hour they owe, and no arrangement made here makes them any of the three. That is my part and it is separate.*

He signed it, and Dalla marked it separately, and there was a silence after it that had a different texture from the other silences, and Jo turned the board round and read the rest.

Nera took the cloth off its hook and held it and did not put it down, which she did when she was about to say something she had already written down and was now saying to faces.

"I said in the thirtieth day that I am not the person who has to fix the shaft, and I have thought about it every day since and I have not moved, and I am going to say the whole of it so that no later person can say I was unclear." She looked at Marek and she did not soften it for him, which is not a thing she had ever done. "The shaft under that corridor is not mine. Not my air works, not my valve, not my grille, not my baffle, not my bypass. I maintain air. I run what I can see and test and reach, and I stop what I cannot, and the shaft is a hole I have never seen into and cannot see into and cannot maintain, and it belongs to a stair that is corded by the people who live beside it. The moment anybody in this room says the crew will fix that shaft, I pull the valve, and I will not argue it first, and I will not be asked afterwards whether I meant it."

"That is fair," Marek said.

"It is not fair. It is a limit. You are going to hear it again in a month when somebody has spent a week of work on it, and I would like it entered in the ledger now so that nobody can say afterwards that I moved." She hung the cloth on its hook. "And the second half, which is worse. The shaft is not the crew's to promise either. You do not have a corridor and a stair and a route, and you do not have a way out, and if anybody from this room has said so to a person in this room I would like to hear about it and have it corrected. It is not a thing anybody here has. It is a thing six people from above are thinking about, and thinking about it is free, and saying it out loud is not."

Iven came last and he was the one who made the room go quiet, because he had been turning a thing over for four days and had not mentioned it to anybody.

"Then let me put the guild on the page," he said, "because nobody in this room has, and I have known about it for four days and I have been waiting to see if one of you would."

Nobody had.

"A stair is not a wall," Iven said. "You can stand a post under a wall and nobody writes a charter about it. A stair is a route. A route is the one thing in this building that the Cartwrights have ever had a legal instrument for. Every route I have ever worked on had a charter on it, and the charter names who may use it, who maintains it, and who answers when it fails, and it is issued by an office that has been closed to us since the fifteenth day." He turned the file over in his hands. "The salvage lien does not reach a route. The custody order does not reach a route. If the six of you put the lower side of that stair back into service, with a person on it, then there is a route in this Hearthhold with no charter, and the guild will say exactly that, and the answer they will want is a charter, and a charter is a document with a holder."

"Somebody will want to issue it," Sefu said.

"Somebody will want to issue it," Iven agreed, "and the person who signs it becomes the party to it, and if it is signed by a Hearthhold then your neighbour in the wash rooms is in a route he did not agree to, and if it is signed by one of the six of you then one of the six of you has standing here, and Sefu has just spent ten minutes saying that nobody from above has standing here." He set the file down. "So the restriction is not only protection for the rooms. It is protection for the six of you. The day the lower side works, the guild has a document to ask for. I would rather you were all aware of that when somebody in a hearing asks you why you did not simply open it."

"Nobody in a hearing is going to ask us that," Marek said.

"Nobody in a hearing is going to ask you that," Iven agreed, "because the six of you are not currently in a hearing, and I would like to keep it that way, and I would like to have said this out loud in a room where the people who will be affected heard me say it, and not in a hearing where they hear it from an office."

And then Dalla read the terms, which she had written in nine hands over four days, and she read them slowly, and she stopped at each one and let the room have it, and four of the nine were changed on the spot and two were struck and one of the two that were struck was a line Marek had wanted.

She read the first: the stair stays corded, and nothing in this project opens it, and no person is put on the slab.

"Agreed," said Sefu, and nobody argued, because it was the oldest thing in the room.

The second: no work at the near side except inside a named period, on a day the room has agreed, with the period's end set by the cord, the cloth, or any watcher's word, and the party out of the step in under a minute and the near side as found.

"Add *and the cord re-tied by Sefu and Renn and not by the crew*," Renn said, and it went in.

The third: no work on a day the corridor is open, and none on a day the school runs, and none between the second bell and the fourth.

"That is three days in seven and it is most of the week," Marek said.

"It is a school," Ilsa said.

The fourth: the school corner floor is opened and traced before anything is built, and the people who keep that floor are asked first, and the work is done on a day the school does not run.

The fifth: the crew does not stand a watch in the boundary, and is not on the rota, and is not a maintenance party, and its hours are given and are not owed and are not a fund.

The sixth, which was Marek's, and which Jo read twice and then would not read a third time: *the boundary agrees to the six of them continuing to hold the pump sequence, the corridor watch, and the training of named Hearthholds, in order that a stair project may be attempted.*

"That is a line I wrote," Marek said.

"It is a line you wanted," Jo said, "and it is the same line as a maintenance party with better manners, and if you cannot see that then I cannot make it plainer by saying it again."

"Struck," Sefu said, and it went.

The seventh: the bypass hatch stays latched through every turn and is not opened for any reason during a stair period.

The eighth, from Nera, in her own words: *the shaft under the corridor is not maintained by the air works and is not promised by anybody, and any statement to the contrary is corrected on the day it is made.*

The ninth, from Jo, in her own hand, which Dalla read twice because it was the only line on the page that anybody had written alone: *a change to a room people use is written by the person who will keep it, and Jo Verrick holds the board, and this page is a proposal until the people whose names are on it have read the line their name is under and said so out loud, and a refusal is written in the same hand and the same size as an agreement.*

And then Dalla stopped, and put her finger on the bottom of the page, and said, "And here is the one nobody has filled in, and I am not going to fill it in, because it is not mine."

The line read: *the person who may call the work off, on a stair day, from the stair itself, is .*

"It cannot be the crew," Leena said.

"It cannot be Sefu, because Sefu is at the gate," Nera said. "And it cannot be me, because I am at the valve and I am not on the near side and I cannot see it and I am not going to be given authority over a thing I cannot see, which is exactly the mistake this building has been making for six weeks."

"It cannot be Renn, because she has said it in front of nine people and she meant it," Jo said.

"Mikka's is the kitchen and Ilsa's is the school and the woman from the wash rooms has a room of her own and a morning in which she lifts a cover, and every one of those is a person who can stop the work nearest to them." Dalla looked up. "And the stair is not a room. Nobody keeps the stair. That is the entire difficulty with it, and it has been the difficulty with it since the eighteenth day, and we have been writing round it for six weeks with terms and refusals and hours, and here is the hole in the page. The project wants a stop-holder and the stop-holder has to be somebody who keeps the thing nearest the work, and the thing nearest the work is a mechanism that six rooms breathe through and nobody lives in."

The anteroom was very quiet. Above them a barricade plank held a slate with four dates and a word. Beside them the pump ran its twenty-six and stopped, and the corridor stood empty with a chalk figure on the floor telling each person their own width, and on the anteroom bench a wrapped plate lay under a spread of work cloth with an empty seat in the middle of it, and it had still not said one word.

"Everybody in this boundary, and six above," Renn said, "and not one of us can be that person. And you cannot write a term that says *nobody*, because a nobody with a rope and a stop order is the first thing a building grows when it is left alone long enough."
