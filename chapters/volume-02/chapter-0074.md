# Chapter 74 — On the Step

Renn Vale wrote the day on her own slate on Thursday morning, at the top of three steps, in front of Dalla Sen, and she wrote it before she said anything else, and nobody had given her a day and nobody was going to.

"It is the fourth day of this week," she said. "Put that in your hand, and the time, and put that I said it. I have spent three weeks in this room being told what day it is going to be and the last time somebody handed me a date for a thing I had refused to do I did it in a hurry and it cost a person a morning, and that person was in a shaft and I have never got the morning back for anybody."

"Written in your words," said Dalla Sen, "and read out, and I am adding that nobody in this anteroom gave you a day and that I have been asked to be the one who confirms it, which is a thing I have never been asked to do before."

Renn Vale came down the three steps. She had said in the sixth week that she does not stand on the stair and that this is a wider refusal than the wet seam and it has not been withdrawn, and everybody in that building had heard her say it four times a week for eleven weeks, and it is the reason the next part of the morning is in an anteroom and not on a stair.

"Right," she said. "Here is what I am going to do and what everybody is going to be told afterwards.

"**I am going alone. None of you. Not the crew, not the clerk, not the air engineer, not the cook, not the keeper.** I am going down the corridor as far as the end of it and along the wall and I am going to knock on that door, and I am going to stand on her step, and I am going to be standing on her step while she says whatever she is going to say, and that includes *no*, and I have said out loud that it includes *no* and I am saying it again now in a room with eleven people in it so that nobody can be surprised by it in an hour.

"And afterwards the whole of you are going to be told what she said, in my words and hers, and none of you is going to be in it, and that is not a courtesy I am doing her. **That is her arrangement and it is older than any of us and I am not the one who is granting it.**"

"Then say what the asking is," said Dalla Sen, pen up. "There has to be an asking in the book or there is no answer, and the answer is the whole of the day."

"I am asking for a piece of wood," said Renn Vale. "I have been not asking for it for a fortnight, in four rooms, in front of a search, on a page with thirteen lines on it, and this morning I am going to ask. That is the asking. It goes in your hand in those words and not a softer version of them, and I want the word *wood* in it, and I want it read out at the fifth bell tonight, whatever she says, because if that page ever has a soft word on it I will know who put it there."

---

She was gone about forty minutes, and nobody in the anteroom did anything with the forty minutes except the things that get done in an anteroom when a keeper is out of the room, which is that they get louder and then they get quieter and then one person says the thing and everybody agrees with it too fast.

Marek Venn stood at the end of the tally board with the slate under his arm and did the thing he had discovered in the seventh week and had got better at in the eleventh, which was to be a man in a room where the best available contribution was silence, and to notice, every time, that the silence was not humility. It was a professional skill, and the reason he was good at it was that he had spent thirty-two years being the man with the instrument, and there was not one hour of this Hearthhold in which the instrument had been the thing anybody needed.

There was a thing he wanted to say to that woman, and it was fifteen words long, and it was the sentence he had been in this building to learn, and it was this: *I am asking for your afternoon, and I want to know what it is like.* And it was not a sentence he was entitled to say, because a man who asks a woman for her afternoon is a man asking for a room, and the room was not his, and the woman in it had been asked for nothing in eleven weeks and had said yes to two things, one of which had put a dead man's mother under a slab.

So the sentence stayed in his mouth, and what he did with it instead was to understand, standing at the end of a board, that the sentence he had come eleven weeks down a shaft to learn was *may I have your afternoon*, and that the sentence the thirteenth week had actually taught him, in a room with a key in an apron and nine lines on a dowel, was *what is it that you already know how to do that nobody has thought to ask you about*.

Not one person in eleven weeks had thought to ask her that. And she had been asked twice, both times about a route, and both times she had said the truth about a rope and a cover.

---

Renn Vale came up the three steps at the second hour and came into the anteroom and stood in the middle of the floor, and eleven people stopped what they were doing, and Dalla Sen opened the ledger to a clean place, and Renn Vale did not sit down.

"She said no," she said.

Nobody said anything, and the pump went round under the floor, and the check hissed on its one note.

"Then I am going to say it in the order she said it, and I am going to say it in her words where I can remember her words and in mine where I cannot, and I am going to mark the difference both times so that a person in a year knows which sentences are hers.

"**One, her words.** I knocked and she opened the door and she did not open it all the way, and she said, *that is not you.* And I said, it is me, and I have come about the wood, and I am going to stand on your step while you say whatever you are going to say, and I have said that in this room and I am saying it here.

"And she said: *say the thing you came for.* And I said it. I said, this boundary has a pump under the anteroom floor with a scored seat in it and one cleaning left on it, and there is no wood in this Hearthhold, and the only piece of wood that a seat could sit in and stay put is on the rack in your room, and I have come to ask her for it.

"And she said: **no.**

"**Two, her words, continuing, and I am going to be exact because this is the part I have not been able to put down in the right order all the way down nine steps.** She said: *the floor of this room is not yours and it is not anybody's and it is not going to be yours, and you have not asked me about the floor and I want that noted.* And I said, noted, and the floor stays refused.

"And she said: *I have had a piece of wood on a rack in a wet room for thirty years and I have never once been asked about it, and now there are four people in this Hearthhold who know it is there, and one of them has written it on a page, and I want you to know that I know what has happened, and I am not complaining about it, and I am answering it.*

"And I said: that is fair, and I am sorry about it, and I will put that in the book.

"And she said: *do not be sorry about it in a book, be sorry about it to the people who have to walk past it.*"

Dalla Sen read that out twice, and the second time it went through the anteroom differently.

"**Three, and this is the whole of it, and it is hers, and I am going to say it slowly.** She said: *no, and here is the reason, and the reason is not that the wood is mine, because it is not mine and I have never said it was.

"The wood is on the rack because the rack is what you put things on when the floor is bad. That is what a rack is for. That is the whole trade of it. Thirty years there has been a floor in that room that will not take weight, and a rack, and on the rack the things that had to be kept dry.

"And if you take the piece of wood off that rack then the rack is empty and the floor is still bad, and that room is a room with a bad floor and an empty rack in it, and it is the emptiest room in this Hearthhold and it is emptier than a room with nothing in it, because a room with nothing in it at least admits it.

"And in about a year somebody is going to come down that corridor with a list, and I know there is going to be a list, because I have watched this building for eleven weeks and a list is the only thing anybody in it has ever been given. And they will put their head in the door and they will see an empty rack and a floor they have been told they cannot walk on, and they will go away and write down that there was nothing in that room. **And there is me, in it, every second bell and every fifth, lifting the covers.** And that is what is on the rack. That is the thing the piece of wood is standing up for, and it is not the wood.*"

She stopped there and let the anteroom have it, and it did not deserve it and it got it anyway, because eleven people had spent thirteen weeks learning what a refusal with a reason in it sounds like.

"**Four.** She had a condition and she gave it as a condition and she had it written out before I came up the stairs, which I found out afterwards, and she asked me one question at the door, and the question was: *is anybody going to have to come down that corridor to keep the record true.*"

"And what did you say," said Nera Oduya.

"I said no. And I meant it, and I have thought about it on the way up and I still mean it, and I am going to tell this room what she said about it, and then I am going to be asked whether my answer survives, and I would like to have said it before I am asked.

"**Five, her words.** She said: *then put it in the kitchen's book and not in the route's book, and write it in the words I am going to give you now, and write down that I gave it and not that you thought of it.*

"And what she gave me is this, and I have got it exactly because she said it twice and made me say it back to her on her step.

"**The wash rooms are kept. They are kept by the person who lifts the covers, at the second bell and the fifth, and by nobody else. There is no work in them. Nobody is to be sent down that corridor to do anything, ever, and the floor is refused and it stays refused. If anybody writes them down, write them in the kitchen's book, and write *kept, and nobody's work*, and do not write a name against them, and do not let a name get put there by accident in four years.**"

---

"**And that last bit is mine and not hers, and I am putting it in my own hand on my own slate before I come off this landing, and Dalla is to copy it, not the other way round,** because the eleven words she has said twice in her own hand are not to be copied into anybody's hand, and I asked her about this on the step and she said yes, and she said why, and I am going to say it in this room because it is the reason I am not going to argue with her.

"I said: I am going to have this written down. And she said: *that one is about when you say the word. This one is about the room.* And she was right and I had not seen the difference, and I have been in this building eleven weeks and she told me the difference on her own step in about nine seconds.

"So here is my term and it is mine. **The wash rooms are kept, and there is no work in them, and nothing is to be sent down that corridor, and the floor is refused.** And if that word *work* ever appears on that line, somebody has changed my sentence, and the change is to be read out and the sentence is to be put back."

"Entered in your hand and read out," said Dalla Sen, "and the copy is from your slate and not from hers, and her condition is entered in the words she gave twice on her step, and the eleven words are not copied and are not in this book and I have looked twice to be sure."

---

Jo Verrick had been standing at the frame for the whole of it with her hand flat on the wood, and she came over now, and she did not ask, and nobody made her.

"Right," she said. "Then that is the end of it and I am going to say the end of it in a room where nine people can hear me, because the alternative is that I say it to myself at a frame in March.

"I have been looking for a piece of wood since the third day of the twelfth week. Four places, and a day in the cold, and a page with thirteen lines on it and a *no* against twelve of them. **The thirteenth line is what the seat wants, and the person who has it is the woman who just said no, and she said it with a reason that is better than hardness, which is what every other owner in this Hearthhold gave me, and this is the fifth.**

"So the seat is not made. I am not saying it is not made this month. I am saying **the hunt is over**, and there is nobody left to ask, and no amount of standing in corridors is going to change it, and I would like that entered as a fact and not as a failure, because I went to five owners and every one of them said no for a better reason than hardness and the sixth said no for the best reason anybody has given me in eleven weeks.

"And I want to say one more thing and then I am going back to my frame. I priced that seat at a day and a half of my shutter route, and a day of corridor, and a day of school, and a day of a pot. I am not going to have that price back. I spent it. **And the pump is running on one cleaning with the bolt-hole a quarter inch open and a check that hisses on one note, and the only thing between that machine and the dip is me and Isel and a chalk line, and I have just run out of the other way.**"

The sentence *it will be all right* did not get said. Nobody in that anteroom had said it in thirteen weeks and nobody was going to start, and the pump went on being a pump underneath them.

"Noted and read out," said Dalla Sen, and read it out, and then she read out the entry beneath it in her own hand, which she had written while Jo was talking, and which the room had not seen: *the delivery seat, named by the keeper, struck in part at the naming, unfinished; the material hunt closed on the fourth day of this week by the refusal of the sixth owner, in a room none of the boundary was in; the estimate stands at one, in the pump maintainer's own hand, and zero is still refused, and a decision gets made on a day with a man standing in front of you and this boundary does not yet have that day.*

And at the fifth bell, on the same line, read out in Renn Vale's hand and in Jo Verrick's hand and in the clerk's between them, three things: the lip's range, the seventh term, and now a fourth — a woman at a frame, and a wood she would not have, and a room that is kept with nothing in the third column of anybody's book, which is a thing nine hundred years old and has been discovered this week, four doors down, by the person nobody had asked.
