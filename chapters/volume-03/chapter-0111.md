# Chapter 111 — The Terms Of The Asking

**The twenty-second week opened in a boundary with no elected keeper in it and a doorframe with no ordinary means left, and the second of those was the reason the first was survivable.** The corridor was shut, which is the arrangement, and the school was at the far end of it not running, which is also the arrangement, and the anteroom was going to be emptied this morning for the first time in twenty-two weeks, which was not the arrangement at all and had never been done before.

Dalla Sen had it on the board at the first bell with the reason in her own hand, and the reason was not hers either.

"**The anteroom is emptied at the second bell and refilled at the fifth,**" she said. "**It is emptied in the strokes and not in the six minutes, and the six minutes are the low end of the corridor standing in water two foot eight wide on the edge of eleven feet of shaft, and nobody is going to stand in that and count people out of a room. Twenty-six strokes and nineteen are the whole of the window. Nine people, in the strokes, at a walk, and a reader at this door and a reader at the school door, and the plinth reader's word is a warning and not a stop.**"

"**Nine,**" said Renn Vale. "**It holds ten.**"

"**It holds ten including one litter, and there is no litter, and the ten is a number of people and I am going to count nine of them because nine is what is in there.**"

Then she stopped with the chalk up, which is the thing she does, and said: "**No. I am not going to be the woman who argues about the count in the room while the room is standing in the doorway. Read the seventh term.**"

Renn Vale read the seventh term out loud from her own slate in her own words, standing on the landing, and it was right, and every figure on it was hers, and it took four minutes.

---

Jo Verrick was in the anteroom with her three-foot folding rule and a lamp before anybody else, because Jo Verrick is in a room before anybody else and has been for twenty-two weeks, and she had the frame's head lit and her knee on the sill and she had not touched anything.

"**Four people, one hour,**" she said, to nobody, to the room. "**That is what a head of a frame is worth. Not the shoring, which is holding, and not the packing, which is clay, and not the pins, which are four and rusted and are not going to be replaced in this Hearthhold in my lifetime. The head. That is the only part of it that has moved in a fortnight and it is moving because the clay is drying and the head is where the clay goes.**"

Marek Venn came up the nine steps behind her with his left hand already out of his coat, because that is the hand and it is the only one, and he stopped in the doorway of a room he had been in every day for twenty-two weeks and looked at it.

"**Say the rest of it,**" he said.

"**The rest of it is that the head is not failing. The head is doing what a head does. The clay is shrinking and something has to take up the shrinkage and the head is the only soft thing in that frame, so the head goes out, and it will keep going out at a finger a week in this cold until there is nothing left of the head to go out.**"

"**And you cannot pack it with stone.**"

"**I cannot pack it with stone and you already know that or you would have done it.**" She folded the rule into her apron in four movements. "**Stone in a shrinking hole is a stone in a shrinking hole. Every time anybody has packed that head in this building the head has gone further afterwards, and I have watched it three times and I have the rule marks on the jamb to prove it and nobody has ever wanted to see the rule marks.**"

---

**Emptying the anteroom went the way the ordinary thing goes, and it went wrong in an ordinary way, which is the way things in this building go.**

They went out in the strokes, at a walk, four at a time with the fifth on Isel Ferrow's shoulder because the fifth is always the fifth, and Dalla Sen counted at both ends and said the number out loud at each end, and at the fourth stroke the anteroom was empty and the pump was still running and the room stood there in the middle of the corridor with a lamp in it looking like a room that had been put down.

"**Ten,**" said Dalla Sen, from the school door, in the voice she uses for numbers.

Nobody filled the space after it.

"**Ten is what the page says.**"

"**Ten is what the page says,**" she said, "**and the room is empty, and I wrote ten down, and the page was right and the room is right, and I am going to say the thing I have been not saying for two days and I am going to say it once.** "**The number did not go anywhere when the people did.**"

She said it into a corridor with nine people in it and a lamp in an empty room, and the pump went on, and the sixth minute was still six minutes away.

Then she put the chalk down instead of in her hand, which she has done twice in twenty-two weeks.

"**I am also entering something else and I want somebody to object to it before I enter it. I did not say the word. Not once this morning, in the anteroom or on this corridor, and the clerk did not repeat it.**"

For about four seconds the room waited, and it was a good four seconds.

"**Because the room was empty.**" She said it flatly, the way she says the parts she has no opinion about. "**Ten people including one litter for no more than two hours, and the outer service door, and the pump attended, and the word *door* said in a room and repeated by the clerk. The word is the second way out of that room and there was nobody in the room and I did not say the word, and the rating did not need saying, and nothing has gone wrong, and the pump is running.**"

Renn Vale came down one step, which she does when she is going to hold a term.

"**Then write this and write who said it and do not say it twice.**"

"**You have something to say about it.**"

"**I have one sentence and I am going to say it and then I am not going to say it again, and if it goes in a book it goes in once, and if a room in a year wants to make a finding out of it, it can come and stand next to me.**" She had her hand flat on the landing rail and she was not looking at Marek Venn and she was speaking to the corridor. "**A room nobody is in does not need a second way out. That is the reason it does not have one. Do not let anybody in here find that out before the rest of us has.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it once, and read it out, and Renn Vale said yes, and the pump's check came off, and the sixth minute of the low end of the corridor came and went with nobody standing at the low end of it, because nobody is ever going to stand at the low end of it.

---

**At the third bell the anteroom was empty and four people were in it with a lamp, and the boundary did the thing it has not done since the fifth day of the nineteenth week.**

It was not a large thing. It was a sentence, said standing up, with the terms in it, in a room, with a person named.

Dalla Sen had it written on the back of a day-board, and Mikka Hale had looked at it before the second bell and had not touched it, which from the cook is a review.

"**I am going to ask the five of them for a piece of work,**" the clerk said, "**and I am going to say the terms out loud, and I am going to name the person who can be told no, and I am not going to improve any of it, and Renn Vale is going to correct me before I say it and not after.**"

"**Go on,**" said Renn Vale, and corrected nothing, and that was a thing the room noticed.

"**Iven Tallo is asked for one hour of one piece of work. To look at the head of the outer service door frame in the anteroom, in the anteroom emptied, in the pump's window, and to say out loud in a room what he thinks is not going to hold. The person who can be told no is Renn Vale, because she keeps the road and the terms and the anteroom is on her road. The hour goes in the crew's column and not in the boundary's, because the crew's column is a quantity of asking and this is the asking, and that is the whole reason the crew's column has stood still for two weeks and I would like the room to hear me say that out loud. And Iven Tallo may say no, and if he says no the asking is over and nothing is owed and it goes in the book as a no.**"

She read the whole of it back and nobody found a word wrong, and she said so, and then she said: "**Iven Tallo. It is a question and I am asking it in front of everyone.**"

He was at a bench forty feet away with a man's account of his own unpaid trade open in front of him, and he had been listening to all of it, and he did not close the book.

"**Yes,**" he said. "**On my terms, out loud, and you are going to write the terms down before I say them and not after.**"

Dalla Sen had the chalk up before he finished the first sentence.

"**One hour. And I am not signing anything. And I am not the boundary's carpenter and I am not going to be asked to be, and the carpenter's answer goes in the clerk's book in the carpenter's words and not in a page of anybody's.**

"**And if the answer is that nothing in this Hearthhold can hold that head, I am going to say that in a room, and I am going to say it in a room because I have watched two men in my trade say a thing on a landing and have it repeated wrong in a month.**"

"**Said and entered,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And I am entering that the crew's column moved today and that it is the first thing in it since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and I am entering the reason, which is that somebody asked, and the reason is a person and not a favour.**"

Tams Quill came down off the board with the chalk in his left hand and his right one open and flat in his lap, and read the crew's figure, and gave no reason for it, because the reason had just been given out loud in a room by a person who was not him.

"**Fifty-three and a half,**" he said. "**And I am not going to give you the reason twice this week and I gave it last week and the week before, and a clerk has just given it better than I do.**"

---

**Iven Tallo looked at the head of that frame for fifty-one minutes with a lamp and a rule and his own two hands, and he did not touch the packing, and at the end of it he said four things in a room and the clerk wrote all four down.**

"**One. The head is not broken and the shoring is holding and the pins are pins and none of that is the problem. The problem is the clay and the clay is the only thing anybody in this Hearthhold has to pack with, and clay shrinks, and every pack this head has had has made it go out further and not less.**

"**Two. There is no way to fix that with anything we own. Not with stone, not with the wedges — and he can count them, there are seven sound and two dead sticks and one of the seven is in this frame and one of the other six is up a quarter of an inch at the waist and nobody has touched it. Not with a shore, because a shore carries a weight and this head is not carrying a weight, this head is taking up a shrinkage, and a shore will not take up a shrinkage, a shore will break.**

"**Three. If this boundary had a quantity of stone in lime, laid in courses, packed hard and struck off flush and left, the head would be a head again inside a season. That is not a thing anybody can make. That is a thing somebody has to know how to do, and there are no such people here, and I am one of about four people in this region who could teach it and I have no bond and no apprentice on the roll and no timber.**"

Then he stopped, and looked at the jamb, and at the chalk mark Renn Vale had put there, and said the fourth thing, and it was shorter.

"**Four. I have watched that door shut and open every day for twenty-two weeks and I have watched the head go out a finger and a bit in a fortnight. I am telling you what is not going to hold and the answer is the head. And I am a carpenter. And I have never in my life seen a man hold a frame with anything except what the frame is made of.**"

Marek Venn said, "**Which is stone.**"

"**Which is stone,**" said Iven Tallo, "**and lime, and time, and a person who has done it before, and this Hearthhold has got the first two and the third and not the fourth.**"

---

**Refilled at the fifth bell in the strokes, the anteroom was a rated room again,** the word was said, the clerk repeated it, and the sixth bell read the six things, and the crew was on it at fifty-three and a half, and a man of twenty-two was forty-six days and forty-six nights into a count that was his own.

Then Dalla Sen said one more thing, standing, with the room at its number, and it was not a thing she had found and it was a thing she had been carrying since Friday.

"**Ten people went out of that room at the second bell and the page says ten and the page was right. I wrote ten down at the school door and I wrote it while the room was empty and I would write it again tomorrow.**

"**And I am entering the other half, and nobody may join the two halves. The plan's first column is a permission. It has been a permission for twenty-two weeks and on Friday we found out that it is one, and this morning it stood up in an empty room and was still a permission and cost nothing and was wrong about nothing at all.**

"**A page with a number on it survives the room. That is all this morning found out. I am not writing down what it means.**"

The room let it stand. The lamp was still in the anteroom, and the head of the frame was where it had been at the second bell, and it was going to be a finger and a bit out of true in the morning, and going a finger a week in this cold, and the man who could see that was on a bench forty feet away with an account of his own unpaid trade and a carpenter's answer in the clerk's book, and the boundary had a week to find out what a week is for.
