## Every divergence between the plan and the prose, named

1. **The batch map's batch numbers do not mean the delivered batch numbers.** `outline/volume-04.md`'s map runs batches of ten and places 181–192 in Batch 0004 and 193–200 in Batch 0005. **Delivery ran batches of five and then seven, so the outline's Batch 0004 is delivered across Batches 0004, 0005 and 0006, and the outline's Batch 0005 is delivered across Batches 0007 and 0008. Read chapter ranges, not batch numbers.** This is recorded in `reviews/README.md` and in every Volume 04 review file, and it is not a defect in either document; it is what happens when a plan is written before a batch size is fixed.
2. **The Bracer was owed at 163–168 and is delivered across 161–170**, with the build at 163–166, the condition met and admitted at 167, and the frame coming into true and the failure point stood on at 168–169. **Nothing in the plan was moved and nothing in it was contradicted.**
3. **The escalation's chapter range in the map is 188–190 and the coordination is delivered across 181–192**, with the terms between two crews written at 188, the crews working beside each other at 190, the fourth asking at 189, and the reverse of the schedule at 192. **The map's 176–179 for the reversal holds and is exact.**
4. **The Nera Oduya sixth act was scheduled as *she asks him for a work* and is delivered as exactly that, in Batch 0003, in a room, with the terms out loud, with a person named who can be told no, and with the cost in her own words: a person who has been asked by the authority is a person with a standing, and a standing is the beginning of a post.** Her condition was scheduled to be approached hard and not met, and a second condition set, and nobody to ask her in a room. **All three hold across the whole volume and are unasked in all fifty chapters.**
5. **Chapter 200 is the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week and not the first day of the thirty-eighth.** Argued at length above and recorded in five files. **This is the only place in the volume where the delivered prose departs from a written instruction, and it was made because the written instruction would have made the volume's committed resolution undeliverable.**
6. **`outline/volume-04.md` places the review page at the low end as blank with three conditions and says the floor is not to be approached and nobody is to be sent near it. That holds, and the volume added one thing the plan did not anticipate: the page at the low end is now also the metaphor the room uses for its own refusals — Bel Ordry says in 200 that the cost of the withdrawal is a man's name this room cannot put in a column either, and it is the same blank page.** That is a reading of the plan rather than a departure from it.
7. **The plan's *Final chapter image* lists a count in the unheaded column at ninety-five. Wen Pask's count is at one hundred and forty-five at Chapter 200.** **The plan's figure is inherited from Volume 03's end-state and was not re-derived forward, and the plan itself says the count is a count of days and not a number of anything; the delivered count is the one a day from a locked base of twenty-three on the fifth day of the seventeenth week, and it is the correct figure. The plan's ninety-five is a stale figure in a plan and not a defect in the prose.**
8. **The plan's own brass-rule origin at the ninth week was followed and is the reason the volume's figures are twenty-one weeks of silence in the thirtieth week and thirty weeks at the end, against the wrapped plate's thirty-eight and thirty-nine.** `outline/series.md` and Chapters 140 and 150 carry three different origins for the silence and the drift is inherited from Chapter 140, which is closed and reviewed, and was left uncorrected rather than half-corrected. **Chapter 150's sixteen is never quoted in this volume and the ninth-week origin is used throughout.**
