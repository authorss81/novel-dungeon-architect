# Character State — End of Volume 01

## Opening party

Six people came out of the Cinder Steps. **Five are here at the end of Volume 01.**

### Marek Venn

- **Position:** the Hearthhold air-gate anteroom and its lower level, the common landing, and — by named work period and named terms — the lower corridor, the school corner, and the stair's service bend and near landing.
- **Body:** Right hand permanently lost fine control from the Chapter 5 jack crush; left hand usable and strained. **Six written nulls**, the sixth in Chapter 45, and nothing usable has ever come back. He said in Chapter 37 in front of a full room that he has a rule, a lamp, and a woman who counts, and he has not revised it.
- **Mind:** His control flaw appears seven more times in Batch 0005 and is corrected seven more times, and he makes one correction himself. He names a jack and is told that a jack is not a work; he writes a departure sentence and is told to write it again; he writes *some day they will be worse* and is told by Sefu Aran to write that sentence in December instead of in a full room; he puts an outcome into a step and is corrected; and in Chapter 44, on his own and out loud, he says he has stopped trying to hold the uncountable part of the work. In Chapter 40 he refused to mark the stair's stone. In Chapter 46 a line was made fast in that mechanism — on a boundary's own rope, on a keeper's release, with a keeper's name under it, and the shore is the keeper's. That is the same habit pointed outward, and it is the volume's last move on him.
- **Immediate choice:** the next step is a post under the near landing's lip, and the timber for it is under six feet of water on a numbered shelf. He has told a room the six of them are going to leave, and Sefu has made that a spring conversation with a named air hand-over before he goes.

### Leena Orr

- **Position:** litter in the anteroom, the lower corridor, and the service bend; limited clinic care access, not residence. Her litter does not go past the service bend for anything on the returning stair.
- **Body:** Right shoulder dislocated in Chapter 1, reduced in Chapter 2, slinged, no weight through the right arm, **not currently dislocated**. Four bearers and four minutes through a two foot eight inch gap on a wet floor, in the record in three hands and stated on the day of Oren Sill's death.
- **Mind:** She is still the structural critic and she is now also the survey authority for the stair. In Chapter 45 she takes the marks on the common landing because she does not go down three steps, and she sets the discipline that governs the whole batch's craft: *a chalk line is a reference and does not move, and if the slate and my chalk ever disagree then the slate is a copy and mine is the original.* She continues to dispute the order of lines as a matter of professional principle.
- **Immediate need:** shoulder recovery, a dry place, and her four minutes kept in every page somebody writes.

### Iven Tallo

- **Position:** anteroom, lower corridor, common landing, the fixed east stair, the service bend, and the stair's near landing inside the cord.
- **Body:** uninjured; exhausted. Thirty-six years old. **Twenty-nine hours of uncompensated instruction** as last counted, plus two days of shore work he has asked Dalla Sen to keep off the front of the ledger. He prices work out loud as a matter of principle and in Chapter 45 he prices a shore.
- **Role:** He wrote the geography of the stair end into the record in one sentence so that everything after it can be checked. He killed the idea of leading the line into a bed joint and put it on the open face where a child can touch it and anybody can count the wear. He made the line up in three turns and a seizing because he wants it to be a job with a bar in it. He named the band as line wear and then stopped, and said he is not going to be the man who tells a woman with a dead mother that her mother's mother knew something about a stone. He said, in the ledger, the sentence the volume ends on: **there needed to be a post under that lip.** And he said the honest second half of everything the crew has done: the route's page is a position and not a shield, and the wall will hold longer than people think and will cost somebody something that is not written down anywhere.
- **Immediate tension:** he is a suspended-bond carpenter in a building that will not pay him, and he has put public-works equipment into a boundary work that he will never be able to take out again, and he has said he is fine with that and he is, and nobody has thanked him.

### Pell Roke

- **Position:** the anteroom bench, which the wrapped plate used to lie on, the top of the nine steps, the stair head, the service bend, on his crutch. He is never on a step.
- **Body:** fourteen; left ankle sprained in Chapter 8, splinted, supported. Cannot run, take unsafe loads, cross the gallery deck, or take three steps and a lip.
- **Mind:** He is the person this volume cannot do without and he is now on the page in three different ways. **He is the plinth reader** — in Chapter 48 he calls a turn eight seconds before Nera and Isel, and his name is in the margin of the plinth rota in Tams Quill's hand as a debt and not a duty. **He is the reader of counts**, and the ledger carries, on his instruction, *a building does not announce its own population and a count is a photograph.* **He is the one who said the sentence nobody had said**: the record has six names in a column and has never once had a line in it that says what happens to the six of them, and somebody better say it before somebody else says it for them. He also said the thing four people arguing the air plan had all missed: *you are all arguing about who goes down it; nobody has said how the other one gets out.*
- **Continuity note:** Talla Roke's younger brother in the bible; still not revealed in prose. **May now be introduced if a planned beat requires it, and not as a coincidence.**

### Tams Quill

- **Position:** the tally board, the count, the water record, the case board, the training of Isel Ferrow, and — since Chapter 42 — a sling on a right hand that does not close.
- **Body:** **In Chapter 42, at the end of the nine days, his hand was opened in front of nine people and it did not open, and he said what he said in Chapter 37 he would say, at the time and not before it: a month and not nine days.** He cannot make a fist, cannot pull, and cannot work the two-handed lever for the month. He is off the lever entirely for the batch.
- **Role:** He invented the tally's new columns as the batch made them necessary: **spent** (one nine-metre line on a ladder foot; then the lamp, the pry bar, the jack, and two salvage refusals), **kept** (one shore, kept by Renn Vale), and **carried** (Oren Sill's four lines about packing a foot to bearing, written out longhand in Tams's left hand, slowly, on purpose, because he was frightened of losing them). His finding at the end of the batch is the volume's economic argument inverted: the crew's hours came **down** and the boundary's went up, and the crew's share is the half that gets counted, because nineteen days of meals, a shutter route, a school day and a watched flue wall do not go into a column of hours.
- **Visibility:** named in every chapter of the batch.

### Oren Sill

- **Position:** dead.
- **Death:** Chapter 48, the Thursday of the full-room crossing, at the near landing's lip, bearing, with four people behind him and the stair turning. **Not recovered. Not converted. Not erased.** The record says, in that order and in Mikka Hale's reason for the order, that he was last seen at the near lip bearing, that he is not recovered, and that he is dead. Nobody went down the shaft and the chapter gives four lines for why.
- **How it was earned:** he is a trained bearing, propping and stretcher hand in Chapters 41, 43, 44, 45, 46 and 47 before it happens. He packs four joints. He demonstrates in Chapter 36 with a line and a stone that the near lip is one piece and moves about an inch when it is pulled at both ends — and that figure is the figure that kills him. He takes the pump lever for three weeks because Tams cannot make a fist, and asks that the frame be held by somebody who is bored. He holds the shore's head for the count of thirty-one when the line first comes tight, chosen by Iven by craft and not by size. He tells Ilsa's school children to go at a walk. He gives the lever to Jo in one sentence on the morning. And on the Wednesday night at the review, in front of eleven people, he says **"Then somebody is under the lip,"** and eleven people write it down and nobody says it out loud again, because by then it is on a page and on a page it is a sentence.

## Nera Oduya

- **Position:** the valve, the stair-side grille, the clinic return channel, the school corner's floor, and the stair's new air.
- **Authority:** technical authority over airflow, air testing, and safe operation of the air gate. Unchanged and independent. In Batch 0005 she **refuses to certify the stair's air** and says why, and puts a watch on it instead: read at the second bell and the fifth by a person, out loud, to a person, and not to a page. She finds the boundary is now breathing through the stair, that the shut school corner has moved something she cannot see into, and that she has two readings on her board she cannot account for.
- **Relationship:** professional complementarity with Marek and a fourth public disagreement — he says *the jack*, she requires a *work*, and the work turns out to be a proposal nobody has agreed to, which she writes on the first line of her own plan. No intimacy and no recognition scene. The relationship remains slow, professional, and unaccelerated.
- **The batch's most expensive decision:** she will not publish a rate for the dead window, on the grounds that she will not put an unproven pattern on a page she does not hold, and the ledger records the consequence — *the dead window after the lever comes off is shorter, the air engineer holds the number, and no person is to plan a crossing to a number they have been given by nobody.* **A plan built on that refusal is the plan Oren Sill died inside, and she does not revoke it and nobody asks her to.** She asks only that *noted* be struck out of Dalla's ledger, because a page carrying a warning in the language of a duty is a page that has already forgotten how to be frightened.

## Sefu Aran

- **Position:** the civic wheel, the deeper gate, the threshold, the cord, the black cord's hook, and the boundary's stores.
- **Role:** elected boundary route keeper; his paragraph is read and marked separately, every time. In Batch 0005 he **releases the forty feet of black cord** — released by nobody since the eighteenth day — for a named work, to the boundary and not to the crew, and says the price before he says yes: a lead wears, the joint will cut it, and the day it goes through somebody has to hold two ends of a line on a stair, and Renn Vale has already refused that in front of nine people. He ties the cord with Renn and nobody else may touch it. He asks Isel Ferrow to hold the bell and **tells him what he is being given**, which nobody did for the last person. He adds to his paragraph that the day the route is first used for anything but air it goes on the clerk's page with names and a date. He says of the boundary's over-full anteroom that a page is not a thing that fixes a floor.
- **His refusal of Marek's departure sentence** is new canon and is a refusal of a *sentence*, not of a man: *you have written yourself out of this building on a future, and the future has not happened, and if you want to write it then write it in December when your hand is what it is in December.*
- **Relationship to Nera:** deliberately separate authority, still the reason the boundary is defensible. He told her the crew's work's result before he told the room, twice, and did not remark on it either time.

## Hearthhold boundary maintainers

- **Renn Vale:** keeper of the shore, the road, and the lead line. She **ties and re-ties the stair cord with Sefu and nobody else**, and in Chapter 50 she ties the bell rope's other end to the newel so that nobody can reach the cord without passing the bell, which is the first thing in this Hearthhold built by a person who was not asked to build anything. Her sixth-watch position is **four days in seven, unchanged**. Her Chapter 38 stair-watch refusal is a **wider** refusal of the same thing and is **not withdrawn**; in Chapter 47 she distinguishes it out loud from counting a crossing and says she will leave the room rather than let the two be conflated. She **refused the school-door head its fourth wedge in her own words**, on the grounds that the queue of things in this building that want a wedge is longer than the seven. She **refused the crew access to the woman from the wash rooms** and the whole batch turned on that refusal being honoured: the crew went to the woman's door and the room, not to the woman. She has said twice, in the same words, that her mother's piece is a stone in a hole and is not a code. She has a written *not yet* under the day somebody has to hold two ends of a line, with her name on it.
- **Dalla Sen:** repair ledger, route record, work-bell relay, cost witness, and the person who asks who checks the tally. She reads every page back before she writes the next and the room takes each one apart. She received nothing and wrote everything. She **refused the fallen parapet slab a third time, to Iven Tallo by name, with his name entered under the refusal**, and she **struck the word *noted* out of her own entry** at Nera's request. She asks, of every unresolved item, what a person reading it in a year will need, and she writes the two volume questions down without answering them, because a question that is not written down is a question that has been made to have never been asked.
- **Jo Verrick:** trial pump maintainer with **five paid cleanings** and an estimate of **two or fewer** in her own hand, holder of the work board, the tool basket, the old flat file, and the plan in her own hand. She declared the fifth cleaning's price before she started it, and the estimate after it, in that order. She refused the cart and gave the best reason in the batch: every number in that route is a number about bodies, and take the bodies out and you have a more dangerous thing and not a bigger route. She argued to keep the crew on the plinth rota and lost in one sentence — *he is the boundary's* — and was glad to lose it. She is now a bounded maintainer and refuses to be a maintenance party.
- **Mikka Hale:** kitchen, clinic return watch, pump frame, and meals. She lifted the school pot twice and refused to feed the stair work. She is the one who said **"He is dead"** and who refused to write *missing*, because a missing man in a building like this one is a debt and the debtor is always a woman with a door. She ordered the relay with the slow in the middle and the fast at the edges and **said out loud what happens if the window comes early while the middle is out**, and the room did not write it down until that night. She stopped the kitchen brace in Chapter 35 and still has nothing left.
- **Ilsa Norr (nine children and herself, which is ten, and twelve on a market day):** the school corner's keeper, the mouth's keeper, and the person who closed it. She **refused to have the void called a fill** and said she would have a floor laid and know what was under it for as long as she keeps it, and she **lifted her own term** for one day so that a route could carry a school, and said out loud that it was the first thing she had ever unsaid in this building and that it went in the direction of *yes*. She asks, of any decision offered on her behalf, what the thing actually is before she lets anybody answer for her.
- **Isel Ferrow:** **the first reader of a stair in the Hearthhold**, as of the fourth of the month, after nine days as the second pair of eyes at the water mark and a month of not being first at anything. He held the threshold bell twice and was **told what he was being given** and asked what a bell is for, and Mikka took the water mark for the length of those periods so that he was not doing two things. He rang the bell when the route was shut in Chapter 50, and he read the stair's plinth on the fatal Thursday in Chapter 48 and called the turn about eight seconds behind Pell Roke and about the same moment as Nera.
- **Ruk Sella:** threshold-bell deputy three times in Batch 0005, asked in person every time, and both times she repeated the whole of it back, which is the practice now and is not the practice she was got the first time. Her door still closes and will not stay shut against the wheel, and it is the reason a household came down in the night on the morning of the crossing.
- **The woman from the wash rooms:** **she speaks, and she owns a clause.** Nine words in Chapter 47 that found four treads going down under the returning stair. Eleven words in Chapter 49 about a shut carrying a reason, which are in the ledger **in her own hand and not Renn's**, because Renn already has a hand and a road and a line and a door and four people and she has a mother and a room and a morning in which she lifts a cover. She insisted on going across the stair **without being asked**, and had that insistence entered in Renn's hand so that nobody could later say she was protected. She shut the route once during the Chapter 50 check, for the reason Mikka's own thirty-eighth-day term gave, and it cost a half-day and a school day and it was right. She refused help on the crossing in front of Renn. She is the model of how consent is supposed to arrive and the reason the crew's work could be handed over at all.

## Dael Harrow and the Cartwright pressure

Not physically present. His order still locks the guild-controlled surface door and still cannot open the Hearthhold gate, order air, create consent, or establish ownership. **The lower side of the returning stair is now in service and there is a route in this Hearthhold with no charter, and the guild has not yet heard about a shore.** Iven's warning has become a fact rather than a warning, and the crew's protection is that the route is maintained by the people whose rooms it is under, held by nobody, with a keeper's name on a boundary's own line and a slate that says no person may add a name to it — and Iven has said out loud that this is a position and not a shield.

## Returning stair and evidence

The stair is **restricted, corded, and in service on its near side only**. The line-block is a running block with a line in it, made up in three turns and a seizing beside Renn's mother's stop piece, which was not taken out and not built on. A **timber shore on the common landing** takes the line through a cut bearing face in the wall, hold-down through the fixed east stair's rail on forty feet of the boundary's own black cord. **Four treads go down behind the rail on the far side** and nobody has been on them. The return is quicker. The bright band is named as line wear and otherwise unexplained. The far wall of the throat is eleven feet down and has never been seen. The **wrapped empty-seat plate** lies in a knotted bundle of work cloth in the crew's hands, unexplained, unidentified, unused and unclaimed, with the shortest entry in the ledger.

## Relationship and power guardrails

- No harem structure and no instant romance. Nera remains an independent air engineer and civic adult. **No viewpoint shift occurs anywhere in Batch 0005.**
- **No formal loadcraft stage, bearing mark, repeatable reading, route change, binding, relay, or automatic repair has been granted.** Six nulls, all written down, all giving nothing usable.
- The five Room Checks and the declared-occupancy discipline remain the growth framework, and the occupancy check in Chapter 50 is the first one in the volume that is **run by the people who live in the room** rather than by a crew testing a room it is not responsible for.
- The working practice — the person who will keep a room writes the change to it — held under the worst pressure of the volume and produced its best work: a teacher's slate, a boundary keeper's slate, a clerk's struck-out word, and a woman from the wash rooms' own hand on the clause that closes a route. It remains a practice, not a title, and it is revocable.
