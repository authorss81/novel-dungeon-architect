## Calendar (locked, and the next batch must check against it)

The batch and the volume run on a **week clock**: the volume ends in the **ninth week**, and *nine weeks* is the book's standing measure of the crew's time in the Hearthhold boundary. A **day-number register** also runs through the manuscript, referring to named past events. The two are reconciled as follows and the reconciliation is now canon:

- Day-number references in the prose are **always events in the past**. The highest is the forty-sixth day, and every day number in the manuscript sits in the past relative to the ninth week.
- **No day number may be attached to an event that happens inside the current batch.** Chapter 45's first shore, Chapter 48's death, and Chapter 50's hand-over are in the ninth week and are referred to by what they are, never by a day.
- The hand-over chapter therefore carries *the ninth week*, *nine weeks* and *ten weeks* only. It carries no day number at all.
- If Volume 02 needs a day, it should continue the **week** clock, or add new past day numbers above the forty-sixth, never re-use or lower the existing ones.

## The route as handed over (Chapters 49–50)

Six terms, on **Renn Vale's slate**, agreed out loud one at a time by eleven named people, **none of them on the crew**:

1. **Four at a time, four minutes, and nobody on the stair in the six minutes after the lever comes off.**
2. **A bearer at the near lip** for the whole of any crossing, named on the day's board, a person of this boundary, not a person doing something else.
3. **Two readers** — the plinth and the string — for eight crossings, because a person who reads the plinth and cannot leave it is no use as a reader. **Isel Ferrow and the woman from the wash rooms.** The plinth reader's word is a **warning and not a stop**.
4. **The stop is the cloth, the cord, and any of the four people who keep a room.**
5. **Any of the four may shut it**, and the shut carries a reason in the person shutting it's own hand, and is shut **until somebody looks at the reason**, and if it stays shut that is a decision with a name and a date read out at the next bell. These eleven words are in the ledger **in her own hand and not Renn's**.
6. **The route is not for a cart.** Four reasons were given; the best is that every number in the route is a number about bodies.

The route **changes the escape and does not change the occupancy.** The anteroom's Chapter 10 rating is unchanged: **ten people including one litter, no more than two hours, outer service door open, pump attended, Nera or Sefu able to stop it.** The boundary's air now moves through the stair as well as through the valve, and Nera has put a **watch** on it — read at the second bell and the fifth by a person, out loud, to a person, not to a page — and has **refused to certify** the stair's air and has two unexplained readings on her own board.

**Anteroom clearance is two figures, and they are not interchangeable.** The anteroom's ten are **nine people on their feet and one litter**. On foot the nine clear through the kitchen line **inside a minute and a half**; the litter goes on four bearers through two feet eight and the anteroom is **not clear of it until four minutes and six**, which is the figure Dalla Sen wrote in her own hand in Chapter 48 without a cause beside it, and which is the litter's own transit time, not a clearance. The litter therefore **sets the clearance number** and always has. In Chapter 50's closure, everybody on their feet was out to the outer step in a minute and a quarter and the litter was still coming down the corridor when the pump came off the lever. No future batch may put a litter inside a walking-clearance figure.

**No person may add a name to the route's slate.** There is a **separate** page for what work the crew did, kept apart from the page that says what the route is, and Dalla Sen reads them back separately.

## Crew hours and the maintenance obligation

The crew's hours are **thirty-eight and a half**, given, owed to nobody, on a project that is not a maintenance party and is not on a rota. The boundary's hours in the same period are **forty-six**, and the finding Tams Quill arrived at is that the crew's share is the half that gets counted, because nineteen days of meals, a shutter route, a school day, and a watched flue wall do not go into a column of hours.

The maintenance obligation is bounded and written: **the five of them** hold the maintenance of what they built **until the boundary can do it without them and a person named by the boundary has said so out loud, in the room, with the reason**. Until then they are not tenants, not neighbours, and not residents, and no hour of it is owed. **The number of obligors is five and the hand-over page was corrected to say so in the room:** Marek said six at the tally, Tams Quill corrected him, and Dalla Sen's hand stayed off the pen. The crew is **five**, not six, and the six belongs to the opening party and to the work that was done, not to anything that is still owed.
