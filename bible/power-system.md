# Power System: Loadcraft

## 1. Core concept

**Loadcraft** is the practice of reading and safely redirecting the forces that keep a room, route, or civic network standing. It is not unrestricted construction magic. A loadcraft worker still needs materials, measurements, labor, light, time, and an exit.

The Lattice makes forces unusually visible to a trained reader. The gift does not create matter, grant air, solve politics, or predict the future. It lets a person perceive the direction and relative danger of a load, then make a limited change to where that load travels.

## 2. The two kinds of structure

### Ordinary structure

Wood, stone, brick, rope, metal, soil, and biological materials obey ordinary physical rules. They remain useful when the Lattice is not involved. A well-built surface bridge can be safer than a magical one; a bad survey can kill in either world.

### Lattice structure

Lattice rooms are built around **rule-lines**, mineral seams that carry stress, air, or civic function farther than ordinary materials can. A rule-line is not visible to everyone. When it is stable, it feels like a persistent pressure or a clean vibration. When it is overloaded, it leaks heat, sound, dust, water, or a faultform.

A structure that uses a rule-line is still physical. Its supports have weight, its air shafts have flow, and its doors have hinges. The difference is that the Lattice can preserve a pattern of use and carry a share of the force through connected rooms.

## 3. The five Room Checks

Every safe project must account for all five checks. A loadcraft ability may make one check easier to read, but it cannot replace the other four.

1. **Bearing:** at least two independent paths carry weight into stable ground.
2. **Breath:** air can enter and leave without using the only safe escape shaft.
3. **Drainage:** water, heat, waste, and smoke have a route away.
4. **Escape:** a full room can still be evacuated.
5. **Change:** the design tolerates a realistic change in occupancy, weather, equipment, or panic.

A project that ignores any check is structurally vulnerable even if it looks impressive or passes a magical reading.

## 4. The operations

### Reading

A reader can feel the direction of forces after touching a surface, inspecting a plan, or spending time in a room. Reading gives relative danger, not an exact measurement. It cannot identify intention, ownership, or a hidden lie.

- **Skill requirement:** observation, a physical contact surface, and enough time to notice changes.
- **Limit:** a deliberately false load path, a living mass, or a noisy shock can mislead a reader.
- **Example:** Marek notices that a stone stair is not carrying its own weight but is being held up by a thin rule-line behind the wall. The line may break when the room fills with people.

### Bearing mark

A bearing mark is a short, deliberate inscription made with chalk, grease, or a carved anchorstone groove. It tells a rule-line which way to carry a limited force. One mark has one direction and one stated limit.

- **Requirement:** a readable surface, a clear intention, and a physical mark or equivalent anchor.
- **Duration:** until the load changes beyond the mark's condition, the material fails, or a stronger pattern overwrites it.
- **Limit:** a mark cannot repair a missing support, purify air, or create a door where the surrounding shell cannot carry one.
- **Cost:** focused fatigue, hand tremor, a temporary burning sensation, and a load echo in the reader's body.

A coercive mark is brittle. If people are forced to act as counter-supports while they believe the structure is safe, their inconsistent intent shows in the load and creates a fracture point. Consent is a power-system rule, not merely a political metaphor.

### Brace

A brace is a physical support joined to a bearing mark. It can hold a wall, prop a stair, divide a crowd load, or protect a temporary work site.

- **Requirement:** joinwood, timber, stone, cable, or another tested support; a counter-bearing; and time to let the load settle.
- **Limit:** braces do not increase a room's total capacity. They redistribute force and create a new failure point.
- **Cost:** material, labor, time, and a clear evacuation plan. A brace built during a fight may be faster but less reliable.
- **Failure example:** a brace that keeps a ceiling from dropping can redirect its weight into a room full of sleeping children. The ceiling is stable; the shelter is not.

### Route change

A route change alters a door, shaft, stair, vent, or boundary enough to let people, air, or water move differently.

- **Requirement:** a bearing plan, an air plan, an exit, and a receiving space able to take the transferred load.
- **Limit:** a route cannot bypass an unknown pressure field. It must be physically continuous or paired with a construction that makes the discontinuity survivable.
- **Cost:** the new route changes neighboring loads, water, and escape plans. A successful opening can be politically or socially catastrophic.
- **Failure example:** a sealed door saves a leaking room from water but traps six people and cuts off the only ventilation shaft.

### Binding

Binding joins a repaired or newly designed structure to the Lattice's existing pattern. The structure gains a useful memory of its function and can be maintained by compatible workers, but it also becomes part of the network's obligations.

- **Requirement:** repeated use, a clear public purpose, and a maintenance path that does not depend on one hidden operator.
- **Limit:** a binding cannot erase a disagreeing community. If residents stop caring for a structure, the Lattice gradually forgets its intended function.
- **Cost:** the builder accepts a longer responsibility. A private shelter that becomes a public route can be lawfully and morally contested.

### Relay

A relay is a distributed structure that passes load between independent anchors. It is the practical form of the Civic Loom. No single person can safely carry a whole relay.

- **Requirement:** multiple teams, compatible marks, maintenance schedules, a shared record, and a way to withdraw a failed anchor without killing the others.
- **Limit:** a relay is only as good as its least maintained link. It cannot hide disagreement; disagreement may appear as uneven stress and must be handled openly.
- **Cost:** negotiation, redundancy, public trust, and the loss of a single hero's speed.

## 5. How growth is earned

There are no experience bars, ranks, or automatic skill purchases. Marek advances when he:

- performs difficult work and studies its consequences;
- fails in a way that teaches a new limit;
- learns a craft he does not possess from a person who does;
- accepts help after trying to carry the load alone;
- builds a structure that can be understood by people besides himself;
- gives up a private advantage to make a system more resilient;
- takes responsibility for maintenance and political consequences, not only the opening ceremony.

A major breakthrough cannot occur merely because he is tired, trapped, or surrounded by danger. The setting may force a decision, but the decision still depends on preparation and relationships.

## 6. Power stages and costs

The chapter ranges are planning anchors, not rigid level thresholds.

| Stage | Approximate range | What Marek can do | What it costs |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Unschooled eye** | 1–50 | Ordinary engineering plus brief, unreliable impressions of load shifts; the first repeatable reading appears near the end of this range. | No dependable supernatural operation; mistakes are ordinary and dangerous; fear can distort judgment. |
| **Reader** | 51–100 | Read a room's main force directions and identify a likely failure before it becomes obvious. | Headaches, vertigo, sensory bleed, and the risk of treating a first impression as a fact. |
| **Marker** | 101–150 | Place one temporary bearing mark; reinforce a small support or redirect a minor pressure. | Burning hands, fatigue, a visible mark that enemies can target, and the obligation to state the mark's limit. |
| **Bracer** | 151–250 | Build a temporary brace, refuge, or protected work corridor with a team. | Time, materials, labor, and a failure point that must be watched; people can be hurt while he is protecting them. |
| **Router** | 251–350 | Change a route or airflow within one sector when the counter-load is understood. | Nearby rooms lose safety margin; communities depend on the route; political rivals can turn a public route against him. |
| **District engineer** | 351–500 | Coordinate several linked structures, repair a district-scale network, and teach a limited craft to others. | Public responsibility, weakened personal boundaries, and the loss of freedom to make unilateral “temporary” decisions. |
| **Loomwright** | 501–650 | Operate a limited relay or partial Loom with multiple crews; repair a shared civic pattern without taking ownership of it. | Recurring load echoes, accumulated sensory strain, dependence on consent, and the possibility of being mistaken for a new central authority. |
| **Commons architect / anchorless steward** | 651–800 | Design a network of independent anchors and institutions that can operate without a permanent builder at its center; spend one extraordinary coordinated intervention in the final volume. | Marek must share authority, cannot safely work alone, and must accept that the city can make choices he would not have chosen. |

The final stage is not “unlimited architect.” It is **Anchorless stewardship**: the ability to make one extraordinary, coordinated intervention as part of a network, followed by ordinary limitations. The intervention can be spent once without making Marek omnipotent.

## 7. Failure modes

- **False certainty:** the reader sees a force but not its cause. A person, a magical ward, or a second room may alter the result.
- **Overconfidence:** a stable reading at low occupancy becomes a collapse when a crowd, cart, or water column arrives.
- **Single-exit thinking:** a route improves travel but removes the only breathable air or escape path.
- **Forced cooperation:** people pressured into maintaining a mark or brace make the structure unstable.
- **Hidden maintenance:** a project is celebrated at opening and neglected after the builder leaves.
- **Political capture:** a public route becomes a toll road, a private shelter becomes a checkpoint, or a safety record becomes a weapon.
- **Sensory injury:** repeated load carrying gives Marek phantom stresses and eventually prevents him from trusting solitary readings.

Failure must remain consequential. A corrected plan is not the same as a cost-free victory.

## 8. The System boundary

There is no stat-bearing game System in the core canon. The Lattice can leave **Cairn marks**, old worker's inscriptions that briefly communicate one condition, limit, or missing link. A Cairn mark is incomplete, may be misread, and never solves the surrounding problem. If a later volume uses a single plain-language notice, it should be a translated Cairn mark or a practical field convention, not a quest generator.

The point of the power is to change what Marek chooses to carry and whom he trusts to carry it with him.
