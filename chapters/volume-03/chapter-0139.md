# Chapter 139 — The Only Plan Anybody Can Use

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the twenty-seventh week, a Thursday, and the school did not run, and the water at the waist had not been off the fill since the Thursday before, and the boundary emptied two rooms into the anteroom using a page that five people refused to sign, and the anteroom's second way out is a word, and the word could not be said by anybody in it, and nobody died and the plan worked.**

**It is the best thing anybody in this building has done in twenty-seven weeks and a physician said so in a room and then said the other half of it, and the board was walked, and a man with a hand that will not close wrote a number of people in his own book and gave it to a woman and not to a page.**

Jo Verrick found it at the first bell and came up the nine steps at a run, which she has not done in this building in twenty-seven weeks, and got to the top of the steps and had to stop.

"**It is over the fill and it has been over the fill since the Thursday before, and nobody in this room said that out loud this morning, and that is the whole of why I am standing here. Not into the channel, not into the low end, over the fill, and it did not come up today. It is standing on the ground at the waist about the height of my knee and it is not moving and there is nobody putting water there."

"**And I have said out loud in this building, and Jo Verrick said it, that nobody measures that fill. Three of us agreed not to and I gave the reason on the Thursday before and the reason was that if we ever measured it and it was moving we would have to say so out loud, and then people would stand on it, and then a person would be under it who is not the one already there."

"**So the water is on it and it is not moving and we do not know whether it is holding, and I am going to say the next part in the shape of a woman who has kept a rule for twenty-seven weeks and has just found out what a rule costs when the thing it was about turns up."

"**If I had measured that fill in the twentieth week I would have a number. I do not have a number. I have a rule and a reason and a woman with a lamp, and I am telling the room that the reason was good and the rule was right and the water is on the ground anyway.**"

Renn Vale said the sentence that opened the morning, and she said it on the landing, and the whole of the building heard it.

"**Then the waist is shut to everybody except what we are going to carry, and I am closing it, and I am the person who closes it, and I am not asking anybody's permission and I am not putting it in a book with a number in it, and the fifth bell is in an hour and there are nine children at the school door who are going to be somewhere in twenty minutes."

"**The anteroom takes ten including one litter for no more than two hours. It is the only room in this Hearthhold with a row on the plan that says a number and a condition and a person who keeps it. We are going to use it, and I am going to say out loud before we start that we are about to use it above its number, and I am the person who keeps its terms and I am the one who says so.**"

---

**In about fifty minutes the boundary emptied the kitchen-line room and the common landing into the anteroom, and it did it out of the third column, in four hands, and the third column is the only list of anybody in this Hearthhold that anybody did not have to write this week.**

Dalla Sen had the plan in four parts on the anteroom step and read the third column out loud, one row at a time, and everybody in the room found their own handwriting being read back at them at their number, and it was not a muster and it was not a roll and it was not the count of the trapped, and it took fifty minutes.

"**The third column is what is done, in the hand of the person who does it, and there are twelve rows and eleven of them are filled and one is empty and the man who keeps that one has never said which it is. And on Tuesday the eleventh row gained three hands and an objection, and I am not reading that one out, because that is a revision and a revision is not a plan."

"**What I am reading out is this: each person in this room is going to say what their row says they do, and everybody else is going to believe them, because they wrote it."

"**Ilsa Norr.**"

"**Nine children, one of whom sits, and the fire is not lit, and the strip under the ninth stone is said out loud in the doorway every morning the room is used.**"

"**Renn Vale.**"

"**The corridor is shut on the first, second and fourth days and open on the third, fifth and sixth, and the crossing is a walk and a bearer on the fifth, and first over and last back.**"

"**Mikka Hale.**"

"**The pot is ten days and has not gone up, and the jars are two a day and are never added to the pot, and the fire in the corner of that room is not lit and I would like it entered that I have been asked about it four times this week and I have said the same thing four times and I would like it asked a fifth time and refused a fifth time.**"

"**Jo Verrick.**"

"**A hand on the bed of the ninth stone at the first bell, out loud, in a room, yes or no, and whether anybody is going to be in the room.**"

---

**Somebody read the anteroom's terms out against the original, in a room, and compared them, because that is what this building does with a condition and it is a decision on a page in a clerk's hand and it does not care what day it is.**

Bel Ordry said: "**Read the anteroom's terms out of the book, Renn, and then I will read the copy, and we will do it the way we do everything, because the anteroom is about to be full of other people's children and it is the busiest room in this Hearthhold and its second way out is a sentence.**"

"**And I want the fifth one read twice.**"

"**Ten including one litter, for no more than two hours,**" said Renn Vale, out of the book. "**The outer service door. The pump attended. And the air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it.**"

Bel Ordry read the copy out after her and the two were the same and it took a minute and a half and nobody in that room needed it and everybody in that room listened to it, and then the clerk said the part that was in nobody's copy.

"**Entered, and entered again because it is the third time I have entered it: the fifth condition has not been true since the middle of last month. The elected keeper is four days up a road and has been since before the first of the month, and there is a line in this book in my hand that says nobody in this building knows where he is, and the room decided not to write down how long he has been gone."

"**So the anteroom has been over its rating on one of its five conditions for a fortnight, and I knew it on the day it stopped being true, and I did not write it down, and the reason I did not write it down is that a condition you have broken and written down is a condition you have agreed to keep."

"**I have been a clerk for twenty-seven weeks and that is the first thing I have entered in this book that I did wrong on purpose.**"

---

**By the sixth bell the room was full and the word could not be said, and nobody in the anteroom had any way out of it except nine steps and a landing, and a physician said the thing about it before anybody asked him to.**

"**The second way out of this room is a word, and the rule is that the word is said by the person who is going to be in the room, and the clerk repeats it."

"**And there are nine people in this anteroom who are going to be in it for about an hour and not one of them is standing in a room with a clerk in it, and so the rule cannot be kept this morning, in the same way it could not be kept on the first day of the twenty-fifth week at a shut door into an empty room."

"**So I am saying the thing out loud before it happens, and I am not going to wait for it. The anteroom is at its number and then above its number, and its way out is nine steps and a landing and a stair head, and a man at a bench on the stair head, and a room at the bottom of some steps in this building that has never been a room anybody is in."

"**And the last of those is the only one I would send a child to and I am not going to, and nobody else is going to, and I want that entered in the same sentence as the word and not in a kinder one.**"

Marek Venn stood at the anteroom step with nine children coming up past him and said the thing he had come to say, and he said it in a room and not to a book, and it was the sentence he had been carrying since the first day of the twenty-sixth week.

"**A plan nobody has signed is the only plan anybody can use. I want that said and I want it said with the whole of it in the same breath, and then I am going to take the other half back out of the room, because I have not been able to for six weeks."

"**Half of it is true and it is this. Twelve rows with a permission and a condition and a person in each of them, and a page nobody can be given, and a plan that does not leave this building, and it moved about forty people out of two rooms into a third in fifty minutes on a Thursday morning, and there is nothing else in this Hearthhold that could have done that. Not the charter, which is eight clauses and no name. Not the evacuation sheet with a heading struck through it. Not the crew, who are not on a rota and cannot stand a watch. A page."

"**And the other half is this, and I am not going to be the man who says only the first half. A page with no signature on it has nobody in it. Everybody who wrote a third column wrote what they do. Nobody wrote what happens if they are wrong, and there is a difference between those two documents and the difference is the person, and I have spent twenty-seven weeks in this building learning that the person is the only protection anything in here has ever had, and I have just said out loud that it is not, in a room with forty people in it, and I would like somebody to hold me to the second half for the rest of my life.**"

"**I will hold you to it,**" said Renn Vale, "**and I will tell you why it does not stop the sentence being true. A plan with a signature on it is a plan about a person and gets sent for. A plan with no signature on it is a plan about a building and gets argued with. And this boundary has spent a month learning that we can be argued with, and I would rather be argued with than sent for, and I have not got a better answer than that and I have looked."

"**So the plan stands and nobody signs it, and I am going to stand in this anteroom with nine children in it above its number for an hour on a Thursday morning, and I am going to be the one who did that, and nobody is to blame me, because the rule says the person who writes a change to a room is not to blame for it, and I did not write this one. Somebody else did. And they are standing on the step and they are not going to be comfortable about it, and I am not going to help them.**"

Mero Senn said the two halves in a room with a physician's flatness and a room full of children in it, and he was not looking at anybody.

"**I am going to say the thing that is against the room and not against him, and then I am going to stop, because there is a schoolteacher standing in it."

"**A page with four columns and no signature moved about forty people through a building in fifty minutes. That is either the best thing anybody in this Hearthhold has done in twenty-seven weeks or it is the reason one of them is not here in a year, and I cannot tell you which one, and neither can he, and neither can the clerk, and that is not modesty and it is not a refusal to be useful. It is that I have been doing this for twenty-two years and I have never once been able to tell you which one."

"**I will say one thing that I can tell you. There is a girl of nine in this room with a foot in crate board, and she is in this anteroom because of a decision that was correct, and she will be in this anteroom again next week, and there is no page anybody can write that will put her in a different room, and that is not a plan failing. That is a plan working, in a building with no timber, in a cold month, on the far side of a waist nobody measures."

"**And I am not going to be asked about whether a number in a column is acceptable. I have been asked three times and I have given the same answer three times and I am going to keep giving it until somebody asks me a different question.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked, briefly, because a corridor was shut and a room was full and the count said so before anybody asked him to, and the clerk read the six things and the day's number and the count and a clerk's entry of her own wrongdoing which she read twice.**

"**Four today and a hundred and thirty-four since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and fifty-one and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half."

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is on the fill at the waist and it is not moving and there is no number for that fill anywhere in this building, and that is the second time a thing I have entered has been about a thing nobody measures."

"**The count. Wen Pask is seventy-four days and seventy-four nights into a count that is his own, and I am going to keep writing it, and I said on the second day of last month that I would, and I have, and I would like one person in this room to notice that a clerk who has entered her own mistake twice in a fortnight is a clerk who has stopped putting a reason beside two facts, and has started putting a decision beside them, and I do not know yet which of those is worse.**"

**A man wrote a number in his own book at the seventh bell and gave it to a woman and not to a page, because that is what he does, and what he wrote was the number of people in the anteroom, and it is not on the plan and the plan's first column does not say it and the plan is in a coat.**

**And the market day is the day after tomorrow, and the only road out of this Hearthhold for anybody who is not one of the crew has two lines and a space in each of them on a board at a gate four days up the middle passage, and this building has decided that it will not fill them in, and has not decided what it will do instead, and the only other way out of this building is at the low end, where there is a hole with no grate in it under two flags and a length of spent line, and a page that has no row for it, and a blank review page with three conditions on it and no name against any of them.**
