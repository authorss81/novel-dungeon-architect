# Chapter 195 — Two Networks That Do Not Touch

The third day of the thirty-seventh week is a Wednesday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the thirty-ninth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end. Nine children went up the nine steps at the first bell with a woman ahead of them, and nine children crossed the anteroom twice, and they went round the corner of the floor without being told to, which is a thing nine children now do.

Renn Vale watched the line in the party wall at the first bell, which is her bell on an open morning, and was there about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times. The clerk entered it as a figure of nothing, and entered that it was the figure of nothing for the fifth morning running, and that she was not giving the run a number, because a clerk who counts her own nothings ends up keeping the count.

Then the anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and the pump was attended and stopped on a rising water line that has not moved since the week before last.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the same as sixteen, and the string was where the string was, and she did not put the rule in the join. When Tams Quill put it to her from the bench that a rule in a gap would be the only honest instrument in the building, she told him that a rule belongs in a joint and that a joint is not a gap, and he wrote nothing on his knee and looked satisfied in a way that nobody else in that room could see.

Two slates were on the trestle by the second bell, side by side, and both of them were blank.

Nella Bray's was the one she had carried for six weeks with nothing written on it in this room. Dalla Sen's was the one she had turned face down over a number a fortnight ago. They lay on the trestle about a hand's width apart, and nobody had put them there and nobody owned up to it, and Dalla Sen entered that she had not put hers there and that she had not asked.

Then Emrys Callow came off the bench where his packs were and stood in the middle of the anteroom floor, and he did not touch either slate, and the room needed about four strokes of the pump to get used to the idea of him opening.

"**I am going to say the thing that stops this before anybody puts a shape on it, and I am saying it first so that nobody can be accused afterwards of proposing something they did not know was impossible.**"

Nobody in the room had been expecting a foreman to open.

"**Last Friday, on the fifth day, this room agreed a third thing, and the third thing was that the check crosses. The person who checks the work is a person from the other crew. Not this room, not this building, from the crew that did not do it. And the reason is in the clerk's book in a keeper's mouth and it is the best sentence anybody has said in here in a month, which is that a crew that checks its own work has stopped being able to find out, and that is not dishonesty, it is that a man holding a bar cannot see the end of his own timber.**"

He put his hand flat on his own chest, which is what a man does when he is talking about the five people behind him.

"**And that is the clause that cannot be used here. A check that crosses from one of these into the other is a pair of hands in both of them. A person with a hand in both of them is a keeper of both of them. And two networks with one keeper in them are not two networks, they are one network with a long name on it, and one network has one keeper over it, and one keeper over a set of rooms is a post.**"

"**And a post,**" from the step, "**is a name in a line in a register kept by an office, and the offices that keep registers of this kind are four and a half days up that passage, and I have been a man in this building for thirty-seven weeks who has watched one such name cost a boy a roll number.**"

"**Then it is said,**" and he put his hands behind his back, "**and I have said it before the room has a shape, and now everybody in here can go back to their own trade and check me.**"

Then Nella Bray took her slate off the trestle, which she had not done since she put it down, and held it against her hip the way a woman holds a thing she is about to use as a counter clerk.

"**And the cost, before the decision,**" she said. "**You have all been talking about two ways out of a room and none of you has said what the second one is, so I am going to say it, and then I am going to say what it takes, and then you can decide whether you still want it.**"

"**We are in it,**" from the fourth step.

"**The second one is the two rooms off the far end of this anteroom, past the pump,**" and she did not put the slate down to say it. "**Where six people who are not this building's have slept for a fortnight. And I am going to say four things about those two rooms and I have checked every one of them twice this week and I do not intend to be wrong about them in front of you.**"

"**They are not in the sixteen rows,**" she said. "**They have never been in the sixteen rows. They are not in a plan, they are not in the charter, they are not in a row, they are not on a board, they are not in anybody's column, and this building has kept them for a fortnight by feeding them and has never once written a line about them, which is either the best thing it has done in thirty-seven weeks or the worst and I have not been able to work out which and I have had a fortnight to try.**"

"**They can be emptied one into the other,**" and she held up one finger to mark that it was the third thing and not the second. "**Both doors are on and both latch. That is the whole of what I know about them and it is the only thing that has to be true.**"

"**And here is the cost, and it is mine to state because they are my two rooms, and I will not be able to improve on it later.**" She laid the slate down flat on the trestle and left her hand on it. "**The two rooms do not empty into this anteroom. From this morning they do not. The six of us are not in this building's way out. And the reason is the thing that man just said, and the reason is right, and this room is not going to be let off it.**"

"**Then the reason has to be in the terms,**" from the fourth step, and she wrote nothing down while she said it.

"**Because the anteroom is the first room in the first network, and if the six of us are in the anteroom and the anteroom is in a network, then we are inside a network, and a person inside a network is inside a permission, and a permission is a column, and this boundary has spent thirty-seven weeks refusing to let a column get a name in it, and it will not be a Wednesday morning that gives one.**"

"**And the price of that,**" said Nella Bray, "**which is the price and I have thought about it for two days and there is no kind version of it. After this morning nobody in this building is going to be able to say how many people are in the anteroom on a school morning. There is a figure of ten on a district's map and it is what this room is permitted to hold, and it is not what is in it, and it has never been what is in it, and after this morning it is going to be the only number anybody can find, and I am going to know the real one and I am going to be the only one who knows it, and I am going to know it because I feed people.**"

The pump got five strokes in and nobody in that room used one of them.

"**That is the cost,**" she said, "**and it does not go away, and I am not going to be the woman who is thanked for paying it, because I am not paying it. Six of us are. And I would like it entered that the woman at a counter has stated the cost of a thing in a room before the room has decided to have the thing, and that nobody in this room improved on it, and that two people in this room wanted to.**"

Dalla Sen entered it in Nella Bray's words, and entered that it was the cost and not a condition, and entered that a cost and a condition are different instruments and that this building has spent thirty-seven weeks being careful about the difference and that this morning the difference earned itself.

"**Then the half against, and it is mine, and it has to be said before I agree to anything,**" and she came down one step, which is not all the way and is more than she usually gives.

"**A second place for six people to be, at the moment when the way out is the thing that has gone, is a second place for six people to be in when the way out is gone. I have spent thirty-seven weeks refusing to give this building a room with a condition on it that I cannot open, and I am about to say yes to two, and the second one has no door, and I want somebody in this room to say out loud that a network which ends in a room is a room with a condition on it and a condition with a keeper in it is a thing that can be kept shut by a keeper who is frightened.**"

"**A condition with a keeper in it is the only kind there is,**" from the anvil end, the chisel still in his right hand and the left open and flat where it always is. "**There is no other kind. A condition written by an office is a thing a person can be sent to keep. A condition written in the hand of the person who is going to keep it is a thing a person can be told to stop keeping, and that is the whole of the difference, and I have read both, and one of them is on a piece of paper four and a half days up a passage with my name in the first line of it and nothing in the second, and I would rather this building had eleven of the second kind than one of the first, and I would rather it had none of the first kind and that is not a thing I can say in a room without somebody hearing it as a virtue.**"

"**Entered, in a carpenter's words, and entered that the man who said it has a piece of paper four and a half days up a road with his name in it, and entered that nobody in this room is going to hold that against him today,**" and she wrote the whole of it, which took longer than it should have.

"**Two networks that do not touch,**" said Nella Bray. "**Give it to the room as a rule and not as a wish, because I have watched four hundred people say a wish and turn it into a rule in a fortnight.**"

Then Renn Vale came down all four steps and stood in the middle of the anteroom floor with the hole in the corner at her feet, and she said the shape of it out loud, in the order a person walks it, and she said it once.

"**The first network is this building. The room at the kitchen-line end, into the common landing, into the corridor, into this anteroom, and out at the outer service door. Every room on it can be emptied into the next one. The last one out of a room decides when that room is empty, and that has been true in this building for longer than anybody standing in it has been alive, and it is not on anybody's page and it is not going on anybody's page.**"

"**I keep it,**" she said. "**And my condition is written on this slate in my hand and there is no number on it and there is no day on it, and it is falsifiable, and I can stop it, and I do not owe anybody a reason and nobody has to ask me for one.**"

Then Nella Bray picked up the second slate and uncapped nothing, because there was no chalk, and looked at it, and put it back down.

"**Mine is going to take a moment,**" "**because I have been at a counter for nineteen years and I write things other people's hands are going to read, and this is the first piece of paper in that time that nobody is going to read, and I find that I do not know how to start one.**"

"**Start it as a term,**" said Renn Vale. "**Six terms, and nobody reads them out, and a clerk writes them and a foreman reads them back.**"

"**Then this is not that,**" said Nella Bray. "**And I am going to say why, because if I do not say it now then somebody will do it in a month and call it tidying up.**"

She did not pick the chalk up. She put her own hand flat on the slate and then took it off again and said the condition out loud first, and then asked for the chalk with her eyes instead of her voice, and then wrote it, and the room watched a clerk's hand at a counter do a thing it has not done in this building at all, which is write something down for nobody.

"**I keep these two rooms,**" she said, reading it off the slate in her own voice, "**while the last one out of them is in the other one, and I stop keeping them the moment one of the five of them says in a room that he is not the one who is keeping them, and I do not need a second reason from anybody, and if I am the only one left in this building who knows how these two rooms are, that is not a reason to go on keeping them.**"

"**Your slate, in your own mouth, back to you,**" and she put both hands behind her back to make the point that she was not going to touch it.

Nella Bray read Renn Vale's slate back to her, word for word, out loud, in the anteroom, and found nothing to change, and Renn Vale read Nella Bray's back and found nothing to change. Dalla Sen entered that two conditions had been written that morning in two hands on two slates, and that she had entered that both of them existed and that she had entered neither of them, and gave the reason: a clerk who writes down a condition is a clerk who has a copy of it, and a copy is the beginning of a column, and a column is a post. The two slates went into two pockets.

"**And the terms of the two of them, and they are being said once,**" and she held up her slate edge-on so that the room could see it was still blank except for her own hand, "**and they are the same four, and nobody is writing them down and nobody is reading them back off a slate.**"

"**One. No order on either of them. Nobody is told to use either. Nobody is posted to either. No bell, no day of the week, no register, no column, no heading, and no name in a second line anywhere in this building.**"

"**Two. The hours do not cross and do not join. There are two lines under no heading and nobody adds them up, not this week, not in a month, not ever, and that has been in the clerk's book since the fifth day of last week and it covers these as well.**"

"**Three. Neither network is a load on a road. Nobody has put a name on a line, nothing goes out on a cart, and the middle passage is four days and this boundary does not hand anybody a bundle.**"

"**Four. The two of them do not touch. Not a room in both. Not a person in both. And that is the clause that just cost a foreman his best rule, and I am putting it in the list and not leaving it out of the list because it is the expensive one.**"

Then Emrys Callow gave the room the last thing, and he gave it standing, and he did not put his hand on his chest.

"**Then the third clause of last Friday's arrangement is off,**" he said. "**The check does not cross. Not for this, not for anything two of us touch. And I want it in the room in my own words, because I said the sentence on Friday that made it true and I am the man taking it back, and if nobody writes that down then in six weeks this room will have forgotten that it gave something up here.**"

"**What it costs,**" he said, "**is that five men of mine are going to check their own work from today, and there is no second set of eyes on anything they do, and I have just told this room in my own words that a crew that checks its own work has stopped being able to find out. So I have said a true thing and then I have made it false, in one morning, in one room, and I did it because the alternative is one keeper, and one keeper is a post, and a post is what a man four and a half days up this passage put in the first line of a form and I have read that form and I have my own name on it.**"

"**I am not asking anybody to trust me and I am not asking anybody to forgive me,**" he said. "**I am telling a room what it is getting instead of what it had, and it is getting less, and I would like a clerk to enter that a foreman gave up the only arrangement he had on purpose and that nobody in this room thanked him and that the clerk does not either, and she enters that she noticed herself not doing it.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that I have not thanked him either, and that I noticed myself not doing it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**And the half against the giving up,**" said Nella Bray, from the trestle. "**The check not crossing is not the check being smaller. The check not crossing is the check not existing, and the reason you can live with it is that nobody in this building is going to look at your five either. I want that said in a room by the man whose hours are the ones not being added up, and I have said it, and it is the half against.**"

"**It is,**" "**and I have nothing, and I have had nothing for eleven days, and I would rather have nothing in a room than a something in a form.**"

"**And the half against the whole of it, and it is mine, and it is a trade objection and not a moral one,**" said Iven Tallo, and he put the chisel down, which is what he does.

"**None of this is a relay. I want that in the room because somebody in an office is going to call it one and be wrong, and because a relay passes force between anchors and nothing here passes anything anywhere. A person walks from one room to the next room. The last one out of a room carries a word to the next door and nothing else. There is no load, there is no mark, there is no anchor, and I have not cut anything and I am not going to.**"

"**There is one thing wrong with that, and a fortnight ago I would not have seen it,**" from the trestle, "**which is that a person on a road is a load. That is the entire rule of the office that put that form out, and it is not a joke, and it has been the rule of that office for longer than anybody in this room has been standing in it. A person in a corridor moving a case of lamp oil is a load. A person walking from one room to the next room with nothing is not a load, and I agree with the carpenter, and I want it entered that the woman at the counter agrees, and I want it entered that she has spent nineteen years writing the other half of that sentence on forms and has never once been asked whether the second half was true.**"

"**Then you have been wrong for nineteen years,**" from the step, flatly.

"**I have been paid for nineteen years,**" and she turned the slate over on the trestle, "**and those are not the same and I have known they were not the same for about eleven of them, and the clerk can enter the eleven.**"

Tams Quill had been at the end of the bench through the whole of it with the piece of chalk he has not used, and now he was asked, because Dalla Sen asked him, and she asked him because she had watched him move the chalk twice.

"**A nineteenth entry.**" "**No.**" and he did not look up.

"**Reason,**" with the chalk up and the point of it resting against her knuckle.

"**A set of rooms is not a thing in this building,**" he said. "**A set of rooms is either in the plan or it is not, and the plan is sixteen rows with a date in the corner of it and it does not leave this building, and the two rooms off the far end of this anteroom are not in the plan and are not going in. And a board is where the things that are not rooms go, so that a person can find a room by looking at a piece of slate instead of by walking into it. The day a set of rooms goes on that board, that board is a plan without a date in the corner, and a plan without a date in the corner is a page in a coat that somebody has lost.**"

"**That is a fifth reason and it is the best of the five,**" said Bel Ordry from the step, "**and it does not need improving on, and I will say in a room that a man has now refused five things in three weeks, and that I do not know what happens when he stops.**"

"**Then it is a sixth thing, and it goes the same way,**" and he put the chalk down on his knee, "**and I am not the man who works out in advance what it is going to be.**"

Sefu Aran had been at the head of the nine steps through the whole of it, and he came down two of them, which is as far as he has come in a fortnight, and he stood there while a foreman gave up the only rule he had and a woman from a counter gave up the only figure she had.

"**I am the elected keeper of this boundary,**" he said, "**and my whole instrument is admission and a crossing, and neither of those is a crossing, and my name goes on neither of them, and nobody is going to ask me to put it there. And I am not going to say how long I have been standing at the top of nine steps this morning, because the room decided a long time ago not to count that and I have stopped supplying it.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the elected keeper of this boundary was in a room for the whole of the morning while two networks of rooms were made in it, and that he said that his instrument is admission and a crossing and that neither of those is a crossing, and that he put no name on either of them, and that nobody asked him to.**"

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell with his book shut under his forearm, and he had said nothing at all through any of it, which is the first morning in a fortnight he has said nothing through two arrangements, and both of them were made by other people in a room he was standing in.

He had a place in one of them. He had a place in one of them because the first network is a way for people to get out of rooms and a man who cannot stand a watch can walk a corridor, and there is no part of either of these that requires a person to hold anything. He had known that at the fourth bell of the second day and he had been carrying the sentence since, and it was a good sentence, and it was two sentences, and the second one was the one he could not get out from under.

"**I am on neither of them,**" he said, to the room, in the voice he used when he had decided that saying it to nobody was still saying it. "**And I am not on either of them because I cannot stand a watch, and there is a watch in both of those, and a watch is a person who is somewhere at a time, and there is no version of me that is somewhere at a time. And the second half of it is that if I were on one of them then somebody would have to be standing where I should be, and there is nobody, and the page where that person's hours would go is blank and has been for thirty-seven weeks, and I know the three conditions on it well enough to know that the third of them is a sentence about me and not about the work, and I would rather it stayed on the page it is on than be said out loud in a room that has a keeper standing in it.**"

He put his left hand flat on the shut book to make the point and the hand did not close on it, and the room had seen that hand not close eleven times in a fortnight and nobody looked at it and nothing in the moment was about it.

"**And the half against, which is mine, and it is the only part of this I have any standing to say,**" he said. "**An arrangement that let me put my number in would be a better arrangement for this building and a worse one for me. I said that on the fifth day of last week about two lines of hours and I have now said it twice about three different instruments, and a man who has made the same argument three times in a fortnight has stopped arguing and started collecting, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that about me and not do anything about it today.**"

"**I noticed it in the second week of this building,**" from the step, and nobody improved on him and he did not improve on himself.

"**He is right, and it is the best thing anybody has said at that end of the trestle in a month,**" and she turned her slate over and put it face down on the step, "**and it is also a man asking a room for a thing with the word *noticed* in it, and he does not get it from me and it is not refused either, and I would like that entered as a third thing, which is that a keeper has declined to be the instrument of a man asking to be seen asking.**"

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, both of them, and put them down, and the two slates went into two pockets in the same minute, one into the apron of a woman on the fourth step and one into the belt of a woman from a counter, and the clerk entered that the slates had gone into two pockets and that she had not seen them again and that nobody added the two of them to anything.

Five today and two hundred and eighty-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and five and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in at the sixth bell under that, in the same line as the day's figure, with nothing over it, which is the only heading it has ever had and the only one it is going to get, and it is one hundred and thirty days and one hundred and thirty nights, and it is the thirty-ninth week of a sentence she has put in a room in every week of it. It is not going to be one hundred and thirty-one. One hundred and thirty is a number a person might notice, and four people in that anteroom could have noticed it, and none of them did, and the clerk did not offer it, and she entered that nobody had and that she had not, and that the two of those facts were the same fact.

The board was not walked, and the reason is that a man who reads it gave a fifth refusal in that room four hours ago and a board is not the place to go and find out what a man is holding.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a room spent the morning taking two things onto itself that nobody asked it to take, and a crew that is owed nothing and has been in the building a fortnight is still not asking, and a clerk is not going to hand five people a third unfamiliar instrument on a Wednesday.

The pot is three days and the seam gave nothing on Tuesday and nothing this morning, and the four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and there is no figure on this building's anteroom either, and after this morning there is not going to be one.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with nothing on it where two slates had been, and a chalk line on that table running under two documents which have lain in the same order, doing nothing about each other, since a Friday in the thirty-fourth week, and a wax head at each end of a stranger's sheet. Nine children were going down nine steps at the fourth bell in a room behind a wall with five and a half shores and a spreader under the head of the sill and the sixth place empty, and a bad repair on one face only, and a keeper's nine on the other side of two feet of it. And nowhere in the building was there a single thing that had been ordered, dated, posted, registered or headed.
