# Chapter 172 — Nothing Crosses Today

**Shut for the corridor on the seventh day of the thirty-third week, a Sunday, and shut by the standing arrangement alone, because the seventh day is not a crossing day, and the school did not run. A man who had been up nine steps for one day walked down them on the second morning, and looked at a piece of a school's own beech going across the head of a door, and did not put his hand on it.**

**A keeper can be given a door. A woman who keeps six things said no, and a keeper said no, and the door is still shut with no wedge in it, and the page where a person's hours would go is still blank.**

On a shut day the nine steps are the only stairs anybody uses and the anteroom is the only room that is warm, and the pump runs from the second bell to the fifth and the rest of the building is the sound of water finding its level.

Ilsa Norr had gone into the room at the kitchen-line end at the first bell with her hand on nine hearth stones in order, and had knelt at the ninth one for longer than the other eight took together, and had come out and said nothing, and Dalla Sen had written nothing, and that was how the day began and how a good deal of it went.

Jo Verrick read the wall at the first bell. It was a tenth reading and the string was where the string was, and the number was a quarter of an inch, and the two nothings from last week were still the two nothings, and she entered it in the place where those numbers go.

"**A wall that has stopped and started again is not a wall that has settled,**" she said, when Renn Vale asked her from the landing. "**And I am not going to say it has begun, and I will not say it has not, and I have got a piece of chalk and a piece of string and a habit of nine weeks and that is the whole of my instrument.**"

"**Then say what would be a figure,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Two nothings and a quarter of an inch in between. I am not putting those three in a column and I am not writing a word under them, and if I did, a person in a year would read it as a beginning.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that I have asked twice this week whether a thing may be joined to another thing and have said no twice, and that the two refusals are in a clerk's book and not on a page.**"

Then the man who had come up the steps the day before came down them, all nine, at the second bell, and stopped at the bottom, and looked up the corridor at the anteroom for a while before he came up it.

Marek Venn was in the anteroom and had been since the first bell with his own book open on his forearm, and he had been in it for three hours writing nothing, which was the first morning in thirty-three weeks he had spent in this building with a book open and no line on it.

The reason was worth putting down, because it was not a feeling and it would not survive a week.

The fifth term had been said yesterday and a second person had read it against the book and the two had been the same, and the clerk had entered a span of weeks in a hand, and none of it had required an engineer. He had gone over the four sentences of the survey in his head while the pump ran and found that he did not know a single one of them as a thing a person could say out loud, because every one of them had been about his hands.

He tapped the top of the trestle twice with the knuckle of the hand that will not close, out of habit, and got dry beech and pale end grain, and the brace over the door did the same thing he had always done when he wanted to know whether it was still there, which was to have a second opinion from his own knuckle before he asked anybody.

"**Say what you are looking at,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I am looking at a piece of beech and a shim and a hole in a stone that somebody cut nine hundred years ago,**" said Iven Tallo, from the anvil end, where he had been for an hour not carving anything. "**And I am looking at it the way you look at a thing you have made, which is the worst way there is.**"

"**And what does it do today, in front of three people who were not in this room when you made it.**"

"**It stands. The frame is a finger and two-thirds and it has been that since Wednesday. The shim is in and it is not bruised any more than it was. The cord is slack in the middle, which is what a spent line does and which I told a room on Monday and which nobody wrote down, and I have been looking at that slack for an hour and I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since Friday.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**I cannot tell you when it dies.**" He put the chisel down. "**I can tell you what it is. I have told you it is beech and it is four inches and it is a finger wide and it is carrying on its edges. And when its edges crush the head settles and the frame goes a twelfth of an inch out of true again, and a twelfth of an inch is the whole of what it was worth, and nobody standing in that anteroom is going to feel a twelfth of an inch for a long time.**"

"**Say the last part, because you have said it twice before this week and I want to know whether it is the same sentence.**"

"**It is the same sentence and I am going to keep saying it until somebody writes down a day, and the day is not going to be mine.**"

Renn Vale came down four steps and did not go to the landing.

"**There is a door at the far end of these steps with nothing wedged in it,**" she said, "**and I have said in this room three times that the answer to a door that has to be watched is a person and the person is me, and I have said the arithmetic of it out loud and I have not improved on the arithmetic. And there is a keeper in this building.**"

Nobody said anything, and the pump got two strokes in.

"**So I am going to say the obvious thing out loud so that it is in a room and in a book, and then I am going to say no to it. A door at this end of a corridor is a thing about a boundary, and an elected keeper of a boundary can shut a boundary, and the cheapest answer in this building this morning is for me to hand that door to him and be one person lighter.**"

"**And you are not going to do that,**" said Sefu Aran.

"**I am going to say why not, and the reason is not mine. Ask him.**"

He did not answer at once. He came up the four steps and stood in the anteroom the way a man stands in a room he has not stood in for a long time, looking at the brace and then at the frame and then at the door it is over.

"**Because I have been away,**" he said, "**and I do not know what that door is. I know it is a door and I know nine children come past it on three mornings in seven and I know there is nothing wedged in it. And everything else I would be taking on Saturday morning from a woman who has just told a room that she already keeps six things.**"

"**That is a good answer,**" said Renn Vale, "**and it is half of one, and the half that is missing is what you would have to do about it if you had said yes.**"

"**The half is that a keeper who comes back and takes the first thing anybody offers him is not a keeper. He is a change of face.**" He turned round. "**And this boundary has been getting on without one and doing it well, and the woman who told me that on Saturday is the reason, and I have not been in this room for a long time and I will not be the thing that puts a hand on it in the first week. If you want to give it to me, give it to me in a month, and ask me again in a month, and I will still say the same thing this morning.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that a keeper was offered a door in a room and refused it and gave the reason before he was asked,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the door is shut and has no wedge in it. And entered that nobody has been sent anywhere, and that the page at the low end is still blank, and still has three conditions on it.**"

Then the anteroom's terms were said, and the word was said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and the pump was attended, and the fifth term was in a room with a person in it who could keep it.

Afterwards Bel Ordry stood at the head of the nine steps looking down them and put up the question he had been carrying since the twenty-eighth week. The anteroom's second way out is a word. A carpenter said out loud in this room ten days ago that a person does not go out through a word, and nobody answered him, and his question is on the page.

Sefu Aran looked at the step rail and then at the page in the clerk's hand and then at the door, and took his time about it in a way that made three people in the room wait.

"**I have read the terms twice since Saturday and I could say them and I will not, because four of them were written by people who were in this room every day of the time I was not, and a man who comes back is not a man who reads other people's sentences back to them in his own voice.**"

"**Then say the one that is yours,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it.**" He did not look at her while he said it. "**That is the fifth, and I know it is the fifth because I have read the book, and I will not say the other four and the sixth stays unread, because the sixth was put on a landing by a woman and I have not met her yet and a term I have not heard in the room it was made in is not one I am going to perform.**"

"**That is the right answer and I am entering it,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And entered that a man read one term of six out of a book this morning and did not read the other five, and that the one he read is the one that names him.**"

"**Then you know what I was going to say,**" he said, "**and I am going to say it anyway because it has been on that page for ten days and I am the second person named in those terms now, and a man who has just come back is exactly the man somebody asks to answer an old question in a hurry.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**No.**" He put his hand flat on the stair rail. "**A carpenter asked that room a real question and nobody answered it and it is still on the page, and a man who has been away for a while does not get to answer it in his first week by being decisive. I am not that person. Somebody who is going to be standing in this room in ten years has to answer that, and it will have to be answered in a room, and it will take longer than a week.**"

"**Entered that he declined,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered the reason in his words. And entered that I have been asked four times this month, by people in this building, whether that question is going to be answered, and that I have said four times that it is not mine to answer, and that this does not change this week because a man has come up nine steps.**"

Marek Venn shut his own book on his forearm and found that he had been hoping, for about a minute, that the man would answer it.

The hoping had been the whole of the reason he had stayed in that anteroom for three hours with a blank page in front of him. A man who has been here every day of seven weeks, wanting a man who has not to say the thing that would let him say it, is not a small thing, and he was going to have to look at it later and not on a Sunday.

At the sixth bell the clerk put the day's number and the total into the book and read neither of them out, and walked the board at eighteen entries, and the count was read at one hundred and seven.

"**Three today and two hundred and twenty-six since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and forty-three and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and the counter is at sixteen and I am carrying it.**"

"**I entered those and I did not read them, and the reason is that today is the seventh day of a week in which nothing in this building crossed anything, and a figure I read out on a day like this is a figure the next person hears and cannot check. It will be checked on Monday and I will read it then.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one. The pot is nine days. The seam gave one yesterday. Jo's wall read a quarter of an inch this morning and that is a tenth reading and two of them are nothings.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and seven days and one hundred and seven nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and eight, and I have said in sixteen weeks running that I am going to keep writing it.**"

"**And I walked the board, and the reason is that there is a man in this building who could be asked a question about it, and I have decided to ask it on a day when there is nothing else in the room. A man who keeps a board has been in a bad month for eleven weeks. He is getting no reason from me this week, and he is going to be given none.**"

Tams Quill had the chalk in his left hand and his right one flat and open on his knee, and named no day, and went down the eighteen the way a man goes down a stair he has come down a hundred times.

"**Eighteen on it, and it is the same eighteen as Friday, and I am going to give it to you in the order it is in and not in an order that sounds better.**" He put the chalk at the first one. "**Eleven of them are nulls. Then a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace. Nine in the ordinary column, nine in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim's condition has not been met. The mark is not coming off.**"

"**The column is not headed, twelfth batch, and I am not giving a reason, and nobody has asked me, and I have had nothing for a week and I am not manufacturing something for the twelfth.**"

"**And I am going to say one more thing this morning and then I am putting the chalk down, and the thing is not about the column. A man came up those steps yesterday and he is the first thing that has happened in this building in a month that was not a wall or a piece of wood, and I have not decided what to do about it, and I am not going to decide it this week.**"

Dalla Sen read the eighteen entries out, read her own reading back against them, and entered that he had given no reason and that she had not asked, and entered that a man had said a thing about a man and not about a board and had not said what it was.

**The seventh day of the thirty-third week was shut, and a carpenter said out loud that he cannot tell you when a shim dies, and then said the same sentence twice and would not improve on it. A woman who keeps a corridor offered a door to a keeper, and a keeper said no and gave the reason before he was asked.**

**The school door at the far end of nine steps is shut and has nothing wedged in it, and the answer to a door that has to be watched is one person, and a woman who keeps six things was offered a seventh and said no, and nobody was sent anywhere and nobody has been sent anywhere all week.**

**And the anteroom's second way out is still a word, and a carpenter's question about it is still on the page.**

**A man who is now the second person named in that room's terms read one term out of six and would not read the other five. Then he looked at the word for a while and said he was not going to answer an old question in his first week by being decisive.**
