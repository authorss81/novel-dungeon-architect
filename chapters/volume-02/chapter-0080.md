# Chapter 80 — What This Hearthhold Will Not Do

Nobody in that anteroom had ever heard the question put out loud, and when Marek Venn put it there on the Wednesday morning of the fifteenth week, four people started to answer it at once and then all four stopped, which is how a room finds out that a thing has been true and unspoken for a long time.

"Has this boundary ever said out loud that it will not come for the middle gate's timber?"

The pump went round under the floor. The check hissed on its one note. And an anteroom with nine people in it discovered, the way it discovers things, by everybody failing to know.

"No," said Sefu Aran. "No, and I have been the keeper of this gate for the whole of the time and I have never said it, and I can tell you exactly why, and it is not a good reason and I am going to say it anyway because a room that cannot say the bad reason is a room that has not got a good one.

"It is because **if I say we will not come, I have said we cannot come.** And a keeper of a boundary cannot say that out loud, because the day the word *cannot* is in the air, a courier comes down the middle passage in three weeks with a charter and a bed and a man who can do something about it, and this Hearthhold has spent thirteen weeks refusing a man who can do something about it. **I have been holding a door shut by never saying what is behind it, and it has worked for thirteen weeks, and a twenty-two-year-old with a scrap of paper has just told me that the other side of that door has a woman at a counter who has said the same sentence three times and slower the third time.**"

"Marek," said Jo Verrick, from the frame, "you have found the thing. Now say the other thing, because I have been a woman with a board in a room for thirteen weeks and I can see the shape of what happens next and I do not want it to happen by accident.

"If the keeper says *we will not come*, then a piece of paper goes out of this building, in a hand, with a date on it, saying that this boundary declines four hundred paces of seasoned hardwood, which is **line eleven on the air engineer's page, and the only thing in this Hearthhold that has ever been a *yes* anywhere.** And the room that has spent a week being frightened of its own book is going to write down the one thing it has ever wanted, in the one form that can be carried out of here.

"And it will cost us the plank."

"It will cost us the plank," Sefu agreed. "I know. I am the keeper and I know what a line is and I am telling you that I would rather release four hundred paces of hardwood on a Friday in December than stand in a gate in a year and explain that I had let a man believe we were coming."

---

"Name it, then," said Renn Vale, from the upper landing, "as a keeper, in your own words, and the naming can be struck by anybody I name it to, and I am naming it to Dalla Sen and to the count."

Sefu Aran came off the wheel. He had not come off the wheel in front of a full room once in thirteen weeks and the room stopped what it was doing.

"**A water-carrying, every day in seven, twelve pails on a day the corridor is open and eight on a day it is shut, by named people, at named hours, at a walk, priced by the people who carry it, received by this boundary, kept by this boundary, and not by anybody above it.**

"And I am going to say the four sentences underneath it before anybody in this room starts feeling pleased, because I have listened to a week of this building being frightened of paper and I am the man signing it, and a keeper who signs a thing without saying what it costs is a keeper who is keeping a file.

"One: this is a record of keeping, and Dalla Sen said on Saturday in this room what that means, and she is right, and a person forty days away can pick this up. Two: it is a document and it is not a holder, and there is no name on it anywhere except mine and the clerk's and the names of the people who carry the water, and the names of the people who carry the water are not a holder, they are a rota, and a rota is a rota. Three: **it does not include the stair.** The stair is not in this work. The stair is not in any work. The stair is on Renn Vale's slate with seven terms and an eighth and it belongs to nobody and it is not being brought into a work by the keeper in the fifteenth week. Four: the day the slab under that lip turns, this work stops, and the lever is not touched by anybody, and that is not a term I am adding, it is the seventh term and it is hers, and I am putting it in this document because a document nobody can hold cannot be blamed and a document somebody can hold can be, and I would rather this one could be."

"Struck in nothing," said Renn Vale. "**Seconded, and I want one word changed, and the word is *received*, and I want *received by this boundary and not held by it*, because received is how a gift is written and I have watched this room spend a week learning the difference between a gift and a lock.**"

"Changed in the keeper's words and in the clerk's hand," said Dalla Sen, and read it out, and made the keeper read it out himself, which is now the sixth time in two weeks that she has made somebody read a sentence she has just written.

---

Wen Pask did the last carrying of the week and it was the first thing anybody in this Hearthhold had ever handed him, and they handed it to him by asking him, in a room, out loud, with the terms, which is a habit a man called Bel Ordry put into this building six weeks ago out of nothing and which had spread.

"Two crossings, the first bell and after the fifth, four pails in each and eight on the day, at a walk, and you are not on the lip and you are not on the plinth and you are not on the string, you are in the middle of four with a pail in your hand, and if somebody says the word you put it down on the stone and you do not put it down anywhere else."

"I will put it down on the stone," said Wen Pask, and he did, and it was a pail on a stone floor in a corridor two foot eight wide with nine inches of standing water in it, and he put it down without dropping it, and nobody in that corridor had to tell him twice.

And that is how the work was done. Seven days in seven. Twelve pails on an open day and eight on a shut one, and on the shut ones a day and a half of a shutter route and a day of a man's unpaid trade and a day of school, priced out loud in three hands. And the price went in a book in a kitchen and in a book in an anteroom and on a slate on a landing, and **the crew's column went up for the first time since the eleventh week, by two hours, on a named work, asked for by name by a keeper in a room, and the work was not the water. The work was reading the water's terms and its price at the top of nine steps and entering them in this book, which is what this crew is for, and which took two of them two hours, and which is the first thing anybody in this building has asked us for in three weeks.**

"Two," said Tams Quill, at the fifth bell, with the chalk in his left hand and his right in his lap, and the anteroom heard the number and then heard what came after it, which was the part he had been sitting on since the Sunday. "Two hours, and it is the first thing in three weeks that anybody in this building has asked us for, and I want the room to hear that I did not go looking for it and I would not have found it.

"And the number is nothing. Two hours. It is a quarter of what Isel Ferrow gave to a shutter route in one morning, and it is nothing, and it is in the ordinary column, and **it is the first line in that column in three weeks that has a person's name against it in somebody else's writing saying *I would like your afternoon*, and that is what the column is for and I have had it wrong in my head for eleven weeks and I found out on Sunday and you have all just watched me be wrong in public, which is the correct way for me to find out.**"

"Two hours and a named work," said Dalla Sen, "and the work is finished: yes, and I am entering the word *finished* for the first time in three weeks, and it is the water, and not the seat, and not the stair, and not the waist, and I want a person reading this in a year to see which of the four things in this Hearthhold got a word and which did not."

---

And then, on the Friday, in the cold, with the anteroom at ten including one litter and the door open and the pump attended and the air engineer able to stop it, Dalla Sen wrote the thing that is going to be the most-quoted page in this boundary's book, and she wrote it because a keeper named it and a clerk does what a keeper names.

She read it out and then she made the keeper read it out, and then she made the man who had come up the middle passage four days' walk with a piece of paper and a bad leg read it out, and he read it out twice, and it is in the book in her hand and in his and it is the only thing in thirteen weeks that anybody in this Hearthhold has ever been able to hand to a stranger.

"**This Hearthhold declines the middle gate's timber.**

"It will not come for the four hundred paces of seasoned hardwood held in the middle gate's store, and it does not ask that gate to hold it, and it does not ask that gate to send it, and it asks that gate to stop holding it and to put the water-lock in front of the people who live behind it.

"No date is attached to this, because a date would be a promise. **This is not a promise and it is not a refusal of a gift; it is a notice, and it is a notice given freely, by a boundary, in a hand, to a person forty days away, and it may be read by anybody, and it is the first thing this boundary has ever written down that it will not do.**"

Silence, and the wheel.

"That is going out on Monday," said Sefu Aran, "with whoever walks up the middle passage, and it is not going out with Wen Pask, because he is a man of this boundary and a message a man of this boundary carries is a message this boundary can be asked about twice."

"It goes with a courier," said Dalla Sen, "or it does not go, and I will not put it in a satchel."

"Then it goes with a courier, and a courier is four days up the middle passage and forty days on from the top of it to the guild file, and the middle's water-lock will have been dry in the spring by the time it lands, and that is a cost and it is the cost of doing this properly, and I have had it both ways in one morning and I have chosen."

And it was read out once more at the fifth bell on the Friday, and Dalla Sen read out the arithmetic of the pot, which was **thirteen days**, and the lip's range, which was a finger and a half to a finger and three-quarters and worse on a cold morning, and Renn Vale's seventh term, and the ninth line, and the blank on the review page with the two sentences on it instead of a name, and the kitchen's book, and the wash rooms with *kept, and nobody's work* in them, and Wen Pask's forty pails which had no heading and which he had asked the count to take off him and which the count had taken off him and put in the column with no heading, where a leg and a bundle and a half a hinge already were.

---

Two things were said at the sixth bell that Dalla Sen entered and that the room has not stopped arguing about.

Sefu Aran said the first.

"There is a term on the water-carrying and it is written on that page and it is the seventh term and it is not mine. And there is another term and it is mine and I am going to say it out loud and then I am going to ask for it to be entered as a refusal, because I do not know how else a keeper enters a thing he is not going to do.

"**This boundary can now do this work without the crew.** That is not a boast, it is a fact, and it has been true since the fourth day of the thirteenth week and the count worked it out on a Sunday and the count is right. The water is carried by people of this boundary. The keeping is in a book this boundary holds. The price is in three hands in this building.

"And the end of the crew's obligation is not that. **The end of it is that a person named by this boundary says so out loud, in the room, with the reason.** And I am the person who can say it, and I am not going to, and I would like the clerk to write down that I did not, and the hour, and the fact that I was asked by the count on Sunday in this room and did not do it in the same breath.

"And the reason is not the work. The work is finished. The reason is that **if I say it in the fifteenth week, then the crew are not needed in the fifteenth week, and this Hearthhold is being kept in the fifteenth week by people it has not asked, and I have a clerk's book in front of me with forty pails in it and eleven days of his own legs in it that has nobody's work in it, and I am not releasing five people into a building this cold on the strength of a single morning.** So: not yet. And I want *not yet* in the book in my own hand with a date on it, and the date is the first day of the month the pot stops falling."

"Entered and read out," said Dalla Sen, "in the keeper's words, in his own hand, with the time on it, and the count's Sunday is entered underneath it in the count's words, and the two are in the book next to each other, because they are the same refusal made by two men on two days with two different reasons and the room is going to have to read both of them in a year."

And Ilsa Norr said the second, at the sixth bell, in a room with ten people in it, and she said it standing up, and she had had it since the first day of the thirteenth week, when five women had unrolled a dowel in a room the crew were not in, and had not said it because she had a week of school to teach.

"There is one thing about that page in my store that nobody in this room has asked the right question about, and I am going to ask it now and I am not going to answer it, and I am not going to be the one who answers it.

"**There is not one name on it.** Nine rows. Nine rooms. Counts and conditions and six empty work columns and a *none* and a one at the bottom of four unwalked treads. And not a single name anywhere on the page. Not a holder, not a keeper, not a clerk, not a steward, not a builder, nobody. Whoever made that list kept nine rooms in a building for a very long time and **did not once write down whose rooms they were**, and he wrote down how many people, which is a harder thing to write down than a name, and he wrote down the weather, which is a thing you only bother about if you intend to be there for all of it.

"And in a fortnight this Hearthhold has written down, in a book in an anteroom, forty-one writes of the word water in a cook's hand and forty pails in a young man's and a woman's *no* at a door and a woman's *no* at a landing and a name against a date on a page for a post that was refused and a line that says *the wash rooms are kept and nobody's work* and a word — **maintainer** — that a clerk put in her own book on the Saturday of the last week and did not know she was putting it in.

"So we have two books now. The oldest one in this building has got nine rooms in it and no names. **The newest one has got a name against everything, including a name against a refusal, and it is a month old, and it is in a clerk's hand, and it can be picked up by a person who has never heard of us.**

"And I am not saying which of those is the better book. I have been in this room fourteen days and I have read both of them and I cannot tell you which one is the safer one, and I am not going to stand here and pretend that a woman of forty-eight who has run a school under a collapse for eleven weeks can look at nine hundred years of somebody's care and say *we have it right.*"

"Nobody in this room can," said Nera Oduya, from the top of three steps, and she said it quietly and she did not come down and everybody heard her. "That is the answer and it is the whole of it. **Nobody can.** And that is not a failure of anybody in this room, and it is going to be the argument for as long as this place stands, and it cannot be settled in a fortnight in a cold month by eleven people and a pot of thirteen days, and I would like it written at the fifth bell that the air engineer has said that, so that when somebody in a year asks me what the argument is about, I can read it out and be believed."

---

At the sixth bell the anteroom went back to being an anteroom, and the covers were lifted at the second bell and the fifth by a woman in a room nobody had walked into, and the stair was corded and shut and stayed shut because it is a stair and not a water, and the lip's range was a finger and a half to a finger and three-quarters and it was worse on a cold morning, and the third wedge at the waist was up a quarter of an inch and a bit, and the ground it was standing in had moved three times, and nobody had measured any of it because a boundary has three boots and a wedge, and the wedge is the finest instrument in the Hearthhold and it is telling them.

A person was still under that lip.

There was no post under it. There had never been a post under it. It was packed and not held, and the packing was a knee, and the knee would be Jo Verrick's if the slab turned while she was on the lever, and she knew that, and it was in a slate in a woman's hand, and it was read out at the fifth bell every day of the fifteenth week, and nobody had built anything to change it, and nobody was going to, not this month, not with a pot of thirteen days and a hand with two fingers missing on a bench in the anteroom.

On the shelf above the bench there was a knotted bundle of work cloth with something wrapped inside it, and it has not been identified, and it has not been opened, and it is not going to be, and nobody in this building has said out loud what anybody thinks it is, and the bundle has not been nearer to anybody's hand in a fortnight than the width of that anteroom.

In the pocket of the coat on the hook by the door there was a brass survey rule that had said nothing for seven weeks.

In a locked box in a school store, under two lengths of beech that are the props for a dressed-stone shelf, there are nine boxes of registers, and there is one sheet of paper rolled on a dowel, and the dowel came off a shelf in a locked room on the first day of the thirteenth week in front of five women and went back onto it the same hour, and the fourth row of that sheet is the anteroom, and the count in it is higher than any other count on the page, and the column beside it says *wet*, and the third column is empty, and that is the whole of what anybody in this Hearthhold knows about a document nine hundred years old, and it is not enough, and it is not going to be enough this month.

And on the last line of the last page of the boundary's book, in Dalla Sen's own hand, because the keeper named it and a clerk writes what a keeper names, there is a line and nothing else, and it is this:

*Reviewed on the first day of the month the pot stops falling. Named by this boundary: Dalla Sen, clerk. In the room. With the reason.*

Which is a threshold, and it is not the last one, and there is a whole month in front of it, and every day of that month has to be got to by eleven people with thirteen days of water and a bar that ticks in the cold and a page nobody has answered and a stair with a person under it that nobody has thought of naming because there is no post, and the post would be a person, and the person is Jo Verrick.
