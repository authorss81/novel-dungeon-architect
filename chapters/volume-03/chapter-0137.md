# Chapter 137 — A Hand Is Not A Person Being Written About

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the twenty-seventh week, a Tuesday, and the school did not run, and a man in a coat was doubted out loud in a room by a woman with a reason, and he gave a better answer than the one he had given in the twentieth week, and it was still not an answer, and a carpenter was asked about a mark and said yes and then said the thing that killed it.**

**A boy of fourteen read the plan's first column out loud in a room, and there are twelve numbers in it now, and he said all twelve and said nothing about any of them.**

Dalla Sen had the kitchen part of the plan in the anteroom at the second bell with the day's board on top of it, and she said what she was doing before she did it, which is that a column gets read when a number has gone into it.

"**The first column has two new numbers in it and a boy of fourteen is going to read them, and I am not going to tell him what they are and he is not going to be told, and the room is going to hear all twelve in one breath and the reason I want all twelve is that a number read on its own is a fact about a column and a number read in a list is a fact about a page."

"**Say it,**" she said to the top of the nine steps.

"**Ten. Four. Eight. Ten. Six. Nine. Five. Two. And a dash. And ten. And four. And six.**"

Nothing in the room filled in after it, and nobody asked him about any of the twelve, and one of the twelve is a new one and he said it in the same voice as the other eleven.

"**The second column,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Twelve conditions with no number and no day of the week on any of them,**" said Pell Roke. "**The fourth is the word *wet*. The tenth is a dash. The eleventh is new and the twelfth is new and neither of them has a number and neither of them has a day.**"

"**The third column.**"

"**Eleven lines filled and one empty,**" he said, "**and I am not going to say which one is empty because the man who keeps it has said in a room that he is not going to say, and I read numbers and that is the end of what I do.**"

"**The fourth column.**"

He looked at it, and it was empty, and it had been empty when he read it a fortnight ago, and he had said four words about it then that are on a page in a clerk's hand.

"**There is nothing in it.**"

Bel Ordry read the reason out of the plan and the strike with it, the modal in Renn Vale's hand struck out and Nera Oduya's replacement in her own hand, and then he looked at her, and she came down three steps to say the thing that had to be said about a sentence of hers that was already on a page.

"**That is my sentence and it is still mine and I have not improved it, and I want the modal to stay struck, and I want it struck in the same ink, and if anybody in this room ever reads that strike as a courtesy to me I will take it off the page myself.**"

---

**A man in a coat was doubted, and he did not know it was happening, and Dalla Sen entered afterwards that she had arranged it and entered the reason, which is the practice, and entered that he had not known and that that was on purpose.**

"**I want to ask you a question and I want to say first that I have arranged this and you have not been told, and that is not fair and I am doing it anyway, and the reason is that you asked to be suspicious of in a month in the nineteenth week and this is the month and nobody in this building has been suspicious of you, and I am the clerk and I am the person who reads the books and it had to be me."

"**The question is what you want the plan for. Not the reason you gave at the second bell, which was a promise instead of a hope, and I entered that and it is a good sentence and it is in your mouth. The other one.**"

Bel Ordry put his hand flat on the day-board and did not answer for a moment.

"**I want it for the day the boundary is not looked after by anybody who is standing in it,**" he said. "**There are forty-one people in this Hearthhold this week. There were thirty-two a fortnight ago. I keep a page with twelve rows and four columns and no name on it, and I keep it in a coat, and the coat is on a person, and the person is a man who is going to be gone one day and has not said when."

"**And if the day comes that this building needs a plan kept by somebody who is not in it, then a page with no name on it is a page that nobody can be given, and a plan nobody can be given is a plan that dies with the last person who understands it. I know that. I have said the opposite out loud in this room for six weeks and I have been right, and I have also been wrong, and both of those are in my own hand and one of them is older.**"

"**Say which one is older,**" said Renn Vale.

"**The wrong one is older.**"

"**Then that is a real answer and I am not going to improve it either, and I want it read twice.**"

Dalla Sen read it twice, and Bel Ordry said yes to both readings, and she entered that the keeper of the plan had been doubted in a room by the clerk with a reason, and that he had answered it, and that the answer had been entered, and that she had arranged it and had not told him, and that this was the first time in twenty-seven weeks she had put a fact beside another fact on purpose in order to see what a man would do.

---

**A name went onto the argument, and everybody in the room found out that it was a name and not a plan, and a carpenter was asked about a mark and said the thing about a mark that nobody had thought of.**

What they argued about was short, and it was a fifth thing nobody had put on the page: whether a row should carry the name of the person who keeps it, over and above the hand in the third column.

"**A hand is traced by about three people in this building,**" said Bel Ordry. "**A name is traced by anybody. And there is a form on a road that wants a name, and a woman from another office has told me in plain words that a page nobody can be given is a page that dies. So put a name on the rows. I am not asking for it to be public. I am asking for it to be there.**"

"**No,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Say why and let me have the whole of it this time and not three lines.**"

"**Because a name on that page is not a person on that page. It is a person pointed at. And this boundary has spent twenty-seven weeks learning that a pointed-at person can be sent for, and I have been the person who is pointed at twice in a month by people with permits and I am telling you what it feels like from this end and it is not a feeling, it is a fact about what an office does with a word."

"**And I will say the other half, which is against me, and Dalla has taught me to say both halves. If my name is on that page then a person in a year who wants to know how this building is kept can find out, and that is a good thing, and I have wanted that for a room I keep for a long time, and the reason I have not asked for it is not the principle. It is that I would have to be able to live in a room where a document says what happens to me and I do not know that I can.**"

"**Then a mark,**" said Iven Tallo, from the bench, without being asked twice. "**Not a name. A joiner's mark. I have put one on a frame in this building and I would put one on that page and it would say who laid the work and it cannot be copied by anybody who has not stood in the room and looked at my hands, and there are nine people in this building who would know it and nine is enough."

"**So yes, I would do it. I am a carpenter and it is the oldest honest thing anybody can put on a page and I would be proud to do it.**"

"**Then do it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am going to read out the objection and the objection is a sentence you said on the second day of this month at a table in this room, and I would like it said again by you and not by me."

"**A mark is a claim. A mark is portable. A man with a piece of chalk can put my mark on any wall in this building on any night of the week, and if it is on a page that a person four days up this passage is holding, then that person has a mark that somebody else put there, and the only way anybody can find out is by asking the man, and the man is not the only one who can be asked.**"

"**That is right,**" said Iven Tallo. "**I said it and I meant it about a wall. I did not think about it on a piece of paper, and a piece of paper is worse than a wall, and a man who puts his mark on a page is a man who has put his name on a page with a different alphabet on it. Strike it. I am not a saint and I was not trying to be. I offered a thing because it was mine and I did not look at it.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**the offer and the objection and the withdrawal, in a carpenter's words and compared against the original, and entered that no name and no mark is going on that page, and entered that the third column stands, and entered that the reason the third column stands is a schoolteacher's and not mine.**"

"**A third column is a person writing what they do,**" said Ilsa Norr, from the fourth step. "**A name in a column is a person being written about. Those are not the same thing and the difference is the whole of this building and it has taken twenty-seven weeks and two rows and a refusal to find out. A person is a person. A row is a claim. And I have been the person who keeps that room for twenty-seven weeks and I would like to go on being a person who keeps a room.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the six things and the day's number and the count, and the board was not walked, and she entered that she had not walked it and why, and nobody argued.**

"**Three today and a hundred and twenty-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and forty-five and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half."

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water at the low end has gone back into the channel since Monday and the water at the waist has not gone anywhere, and the third wedge at the waist is up a quarter of an inch and a bit and was not touched and nobody put a hand on the fill."

"**The count. Wen Pask is seventy-two days and seventy-two nights into a count that is his own.**"

"**And I did not walk the board today and I want the reason in my own hand and not improved. I did not walk it because I asked a man a question in this room at the second bell that I had arranged and had not told him about, and I sat here all morning knowing that I had done it, and a board is walked by a person who has nothing else on them.**"

---

**At the seventh bell a man with a hand that will not close found the one thing on that page that was a lie, and it was a lie by leaving something out, and he could not fix it, because the rule in this building is that a change to a room is written by the person who keeps the room, and he is not that person.**

He had the kitchen part of the plan on the anteroom step with a lamp on it and he read the second column of the tenth row four times and then went and got the clerk and made her read it out of the book where the original was.

"**Say the second column of the tenth row out loud, and say it out of your own book and not off the page, and then say what the book says under it.**"

"**The row says: the room at the kitchen-line end is opened when the clay under that sill has stopped shrinking, and the person who decides that is the person who laid it, and they have to be in a room together and both of them have to say it."

"**And your book says that on the first day of the twenty-fifth week at about the second bell, in a room, the woman who wrote that condition lifted it, and gave the reason, and the reason is on the page because she said it out loud twice and you read it out."

"**So the page has a condition on a room and the condition is not on the room. The page says *opened when* and the room has been open since a Monday. Anybody in a year who picks that page up is told there is a condition on that room and there is not one, and if the room is doing what that room is doing, which it is, with a cut in the floor and water in the joint and nine children in it three days in seven, then the page is telling them the room is shut and it is not."

"**That is the whole of it. And I wrote the recommendation that started it and I am not going to be the man who corrects a page about a room I do not keep.**"

"**Who does,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The woman on the landing wrote the condition and the man on the bench laid the stone and the man who held the stones is me, and the rule is the same either way: the person who keeps the room writes the change to it, and the person who keeps the second column of that row is the woman on the landing, and if I put a pen to that page I am a stranger on a page about a room, and a stranger on a page about a room is the thing that has been coming up this passage for six weeks.**"

"**So it goes to me,**" said Renn Vale.

"**It goes to you, and it is going to need somebody to state an objection on the page before anything goes on it, because the last time a condition came off that room it came off in a corridor in front of eleven people and one of them was a girl of nine, and I am not going to have that be the shape of the second time."

"**I am going to write it out as a revision. And I am not going to write it the way I would write it, because a man who writes a revision of a page about a room at the seventh bell on a Tuesday because he has noticed a thing is a man who is doing it on his own, and this page has had one of those in it already this month and it was a stove and it was the right call and it cost a child a foot."

"**Say the revision,**" he said to Dalla Sen, "**and then I am going to go and stand on a step and wait, because the next half of it is not mine and I know that and I have known it since a boy of fourteen read a number out loud in a room three weeks ago and it was not the number anybody thought it was.**"
