# Chapter 121 — The Hearth Stone

**The first day of the twenty-fourth week was shut for the corridor, and the school did not run, and the room at the kitchen-line end of the corridor stood with nine register boxes on its hearth stones and nobody in it, which made it the only room in this building with a full empty morning in it, and by the fourth bell it was the only room in this building with water coming up through the middle of its floor.**

Three separate bodies had told this boundary that it was fine in the last fortnight, and none of them had been wrong, and all three of them had been right about a thing that was not this.

**One.** There was a survey party's sheet on the wall of the anteroom, put there undated with the objection written beside it in this boundary's own hand, and the sheet said that this sector was stable, and the sheet was competent, and eleven men and a camp had gone into it, and the third thing on it said that the floor of the room at the school-door end was a use and not a fault, which was a better sentence than anything this boundary could have written about itself.

**Two.** There was a form with five lines on it in a coat, three of them filled in by a competent woman from an office that is not the Cartwright office, one of them a condition and one of them a limit and neither of them a name, and she had said out loud in a room that she would recommend it closed, and she had been right, and it was closed.

**Three.** There was a printed sheet on the anteroom table, face up, where a clerk had put it on purpose and had said out loud that she had put it there on purpose, and a woman from another office had read all seven lines of it in about eleven seconds, and it was still on the table.

"**I am not going to make a sentence out of those three,**" said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up. "**I have had four bells to make a sentence out of them and I have got the same one every time and it is the same one I got in the last week of the last month, and I am not writing it down, because the moment it is on a page somebody is going to quote it at a stranger.**"

"**What is the sentence,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**It is that three correct answers have come down four roads and every one of them describes this building in a way that can be carried off the hill, and none of them was about the floor of a room with nine register boxes on it.**"

Then Jo Verrick came up off the kitchen line with her lamp in one hand and her three-foot rule in the other and a slate, and she had been on her knees at the hearth corner for about half an hour, and she came up the nine steps and stood in the anteroom and did not sit down, which she does.

---

"**Say it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**I am not going to say it, because I have said it before in a worse room and I want to look at it again with a lamp and I have looked at it again with a lamp.**"

Then she said it anyway, which is what she does.

"**Four threads and a leaf, the same size as the leaf in the haunch, and the same colour, and the same warm, and it is coming up between the second hearth stone and the third where they bed on lime, and it is coming up into the joint, and there is a line of it across the flags in front of the hearth about the width of my hand, and the floor is dished toward the doorway so the line is not level and I have not worked out why it is not level.**"

"**Found,**" said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up, and she said it before anybody else had said the word, because that is the order this room has done things in for twenty-four weeks. "**Found, located, tested, recorded, and not before. Somebody say where.**"

---

**Located took Jo Verrick forty minutes and it turned out to be the ordinary thing and it turned out to be worse than the ordinary thing.**

The water in the school corner's low corner was traced in the first week of the twentieth week. A stone had been taken out of a course and put back in lime about a finger wide, and the bed joint ran out from under that floor, along a party wall, six feet, and came up into the haunch behind the anteroom's outer service door. **The whole of that was in the book in a clerk's hand with *from* struck out of it and *in a joint that runs to the haunch* written over it in the air engineer's own words.**

"**I cannot tell you it is the same joint,**" said Jo Verrick, from the floor, with the lamp beside her knee. "**And I am not going to try, and I want the clerk to write down that I said that before anybody in this room starts saying it for me. That joint is in a wall nine hundred years old and I have been in this building twenty-four weeks and I have never seen it and I am not going to draw it on a slate from a warm thumb.**

"**What I will say is that it is the same water. Same four threads. Same leaf. Same warm. And that it has come up here, which is four rooms down the corridor from where it came up last time, and I do not know how it got here, and I am not going to pretend to.**"

"**The whole of what the survey said was that this sector is stable,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The whole of what the survey said was that this sector is stable,**" said Jo Verrick, "**and the survey is right, and it was right on Friday, and a survey is a photograph of a thing at the moment a man looked at it and it does not come back when the thing changes, and the man who found it said out loud in this room that a sound sector is a sector nobody has a reason to fund, and I would like somebody to say the other half of that sentence to me now.**

"**I would like somebody to say the other half of that sentence now, and there is nobody coming, and the timber for a wall in lime is in a camp store four days up the middle passage with a man in it who said he would do it again.**"

Not one person said the other half. The pump's check came off and went again, and the sixth minute of the low end of the corridor came and went with nobody standing at the low end of it, because nobody ever stands at the low end of it.

---

**The rest of the morning was two available actions and no third one, and Marek Venn said them himself, standing, in a room, with the numbers not yet in it.**

"**I want to say the ordinary things first and I want them in a row, because I have watched this building find a third thing four times this month and every third thing was worse than the two that were on the table.**

"**There is no timber. There is no lath. There is no bronze. There is no mortar of the right kind in this Hearthhold and none is coming while we hold the charter we have signed. There are seven sound wedges and two dead sticks, and two of the seven are in a door head at the top end and three of the seven are at the waist and one is in the school corner's door and one is in the basket, and I have counted them this morning with Jo and I am not moving one of them for this.**

"**And that is everything we own. So there are two things we can do and I am going to say them in the plainest words I have got.**

"**One. We lose the room. The school stops in it, permanently, and nine children are taught nowhere, and the nine register boxes go back on hearth stones somewhere else, and the hearth corner goes to nobody, and the water comes up.**

"**Two. We close it.**"

Renn Vale came down one step, which she does when she is going to hold a term, and said, "**Say the other half of two.**"

"**We close it by packing the threshold. The floor in that room is dished toward the doorway and it has been dished toward the doorway since the ninth of nine children found it. The low place in that room is the sill. The water comes up under the floor and it has got nowhere to go in there and it will find the sill, and the sill is the lowest thing in the room, and a sill is a thing you pack.**

"**We pack it in stone and clay and we shut the door and we leave it alone while the clay takes up what it is going to take up, and clay shrinks as it dries and I cannot tell you how long that is because nobody in this building can tell anybody that, and the room does not get opened until somebody who packed it says it does not need to be.**"

"**That is a week,**" said Mikka Hale.

"**That is not a week,**" said Marek Venn. "**That is a figure, and you have just put a figure on a condition, and Renn Vale will take your chalk away.**"

"**I will take the chalk away,**" said Renn Vale, "**and Mikka is not wrong, and the figure came out of her because she is a cook and cooks count. And I am not going to write it, and here is the reason and it is the same reason I have given for five weeks: a figure on a condition is a day, and a day can be satisfied, and a condition that can be satisfied on a day is not a condition, it is an appointment. And this boundary is about to find out what happens when a room is shut for an appointment.**"

"**Say the two numbers,**" said Bel Ordry.

Marek Venn had been carrying them since the second bell and had not said them, and saying them was the whole of the day.

"**The room holds eleven when it is full and the room holds ten when it is not, and the difference is a cook, because the hearth corner in that room is the cook's job and the cook is in it. Eleven. That is a Friday afternoon's number and it is the number at the end of a school day and it is the number all night, and I have not got a way of making it smaller, and I have looked.**

"**And the number on the other side of the low end of that corridor, on a Friday, at the fifth bell, is fourteen. That is me and Jo and the fourth pair of hands at the pump and the cook and the boy who counts the pails and the four who carry them and Dalla and Bel and Nera and Renn and Isel and Ruk. Fourteen people who cross that low end four times a day, with a bearer at a lip that is packed and not held, over eleven feet of shaft, and nobody standing at the low end of it at any hour of any day in twenty-four weeks.**

"**The higher number is on the other side of the door. That is the sentence and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to hurry it, and I have been in this building twenty-four weeks and I have watched this room make nine decisions in a fortnight and be right about six of them, and I would like everybody in here to understand that I have thought about this since the second bell and I have not found the third thing.**"

---

**And then the air engineer was asked for a piece of work, and did it, and it was four minutes, and she was not consoled by anybody afterwards because nobody has learned to console her and she would object to it.**

"**Ask me for a piece of work and not for an opinion,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps, "**and I have said that in this room before, and I am saying it again because two of you have been asking me for opinions all morning by standing near me and breathing.**"

Dalla Sen had the chalk up and read the terms out before the question was asked, which is now what this room does.

"**Nera Oduya is asked one question about the air in the room at the kitchen-line end: where does it come from, where does it go, and what happens to it when the door is shut and nine people and a teacher and a cook are in it. She is not asked whether the door should be shut. She may say no. The person who can be told no is Renn Vale, and the person who may stop it afterwards is Renn Vale and the air engineer, and the two of them can be stopped by each other. And I am entering now that I have said the terms out loud and that I am not going to improve them after.**"

"**Go on,**" said Renn Vale, and corrected nothing.

"**One door, wider than the anteroom's, with a sill in it about nine inches deep and eleven feet of laid stone, which is the best sill I have ever seen in a building this age and I have not seen it in twenty years and I am not going to pretend I have.**

"**The room has no mouth. There is a hearth in it and a cold hearth is not a vent, and a hearth lit off a fire somebody brings in from a kitchen at the other end of this corridor is a fire in a room with one door, and I have written that sentence in a book in this building before and I am writing it again.**

"**So the air in that room comes in under that door and goes out under that door, and the two are the same gap, and in a cold month the net is very nearly nothing, and I have no figure for it and I am not going to invent one this morning, because the only figure anybody has ever taken for that door is a number on a page, and a number on a page is not an air measurement and I have said so in this room twice.**"

"**Then what do you know,**" said Marek Venn.

"**That I know four things and I have not got a fifth, and I am going to give you all four and the fifth is what I will not say, and you may not ask me for the fifth in a room, and if you do I will strike it out of whatever I write next.**

"**One. A door shut on nine children and a teacher and a cook is a room with no air in it in a cold month, and I say that as a woman who keeps air and not as a woman who wants a door shut.**

"**Two. I do not know whether it matters tonight or tomorrow, because that depends on how long it is shut and nobody will give me a figure, and a condition with no figure on it is the best thing this Hearthhold has ever written down and it is also the thing that makes my job impossible.**

"**Three. If that door is shut and I am asked to open it, I will not be able to. I have said so out loud in a room in advance, on purpose, so that it is on the page before anybody needs it, and I am entering that I said it now and not when it is asked of me.**

"**Four, and this is the one I would like a room to hold on to: this boundary has a rule and the rule is that a change to a room people use is written by the person who will keep it. So the door is not mine to shut and the air in that room is mine to say. Those are two different hands and I am glad of it, and I have been glad of it for twenty-four weeks, and I would like it entered that I have said that and I am not withdrawing it.**"

Dalla Sen read all four out, twice, and Nera Oduya said yes to the second reading, which she does not always do.

---

**The last thing the day did was put a hearth stone back where nine children had worn a line into the floor, and it was a small thing, and it took eleven minutes, and it is the only thing in this building that nobody has cost anything to do today.**

Of the nine hearth stones, the second is the one that matters. They are the light in that room; the room's whole advantage over every other room on this run of corridor is that the light comes off them and the floor is dished toward the doorway for the same reason the school corner's was dished toward its door, which is that nine children and a teacher walk in and out of it fifty times a day and go out the same way they came.

"**The lime under that hearth stone is gone,**" said Jo Verrick, at the fourth bell, on her knees again with the rule laid across the bed. "**I have got my thumb in it and I can put my thumb to the second joint of my forefinger in under the bed of it, and a hearth stone bedded in nothing is a hearth stone with a floor under it, and that is not a fault and it is not a use either. It is a thing that has been wet for six weeks and has been dried and wetted and dried again by a floor nine children warm, and the lime has gone soft and let it go a bit at the bed and the stone has settled into the gap and there is now a hole at the low side of that bed about the width of two fingers.**

"**And I am telling you that in a room with nine register boxes standing on nine hearth stones, and I am telling you that I would not put a cat on that bed this month, and I am telling you that there is a hearth corner in that room with a fire nobody has lit in it for six weeks because a cook will not light a fire with nine children on a hearth-stone floor, and I have wanted to light it every morning and have not.**"

"**Then do not light it,**" said Ilsa Norr, from the fourth step.

"**I have not lit it,**" said Mikka Hale, "**and the reason I have not lit it is a good one and I would like it kept: nine children sit on those stones at a table every school day and I will not have a fire lit under a floor they are on, and that is not a superstition, that is a cook who has been in a bad room.**

"**And the stones have moved anyway, which is the part I am not going to be proud of at the end of a sentence.**"

---

**And at the sixth bell the clerk read the six things, and one of them was a number, and the number was the day's increment and the total, and the pot was ten, and the count in the column with nothing over the top of it was fifty-six.**

"**Four today and seventy-five since the keeper signed,**" said Dalla Sen, reading her own hand, "**and the boundary is at a hundred and ninety-two and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile and they have reconciled for eleven weeks.**

"**The pot is ten. It has not gone up since the fifth day of the nineteenth week and I am going to keep saying it that way, because the day I put a figure on how long it has held is the day a cook in this building has got a record instead of a number.**

"**The corridor is shut and the school is not running, and the school runs in that room tomorrow and on Friday because the arrangement says it runs on the three open days and a lamp and a wet floor are not a closure, and I have been waiting all day for somebody to try to make them one and nobody has.**

"**And there is no row for that room on any page in this building. I noticed it on the first day of the twentieth week and I have entered the noticing twice now and I am not going to say anything else about it.**

"**And a man of twenty-two is fifty-six days and fifty-six nights into a count that is his own, and he is at the kitchen line counting pails, and he is the only person in this Hearthhold who has never once been asked to be anywhere.**"

Marek Venn went down to the foot of the nine steps after that and stood where he has stood every evening of this book with his left hand open on his knee, and it did not close, and the count was in the room again under the pump's check, low and uneven and counted, like a thumb run along the edge of a plate, and he knew what it was and knowing it did nothing at all.

**In twenty-four weeks he had said out loud in a room, more than once, that this boundary was out of ordinary means. It had now said it twice about two different rooms, and the second time it was said about a room with nine children in it and a hearth corner in it and nine register boxes on its stones, and the only thing anybody in this building could pack that threshold with was the same clay that the anteroom's frame is packed with, and the anteroom's frame is a finger and a bit out of true and going a finger a week in this cold.**

**And a competent man with a sheet had told the guild that the ground was behaving itself, and the ground was behaving itself, and being told twice in a month by two good offices that the thing you are standing on is sound had never once before turned out to mean that the thing you are standing on would be looked at again.**

He had been warned the first time he understood that, by a man with a sheet, in the anteroom, on a Friday. He has had no warning at all this time, and nobody is coming, and there is a word for a document that has found a thing correct and gone away.
