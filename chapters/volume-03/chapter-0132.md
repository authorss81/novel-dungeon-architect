# Chapter 132 — The Terms Of The Asking

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the twenty-sixth week, a Tuesday, and the school did not run, and the water in this Hearthhold came up in three places at once, and a clerk asked five people for a piece of work in a room with the terms out loud and a person named who could be told no, and the three she could not ask were named with the reason for each before she asked anybody anything.**

**One crossing opened that corridor and shut it again, and the opening is in a clerk's hand and it is four lines long.**

Dalla Sen had the ask written out on the back of a day-board at the first bell and read it out for herself first, because that is what happens in this room, and then she stood on the anteroom step with it and did not hand it to anybody.

"**I am going to ask five people for three hours of a piece of work, and I am going to say the whole of it before I ask any of them, and I am going to name the three I am not going to ask and the reason for each, and the reasons come first, because that is the half of the terms nobody in this building has ever bothered to say out loud."

"**Leena Orr. She is this side of the waist and the work is the other side, and she is on a sling and takes no weight, and the reason I am not asking her is not her shoulder. It is that there is no version of this work that is on this side of the waist, and I am not going to walk to a room and ask a woman to come and look at a thing that is not where she is."

"**Pell Roke. He is fourteen and he is on a bench at the top of the nine steps with a crutch and a splint and his ear against the stone, and the reason I am not asking him is that he is fourteen. That is the whole reason and I am going to say it out loud at that speed and I am not going to improve it."

"**Tams Quill. His right hand is open and flat and it is not a working hand and nobody has let it be one. And he keeps the water record and the count, and both of those are about to matter more today than the work does, and a man cannot be in a channel and at a sixth bell."

"**Those three. And the two I am asking are Iven Tallo and Marek Venn, and I am asking them in a room, and the terms are these."

"**Three hours. Two of one and one of the other, and I am not going to decide which is which, and the man who is doing two of them may say so out loud and name the split himself, as he did last time, and I would like that on the page again."

"**The work is a cut in the floor at the far end of the corridor, under the hearth corner, to take the water off the bed of the ninth stone and away to the sump, and it is stone in clay and a lump of the ninth hearth stone's own bed is not going anywhere. Nobody is to put a bar into that floor. There is no timber and there is no bar that is not a dead stick and this building has one of those left."

"**The corridor is shut. To do it, the corridor is opened for the length of one crossing and shut again after, and the crossing is a walk and a bearer on the fifth, and Renn Vale goes first over and last back, and the school does not cross and nobody who is not on that list crosses."

"**And the last of the terms, and it is the one I am going to say twice. This is three hours and it is an hour and it is a wage. It is not a favour. A favour is a loan with no date on it and this boundary is not going to be owed a favour by anybody, least of all by the two men I am standing here looking at."

"**Renn Vale is the person who can be told no. If she says the corridor does not open, the corridor does not open and the work does not happen and I will enter that it did not happen and nobody will be blamed."

"**Anybody can say no and nobody will be asked why.**"

---

**Water had been coming up in three places since the first stroke and by the second bell the room at the kitchen-line end had it between the second and third hearth stones and up the joint at the skirting on the door side, and the sill was holding, and everybody who could be told was told at the same time.**

Jo Verrick had a lamp and a rule and no opinion about what would happen next, which is her condition and her rule, and she said the numbers from the doorway and did not come in.

"**Four threads at the seam behind the anteroom door at the first bell. That is two a day that this building has been taking out of a lime joint in a haunch since the fourteenth week, and I have said maybe three since, and today it is four, and I would like it entered that I said four before the number settled and not after."

"**Two jars. And I have been standing in the haunch with the pot in my hand and I want to say the thing that goes with it: I could take five out of that haunch today and nobody in this building would find out for about a week, because the haunch is nine feet of wall behind a door and there is no mark on it and no line and nobody has ever looked at it but me and I look at it every morning."

"**I am telling this room that I could. I am not doing it, and I am not going to, and I want the sentence in the book in my own words so that if I ever do, the sentence is here and it is mine and not somebody else's.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in her own words, and entered that she has not taken five, and entered that nobody in this room has asked her to, and entered that I am not going to ask her to, and entered that a pot at ten days is a thing this building decided and not a thing anybody is starving for, and I am not going to have the two sentences in the same mouth.**"

---

**Iven Tallo asked for the split to be named before he said yes to anything, and he said the sentence about a hole that the Quiet Hands had not invented, and Dalla Sen read the terms out again to the room because that is the practice and it is now a decision on a page in her hand.**

"**Two and one, and I want the two to be mine, and I want the reason said out loud and not in a book."

"**I am a carpenter and this is a cut in a floor and there is nothing in it that is a mystery. The two hours are mine because the second hour of a cut is the hour where you find out whether the first hour was any good, and a man who has not been in the room for the second hour does not know whether the first hour was any good."

"**And here is the thing I have been not saying since Friday. Two sheets of cheap paper came out of a channel at the low end of this corridor weighted with a dead stick, and there is one sentence on them that a person in this building wrote themselves, and it is a sentence about a hole. And that hole is the hole we are standing nine steps above, and it has a grate that is off it and two flags over it and a length of the crew's own frayed line over it, and the line is spent."

"**So I am going to say the sentence the room is not saying. That hole is the only thing in this building that has been attacked, and it is attacked with what we have got, and nobody has been caught, and the water is coming. I would like the crew asked for this work in a room with a person named, and I would like it entered that the man who lays the packing in a door is asking to be put on the other side of that door for a piece of a day.**"

"**Say the part that is hard,**" said Renn Vale.

"**The hard part is that I was the man who would not pack the sill two weeks ago and asked for my no in my own words, because a man who is the only person who knows what is under a room gets asked about that room by somebody four days up a road. That is still true. Nothing about today makes it less true. I am asking to go and stand on the other side of a shut corridor, in a room with a hole in the floor under a stone, with water coming up, for two hours, and I would like the no still to stand afterwards.**"

"**It stands,**" said Renn Vale. "**And I am going to open the corridor for the crossing and I am not doing it because he asked me. I am doing it because a room with a packed sill and a hole under a stone and four threads of water in a joint is a room that will be a room nobody is in by the fourth day of this week, and a room nobody is in is the thing I have been frightened of since the first day of the twentieth week and have not had a name for."

"**Say the crossing, Dalla, and say the list, and then go and stand where you are going to stand.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**the terms out loud, the three that could not be asked with the reason for each before the asking, the two that could, the split named by the man doing two hours of it, the corridor opened by the person who keeps the terms for the length of one crossing and shut again after, and the person who could be told no named, and entered that a carpenter asked to be put on the other side of the door he would not pack and asked for his no from last time to stand afterwards, and entered that it stands.**"

---

**They crossed at the second bell, and the corridor was open for about a quarter of an hour, and the crossing took eleven minutes and a fifth at a walk, and nobody put a hand on the fill at the waist, and the third wedge there is up a quarter of an inch and a bit and was not touched.**

Not one person watched them go who was not asked to, and nobody made a thing of it, because this boundary has spent five weeks learning that a man walking a corridor is not a post.

At the far end, Iven Tallo got his knee on the floor of the room at the kitchen-line end and put his thumb in the joint at the skirting and said the number and said it out loud in a room with a schoolteacher and a cook in it, which is the practice and has been a decision on a page in a clerk's hand since the third day of the last month.

"**A finger and a half, at the low side of the third stone, and a finger under the second, and it is going along the joint under the hearth corner and not across it, which means the water is coming from under the floor and not out of the wall, and I have got about a week before it is under the bed of the ninth stone and I do not know what happens then and neither does anybody in this room."

"**And I want the sill said out loud while I am here, because the two of us put it in and it is the only thing in this building that is holding this floor up, and it is holding because the clay has gone most of the way to where it is going, and it is not holding because of us."

"**Iven. The joint is in the sill and the sill is in the door and the door is not wedged, and if I put my thumb in that joint this evening and it has gone further than it went this morning then I say so out loud in a room and nobody argues with me about it.**"

"**Then that is what happens,**" said Marek Venn, from the doorway, "**and I would like it entered that the two people who laid it said it in that order in a room with the people in it, and that I have no condition of my own on it and have never had one and am not going to be given one.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the six things were read out and the board was walked, and the count gave the seventh reason and it was worse and it was not about a word.**

Tams Quill had the chalk in his left hand and his right one open and flat in his lap where it lives, and he read both columns, and named no day, and gave no reason for not naming one.

"**Four today and a hundred and thirteen since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and thirty and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and both columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half, up three, and it is the second time this column has moved since the obligation was discharged and both times it moved because somebody said the terms out loud in a room and named a person who could be told no."

"**Fourteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, and a row. Nine in the ordinary column and five in the one with nothing over the top of it, and the claim's condition is not met, and the mark is on this board and is not a null and is not a reading."

"**Seven batches.** I refused six batches running to head that column and on the third day of the last month I gave the best reason I had and it was that a heading would put a room under a word, and a word is portable and rooms are not. I said I would not improve on it and I am not going to."

"**This one is worse and it is not about words at all."

"**A heading says what goes in a column. The moment it says what goes in, then a person can work out what would have to be left out to make room. And this column has been making room for twenty-six weeks. Eleven nulls is not a list of nothing. Eleven nulls is a list of things I could not file anywhere else in this building. A leg. A bundle. A man's count. A hinge. A wage. A line somebody refused. A mark that is not a mark. A row with a number in it. And now a plan nobody in this building is going to sign."

"**A heading would turn that column into a room with a door in it, and the first thing that happens in a room with a door in it is that a man stands in the doorway in a bad month and says there is no room for that in here."

"**And I will say the worst part, because I have been asked for worse and worse and this is the worst one. The worst version of a heading on that column is not a clerk four days up the middle passage. It is a person in this building. In a bad month. With a heading that says what that column is for, deciding that a room is not a wage.**"

"**Then what has the unheaded column got,**" said Mikka Hale, from the pump frame, "**that a headed one has not?**"

"**One thing, and it is the only thing, and I have been the man who says one thing for seven batches.**" He put the chalk down against the board, which he does at the end. "**It has got the doorway in it. And I am not going to be moved off that by a person asking me a fair question twice.**"

Dalla Sen read the fourteen entries out and read her own reading of them back, and the corridor was shut behind her, and the flags over the hole at the low end had a foot of water round them that had not been there at the first bell, and the pot was ten, and a man of twenty-two was sixty-seven days and sixty-seven nights into a count that was his own, and the clerk read it out in the same breath and read nothing else around it.

---

**And what came back up the corridor at the third bell, with a carpenter's knee in the joint and water round the flags, was that the one repair that would have kept that floor dry needs a wall in lime, and the wall in lime is four days up the middle passage in a camp store with a man in it who said he would do it again.**

There was no answer to that in the anteroom and nobody pretended otherwise, and the boundary went on being a building with a packed sill and a propped hearth stone and a grate that was off, and a strip of crate board under a girl's foot, and a plan with ten rows and a ten in the newest one that nobody had counted.
