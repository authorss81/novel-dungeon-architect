# Batch Summary — Volume 02, Batch 0002

## Movement

- **Chapter 61** is the hand and the price. Tams Quill's right hand opens on the anteroom bench in the eleventh week, a month from a day in the seventh, and opens **flat and stays open for as long as he asks** — twenty-two seconds, because somebody in the room would otherwise have counted. The room does the arithmetic on its own and arrives at the seat. **Jo Verrick makes him stand at the other end of the room so she can watch his face while she refuses him the seat**, and the refusal is not *the hand is not ready* — it is *I will not cut a repair out of the one thing in this building that is certain to be wrong in four months*, which is her own September sentence applied against the room's relief. She then prices the work in her own hand, line by line, read as it is written, and line nine is the batch's first job: **I do not have the wood.** **Sefu Aran names the work and strikes half of his own naming aloud**, because a keeper who strikes half his own sentence and does not say so is a keeper who has learned to write.
- **Chapter 62** is the search, and it is a walk with four stops, not a summary. The cold pushes up the nine steps and **the shaft has stopped being a chimney — the dyed cloth sank**. Iven Tallo states the search's rule before anybody asks him: *the best wood in a building is almost never the wood anybody can have, because it is the piece that had to survive, and the piece that had to survive is the piece somebody needed to be able to forget about.* Then three refusals with reasons better than hardness and one refusal that is a gift nobody will be thanked for. **Bel Ordry says the sentence the batch turns on** — a man who gives something to a boundary becomes a person who can be asked what else he has, and he has held nothing for eleven weeks and will not trade that for a floor. **Ilsa Norr refuses the school store and then says she would have said yes if the shelf had been sound.** **Renn Vale will not name the wash rooms on a list**, because the one person who could answer for that room has a mother in the same hole and a list would be the first thing anybody has ever asked her for. And the last stop is the best timber in the Hearthhold — the pump's own bed, thirty years in standing water — which **cannot be cut because the housing bolts into it.** Tams reads Oren's carried column out loud **once**, at the moment it decides something. Sefu Aran then says the sentence that becomes the week: **a shortage of reach, not a shortage of planks.**
- **Chapter 63** is the first real cold, and it kills the instruments. Jo cannot tell a thin hiss from a full hiss, **because a hole hisses thinner in cold air**, and her own two-note vocabulary from the tenth week is dead and she has been mishearing it for three days. The pump goes on **attended**, by hand, stopped on a water line chalked on the frame by Leena Orr. Nera arrives from the other direction and says what the cold has done to the stair: **for six minutes in forty the shaft is a sink and not a vent, and that has never been the safe part — it was only ever the part where nothing moved because the shaft was breathing out.** Bel Ordry's bar comes back with three ticks and a slide on the third, **and the same tick in two other places on the same cold wall**, and Nera refuses to write it as a knock and refuses to write it as nothing and says out loud that **her own refusal and her own method are standing on opposite sides of the same wall.** Marek refuses to write a null in the cold, Tams opens a column with no heading, and **the sixth item — somebody at the low end of the corridor in the six minutes — is offered to nobody.**
- **Chapter 64** is the guild, and the somebody who is about to be a criminal is **the man who has spent nine weeks teaching eleven people why nobody should sign a guild line.** **Sabra Kest** walks forty days with a reply and a Form 9-C with three boxes and a person to fill one, and tells Iven Tallo in front of everybody, before he can find it in a file, that his suspended bond can be reinstated on a single document. He refuses, and the reason is the best argument in the batch: **a licensed man who signs a route does not stop being the man who signed it when he leaves the room, and there are five of us who are leaving.** **Renn Vale reads her own seventh term out to a stranger and then refuses to put the fault in the one field that could have put a person in the boundary's corner**, and the assessor writes *none reported* about a condition she was told about and says she will be asked and will answer the same way. The route goes down nine steps **unattended**, which is simultaneously the best thing that has ever happened to that boundary and the reason nobody will ever help it. And Marek admits in his own words that he filled one of the three boxes in his head in four seconds with no name in it.
- **Chapter 65** is the review and the reckoning. Five items, fifty minutes, the shortest review in eleven weeks. The throat, the check, the six minutes, the form, and **the waist, which Mikka Hale puts on a list it was not on: the fill went down a hand's breadth in the thaw under nine children's feet, and the packing that would have gone into it went out of the anteroom's four foot eleven and into a shore's head six weeks earlier — both lines in Tams Quill's column, on the same page**, and he says *that is mine* and asks for his name under it and asks nobody to be kind to him about it. The water is **read out loud for the first time in eleven weeks** and asked for a column of its own with no heading, because it is a pair of shoulders. The crew's **forty-four and a half** goes up for the first time since the second week, and **not one of the six hours was work anybody in the boundary needed done**; the boundary's goes to **eighty-one and three-quarters**, and the list that makes it reconciles exactly, because Tams puts **half a day of his own into the stranger's day — he was in that anteroom with a stranger's form on the bench and said no whole sentence about it** — and strikes Mikka Hale's pot out of it as money and not a day. And then **Mikka Hale asks the volume's question a second time in a harder form** — *who keeps the Hearthhold rooms on the day there is no work left to do in them, and who notices, and how does anybody find out* — and nobody answers. Sefu Aran **schedules the spring conversation with a date, an hour, a room and two people**, and Nera Oduya gives the hand-over in four sentences and says the person she will name **will not be in the room** and that she will go and stand at their door on the third day.
- **Chapter 66** is the escalation, arriving through Batch 0001's own machinery, in an ordinary afternoon. The pot gives twelve pails on the open days and eight on the shut ones, and Mikka is **not sorry about the four**. The crossing gives **two a day, three days a week, at the first bell and after the fifth**, because a stair period may not fall on a day the school runs. **Jo Verrick applies her own ninth-week rule against herself** and asks for the sentence that forbids the water being on the route *instead of* the people to sit on the same line as the one that forbids it. ## Relationship, thematic, and power change

Marek's Chapter 40 refusal to mark a stone in a mechanism he does not own is answered in this batch by **a man who does not take the chalk out of his pocket over a quarter inch that is inside somebody else's number**, and by a chapter that ends with a slate put down blank side up because the record belonged to a woman at a frame. His growth is not insight. It is that he has worked out what he is for, which is to ask a question out loud in a corridor and be willing to be told no — and the batch's last line is him doing that to Jo Verrick about a post that Nera Oduya has left deliberately blank.

**Iven Tallo's arc turns.** He taught this room for nine weeks that a page in a file is how a Hearthhold ends up owned, and in Chapter 64 he is offered a reinstated bond and refuses it, and in Chapter 68 he argues for the charter that bond would have come from, and he says out loud, before anybody can say it for him, that both positions are the same sentence and he has not changed his mind about anything. He is the first person in this building to be offered the thing he wants by an honest person in front of witnesses.

**Bel Ordry's refusal to be thanked is the batch's thesis arriving through a man with nothing.** A favour is a lock, a gift is a claim, a courier's order is a second lock on the same door — and by Chapter 67 the woman from the wash rooms has been pushed into a position where the only thing keeping her safe is a rule she wrote herself, and by Chapter 69 she has to say out loud that the reason she gave for shutting the route is now the condition she personally stands in. **And Mikka Hale's answer to all of it is a book of favours in a cook's hand**, which is the first instrument in eleven weeks that makes a cost legible without turning it into a holder.

Nera Oduya's refusal to publish a rate is no longer an abstraction. In this batch it cost her a plank of certainty, three of four instruments, and a wall she cannot read, and she has said out loud that **her own refusal and her own method are standing on opposite sides of the same wall** — and she has kept the watch, and refused to name a person for the water, and gone to somebody's door. **The volume's argument is now concrete and it is not a document: a public thing has to be maintained by people who can reach its materials, and a Hearthhold that cannot get a plank is a Hearthhold that cannot keep a charter, and this one cannot get a plank because the corridor is two foot eight wide over an eleven-foot shaft on ground that has moved.**

**No power stage is granted.** Marek has seven written nulls and nothing usable, one null he refused to write, and a column with no heading that holds a one and a temperature. The route, the water, the check, and the waist are all worked by people who live in the building, and the crew's hours went up for the first time in eleven weeks and not one of the hours was craft.
