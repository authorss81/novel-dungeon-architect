# Chapter 152 — Two Lines And A Load

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the thirtieth week, a Tuesday, and the school did not run, and a carrier came up the middle passage on foot with a paper in his hand and said what he was before anybody was asked, and Dalla Sen argued five of the seven lines out loud before anything was marked, and an air engineer struck a column and a room out of a printed schedule in nine seconds and gave no reason at all.**

**A load and a form are the same instrument, and the room established the facts and the two available actions and did not choose, because a choice about beech on a Tuesday is a choice about a schoolroom and the person who keeps the schoolroom was not in the anteroom and was not to be asked about it in a room she was not standing in.**

Paper went onto the anteroom table and the carrier's hand stayed on it, the way a man keeps hold of a thing he has carried four days' worth of road on, and he gave his name and his office and gave neither of them twice.

"**I am not a courier and I am not carrying. I have brought a paper up this passage and I have come up on foot because there is a cart at the middle gate and the cart is not coming up it. That is the whole of the news and I would rather it went into your clerk's book in that order than in the order a man would put it in.**"

"**Say the order a man would put it in,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**That there is timber coming.**" He took his hand off the paper. "**There is timber coming. That is the order a man would put it in and I have put it in first so that everybody in this room hears it from me and not from somebody with a tally.**"

Dalla Sen said what she was going to do before she did it, which is that everything a stranger puts on that table gets read out in this anteroom within a day, and she would rather it got read out by the people who keep the rooms.

Then she read it, and it was seven lines, and it was not the market form, and it was the same two lines in a different hat.

"**One. The place of origin of the load. Two. The day the load leaves. Three. The number of pieces. Four. What is carried. Five. A name. Six. The person responsible for the load. Seven. A countersignature by a person of that office.**"

"**And the word in it for not doing the carrying,**" she said, **"is *attended*, and I have got that in my hand in a clerk's words about a water-lock notice at a second gate in the fifteenth week, and this is the fourth time a word this building reads four hundred times a day has turned up written by somebody else in a way that means something else, and I am not going to improve on what I said about it the first time.**"

"**Then argue it,**" said the carrier, **"because I have been told to wait and I would rather wait on a room than on a gate.**"

She argued it. She said out loud, in the anteroom, in front of a man who had carried it four days up a road, that lines one, two, three and four were facts about a load and facts about a load are a thing a person can be told at a gate and a thing a person can be checked at a gate, and that this boundary would fill in four lines about beech if somebody in a room told her the four lines were true. She said line seven was a countersignature by a person of an office, and that an office's signature on a paper about a load in this Hearthhold is the thing clause four of this boundary's charter is against and has been against since the nineteenth week, and that the office that wants it is not the office clause four names.

Then she stopped, and Bel Ordry took the rest, and he took it standing with his hand flat on a date he does not say.

"**Lines five and six are the whole of the paper and the two of them are one sentence. A name, and the person responsible for the load. And a person responsible for a load is a person responsible for a route, and a name against a route is a handle, and a handle is what four documents from three offices have been trying to put on this building for a month with a space in it for a person and nothing in the space.**"

"**Say the half against it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The half against it is that the four lines about the load are the only part of this paper that would help anybody. If those four are filled in, then a competent man at a competent counter can tell a competent man at another competent counter that a load came up this passage, and that is a thing this Hearthhold has never been able to say about anything it has ever sent out of here, and I have wanted to be able to say it for thirty weeks.**"

"**Enter that as his words and not as ours,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that the man who keeps the page said a thing that was true and did not say it as a reason to fill in two lines, and enter that I have heard it and that I am not going to answer it this morning.**"

The carrier nodded at that as though it were the correct response, and said he had been told to bring the paper up and stand there, that he did not know what the office wanted of him if nobody filled it in, that nobody in that anteroom could tell him, and that this was the most honest answer he had had all week.

Then Nera Oduya, from the second step, said one word.

It took nine seconds. A printed sheet had come down the passage with the paper, folded in with it, and it had been lying face up on the anteroom table since the carrier put it down, and she had read it upside down on her way past, and it was a schedule, and the schedule had four columns and the second of them was headed for the person who will be present, and three rooms were under it.

She struck the heading and the three rooms in one stroke of her own hand from three steps up, without coming down, and the space where a reason would go is empty, and she said one word and went back up.

"**Say what you struck,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**A column.**"

"**Say what else you struck.**"

"**A room.**" Nera Oduya did not turn round. "**I have said twice in a month that a room is what I strike, and I am not going to say which one, and you did not ask me, and I am going back up the steps.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The time, which was nine seconds. The absence, which is the third time this month and the eighth time in this building. And the fact that there is no reason in the space where a reason would go, and that a page with a reason on it is a page somebody can read back to her, and that I have now entered that sentence twice and I am not going to enter it a third time in a better way.**"

Nobody asked her. The floor was not approached. Nobody went near it and nobody was sent, and the page in the clerk's book that says somebody stands at the low end of the corridor is blank and has been blank since the fifteenth week and is still blank.

"**Say the column out loud,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because a column is the whole of the fifth clause and I would like a room to hear it in the words a man's office printed.**"

Dalla Sen read the second column's heading aloud, twice, in the anteroom, and it was *person who will be present*, and then she read the fifth clause out of a book in which it had been entered a fortnight ago by a man who did not write it and carried it up a passage anyway.

"**A room within a boundary whose condition is not being checked at the time the room is in use may be entered by a person sent by the office of the federation, and the boundary may not refuse, and the refusal of the person who keeps the room is not a ground.**"

"**That is a column with a heading on it,**" said Renn Vale from the landing, and she did not come down, "**and it is the fifth clause, and the fifth clause is the only clause in that document that is not about a room, and it is about a person in a room being unable to say no.**"

"**I have spent thirty weeks in this building teaching people that their refusal is a ground. And somebody has printed a column with a heading on it and put three rooms under it and sent it down a passage, and my answer is that the column is struck and the three rooms are struck and nobody is to be asked which rooms they were, and my second answer is that a person whose name is not on a schedule is not on it, and that is the answer I have been giving for a month and I am not going to improve on it for a printed sheet.**"

For about a minute nobody spoke, and then Talla Roke said the half of it that was hers, and she said it from the step with her three behind her and her hands loose.

"**I said last week that a form comes back on a board by a schedule, and I was right, and the schedule has been down that passage already, and nobody noticed, because a schedule is not a threat and I have been saying that for a month and this is the first time anybody has handed me one.**"

"**Here is the part I came to say and it is the part that matters and it is not about this Hearthhold. Two of those three rooms were struck by a woman who will not say which. One was not. Somebody down that passage put a name-free column on a sheet and put three rooms under it and struck two and left one standing, and I want to ask this room what it thinks it says about the third.**"

For about four seconds nothing came, and then the carrier said it, and he said it flat and he was not trying to be clever.

"**It says the third room is one somebody is coming to on a day, and nobody has put anybody's name on it because there is not anybody to put. That is what the schedule is for. That is what a schedule is.**"

There was no improving on that, and Dalla Sen entered it in a carrier's own words, and entered that the carrier had said it in an anteroom in a building under a street without knowing that four weeks ago a man in the same room had said the sentence about schedules and had said it about himself.

Then Marek Venn said the thing the morning had been walking toward, and he said it in the plainest words he had.

"**There are two actions. One: we fill in lines five and six, and the load comes up the middle passage, and there is a wall in lime in this building before the weather turns. Two: we do not, and it does not come, and there is no wall in lime in this building before the weather turns, or ever.**"

"**And I want the other half of that said out loud, and I want the clerk to enter it, because it is the first time in thirty weeks that anybody in this building has written down the price of a refusal before anybody paid it.**"

"**We have had a bundle in that anteroom for four weeks because we will not fill in two lines on somebody else's paper, and I have watched a woman put that fact on a table as a cost and not as a grievance and I did not argue with her. I have never once asked who that bundle belongs to, and I am not going to ask today, and the reason I am not going to ask is that a name is the two lines and I have known that for a month.**"

"**So the price is not a bundle. The bundle is the smallest thing in this room. The price is a sill.**"

"**There is a room at the kitchen-line end of that corridor with nine hearth stones in it and one of the nine is propped two flags off its bed on a strip of crate board, and the bed under it is a hand and a half down, and the sill under that room is packed in stone in clay and that packing is the only thing in this Hearthhold holding that floor up, and clay shrinks as it dries and it has been drying for thirty weeks. A wall in lime goes round that sill. A wall in lime is nine hundred years of somebody doing it properly and it is the only thing anybody has ever told me is the answer to that floor.**"

"**And the person who keeps that room is not in this anteroom and she was not going to be, and I am not going to go up those nine steps and ask her at the second bell on a Tuesday whether this boundary should fill in a stranger's paper to buy her a wall, because that is asking her to be the reason.**"

"**Enter it as a question and not as a finding. And enter that I have said both prices out loud in that order, and that I have not said which one I would pay, and that I do not intend to say that in a room either.**"

Dalla Sen entered the day's number and the total and did not read either of them out, and gave the reason, and the reason was in her own hand and it was not dignity.

"**Two today and a hundred and seventy since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and eighty-seven and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks. The crew are at sixty-three and a half.**"

"**I entered those and I am not going to read them, and I am not going to read them because there is a printed sheet on this table with a column with a heading on it, and I have been a clerk in this building for thirty weeks, and I am not going to stand up at a sixth bell and say a number out loud over a piece of paper that wants a name in it.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water went off the fill at the waist yesterday afternoon and it has not been on it since, and I am not going to read a figure out loud on the morning it stopped standing somewhere it had been standing for four weeks, and Jo has not told me what it did and I have not asked her, and I am entering that I did not ask her.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-seven days and eighty-seven nights into a count that is his own, and I said in five weeks running that I was going to keep writing it and I am writing it, and it is eighty-seven, and it is not going to be eighty-eight, and nobody has thanked me and a woman with a wooden tally has now been in this room and has not thanked me either, and I am entering that, and I am glad, and that is the second time this month I have entered that I am glad.**"

"**And I did not walk the board. A printed thing is on this table and a board is the thing in this building a stranger has not asked for, and I have had a carrier and a schedule and two lines on a Monday, and I am not going to improve on that by saying it was a busy morning.**"

Tams Quill did not walk it either, and gave his own reason in his own words, and it was the reason a man gives when the reason is that he is the man who reads it.

"**There is a sheet on that table with a column with a heading on it. And there is a board in this building with a column with nothing on the top of it. And both of those are about what a room is for, and one of them came down a passage and the other one has been here for thirty weeks, and I am not going to put a man's readings next to a stranger's column in a room with a carrier in it. That is the reason. It is about me and not about him and I am content with it.**"

---

**On the third day of this week a clerk is going to ask five people for three hours of a piece of work in a room with the terms out loud, and she is going to name the three she is not going to ask and the reason for each before she asks anybody anything.**

**There is a store four days up the middle passage with beech in it, and the camp that stocked it is breaking up, and nobody in this Hearthhold has been told that by anybody but a woman with a tally who was not asked to look at a cart.**

**From three steps up, in nine seconds, a column on a printed sheet went out, and a carrier has said in a room that a schedule is what it is for, and a man has said in a room that the price of a refusal is a sill, and the clerk has entered both of those in her own hand and read neither of them out.**
