# Chapter 164 — Cut To The Stone

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the thirty-second week, a Thursday, and the school did not run, and a woman read four sentences out of a survey and a man compared them with a book, and a carpenter cut a piece of beech to fit a stone instead of cutting the stone to fit the piece, and a man who reads a board gave a tenth reason for not heading a column and it was worse than the nine.**

**The brace's mark is four damaged strokes and one undamaged groove in a lintel, and it is joined to the brace the only way this building can afford, which is by fitting the wood to the stone. No mark has been cut in the stone anywhere in this building and none was cut this week.**

Nine steps up and a little to the left of the anteroom bench there is a lintel, and it is the only dressed stone in this building with a mark cut in it. Getting a look at it took a woman with a folding rule and a man on a step, and nobody standing on a chair.

"**Read the four sentences,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and then Jo is going to read them again out of the book, and I am going to stand here and not read anything, because the last time I read a survey out I was the person who had brought it into the building.**"

She read them, and they were the whole of what a brace is, and they were four sentences long.

"**A brace is a physical support joined to a bearing mark. It needs joinwood, timber, stone, cable, or another tested support. It needs a counter-bearing. And it needs time to let the load settle.**"

Jo Verrick read the same four out of the stiff cover and the two were the same, and Dalla Sen entered that they were the same and that the object this time was a survey, which is a thing in this building that has been read aloud exactly once, and that she was not numbering anything.

"**Now say them back against the four things we have,**" said Bel Ordry, "**one at a time, and say which one is still missing, because a room that has been listening to requirements for a month gets a list into its head and a list is a thing that can be ticked.**"

Marek Venn got up off the bench, which he has not done to speak in a month, and stood in the middle of that anteroom with his own book in his left hand and his right one open at his side.

"**One. A tested support. Stone, clay, six pieces of a school's own beech, and two sound wedges off a school door. The only one of those tested in this room by a man who is paid is the stone, and the stone is what the frame is already packed with, and it has been packing for thirty-two weeks while the frame went to a finger and three-quarters and held.**"

"**Say the one after that.**"

"**Two. A counter-bearing. It is the wall on the other side of that room, and it is the only place in this building that can take it, and the reason the other place is forbidden is in a clerk's book and has been since the fifteenth week, and I am not saying it out loud again, because a room said it once and the room will not forget it.**"

"**Three.**"

"**Time to let the load settle. We have none and we cannot buy any and we cannot beg any, and the only time in this building is four days in seven, and two of the four are this week.**"

"**Four.**"

Marek Venn did not answer for long enough that the pump got three strokes in.

"**Four is the mark, and the mark is nine steps up, and I have been in this building thirty-two weeks and I have never been able to read it and three people have read it three ways this month and the one of them with the right to an opinion says his is worth nothing and gives it anyway.**"

"**Then say how a brace is joined to a mark, since it is a definition and a definition has to do something,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, "**and I want to hear whether the answer needs a chisel and a mason.**"

That is when Iven Tallo put down the piece of chalk he had been holding for an hour and picked up a bevel instead.

"**It does not need a chisel and it does not need a mason, and that is the whole of this morning's work and it took me a fortnight to see it. You do not cut the stone. There is no mark going into that lintel this week or any week, and if one of you goes up there with a tool I will take it out of your hand.**"

"**Say what you do instead, and say it in trade words, because I am an engineer and I have been standing here for ten minutes listening to a man not answer me.**"

"**You cut the wood to the stone.**" He picked up the splayed head of the brace and turned it so the lamplight came across it. "**The head goes up under that lintel and it is cut to the bed, and where the bed is a plane I take the plane off with a chisel and a mallet. Where the bed is not a plane, and it is not, because it is a nine-hundred-year-old cut with a hollow worn in it, then I cut to the hollow and I shim it with a piece of beech a finger wide and I take the hollow out of the bearing.**"

"**Say the last part again, because it is the part I did not expect.**"

"**You cannot make that hollow go. You cannot make a nine-hundred-year-old hollow go with a chisel and a boy. So you fill it with a shim, and then the shim carries the load, and the shim is a piece of beech a finger wide and it is a joint, and this week that is the only joint in the brace and it is the one that matters.**"

"**Then say the part that is against you,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it before this room starts believing what you have just said.**"

"**Against it, and it is a real one. A shim carries load on the edges of itself, and a beech shim on a stone hollow crushes at its corners in about two years, and when it crushes the head settles, and when the head settles the frame goes a twelfth of an inch out of true again, and nobody standing in that anteroom is going to feel a twelfth of an inch for a long time.**"

"**Say the answer to that, and I am asking as the man who has to hold the thing, not as the man who cut it.**"

"**You take it out and put a new shim in, and you know it has to be done because you wrote a shim in the terms. You do not know it has to be done because the frame moved. The carpenter does not know. The engineer who cut it first is dead or gone or sixty. The person who knows is a term in a clerk's book, and a term in a clerk's book is a person who has not been born yet.**"

The room went quiet in the way it went quiet about once a fortnight, and Marek Venn found that he had taken his own book out without noticing and was holding it open at a page he had not looked at in three weeks. His forearm was doing the holding. The hand was not, and never would be again, and there was no arrangement available to him this week or any other that changed that, and the piece of beech in front of him did not care whose hand cut it.

"**Then we have a mark, a shim, a two-year limit, and a term,**" he said. "**And no mark has been cut in the stone. Say that out loud, because in a year somebody is going to find a piece of beech dressed to a hollow in a stone nine hundred years old and they are going to ask whether somebody cut a mark in this building, and I want the answer to be a person and not a story.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that no mark has been cut in the stone at the ninth room or at the school corner's mouth or anywhere else in this building, and that I am entering that in the same book as the third striking-out of a schedule, and that the two facts are in there next to each other on purpose and I am not going to explain why.**"

"**Then say one thing about that mouth, since you have just had a room looking at it,**" said Nera Oduya, from the third step. "**And say it as a thing that breathes and not as a thing that is shut. I have never certified it. It is the only opening in this building that goes from the corridor into a room nine children are taught in, and it is uncertified, and it is the only one I can open without asking anybody's permission, and both of those are on the same lintel.**"

"**Which of those two goes in the terms,**" said Marek Venn.

"**Neither. It goes in as a work, with terms, and I am going to be harder about the air in that room than anybody here expects, because I have been the only person who may turn that wheel for thirty-two weeks and I have never once been asked and I am not going to be grateful about it afterwards.**"

"**Say the evacuation part,**" said Renn Vale, "**because the four sentences you read do not include it and I have been told for six weeks that it does, and I want it said in a room whether it does.**"

"**A brace needs a clear evacuation plan, and this one has four rooms and a door,**" said Marek Venn, "**and the fourth part of that is a word, and a word is not a way out, and you have said that in this building and it does not become news the second time.**"

"**Then the brace has no evacuation plan and it has a fourth part of one,**" she said. "**Enter that, and do not improve it, and enter that I said it and not him.**"

Then Tams Quill gave the tenth reason, and he gave it standing up off the wall where he had been for an hour, and he had the chalk in his left hand and he did not use it.

"**The column is not headed. Tenth batch. I gave the ninth reason on the fourth day of the thirtieth week and it was the first of the nine that was not about me, and this is the tenth, and it is not about me either, and I have been carrying it since Wednesday.**"

"**Say it, and say it as a reason and not as a speech, because you have been in this room a fortnight saying things that were not reasons.**"

"**The ninth reason was that a heading is how a room finds out what the person in it is for, and that there are rooms in this building that have never been asked that. This is not that. This is about the whole column and not about one room.**"

He put the chalk down on the table, and it rolled and stopped against the six pieces of beech, and he did not pick it up.

"**Eighteen of these are going to be on this board by the end of the month, and eleven of them are nothings, and a noththing is a reading somebody was paid to go and look for and did not find. Under a heading they stop being eleven nothings. They become eleven findings. A finding is a thing a person is responsible for. I said that as the seventh reason and it is still true and it is not this one.**"

"**Say this one, Tams, because you have not said it yet and you have had a chalk in your hand for a minute.**"

"**I do not know what this building is for.**" He said it into the room and it went nowhere and came back. "**Thirty-two weeks. Eleven people and a stair and a pump and a school that runs three mornings in seven and a woman who keeps a corridor and a woman who keeps a page and a plan with a date in the top corner and no name on it. I have read every one of those out loud in this room. And if you put a heading over that column tomorrow, then that heading is the sentence this building has never been able to say out loud, and whoever is standing nearest it when it is said is the person who said it.**"

"**And I am not going to print a blank where the answer goes,**" he said, "**because a blank under a heading is a hole with a lid on it, and a man in a bad month in this building is going to fall into it, and I have watched people in a bad month all my life and the ones who fell into a lid were never the ones who were brave. They were the ones who were tired.**"

Dalla Sen wrote for a while, in his words, in that order, and read the last of it back before she put the chalk down, which she does for a man's reason and does not for anybody else's.

"**It is the tenth reason, and it is the second of the ten that is not about him. Enter also that he ended it by saying that a man in a bad month is going to fall into a lid, and that I have written that sentence down in my own book once before, and where it is written is not said.**"

Nobody in that anteroom improved on it, and Bel Ordry, who has refused to write a heading on the plan for a month on the grounds that a heading is a place to keep a person, sat down on the step for the first time anybody had seen him sit on the step in thirty-two weeks. He stayed on it for the length of two sentences, got up again, and said nothing at all about it, and Dalla Sen entered that he had sat down and entered that she was not going to say why, and that a man sitting on a step is not a heading and would not have been one in any case.

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the board was walked at seventeen entries, and the count was read at ninety-nine, and the brass survey rule in the pocket of a coat on a hook had said nothing for thirty-two weeks and the wrapped plate on the shelf above the same bench had been unopened for the same thirty-two.**

"**Two today and two hundred and four since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and twenty-one and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile. The crew are at seventy-one.**"

"**I entered those and did not read them, and the reason is that a man has just said out loud that he does not know what this building is for, in a room with eleven people in it, and I am not going to read a number out loud in the same hour as that and pretend the two are the same kind of fact.**"

"**The pot is nine, the jars are two, and the seam is four threads and was eight in the nineteenth week, and nobody has measured it in between, and I have said in four weeks running that I am not going to make a number for it.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is ninety-nine days and ninety-nine nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred. I have said in ten weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and I am writing it tomorrow at a hundred, and I am telling this room now so that nobody is surprised and nobody thanks me.**"

"**And the board was walked, and I did not walk it, and the reason is his and not mine. He gave a reason in a room, and I would not have a column in front of me for an hour after it, and that is the second time this month a man has made this clerk put a board down and the first time she has let one.**"

Tams Quill walked the board himself, with the chalk in his left hand and the right one open and flat in his lap, and named no day.

"**Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, a plan, a condition and a store. Nine in the ordinary column and eight in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim's condition has not been met, the mark is not coming off the board, and the condition on the brace is on that board too and it is not met either, and I am going to have to say that sentence out loud every week until it stops being true, and it is not going to stop being true this week.**"

"**And the column is not headed, and that is the tenth reason and Dalla has all of it, and the reason cost me something to say and I am not going to tell this room what it cost, and if a clerk writes that down I would like her to leave it out.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it down.

---

**A beech shim a finger wide carries the load in a hollow cut nine hundred years ago, and the carpenter says it will crush at its corners in about two years, and the person who will know is a term in a clerk's book, and a term in a clerk's book is a person who has not been born yet.**

**No mark has been cut in the stone in this building. Two houses on the same bench say so, and the clerk has put the two facts next to each other on purpose and will not say why.**

**A man said out loud in a room with eleven people in it that he does not know what this building is for, and that he is not going to print a blank where the answer goes, because a blank under a heading is a hole with a lid on it.**
