# Chapter 145 — A Person Holding An Opening

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the twenty-eighth week, a Friday, and the school ran for the twentieth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and a schoolteacher agreed to say a sentence to an empty room four mornings a week, and an air engineer stated a limit out loud in a room with no number and no day on it, and named herself as the person who may stop it, and a man went down into a channel at the lowest point in this building with a lamp and his left hand and cleared the bottom of it by hand.**

**He is not the one who decides when it stops being safe. He said so himself on Thursday and he said it again on Friday and the room entered it twice, and the reason the room entered it twice is that this is the second time in twenty-eight weeks that a condition in this building has had a person named against it who was not named on a page.**

At the fourth bell the nine went in and one of them went in sitting down, and the strip under the ninth hearth stone was holding, and the two flags had not moved, and Jo Verrick said the standing term out loud in the doorway, and a physician said it out of his own hand against hers, and Ilsa Norr said yes to it, and Dalla Sen entered that the term is the same in two hands and did not use the word *read* about it, because on Tuesday she used it four times and she has thought about it since and she has decided that a word in a book four days up a road is not a word she gets to spend in her own mouth.

Then the schoolteacher came down two steps and gave Renn Vale the answer she had said on Monday she would not give until it was put to her properly, and she gave it before anybody put it.

"**Yes. Four mornings a week, out loud, in a room, with the room empty, and I will be the one who says it because I am the one who keeps that room and nobody is going to make a woman come and ask me whether I have said it yet.**"

"**And I want the cost of it in the book in my own words and not improved, because it is a stupid thing to agree to and I am agreeing to it. It means that four mornings a week a woman stands in a building and says a sentence to nobody about a stone she is not standing next to, for as long as this Hearthhold is here, and there is no number on it and no day on it, and on the morning somebody stops saying it there is no post to notice and no clerk to notice and nothing at all in this building that would tell anybody.**"

"**And I would rather be doing that than have those nine walk onto a floor on a morning that nobody looked at. That is not a noble position. It is a preference and I am putting it in as a preference.**"

Renn Vale entered the agreement in the second column of the tenth row in her own hand, with no number and no day on it, and the clerk read it against the original and compared the two, and they were the same, and that was the fifth time in a month a condition in this building had been put against its original in a room and the two compared, and the practice is a decision on a page in a clerk's hand, and it has now been varied against a standing term, an anteroom's five terms, a charter clause, an asking's terms and a limit, and the clerk entered that it was five and that she was not going to make it six this week.

---

**At the second bell the air engineer said the limit out loud, and it had no number and no day on it, and she said before she said it that she was going to say the reason she was the one saying it and not asking anybody to say it for her.**

Nera Oduya came down to the second step and did not come further, and Dalla Sen had her book open at a page she does not usually open on a Friday.

"**I am the only person in this Hearthhold who may stop the air gate. That has been true for twenty-eight weeks and I have never used it as anything, and I said on Tuesday that I had never done it and not that I would not, and I want that written down exactly because a room in this building has twice now got a sentence of mine a shade off what I meant.**"

"**So here is the limit on what happens at the low end of that corridor this afternoon, and it is the whole of it, and I am not going to add a second clause to it and I am not going to make it narrower so that it sounds braver.**"

"**The opening is held while there is water standing on the fill at the waist.**"

"**That is it. Not for a number of hours. Not until a thing is done. Until there is no water standing on that fill. And the reason for that clause and not another one is that the water came up over that fill on the Thursday of the twenty-sixth week and it has not been off it since, and while it is on that fill the waist is not a floor anybody should put thirty-one people across, and a person at the low end of that corridor is the price of that, and if the water goes off the fill then the price is not being paid and the opening is shut and there is nothing to argue about.**"

"**And I am the person who says so. Not the man in the channel. Not the woman who keeps the road. Me. And if the water is on that fill on Wednesday morning and I say it is finished, it is finished, and he will not be asked whether he agrees, and he has already said in a room on Thursday that he is not a post and I am not going to be argued out of this by a man who is not one.**"

"**And the last thing, and then I will go back up the steps, because I have got a valve to watch and I have watched it every day for twenty-eight weeks and I am not going to stop today.**"

"**The air at that end goes down that channel before the water does. It always has. A person standing in that channel on a Friday afternoon is going to be standing in the lowest air in this Hearthhold, and if the water comes up the channel while he is in it then the air in the channel goes out from under him before the water reaches him, and I am not going to be able to fix that from the second step of these nine steps with my own hands.**"

"**And that is a thing I am telling this room in advance so that it is on the page before anybody needs it, which is a thing I did in the twenty-fourth week about a different door and nobody has forgotten it, including the door.**"

Out of her own book, against what the air engineer had said, the clerk read the limit, and then the air engineer said it again herself and compared the two, and they were the same, and Dalla Sen entered that the limit has no number and no day on it, and that the person who may stop it is the air engineer, and that the air engineer's name is not on any page in this Hearthhold and has not been put there and is not going to be, and that the person who is going to hold the opening has been entered by the clerk as not a post, in advance, twice.

Iven Tallo was asked about the frame and the joint, because he is a carpenter and it is the only thing anybody in this room is allowed to ask him about, and he answered about the joint and refused the other thing in his own words in a room.

"**That frame is dressed stone and the joint under it is dressed stone and the bed of it is stone, and I have had my hand in it and the stone is sound and the bed is sound and there is nothing in that joint that is anybody's business this afternoon except the person who clears the channel below it. It is a drain frame. It is not a bearing. Nobody is going to ask me about a mark and I am going to say the thing I said in a room in the twenty-seventh week in case anybody was hoping: the mark is off the wall, it has been off the wall since the twenty-third week, and it is not going back on, and I would not put one there, and a man with a piece of chalk can put a carpenter's mark on any wall in this building and that sentence is mine and I said it once.**"

---

**At the fourth bell of the fifth day of the twenty-eighth week a man went down into the channel at the low end of the corridor, and it was the most ordinary act anybody in this room has watched in four weeks, and nobody in that anteroom was watching it.**

Empty, and open, and the school had run, and the nine had gone back across the waist: the corridor was the corridor of a Tuesday morning with nobody on it, and Marek Venn went down the nine steps along it with a lamp in his left hand and stopped at the low end where two flags and a length of the crew's own spent nine-metre line lay over a frame with no grate in it.

"**Say what I am doing out loud, because that is the practice and because I have been the man on the step for four weeks,**" he said, to nobody, in a corridor, at about the fourth bell. "**The water comes up the channel before it comes up the corridor. The grate came off this frame in the twenty-fifth week and it is not coming back on and there is no second one. There is silt in the bottom of this channel and I do not know how much of it because I am not going to guess a number at it in a corridor. So I am going to put a lamp in and I am going to clear it with my hands, and when the water comes up this channel it is going to have somewhere to go instead of standing on a corridor floor at the lowest point in this building.**"

"**And that is the whole of the engineering. There is no mark in it. There is nothing on the stone and nothing in the frame and no bearing and no rule-line and nothing in me that is doing any of it. There is a man with a lamp and a left hand and a channel with silt in it, and the room above me is eleven feet of corridor that nobody has ever put a load on, and I am going to be under it.**"

"**And there is a page in Dalla's book that says somebody stands here, and it has been blank for twenty-eight weeks, and the third of the three conditions on it says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and nobody has been sent and nobody has been asked, and I am going down here because I said on Thursday that I was going to and not because anybody in this building sent me.**"

"**And I am going to be honest about the last part, because I have not been honest about anything all month. If I am not back up those steps in the time it takes the pump to run from its second stroke to its third, then the limit on this is not the water on the fill and it is me, and I want that written down now, by you, before I go down, and I want it in the same column as the water and not underneath it.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it and read it out and entered it in the boundary's column, not the crew's, and entered that the crew's line was spent and that a spent line is not an hour and never had been.

"**Entered, and I am entering the reason I put it in the boundary's column. A man is not an hour. A man's hour is a thing a person can be given and taken back, and there is no version of this in which anybody in this room gives that man an hour today, and if I had put it in that column I would have been handing the five of you a debt, and Renn said the terms out loud on Wednesday for a reason and I am not going to un-say it with a piece of chalk.**"

"**And I am entering a second thing, and it is a thing I have not entered in this clerk's book before and I have thought about it for a day. He asked me to write down what stops it, and then he asked me to write down a second thing in the same column, and the second thing is him. Not a figure and not a time. Him.**"

"**So the limit on that opening has two halves in it and both of them are in the boundary's column and neither of them is in the crew's. The water on the fill at the waist, which is the air engineer's, and the man at the bottom of it, which is mine, because I am the clerk and I am the person who reads the books and it had to be me. And I am going to say the rest of it now while he is standing in a corridor waiting to go down, and then I am not going to improve on it.**"

"**A page in a clerk's book that says somebody stands at the low end has been blank for twenty-eight weeks, and it is still blank, and the third of the three conditions on it says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and nobody has been sent and nobody has been asked and nobody is going near that floor. And in about an hour that page is going to have been made true by a man who was not sent, and the page will still be blank, and both of those are going to be in this clerk's book next to each other on purpose with nothing between them, and I am not going to be the one who explains them to anybody, including myself.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is on the fill at the waist and it is not moving, and there is still no number for that fill in this building.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty days and eighty nights into a count that is his own. I have said out loud in three weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and nobody has thanked me and I am not going to stop.**"

After the lamp went into the channel the man went down after it, and two flags and a length of the crew's own spent line lay over the frame above him, and the water at the waist did not move, and the corridor was open, and the anteroom was empty, and a clerk read a count of eighty out loud at a sixth bell to a room that did not fill in after it.

"**Two today and a hundred and fifty-one since the keeper signed,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and the boundary is at two hundred and sixty-eight and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at sixty-three and a half and have not moved since Wednesday.**"

"**And I did not walk the board today, and I am not going to give a reason that is going to make anybody feel better about it. I did not walk it because at the second bell of this morning a woman told me a limit and at the fourth bell a man went down a hole, and both of those are in this clerk's book now, and a board is walked by a person who has nothing else on them, and I have had a limit and a hole since the first bell and I have carried both of them up nine steps in a room with a count in it.**"

**In four parts in a coat on the anteroom table, a plan of twelve rows had become a network of four rooms and a door, and the network's second way out was a word, and a woman on a landing had agreed to say a sentence to an empty room four mornings a week, and a woman three steps up had struck a room out of a list in nine seconds and given no reason, and a man had gone down into a channel that has no row on the plan and no grate in its frame because the only sentence anybody has ever been able to write about that place is that somebody stands there and nobody has stood there for twenty-eight weeks.**

**A limit had been said out loud in a room with no number and no day on it, by the only person who may stop it, and it was that the opening is held while there is water standing on the fill at the waist, and the water was on the fill, and the clerk had entered that a man is not an hour.**

**A sheet from a surface office with a title on it that describes a woman on this landing went back into a coat unaltered, and a decision about a book that could be shown was made in a room and was not used, and on the third day of the next week a woman from another office is going to sit in this anteroom and ask to be in the room while a page is read out loud, and the page has no name on it and is not going to get one.**
