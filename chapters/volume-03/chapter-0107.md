# Chapter 107 — The Second Sheet

The second day of the twenty-first week was shut for the corridor, which was a Tuesday, and the keeper was four days up a road and was not coming back inside this batch, and the charter said three objects of care and this boundary had four, and the room spent the whole morning on whether to write the fourth one down.

**The three are in the charter's reason clause and they are in the clerk's hand and they were argued out loud before the chalk was picked up in the nineteenth week: a stair, a lip, and a valve.** A stair that is a hole, a lip that is a body, a valve that is a handle. The air engineer took the fourth thing out of that list before anybody had asked the woman it belonged to, and the reason she gave was in the charter, and the reason is a protection and not a softness.

"**A wall with water in it,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps. "**That is the fourth. The haunch behind the anteroom's outer service door, four threads and a leaf, two jars a day maybe three, and the only drinking water in this Hearthhold, and it is in the wall of the room that had nine children in it for twenty weeks.**

"**And I have been asked for a piece of work and not consulted for a decision, and the piece of work is that line, and I have written it, and I would like the room to argue about whether it goes on a page or not, because I have a position and I am not putting it in the room without somebody objecting to it.**"

"Then that is two," said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up, "**and the room is going to do this properly. Mikka. Why leave it out.**"

---

Mikka Hale had the pot side of it and she had it ready, which meant she had been thinking about it since the fifth bell on Friday and possibly since the second day.

"**Because a document that names three things is a description. A document that names four things is a claim,**" she said. "**And we spent nineteen weeks getting one sentence into a charter — that none of them can be entered against a man — and we did it by naming as few as we could, and every time somebody has put one more thing on a piece of paper in this building it has got harder to keep all of them in a room.**

"And here is the part I do not want said out loud and I am going to say it out loud. **The three things in that clause are all of them a person being in danger. A stair, a lip, a valve. A man on a stone, a man under a slab, a hand on a handle. The wall is not a person being in danger. The wall is a thing giving us something, and a wall that gives us something is a wall somebody in four days is going to write *source* against.**"

"**That is the first thing anybody has said in this room about the water that I agree with and I have not agreed with one word about the water,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and I want it entered that the cook is right and that I am still going to write the line.**

"**And the reason is the same reason as the four sentences, and it is a sentence I said on Friday to a stranger and it is the worst thing about this week. *A stranger is going to come and ask what this boundary's objects of care are, and if the answer is not on the page then the answer is in somebody's mouth.* And a thing in a cook's mouth is a thing a stranger writes down. A thing on a page under a charter is a thing a stranger has to come back for."**

"So put it in," said Bel Ordry. "**And I want it in as a second thing and not as a change, and I have thought about this for two days and I am going to say the whole of it and then somebody is going to tell me why I am wrong.**

"**The charter is signed. Seven hands. Clause nine says what is not in it and that is deliberate, and clause four is a noun with two men in it, and the whole of the argument of the last three weeks is that this document is the boundary's own and it is not a page and it does not travel and the office can neither grant it nor refuse it.** And if you go over that sheet with a second clause you have a document with two clauses, and a document with two clauses is a document with two things that can be argued about separately, and the second of them is going to be argued about by somebody who has never been in this room."

"Then it goes under," said Dalla Sen. "**Second sheet, under the first, and it does not replace it and it does not erase a clause and it does not touch clause nine, and if anybody wants to know what this boundary is bound by they read the top sheet, and if anybody wants to know what this boundary is actually looking after they read the one underneath.**

"And it is dated in the top corner."

"**It is not,**" said Bel Ordry.

The clerk stopped with the chalk up.

"**A page with a date in the top corner is a record of what a building was doing on a day,**" he said, "**and that is the whole of the difference between this boundary's paper and a nine-hundred-year-old schedule that is rolled up in a locked school store four hundred feet from here and has never been read out to any of us by anybody. I have got one date in this building and it means *as at*, and I am not going to write a second one in a month, and if the sheet has no date on it then it says *now*, and *now* is the only honest thing a second sheet can say.**

"Write on it, in the clerk's hand, that **it has no date on it because it does not record a day, and the first sheet is dated, and the two are not the same kind of thing.**"

Struck, and written in the keeper's words, and the clerk entered that the room had been told a thing about its own page that it had not been told in five months.

---

Then the thing that made the sheet worth having, and it took eleven seconds and it was Dalla Sen and she said it out loud in a room and then wished she had not, which she entered.

"**There is a fifth,**" she said, "**and I am the clerk and I have written every legal instrument in this building for five months and I am going to say it because nobody else is going to. The wash rooms have a row on the plan and an empty third column and there is a condition on that row in the clerk's hand with no name against it, and there is a woman in this building whose covers are the thing that keeps a floor's condition off a page, and **the covers are an object of care. They have been one for five months and they are the only one in this Hearthhold that a document has been argued about four times and settled four times in her favour and no further.**"

She stopped.

"**So it goes in as a fifth,**" she said, "**and if it goes in with a no-name on it, then the page says *this boundary looks after a set of covers* and that is not a page about a door, it is the only page in the world that says that, and anybody who has it can show anybody.**"

"I will take it out."

Nera Oduya came down three steps, which she has done four times in five months, and she had the chalk out of Dalla Sen's hand and she did not take it, she asked for it, and Dalla Sen gave it to her, and the clerk entered that she gave it.

"**Take out the fifth,**" Nera Oduya said, "**and do not put a dash on it, and do not put the woman's two sentences under it, and do not write *not asked*.**

"**Her three conditions are: no name against it ever on any page including by accident in four years, it is not a rota and nobody is to make it one, and nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true. I have been in this building five months and I have watched this page be built by nine people who have never once been cruel, and the last three weeks every single one of us has been looking for the strongest thing we could put on a page to protect her, and every single thing we have found to put there is another place her condition is now written down.**

"**I struck her covers out of the charter because nobody had asked her. I am striking her covers out of this sheet for exactly the same reason and it is the same reason and it has not changed in a fortnight, and the fact that the reason has not changed is the whole of what I know about her condition and I am not going to improve on it by being cleverer.**

"**And I would like it entered that a clerk in this building found a fifth thing to protect a woman with and that the air engineer took it out of her hand in a room, and that the reason is not that the fifth thing is wrong.**"

"Entered," said Dalla Sen, and her voice was not entirely steady and she let it not be. "**And entered that the second sheet names four objects of care and one of them has no third column and one of them is in a room that is shut.**

"**And entered that the clerk is not going to raise this again in this building.**"

Renn Vale named the price, and she named it to the room and not to the clerk, and she named it in the shape of the thing that had been bothering her since Friday.

"**Two documents can be split,**" she said. "**A charter with a sheet under it is two things, and a thing with two parts can be taken apart, and the part that is easier to hold is the part somebody keeps. In a year — and I mean a year, not a week, I am patient about pages — somebody is going to read the second sheet on its own because it is shorter and it is newer and it has a wall in it that gives them water, and they are not going to read clause nine underneath it, and clause nine is the sentence that says the ninth room and the four treads and the wash rooms and the mouth are not in any of this and that it is deliberate.**

"**That is the price and it is not small and I am not going to argue against it, because the alternative is that a wall with the only drinking water in it in it is on nobody's paper, and I have watched a cook in this room have to describe that wall out loud to a stranger in a corridor on Friday. A page that can be read wrong is better than a secret that has to be told.**"

---

It was written in two hands and signed by one person and refused by another, and that is the shape of it.

Nera Oduya wrote the wall in her own hand. Mikka Hale wrote the water in hers, and the two lines are the second sheet, and the sheet is on the scrub board under the charter with the office's number at the top of the first one and nothing at the top of the second.

"**I do not sign anything in this Hearthhold,**" Nera Oduya said, when it came to that, "**and I said so in a room in the nineteenth week and I have not been asked since and I am not being asked now, and there is no version of a second sheet that changes that.**

"**So this sheet has one signature on it and two hands, and the hands are the air engineer's and the cook's, and the signature is the cook's, and I want that written as a fact and not as an absence.**"

"Then it is a document with a person half in it," said Isel Ferrow, who had said on Monday that a sheet with two names on it is a sheet two people can be sent to.

"**It is a document with one person who can be sent to and one who cannot, and that is the best a document gets in this Hearthhold and I am going to tell you why I am signing it,**" said Mikka Hale. "**A water is the one thing in this building a cook can be held to. Not a stair, not a lip, not a valve — a woman on a landing, a woman at a valve, a woman on a tooth. A kitchen's water, I can be sent for. I would rather be sent for than have our own water in a document that nobody can be identified with, because the day somebody wants a name for that water they are going to come to this room and there will be one on it, and it had better be the woman who washes it.**

"And if I am wrong about that, it is in a clerk's hand, and the clerk is the only person in this building who has never once written down a thing she was not in the room for."

Seven signatures stand on the first sheet and are not touched. Clause nine stands and is not touched. And Dalla Sen entered that **a second thing under a charter is a boundary instrument, and the office can neither grant it nor refuse it, and it will do with this one exactly what it does with the first, which is receive it, file it, and cite it, and it will be cited before the first one is, because a wall with water in it is more interesting than a stair.**

---

Iven Tallo was not asked to sign it, and he noticed, and he said something about it on the third step at the sixth bell that was about the first charter and not the second.

"**The first one I said no to. In four words, in a room, and nobody argued with me and I have had no reason to change my mind for three weeks.** And this one I was not asked. **I have been noticing the difference for two days and I do not like which of the two I would rather have had, and I am going to say the whole of it because I do not want it going into a book as a man being gracious about something.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**The first charter I would have signed if it had a name at the top of it. I have said that to nobody. It had an office's number and no name anywhere on it and that is why I said no, and the air engineer has made the same argument about a different document in a different week and got a different answer, and I have worked out that the difference is that she is a person this boundary asked and I am a man this boundary has not asked for anything since the sixth day of the nineteenth week.**

"**So I would rather be asked. That is all. Write it down as a man who would rather be asked, and do not write it down as a man who is hard.**"

"Entered in his own words," said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that he asked for nothing and was given nothing, which is the correct arrangement, and entered that the count's column does not move and this conversation is not a work.**"

---

And then the four sentences went into the book, because Bel Ordry had written them on a sheet on Friday and a sheet a keeper keeps in a coat is a thing that can go missing, and the clerk copies things into the book and the clerk's book is the one object in this building that has never gone out of sight.

She read them out, and at the second sentence she stopped and asked him whether he wanted the word *room* left in.

"**It says the third column in words in this room, and it does not leave the room,**" she said. "**And it left the room, Bel Ordry. It left the room on Friday in a clerk's hand in a permit case and it is four hundred feet above the Cinder Steps now, and if anybody reads that sentence in a year they are going to read a false one, and it will be false in the one document in this building that is a rule about a stranger.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"**Then it is wrong,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I wrote it, and I wrote it on Friday morning before the third column was in words, and I have not looked at it since, and I would like it entered that I wrote a rule on Friday that was true on Friday and false by the sixth bell and did not know.**

"**And the correction is not mine to make, because I wrote it and a man who corrects his own rule has never had anybody look at it.**"

It was struck, and it was rewritten in his own hand with Dalla Sen reading the correction back and then the strike, in that order, which is the order she has used since a cook taught it to her in the eighteenth week, and what the sheet says now is:

*They may be shown the plan's third column in words. The page does not leave this room. The words in a clerk's hand may leave it, and did, and are a description of this building and not a record of it, and the person who gave them was told what they were and said so and was thanked for it.*

And then Marek Venn said the thing, from the fourth step, with no standing in this room at all and every right to be in it.

"**You have written a rule about a stranger, in a room with no stranger in it, and you are the only person in this building who could be got to write it, and that is not a procedure. That is a man being useful.**

"**And in a year, when a different office sends a different person with a different paper, the only reason your four sentences work is that you are in this building and you are a decent man, and neither of those is in the sentence.**"

"I know," said Bel Ordry.

"**You do not, because you have never had to be a procedure.**"

"**I have had it in a coat for twenty-one weeks,**" Bel Ordry said, "**and it is a week old as a document and four months old as a habit, and you are right and I am not going to argue with you and I am not going to take it back, and I am going to read it out at the fifth bell every day the way we read the six things, and if I am wrong every day then somebody in this room will be able to say so at the fifth bell instead of at a counter.**"

"**I am going to be that somebody,**" said Dalla Sen.

"I know you are," said Bel Ordry, "**and that is why I said it out loud.**"

And then he said the other thing, and he said it to the room and not to Marek, and Marek Venn stood on the fourth step and heard it and had been waiting for it for four days.

"**I asked to be suspicious of in a month, and it is a month, and nobody has been suspicious of me, and I would like it entered that I have noticed a second time and that I am saying so in a room and not to a man.**"

Dalla Sen wrote nothing, and Marek Venn said nothing, and **that was the moment the batch was actually suspicious of him and it was three seconds long and it was inside a man's own sentence and it cost nothing and it was the only suspicious anybody in this Hearthhold has ever been of anybody, and the clerk entered nothing, and she entered that she entered nothing, and that is the whole of what this book has on it.**

---

At the sixth bell the six things were read, and the six were the same six with three more on them, and the three more were a second sheet under a charter with one signature and two hands, four sentences in the book with one of them struck and rewritten, and the keeper's two true sentences read at the fifth bell for the fourth day, and nobody filled the space after the last one.

The boundary's instruments were three now — the charter, the sheet under it, and a page of rules about a stranger — and **all three of them were about this building, and not one of them was about four households four days up a road, and the man who was going to say the true sentence to those four was somewhere on the middle passage with a chalk line under the pump's window and no way of knowing any of it.**
