# Chapter 148 — A Room Kept Open, And A Form Off A Board

**Open for the corridor on the third day of the twenty-ninth week, a Wednesday, and the school ran for the twenty-first morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and thirty-one people of this Hearthhold went up a market road on Tuesday as ordinary residents with nobody at the front of it and came back, and a form came off a board at a gate four days up the middle passage because a woman at a counter read a stranger's mark and decided that a thing a mark can be used on is not exclusive, and a page with sixteen rows on it and no name anywhere was read out loud in an anteroom to a woman from a surface federation who was not given a copy.**

**Shut with eleven people in it in the twenty-fourth week, and open now, the room at the kitchen-line end is open under a public occupancy plan that is the same page it has always been, and it is public, and it is bounded, and the person who may stop it said her own name out loud in a room and it went in a clerk's book and not on the page.**

Twenty-first morning, and the nine went in at the fourth bell, and one of them went in sitting down, and the strip under the ninth hearth stone was holding and the two flags had not moved, and Jo Verrick said the standing term out loud in the doorway and a physician said it out of his own hand against hers and Ilsa Norr said yes to it, and Dalla Sen entered that the term is the same in two hands and entered that she had been entering that same sentence for four weeks and was not going to stop entering it on the day a page got read out to a stranger.

Then the clerk said that the plan was going to be read out in this room to a stranger and that she wanted the reason said first, and Bel Ordry said it standing, with his hand flat on the date in the top corner which he does not say.

"**On the third day of last month a woman wrote four pages and said that a boundary may publish, for anybody who asks in a room, a statement of what each of its rooms is permitted to hold and what condition each of those rooms is under, in a form with no name on it, and that the boundary may decline to show the document itself while showing the statement, and that a person from another office may be present while the statement is read and may be told no.**"

"**And this boundary has read those three things out loud in a room and has said no to them, and the man on this landing asked for a procedure and was refused, and I have said out loud in this anteroom that the plan does not leave this building and the charter does not leave this building and the first column stays in the coat and the fourth column stays empty.**"

"**And I am going to say the thing I said on the fifth day of the twenty-sixth week and say it the other way round, and I would like the clerk to notice that I am saying it the other way round and to enter that I noticed.**"

"**I said: I would rather a federation want to look at this boundary than be able to take it. And I meant it, and I still mean it, and the answer to it turns out to be that a page can be shown to a room and not taken out of it, and that is a thing her third sentence has in it and I have been reading it for a fortnight as a thing about her office and it is a thing about mine.**"

Hollis Ashe sat down facing the door again and put her tally on the table and said the thing she came to say before anything else.

"**I am not here as an office. I am here because I asked to be in the room and I have not asked for the document and I am not going to ask for the document, and I wrote a fourth sentence on Thursday that I did not write down and I am going to say it out loud instead, and it is this: I have been trying to write a page that could be shown for four weeks and I could not write one, and this boundary has one, and I am going to sit in this room and watch it being shown and I am not going to pretend that is not the best thing that has happened to my work in two years.**"

Dalla Sen read the plan out in four parts, and it is sixteen rows, and there is no name anywhere on it, and the first column is a permission and has never held a count of anybody, and the second column has no number and no day on any of the sixteen, and the third column is what is done in the hand of the person who keeps the row with fifteen lines filled and one empty that the man who keeps it will not name, and the fourth column is empty and unheaded with a reason in it in two hands and a modal struck out of one of them.

**Four rows went onto that page and they are the kitchen, the room at the kitchen-line end, the common landing and the anteroom, and the first column took the same number four times and not four numbers, and the cook said why before anybody asked.**

"**One number four times and not four numbers,**" said Mikka Hale. "**The chain has one end and the end is the anteroom and the number is ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and I am not writing it four times. A network with four numbers in it is a queue with a signpost on it, and I have watched a queue of four numbers work in this building and it is a thing that happens on a Friday and nobody is in charge of it.**"

"**And that is the fifth permission I have written on that page and the fifth time I have not counted anybody, and I said the last two times that I am the worst person in this room, and I am not going to say it a fifth time because saying it a fifth time is a way of not saying it, and what I am going to say instead is that I would rather be argued with for the rest of my life than be held to, and that is the sentence I said the first time and it has not got worse and it is not going to.**"

"**And the room at the kitchen-line end is ten in the first column and it has been a permission since the third day of the twenty-fifth week and the room has held eleven, and I have not said the number in this room and I am not going to, and a woman from another office is sitting in it and did not ask me and I noticed that she did not ask me and I have decided that I am not going to be glad about it, because glad is a thing that makes a person stop asking.**"

Each of the four keepers stood up for their own row and said their own condition in their own hand and nobody read it for them, and the conditions had no number and no day on them, and the room's condition was the one the woman on the landing had written in the twenty-seventh week, checked every morning the room is used, with the nine coming out without waiting for a physician if the answer is no twice, and the schoolteacher's own hand in the third column from the third day of the twenty-fifth week which had not changed and which she said was still hers.

Row by row, out of the book against the page, the clerk read the conditions and a second person compared them, and there were no numbers and no days on any of the sixteen, and Dalla Sen entered that this was the fifth time in a month a condition in this building had been read back against an original in a room and compared, and entered that the object this time was sixteen conditions at once and that she had chosen to do them in a row rather than one at a time because a stranger was in the room and a stranger is owed the shape of the thing and not one condition at a time with a conversation in between.

Out loud, in a room, the person who may stop it said her own name, and it went in the clerk's book and not on the page, and the reason for that was said by two women.

"**I am the person who may stop it,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing. "**Any of those four rooms can be shut by me at any bell, without a vote and without a number, and if I shut one then nobody is to be sent anywhere to find out why I shut it, and the clerk writes it down afterwards and not before. That is the whole of my authority over this thing and I have just said all of it out loud in a room with a stranger in it and I would like it entered in those words.**"

"**And I want the second half entered in mine too, and I want Dalla to enter it whether she agrees with it or not. I have said my name out loud in a room and I want it written down that a person named in a room is not a post. A post is on a page and a post can be asked for by a body that has never met the person who holds it, and I have been that page and it is not worth it again.**"

"**And in a year nobody will be able to find out from one sentence in a clerk's book that it was me, because a clerk's book is in a coat as well. And I am not going to improve on that, and I am not going to be the woman who insists on a paper.**"

"**Entered, and I am entering the two halves as two entries, and the second one is the air engineer's and she said it before Renn said it and I am entering that she said it first,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I said one sentence in a room on the second day of last month and it is in your book and I am not going to say it twice,**" said Nera Oduya, from the second step. "**That was the whole of my contribution to this and it was a sentence about a name and I have said it. Go on.**"

Mero Senn said the thing that the page could not say, and he said it standing, and he asked for it to be entered with his name against it in the clerk's book, which is the first thing he has put anywhere in twenty-nine weeks.

"**I am not certifying that room. I want that in the book with my name on it, because a record with no name on it is a thing nobody can be held to and I have been telling this room for four weeks that I will not sign a number, and I am not signing a number now, I am putting my name against a refusal, which is the other thing entirely and I am allowed to do it.**"

"**And I am going to say why, and it is not humility and it is not the physician's modesty, and Dalla is going to enter that I said it was not. I do not certify that room because I cannot. I have been in it. There is a cut in the floor under the hearth corner made with stone and no bar, and a sill packed in stone in clay, and that packing is the only thing in this Hearthhold holding that floor up, and clay shrinks as it dries, and the strip under the ninth stone is crate board and two flags and that is not a repair, it is a fortnight of a hole not getting worse.**"

"**And nine children sit on it three days in seven. And the room is open, and the room is going to stay open, and there is a public plan with sixteen rows on it and a condition in every one of them, and none of those things makes it safe and all of them make it arguable, and arguable is worth more than safe in a building like this and I am not going to be the man who says it is worth more than safe anywhere else.**"

**At the third bell a courier reported what happened at a counter four days up the middle passage, and it was a form coming off a board, and nobody was defeated, and nobody conceded in a word.**

"**Thirty-one of yours and my three and me,**" said Talla Roke. "**Thirty-five people up a market road on Tuesday as ordinary residents. No order. Nobody at the front. I walked it at the back with a bundle that was not mine, and I was not asked who was responsible for it by anybody at any point on that road, and I want that in the book first because it is the half that went well.**"

"**And the other half. I did not win anything and I am going to say that before anybody in this room starts putting it down as a victory, because you have all been in rooms this month watching a thing be decided and a thing being decided is not a thing being won.**"

"**There is a board at that gate with a printed sheet on it and it has seven lines and two of them are for a name and for the person responsible for the load. That sheet is not the road's. It is the Ninefold Charter's, and the Ninefold Charter is the guild cartel that writes the seals and the insurance, and the sheet is on that board because the Charter's own rule is that a road a person carries a load along is not a recognized road unless there is a named person responsible for the load, and nothing on an unrecognized road is insured, and that rule has been on that board longer than anybody can remember and nobody has ever once objected to it because it has never once been tested.**"

"**And on Tuesday a woman at that counter had my sheet in front of her. And she did not ask me for a name. She asked whether my mark was a holder. And I said it is three people's and it is a courier's and it is not entered anywhere and it cannot be entered anywhere. And she read the rule on her own wall, and I watched her find the part of it, and then she took the sheet off the board.**"

"**A thing a stranger's mark can be used on is not exclusive. That is what she said. And then she said four other things and I am going to give you the ones that matter and I am not going to improve on them because I have not got better words than hers.**"

"**She said: the board is a courtesy and the road is unchanged. She said: the seal on the road stands and it always stood. She said: I am not taking the sheet down off the office, I am taking it off the board, and the office does not know it was on the board. And she said: if somebody wants a load carried on this road, come back in a month, because a sheet comes back on a board, and it comes back on the board of a clerk who does not know about Tuesday.**"

"**That is all. And nobody was defeated, and the office did not concede anything in a word, and it will be put back in a month by somebody who has not had this conversation, and I am telling you that so that nobody in this building writes it down as a win. It was not a win. It was a piece of paper that a person could not use, on a board, being taken off a board by a competent person doing her job.**"

Dalla Sen entered it in the clerk's book in Talla Roke's own words and entered that the Ninefold Charter is a guild cartel and not this boundary's charter, and that this boundary's own charter puts no holder against any route and could not have been the instrument that lost anything, and that nobody in this building is going to be told that a guild lost a road on Tuesday.

"**Entered, and I am entering one more thing about it and it is in my own words. My rule has been in this clerk's book since the third day of the twenty-sixth week in the words of a woman who has since been in this building for a week, and on Tuesday it was true. It is still true. The route was used by an ordinary resident and the route was the market road and not a guild's and nobody was at the front of it.**"

"**And I am entering that I do not know whether it will be true in a month, and that the woman at the counter told me it would not be, and that she is competent, and that I am writing down that a competent person told us the truth about how long our own rule lasts.**"

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered a sixteenth thing on a board that has fifteen on it, and she did not walk the board, and the count read the count and not the figures and the reason she gave for both of those was a person standing in the room.**

"**Two today and a hundred and sixty since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and seventy-seven and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at sixty-three and a half and have not moved since Wednesday of last week.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is on the fill at the waist and it has not been off it and it is not moving, and there is no number for that fill in this building, and the water came up the channel at the low end on Monday and it went down the channel.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-three days and eighty-three nights into a count that is his own, and I am writing it, and I have said out loud in four weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to stop.**"

"**And a sixteenth thing went on that board this afternoon and I entered it myself and I want the whole of it said out loud because it is the first time in twenty-nine weeks that a thing has gone on that board that is not a reading and not a wall and not a page, and I am the clerk and I am the one who put it there.**"

"**It is a condition. It is the limit an air engineer stated out loud in a room on the fifth day of last week, and it is that the opening at the low end of the corridor is held while there is water standing on the fill at the waist, and it has no number and no day on it, and the person who may stop it is named in this clerk's book and not on a page, and a man who held that opening is named in it as not a post.**"

"**It goes in the column with nothing over the top of it, and it is not a null because it was tested and it held, and it is not the claim because nothing is built on it, and it is not a mark, and it is not a row and it is not a plan, because those two are on that board already and a board that has two of them does not need a third.**"

"**And I am entering the count's reason for the unheaded column being the right place for it, which he gave me on Tuesday and which I am entering against his advice: a condition with a heading over it is a condition somebody can be held to, and this one has a person who can stop it, and those are the same shape and the same danger, and the difference between them is a piece of chalk.**"

Tams Quill did not walk the board and gave one reason, which was the best one anybody had in the anteroom all day, and nobody argued with it.

"**There is a woman from another office sitting in this room and she has been in it for four hours and she has not asked for the document once, and I have not walked that board in front of her today, and if I had walked it she would have had it, and she would not have asked, and I am not going to be the man who puts a thing in a stranger's hands that she has not asked for. That is the reason. It is about her and not about me and I am content with it.**"

**In a room at the kitchen-line end of a corridor that was open, nine children were sitting on nine hearth stones with one of them propped on a strip of crate board, and the fire in the corner of that room had not been lit in nine weeks, and the light in it was off nine stones, and the room was open, and it was open under a page with sixteen rows and no name anywhere on it, and the condition on it was checked every morning the room was used and four mornings a week in an empty room by a woman who says a sentence to a building about a stone nobody is standing near.**

**Off a board at a gate four days up the middle passage was a form, and the board was still there, and the seal on the road was still there, and the office had not conceded a word, and a courier had said out loud in an anteroom that it was not a win and that a competent person at a counter had told them how long their own rule lasts.**

**Read out loud in a room to a stranger, with no copy given, was a page, and a person had said her own name out loud as the person who may stop it, and it went in a clerk's book in a coat, and a physician had put his name against a refusal in the same book, and sixteen entries on a board had become something that was not a null and not a claim and not a mark and not a row and not a plan.**

**On the anteroom step in a coat he had not taken off since Friday sat Marek Venn, with a left hand that will not close and a mark on the inside of his forearm, and nobody was sitting near him, and he was not on the rota, and nobody in this building has asked him to do anything since Tuesday and nobody is going to.**
