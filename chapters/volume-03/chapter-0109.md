# Chapter 109 — Behind A Good Reason

On the fourth day of the twenty-first week the boundary found out what a form is for, and it was not to tell anybody anything.

It had been in the book since Wednesday afternoon. Dalla Sen read it out at the first bell, standing, in the anteroom, and read it the way she reads everything, which is twice, and then read out the second thing on it in the flat voice she keeps for the parts she does not have an opinion about.

"**The water-lock at the second gate is closed for survey and is attended. A person may attend the survey on giving notice at the counter. Notice must be given in person. This notice is not dated.**

"**There are four households on the near side of the lock. Water is not to be drawn from the sill while the survey is in progress. The office will provide a butt and a hand-pump at the counter for the use of any person attending, on the day of the survey only.**"

For a while there was no sound in the room at all. Then Mikka Hale said the sentence the whole of the room turned on.

"**Read that last part again and then say out loud what it is doing.**

"**The office will provide a butt and a hand-pump at the counter. On the day of the survey only.**"

"**It is providing water,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**It is providing water at a counter, on one day, to people who can walk to a counter, and the survey is not one day. A survey of a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood is two days and it may be three. And the word for the day it is happening is *attended*, and attended means a man is in a hut on the far side of a gate with a book, and the office has told us that the man is there.**

"**So the man is behind the door. The water is in front of it. And the four of them are behind the door with the man.**"

---

**They went through it in the order this room goes through things, and the order is that nobody decides anything until everybody has said the thing they came to say, and the thing Renn Vale said was the one nobody had thought of.***

"**It is not our door,**" she said. "**Nobody in this room has any standing to say one word about that lock and I want that said first, before anybody gets pleased with themselves about the road.**

"**The Cartwrights hold a clearance over that sill. A survey said it should be looked at. The survey is a survey, and it was a survey somebody paid for, and the closing of a water-lock while a sill is examined is the safest thing anybody could possibly do with a water-lock, and whoever did it did it correctly, and I would like this room to understand that I have been trying for two days to find the mistake in it and there is not one.**"

"**Then it is a good closure,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**It is a good closure. That is the whole difficulty. It is a good closure made by a competent office, on a clearance, for a good reason, with a man in a hut, and it is a door, and four households are behind it, and two of them are brothers and one is their sister and the fourth house only carries and does not walk.**

"**And the fourth house is the one I am going to be asked about, so I will say it now: the fourth house has to be carried to a counter and carried back, and it is not carried by us, because we have nine children in a room at the far end of a fill and one cook and a woman with a rule and a boy with a crutch and a man whose right hand does not close.**"

---

**And then the four-sentence procedure came off the anteroom table and got used, which is the first time a document this boundary has written has been used inside the boundary, and it was used badly, and it was used anyway.**

Bel Ordry did it properly, out loud, in front of the room, off the clerk's copy, which is what was on the table, the way he said he would.

"**One. A person of that office who asks is answered in a room, and to a person, and not in a corridor and not through a child. Two. The plan is a page in four parts and it is in a coat and it does not come out of this building. Three. Any writing that leaves this room is written out in words by the clerk. Four. This may be shown to that office and to no other body.**

"**And here is the problem and I am going to say it because I wrote the thing and I am not going to be the man who finds out later that it did not say what I thought.**

"**It says when that office asks. It does not say what to do when a *different* office closes a door four days up a road, and there are two offices in this building's life now, and one of them wrote four sentences on Friday and the other one wrote a form, and the form is better than the four sentences, because the form has a mark on it and the four sentences have nobody's.**"

Renn Vale had been sitting on the landing through all of it and she said one word.

"**Half.**"

"**You have read the whole of one and a half of them,**" she said. "**You have left out the three lines I wrote into the second one, which are the only reason the second one is not a refusal, and you have left out the tail of the third, which is the sentence that says what a thing is and not what it is for. And you have read them off the copy on the table.**"

"**I read them off the copy on the table,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because the original is in a coat, and I did not notice, and I am not going to say that I would have got it right off the original, because a rule that only works in the hands of the man who wrote it is a rule with one holder, and this building has spent nineteen weeks refusing to have one.**"

Dalla Sen put the two pages side by side on the anteroom table, in front of the room, and read both, and they were the same words, and the reading had not been.

"**Entered,**" she said, "**and entered that the two pages agree with each other and that a man reading one of them out loud in a room did not say all of it, and entered that the clause he dropped is the clause about what a thing is for, and that nobody in this room would have known that except the woman who wrote it. Write it next to the anteroom's rating and the word *door*, because they are the same sentence and this building has not noticed that for a month.**"

"**Then amend it,**" said Iven Tallo.

"**No,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and that is the first time in twenty weeks I have said no to a sentence that was obviously right, and the reason is that the four sentences were written in a room with nine people in it about a woman who stood on a step. They are not general. If I amend them on a Thursday because something went wrong four days away, then next month I will amend them again, and a document that gets amended every time it is inconvenient is the guild's counter-draft with better manners.**"

---

**And then they did the small true things, one at a time, out loud, and refused the large one in the same breath, and the refusal was entered.**

**One.** The water-carrying goes down the nine steps and through the school door and along the corridor. It carries six pails a day, every day in seven, in two crossings, four and then two. **The boundary will not change it and it will not add to it and nobody will go down the middle passage and back with a pail, because the road is open and may not be shut for a delivery and nobody may be charged for it, and that is the one thing in this building that is not in dispute and it stays exactly as it is.**

"**Say what that means in a plain sentence,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**It means we are not sending them anything, because the thing we would send is six pails a day that we already carry for ourselves, and if we carry eight we do not have eight, we have six and a bad afternoon,**" said Mikka Hale.

**Two.** The two jars a day are not sent. **They have never been sent. They are in her separate numbers and not in the pot and they are the boundary's only found drinking water and a man of twenty-two drinks one of them.**

"**Enter that I said no before I was asked,**" said Mikka Hale, "**because I am going to be asked, and because a no said first is a no and a no said second is a reaction.**"

**Three.** A copy of the two sentences went up the middle passage on a courier's hand, in the clerk's hand, unsigned and undated, and Bel Ordry did not put his ring on it, and when Dalla Sen said so out loud he said why in one sentence and did not argue.

"**I have cut a ring into paper for twenty weeks because I keep a page in a coat and a page wants a man on it. I am not putting it on this. **A ring on a sheet is a mark on a sheet, and a mark on a sheet is a thing a person can be found by, and I have spent nineteen weeks arguing that no person in this Hearthhold may be found by a mark, and I am not going to be the first one to break it in a bad week.****

"And I would like it entered that I wanted to, and that the reason I did not is that a cook stood in front of me and made it a question.****

Dalla Sen entered it, and entered that the sheet carries a clerk's routing mark and no other mark of any kind, and that she had told the keeper of the page that he was going to be asked about his ring before he made the decision and had not, and entered that she had not known she was going to do that when she started.****

"**And I want the next thing read out and I want it read out by the man who is going to be asked for it,**" said Dalla Sen, and she read it, and then she said it in her own words, because she is the clerk.

"**This is a copy. It is not the keeping of a promise. The keeper said he would say a thing about four households to their faces, at a counter, in person, before the first of the month, and he is on a road doing that, and a piece of paper in a courier's hand is not him and does not stand in for him and cannot be stood in for, and this room knows the difference and has entered that it knows the difference, and the difference is the whole of what has been lost by sending a piece of paper instead of a man.**"

Nothing moved for a moment, and then Bel Ordry said:

"**And nobody sends after him.**

"**He is four days up a middle passage and then on past a gate, and there is no way to recall a man who is not carrying a thing you can run after him with, and I want it entered that this room considered it and refused it, and that the refusal was not because it is expensive. It is not expensive. It is that he said he would say it at the first of the month and not one day before, because a thing said early is a thing that gets repeated wrong on a road, and if we sent after him now we would be spending the only thing he has left to spend.**"

---

**And then the cost was stated by the person whose job it is to state costs, in her own words, and entered, and it is the sentence this building is going to be arguing about for a long time.**

Mikka Hale stood at her own board with her hand flat on the ten and said:

"**Write this in my words and in my hand and do not shorten it and do not put anything after it.**

"**This boundary found two jars a day in the month before last and kept them. Four people are behind a gate four days up a road with a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood and a competent man in a hut, and they have their own water and they have had it since the cold closed, and they did not ask us for any of this, and they are not going to be able to walk to a counter.**

"**And both of those are true at the same time. And one of them was this boundary's choice. And we are not going to find out today whether it was a good choice and we are not going to be told.**

"**And I have been a cook in this building for twenty weeks and I have put two jars a day into nine mouths and into the mouths of nine children this week, and I would do it again on Friday, and I want it in the book that I would do it again on Friday, because in three weeks somebody is going to stand in that room and say that we knew, and I want there to be a line in here in my hand that says we knew and we did it anyway and we are not going to be told it was wrong by a man in a hut with a book.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it. It is in the book. It is the longest line in the clerk's hand in twenty-one weeks and it is not shortened, and the cook read the whole line out in the room to check it and said yes.

---

**And then Marek Venn asked to go, and could not, and the reason he could not is the shape of this whole building, and he said the shape of it out loud in a room with nine people in it and nobody argued with any part of it.**

"**I want to go. I am going to say that out loud before anybody asks me whether I have thought about it, and then I am going to give the four reasons why I cannot, and I have thought about the four reasons for a day and a night and there is no fifth one that is not a lie.**

"**One. It is four days up a middle passage and then on, and past the second gate there is no number in this building and there never has been, and I have a right hand that will not close and I could not get to that counter and stand at it for a day with a document and be any use to anybody at all.**

"**Two. To get there I would go down nine steps and along a corridor two foot eight wide over eleven feet of shaft, and the slab over that shaft is packed and not held, and the packing is Jo Verrick's knee, and the post under it has never been built in twenty-one weeks. And the next time that slab turns somebody will be under it, and I have said for twenty weeks that it will be Jo Verrick, and I am not going to be the reason it is a different person on the fourth day of the twenty-first week.**

"**Three. I am not on the rota and I cannot stand a watch, and there are nine children in the worst room in this building at the far end of a fill that has moved three times, and if I go up that road then this room has one fewer pair of hands for no gain, and I have been a rescue crew for twenty-one weeks and this is the first time going would cost more than it bought.**

"**Four, and this is the one I am ashamed of, and I am going to say it anyway because the other three are engineering and this one is not.**"

He stood in the anteroom with his right hand flat against his thigh, because that is where it lives now, and let it, and nobody in the room helped him and he had not asked for it.

"**To go up that road I would have to be a person somebody at a counter could send for. And this boundary does not put people in registers, and that is not an accident, it is nineteen weeks of arguing, and it is the best thing we have done. And a boundary that does not put people in registers cannot go and get anybody, and there is nobody in this Hearthhold I could stand up here and appoint, and there is nobody outside this Hearthhold who has any reason to be appointed by us.**

"**So I have spent twenty-one weeks teaching a boundary to be exactly the thing that cannot send a man, and today that is the correct thing to be, and it has cost four households a counter they cannot reach, and I do not know what to do with that and I am not going to pretend I know.**"

And then, because there was a quarter of a bell left and nobody had anything else to do, he said the other thing, which he had been carrying since Thursday, and he said it standing up and out loud, because a thing like that has to be said in a room or it has not been said.

"**The ordinary means for that frame are gone, and I am going to say that they are gone in a room, because nobody is going to know it for a month otherwise.** **There is no timber in this Hearthhold and none is coming. There is no mortar of the right kind in this Hearthhold and the only thing anybody can pack with is clay, and clay shrinks as it dries, and Jo Verrick has said twice, in her own words, that it holds and is not fixed. There are six sound wedges left after Thursday and two dead sticks, and one of the six is in the door head of a room nobody is in. My right hand will not close. My left hand closed properly on the Monday and it burned on the night of the reader, and it is serviceable today, and I am not going to put it on anything in the dark.**

"**And that is the whole of what there is. I am out of ordinary answers for that frame, and there is exactly one thing left that is not a reading, and I told this room in the nineteenth week that anybody who comes and asks me to read a boundary is going to be told no, and that is a different sentence from this one and I am not confusing them. And I am not going to do the thing I have just described. And I want the room to hear me say no to it while I am standing here, and I want it written down that I said no.**"

Dalla Sen wrote all of it down, and then she read the last sentence of it back and stopped, and then she said, "**Entered, in your words, in full. And I am going to enter one more thing and I am going to say it as a clerk and not as a person who thinks she knows more than an engineer. I did not understand the second half of it. I have written it down and I do not understand it, and I am not going to pretend that I do, and I am not going to ask you at the sixth bell. I would like it noted that the man has just told a room that he has one thing left that he is not going to do, and that not one person in this room can say what it is.**"

"**Good,**" said Marek Venn.

He went and wrote it in his own book, one line, and the line is in the book and it is the worst line in it and it says: *I have made this building into something that cannot fetch anybody, and it is better than what it was, and four people are behind a gate, and both of those are true, and I am the man who made it, and I am not going to put this anywhere except here.*

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the six things, and one of them was new, and she read it last.**

"**A closure is a post only while its condition is happening.**

"**There is a man in a hut at the second gate and the hut is the post and the man is in it and the post is real and it is being kept by the office, and the office is right, and nobody in this building can go and be that man.**

"**And the water-lock is not a door of ours and we cannot open it and we are not going to claim we can. And nobody in this Hearthhold is a post that can stand a watch on a road four days long, and I am entering that as a fact about the boundary and not as a thing anybody feels bad about at the fifth bell.**"

A man of twenty-two was forty-four days and forty-four nights into a count that is his own, in a column with nothing over the top of it, and he was not asked to read the notice and he was not asked to go anywhere, and the corridor was shut and the sixth minute went and nobody was standing at the low end of it.

**And the road up the middle passage stayed open, and nobody was charged for anything on it,** and nobody was sent, and the fourth house does not walk, and a courier's sheet that carries paper and small goods and no people went down the passage with a clerk's hand's worth of words on it, and the man who wrote them is on a road with the originals in his coat, and four days of passage and then on, and nobody in this building has said when.
