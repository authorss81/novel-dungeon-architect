# Chapter 198 — The Reasons First

The sixth day of the thirty-seventh week is a Saturday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the forty-first morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and nine children went up the nine steps at the first bell and nine came down them at the fourth and went round the corner of the anteroom floor without being told to, which is a thing nine children now do.

No market came into this building on this day and nobody here went looking for one. The one thing anybody knew about a sixth day arrived in a woman's mouth at the first bell, and what she had brought was a fact about a counter.

It was a warm morning for the depth of it, which happens twice, and the flags by the kitchen-line door had gone from cold to cool by the fourth bell, and there was a smell of the seam off the back of the anteroom frame that four people in that room could smell and one of them had stopped being able to. The lamp on the wall was not lit, because the lamp on the wall is not lit until a fifth morning and this was not a fifth morning. Along the common landing, through the corridor, a girl of nine was learning a thing about nine stones and kept stopping to look at the ninth one, and nobody in the anteroom could hear what she was being told and all of them knew it was about the ninth one.

"**On a sixth day the carriers come down off the road,**" said Nella Bray, at the first bell, with her slate under her arm. "**And that is the one day of the week a counter at a middle gate has a queue in it made up of people who are not clerks. A person who wants to be heard at the end of such a day is heard after four people who have been in a carriage for two days, and all four of them want the same thing, and all four of them leave. So the end of a sixth day is the best hour there is and it is the hour nobody plans for, because it is not in the column.**"

"**Entered, as a fact about counters, and not as a finding,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a day of the week is the reason a person would walk up a passage on it, and that this is the first time in thirty-seven weeks that a day of the week has been entered in this book as a reason for anything at all, and I want it findable.**"

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the eighteenth reading, and the same as seventeen, and the string was where the string was. She did not put the rule in the join, and she gave the reason again for anybody who had not been in the room for it, which is that a rule belongs in a joint and a joint is not a gap, and Tams Quill wrote nothing on his knee.

Renn Vale watched the line in the party wall at the first bell, which is her bell on an open morning, and was there about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the clerk entered a figure of nothing and entered that she was not giving the run a number.

The anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and the pump was attended and stopped on a rising water line chalked on the frame that has still not moved.

Two pieces of paper were on the trestle by the second bell, and neither of them had come into the building that morning.

The first was this boundary's own copy of a form that had come off a board at a market gate in the twenty-ninth week, and had come back on a board in a month by a clerk who did not know about the Tuesday, with a seal number on the corner of it that was not the number it had had before. The second was six lines in Dalla Sen's own hand, written out on the fourth day of the thirty-fourth week and read back against the original by a woman who reads walls, and the six lines were a form that had been filled in correctly by a competent clerk with one name in both of its two lines.

"**Neither of those is going anywhere on its own,**" said Nella Bray, "**and I have known that since the twenty-ninth week, and I have not said it out loud in this room before because saying it out loud in this room makes it a thing this room has agreed to, and I have watched four hundred rooms agree to things in a morning and go on doing what they were doing by the afternoon.**"

Bel Ordry gave the half against from the step, where he had not moved from.

"**That is a sentence I have said in nineteen years and never once in a room, and it will not be softened because a man who keeps a page has heard it. It is a true sentence and it is the reason a thing said out loud in this anteroom has never left this anteroom, and today it is going to leave this anteroom, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that the sentence I have been using to keep things in is the sentence I am now using to get one out.**"

Nobody improved on that, which is how the room knows it landed.

"**The whole of the mechanism, in one breath, and then nobody is going to interrupt me for four minutes,**" she said, and she put the slate down on the trestle and left her hand on it. "**Two seals. Both of them the cartel's, and not this boundary's charter, which puts no holder against any route and could not be the thing that lost anything, and I want that said in a room before anybody in this room gets pleased about it. The first seal is the recognised clearance seal on the market gate's form, and a clearance seal is what makes a piece of ground a road that anybody can be responsible for. The second is the route insurance's seal on the second of those two forms, and the reason there is a second one is that the insurance office is a different office and prints its own, and a man in a carriage can be right about both and be refused by both.**"

"**And what is being asked of this room is nothing. That is the part I have to say first, because I have watched people hear the words *two seals* and start planning. Nothing is being asked of this room. A person is going to stand in front of a person at a counter with a form, and the reasons are going to be said out loud in this room first, because that is how it is done, and I have watched it done twice, and neither time was it done in a room.**"

"**The reasons have to be good enough. That is the whole of the standard and there is no other one. Not true enough. True is free. Good enough means the person saying them does not lose their hands, and a person who loses their hands over a form is a person who was sent, and this boundary has spent thirty-seven weeks refusing to have anybody sent anywhere to make a thing true, and the page at the low end is blank and carries three conditions and the third of them says it in a sentence I have never had to say out loud in this room until now.**"

She let the pump get a stroke in.

"**So. A person. On a road. On a sixth day, because the passage is shut on the first, second and fourth days and the counter is shut on the seventh, and a person who walks on a third day arrives on a day the office is shut.**"

Then she named them, and she named them standing up, and she did it in the order a clerk would give a list.

"**Emrys Callow, who is a shoring foreman and not a keeper of anything and not a clerk and not the man with the page in his coat, and who has five men of his own in this building and eleven days of not taking the middle gate's work, and the reason he has not taken it is that he has none and he stopped looking for one on the fourth day. And me, who is a clerk at that counter, and who keeps two rooms off the far end of this anteroom, and whose name is on a slip in a carrier's hand at that gate because I put a form off a board six weeks ago and wrote two sentences in a clerk's book about it.**"

"**I am not a hero and I have said so in this room,**" she said, "**and neither is he, and he would tell you the same about himself in his own mouth and would be right.**"

Then Bel Ordry gave the half against, and he gave it before anybody could improve on it, and he gave it from the step.

"**A person who is none of those three things is a person an office can use. You have said a competent man and a competent woman and you have said that neither of them is a hero, and neither of those is the same as a person who can be put down in front of a counter and used as an example.**" He came down one step, which is more than he does in a week. "**And the half against the whole of it is that we do not know one single thing about what happens at that counter, and I have spent thirty-seven weeks in this building finding out that a thing nobody in a room knows about is the only kind of thing that can be true and be fatal at the same time, and I would like it entered that I said that before anybody decided and not after.**"

The clerk wrote it down while he was still on the step. "**Entered, in his words, and entered that he gave it before the decision rather than after it.**"

Then the foreman stood up off the bench, and he had not said anything for two days, and the room had stopped noticing that, which it had done about him on Thursday and had been wrong to do.

"**Then here is the whole of it in my own words, and it is going to take a while, and nobody is going to help me with it, and that is on purpose.**"

He did not touch the trestle.

"**I asked for six seasoned shores in the thirty-fifth week off a store four days up that passage and five of them came down and one of them was six feet of seasoned shore with a head that had lost about a finger of its length by the time it got here, and I took that one out again in eleven minutes and I have not been able to give a reason for it since. That is the fact. The fact is true, the fact is mine, and the fact is a figure of five and a half where a district's book says six, and it has been a figure of five and a half in a clerk's book in this building for weeks and it has not gone into a district's book, and it is not going to, because a fact that goes into a district's book goes in on a piece of paper and a piece of paper has a day of our choosing on it and a column in it, and I have watched what a column with a person's name over it does to a man I work beside.**"

"**And that is the whole of the reason and I have never said it in a room because a room is not a district.**"

The pump got four strokes in and nobody in that anteroom used one of them.

"**So I am going to say the two reasons, and then somebody who has stood at that counter is going to tell me whether they would get a man's hands taken for saying them, and if the answer is yes then I am not saying them and we are all going to sit here for another fortnight and be right.**"

"**One. A road people use is not a road a person is responsible for a load on.**" He held up one finger. "**That is not my sentence and it is not hers either and it is the rule, and the rule is a rule about loads. A person going to a market with a basket is not a load. A person going up a road with somebody else's pot in a bundle is not a load. A person with a survey lamp and a line and a note-book is not a load, and I would like the woman at the counter to hear me say that last one, because I have been told for eleven weeks that it is one, and I have believed it for about six of them.**"

"**And the reason that gets a form withdrawn is not that anybody lied. It is that the form asks for a name in the second line and there is nothing on that ground for the second line to be the name of, and a form that cannot be answered correctly is a form that cannot be used. That is a sentence about a form. I have not said one word about the office and I never will.**"

"**Two.**" He put the second finger up. "**The second form is correct. Every line of it is correct and the seal on it is a real seal and the woman who filled it in did her work on an ordinary day with an ordinary pen, and the name in the first line is the name in the second line, and that name belongs to a post-holder of a cleared-risk office who does not carry loads and has never been asked to and does not know that a form exists with his name in both lines of it.**"

He put the hand down.

"**So the second reason is that an office cannot certify a person it cannot produce, and I am not withdrawing the ground and I am not opening it, and the ground is still a working road on their paper, and the person responsible for the load on it is a person nobody has ever found. If the seal stays on that form then the office is standing behind a man who does not exist, and the only way out of that is the seal, and the seal is theirs to take off.**"

"**And neither of those two sentences says anybody was wrong,**" he said. "**That is the part I want checked. If one of them has to say somebody was wrong to work then it is not good enough and I am not going up that passage.**"

Nella Bray had not moved her hand off the slate.

"**Neither of them has to. And that is not a small thing and I want the room to hear how small it is. Both of those are about a form and neither of them is about a person, and that is the only reason a person can say them out loud at a counter without losing anything, and the whole of what is going to happen at the end of this week is that two competent people are going to be told the truth about two pieces of paper by a person who has nothing to gain and is carrying them.**"

"**And the half against that, which is mine and which I have to give because I am the one who knows what a counter does with a person. They will not take the seals off. That is not me being pessimistic, that is me telling you what an office does. An office does not take a seal off. An office tells a person that the seal stands, and it is correct, and the seal is the insurance office's and not the Cartwrights', and there is no person in that building who is going to lose a wage over a piece of wax because a foreman from four days down a passage asked.**"

"**So what is actually going to happen is that a person is going to be refused, and a person who is refused comes back the next open day with the same reason, and is refused again, and comes back, and nobody in that office is a villain, and every one of them is a person with a wage and a family and a form that says what a building is permitted to hold, and the whole of the mechanism is that a person has to be embarrassed enough to keep turning up.**"

"**And the market road does not come back. I want that said before anybody starts hoping. The road is not the point. The road was never the point. The point is that a form requiring a named person responsible for a load cannot be used on a road people use, and the day that is written down somewhere is the day this cartel has lost the first thing it ever had, and it will not be written down in this room.**"

Then Mero Senn said it, from the doorway, in the shape he has.

"**A person going up a road with two forms in his coat is a person on a working road, and there is a named person responsible for a load, and the load is two forms, and the person responsible is him.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"**And that is not a joke and I am not making it,**" he said. "**I am telling a room that the mechanism the two seals are aimed at does not stop for the man carrying the thing that aims at it. A plan with a named person in it is not a plan. He is not going to be crushed by this. He is going to be found, and being found is worse, and if he is not back in a week then the person standing at that counter has a name and a description and a reason to write both down and neither of those is anybody in this room.**"

"**The half against that,**" said Iven Tallo from the anvil end, and he had the chisel in his right hand and his left open and flat on the anvil where it has lain all month, "**is that a man who goes up a road with two forms in his coat is not a load, and that this boundary has spent thirty-seven weeks refusing to put a name in a second line and it is not going to start by putting his in one at the top of a passage. And nobody thanks me for that and nobody blames me for it either, and I would like the clerk to enter both of those in the space where a reason goes and to put nothing beside them.**"

"**Entered, in a carpenter's words, and entered that neither of them is a reason and that a clerk has been asked for two things that are not reasons and has been given two things that are not reasons,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the man who said them is the man who wrote a refusal in this room on Thursday evening and that I am entering that again and not because it is news.**"

Tams Quill turned the piece of chalk he has not used over twice while the reasons were going, and Dalla Sen saw him do it, and she waited until the room had finished talking and then asked.

"**A nineteenth entry.**" "**No.**" and he did not look up.

"**Reason,**" with the chalk held up and its point resting against the knuckle of her other hand.

"**Two seals.**" He turned the chalk over once in his hand and shut it away. "**A seal is a thing that is on a thing, and what I have on this board is things a person can find a room by looking at a piece of slate instead of walking into it, and the day a withdrawal of two seals goes on that board then this board has a second line on it. The first line would say what was withdrawn and the second line would say who withdrew it, and there is no version of that which is not a page with two lines and a heading over them, and this building has been refusing those for thirty-seven weeks and I will not hand one to a man with a piece of chalk.**"

"**That is a sixth reason and it is the best of the six,**" said Bel Ordry from the step, "**and it does not need improving on, and I will say in a room that a man has now refused six things in three weeks and that I do not know what happens when he stops, and that I have been saying that for two weeks and that it is the only sentence I own.**"

"**Then it is a seventh thing and it goes the same way,**" and he put the chalk in his pocket, "**and I am not the man who works out in advance what it is going to be.**"

Dalla Sen entered that he had given none for the column with nothing over the top of it, and that she had not asked, and that a man who has refused six things on six different grounds read a board out loud on a Saturday and refused nothing else, and that she was not going to keep a figure of any of it.

The pump ran from the second bell to the fifth and stopped on the chalked line, and nobody in that anteroom counted its strokes, and the six terms were said again before the anteroom filled at the fourth bell and not after, because they are said before and not after whatever else is happening. The anteroom filled. Eleven people live in this Hearthhold and six more do not, and on a sixth day all of them were in the same room with a man going out of it, and a woman at the kitchen-line doorway came in for the first time that morning and stood against the wall by the pump with her arms folded, and stayed there for the rest of it, and nobody asked her why she had come in.

Then it was the anvil end's business and it was not quick.

"**I am going to say the one thing I have and then I am going to go and stand at the other end of the room,**" Iven Tallo said, "**because the man who built the brace over that door is in this room and nobody is going to ask me about the brace today, and if anybody does, I will say that the air in that room has not changed.**"

Ilsa Norr came out of the doorway with her slate in both hands and said nothing at all for about four strokes of the pump, and then gave the sentence she had come out with.

"**Then the clerk enters that the condition stands and that I have not asked him and that nobody in this room is going to ask him, and that it has been unexamined since the fourth day of the thirty-sixth week, and that a man going up a passage on Saturday is not a reason I would use to examine it, and that if he is not back then the condition does not get easier, it gets harder, and I will want it said in a room that I said that on a Saturday and not on the day after.**"

Nobody asked him anything.

Then the going was arranged, and it took eleven minutes, and the room did most of it.

Emrys Callow named one of his five to hold for the four days. He did it out loud, in his own mouth, without a slate. The man he named gave a sentence back, and nobody wrote either of them down. The clerk entered that a foreman had named a person in a room and had not written the name, and that this is the first time in thirty-seven weeks a person has been named here out loud and left off a page, and that she did not ask him to write it and noticed herself not asking.

"**The terms of it are the terms of the crews' arrangement and they cover him. The check does not cross, because the check has not crossed since Wednesday, and my five check their own work, and I have said what that costs in this room and it will not be said a second time.**"

"**The reason I am the one going is that a clerk at that counter cannot take her own office's seal off her own office's form. She has told me that twice today in about nine words and she has not once made it sound like a favour to her. A person who works in a building is a person the building has already said no to. I do not work in one.**"

"**And I want it entered that whatever happens at that counter, refusing it is not one of the things I will have done. I have not taken it for eleven days and I have not refused it, and those are two different things and I have been standing on the difference for a fortnight without knowing what it was for. If a man offers me six weeks of paid shoring at that gate on Saturday I am going to have to do something with my face, and I would like the room to know in advance that I have not worked out what, and that I have not asked anybody to help me work it out.**"

Then the form went out on the table and two people looked at it, and one of them changed it.

Nera Oduya came down all three of her steps, which she does for air and for nothing else, and had the clerk's chalk in her hand, and struck one line out of the market gate's form in nine seconds, and gave the chalk back, and went up the steps without saying a word.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, and her voice was not steady and she did not pretend it was, "**and entered that she was not asked and gave no reason, and entered that nobody in this room asked her, and entered that this is the fifth time and that I have stopped writing the rest of that sentence twice because there is no rest of it and I have written it four times.**"

The line was out. The form went out of the building in a foreman's inside coat at the fourth bell.

Dalla Sen wrote a single sheet to go with it, and put the day of the week on it and not a date, and gave the reason out loud, which is the same reason she has given twice: a person in a year has to be able to tell a message from a form, and this is neither, and it goes to a man who will have to carry it back if he cannot use it.

"**Nobody is going to hand him a bundle,**" she said, "**and nobody is going to hand him a second line, and the second line of that stiff cover in his coat is empty and it was empty this morning and I am entering that it is going to be empty when that paper is next held by anybody at all, and I have entered what it is going to be once before and I am entering it again and the second time it is not a hope, it is a term.**"

And at the second bell of that Saturday, before anything else in the middle of it happened, the anteroom had emptied the way it empties for a carry, last one first, and nobody could say afterwards what had been carried.

Then what came back into the room came back on the last of it, out of the kitchen-line doorway, from a woman with a slate under her arm who had not said anything for an hour.

"**He has gone up the passage,**" said Nella Bray, "**and I am going to say the part nobody is going to like, and then nothing else about it from me, and I would like it entered that I said it and did not soften it.**"

"**My condition is in my pocket and it has no number and no day on it, and it says I keep those two rooms while the last one out of them is in the other one, and one of the five can stop me by standing up in a room and saying he is not the one keeping them. It does not say what happens when the person who keeps them is four days up a passage with a form in her coat, and I will not stand here and pretend that is the same condition I wrote on Wednesday, because it is not, and because I wrote it in a room and I have never once pretended a thing in a room was better than it is.**"

"**I am not stopping. Nobody is going to help me, and the door to those two rooms latches and the anteroom does not empty into them, and the last one out of them will be in the other one at some point in the next four days, and if he is not back then I will be keeping two rooms from a different building and that is not in my condition and I did not write it into my condition this morning and I did not go back and do it.**"

"**And the half against saying any of it, which is that a room of six people that is not this building's has been kept for a fortnight by two people feeding it and a woman who is going away, and I have just told a room the arithmetic of my own absence out loud, and the reason I told you is not that I wanted it entered. It is that a keeper who says nothing about the four days is a keeper who has decided on her own, and I have watched two of those this month and one of them was a cook and she was right to.**"

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell with his book shut under his forearm, and he had not said anything through any of it. The reason he had not was that the only figure in this morning that unlocks anything is a figure he has been carrying since the twenty-eighth week and has not given to anybody. A figure of what is behind a wall is a figure of what is in a room, and this boundary refused that in five mouths five days ago, and it was right five times.

And the thing he had not said, and would have said if he had trusted himself with it, was the plainest thing in the morning. The man going up the passage had four days of walking in him. Marek Venn had a knee that does not bend past about half and a mark on the inside of his forearm from a channel frame in the twenty-eighth week, and he had known the first of those about himself since before the twenty-eighth week, and had told a room in the thirty-fourth that he cannot go down there again and does not know why and is not going to find out.

So the resolution of this morning was being done by a man who could walk, and the man who had found out what was behind the wall would be in this anteroom at the seventh bell on Monday with his hands in his pockets.

That was the whole of the cost of the going. It belonged to nobody, and there was nobody in the room it did not belong to.

At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's figure and the total in the book and read neither of them out, and gave the reason, which was that a person went out of this building at the fourth bell with two pieces of paper in his coat and this building does not yet know one single thing about whether either of them worked, and a clerk does not read a total out loud on a morning whose whole content is somewhere else.

Three today and two hundred and ninety-seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and fourteen and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

Under that, in the ordinary way and in the ordinary hand, the count went in where it goes, under a line that has no word on it and never will have one. Wen Pask is one hundred and thirty-three days and one hundred and thirty-three nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and thirty-four, and she said out loud, once, to nobody in particular, that it was a number of days and not a number of anything, which is the thirty-eighth week running she has said it in a room, and then she said that she had said it, and had not been thanked, and was not going to stop.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book. The reason is that a room which has this morning put the only thing it had out of the building through a door is not going to turn round in the same hour and hand five men a piece of work. A room that has just sent something away and is immediately asking for something back is a room that wants to still be in charge, and this building has spent thirty-seven weeks finding out what that costs.

The pot is two days and the seam gave nothing on the last three mornings and one on Friday, and it is at four threads. Six people who are not this building's ate out of it again today because a cook decided it alone three weeks ago and told a room. The four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for thirteen weeks.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with one piece of paper gone off it that had lain on that trestle since the twenty-ninth week, and the dust of nine children's feet going round the hole in the corner. Along the bench were the packs of a crew of five and one space where a sixth pack had been standing on Friday, and the space was not filled, and nobody moved anything into it. On the table was a stranger's sheet with a wax head at each end of it, lying where it had lain for three weeks, and under two documents that had lain in the same order doing nothing about each other since a Friday in the thirty-fourth week was a chalk line with nothing written under it.

A man with a shut book under his forearm was standing at the end of the trestle. A woman with a slate had three fingers on a piece of paper in her pocket and was not going to take it out this evening. And four days up a middle passage, in an inside coat, a foreman was carrying two pieces of paper with a name nobody was going to write in the second line of either of them.
