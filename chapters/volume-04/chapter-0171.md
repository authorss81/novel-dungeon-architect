# Chapter 171 — The Man Who Came Up Nine Steps

**Open for the corridor on the sixth day of the thirty-third week, a Saturday, and the school did not run, because the school runs on the three days the corridor is open and a sixth day has not been one of them for a long time and nobody has put it back, and a man of thirty-four was sitting on the bench at the top of the nine steps at the first bell with his hands loose and nobody had sent for him.**

**He has his own two clauses and he is owed the right to say them and he does not have to be liked. Nobody asked him where he was. A clerk read out loud a refusal this room made a long time ago and did not write down one number while she wrote down another.**

A sixth day in that building is a day the road four days up the middle passage is busy and the building is not, and the anteroom is the level above the waist with a pump under it and a trestle against the wall and a piece of a school's own beech going across the head of the outer service door a foot and a half lower than the lintel.

The brace had been standing for three days and nobody had stood under it. The cord at the joint was the crew's own spent nine-metre line and it had gone a little slack in the middle in the way a spent line does, and a carpenter who is not in the room can be relied on to have said something about that on Friday. Under the head end, in a hollow worn in nine-hundred-year-old stone, a beech shim a finger wide and four inches long was carrying four tons on its two long edges, and the carpenter's term about it was in a clerk's book in four hands, and the term said about two years and did not say who.

Jo Verrick had read the frame at the first bell the way she reads it every morning now, and the number was a finger and two-thirds, and she entered it without saying anything about it, because she had said everything there was to say about it on Friday and had been right and had been told to put it in a book and not in a column.

Then a man's voice came up the nine steps and stopped, and it was a voice that did not come up those steps every day, and nobody in that anteroom had heard it in a long time.

"**Say what you are, and say it before anybody asks you, because a man who comes up nine steps without saying what he is is a man who wants the room to guess,**" said Dalla Sen, without looking up from the book.

"**Sefu Aran. Elected route keeper of this boundary. I can shut it.**"

"**Entered, and entered the time, and entered that nobody in this building sent for him and that nobody has gone up the passage to see whether he was coming.**"

"**Say the second half of that out loud,**" said Renn Vale, from the fourth step.

"**The second half is that this room decided a long time ago not to write down how long he has been gone, and that decision stands, and I am not going to have it quietly improved because a man has walked into it.**" Dalla Sen put the chalk down. "**I am the clerk. I read the finding out when it is made. And I have never once written that number, and I will not start on a Saturday with a man standing at the bottom of nine steps.**"

Nobody wrote it. Marek Venn watched four people decide not to write a number and thought that he had spent thirty-three weeks learning to want a number put down and had never once been in a room that refused one and got the refusing right.

The man on the bottom step came up four of the nine and stopped there, and the pump got four strokes in behind him, and nobody offered him the bench because the bench at the top of the steps is a boy's bench and everybody in that room knew it.

"**I have two things to say,**" he said, "**and I am going to say them in order, and I am not answering a question about them afterwards, and if the room has a question then the room can have it after.**"

"**Say them,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step he stood on every morning of his life.

"**One. I am not taking anything back. Every decision this boundary made without me stands, and I will not overturn one of them, and I will not stand in this room and say I would have done it differently, because I do not know that and I have not earned the right to say it.**"

"**And the other half of it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have not said it and it is the half that costs.**"

"**The other half is that I am not going to be given this week. The brace, the terms, the two wedges, the wall, the school, the count, the pot, all of it, and I know every one of those is in your books, and I will not be handed the weeks as though I had been standing in the room holding them.**" He turned his head a quarter turn towards her. "**The room kept itself. Say that first, before anything else, and put it on the day you write the day on.**"

"**I do not put a day on anything,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Then say it without a day. The room kept itself, and the man who was its keeper was not in it, and the two facts are going to be next to each other in your book for as long as there is a book.**"

Renn Vale came down one step, which she does not do for people she has known for a long time.

"**Say the half against your own first clause, because this room has been asking you to do that since you were on the fourth step, and you have not done it yet and I am doing it for you.**"

"**The half against it is that it is cheap,**" said Sefu Aran. "**A man who has been away is allowed to come back generous, and generosity after a long absence costs nothing, and it is the only thing in this room that has cost nothing for a long time. I would rather you had refused me and had me here.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he said it before it was asked, and that I am not going to put a reason beside it,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then Bel Ordry stood up off the step all the way, which he does perhaps twice in a month, and had the objection ready and said it in his own shape.

"**I have got one, and I am going to say it before you say your second thing, because you have not said it yet and I would rather this room had it while you are still standing there.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**Your second thing is that you are going to find out who did a keeper's job here while you were not in it.**" Bel Ordry had his hand on his coat where the page was. "**Say the rest of it. Say that you are going to find out by making a list.**"

"**I did not say a list.**"

"**You said *find out*, and a man finds out in this building by writing something down, and you are an elected officer of a body that writes things down about people, and so the second thing you have come up nine steps to say is that you are going to start a list. I want the room to have that said out loud on a Saturday before it becomes a fact on Monday.**"

"**Say the half against your own objection, and do it now, because a man who hears half of an objection does not get a room out of it.**"

"**The half against it is that I keep a list. Sixteen rows, four parts, and a date in the top corner, and I have said out loud for thirty-three weeks that a page in a coat is not a system and that I am the only person who has to say it.**" He did not take his hand off the coat. "**And the half against the half against it is that my list is of rooms. Somebody is going to come to this boundary one day and ask me what I keep, and the honest answer is a plan, and a plan is not a person, and that has been the answer for thirty-three weeks and it has not once cost me anything to say.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that the second clause was not said until a man on a step had objected to it, and that I would rather have the objection in the book than the clause in the room without it.**"

Then he said the second clause, and it was not a list.

"**Two. A room that has been getting on without a keeper has still had a keeper's work done in it every single day. And the somebody who did it was not elected, and nobody is going to be found out this week, and it will not be done in this room, and it will not be done with a piece of paper.**"

Nobody said anything for about three strokes of the pump. On the fourth step a woman who keeps a corridor had put her slate down on her own knee, and nobody in that anteroom had ever seen her put it down on anything.

Renn Vale said it first and she said it flat, in the voice she uses for a term.

"**I did some of it.**"

"**You did a great deal of it, and so did five other people, and I have listed nobody, and this morning is not the morning I start, and this room does not turn six people into a row on my account.**"

"**I am not going to be a row,**" said Renn Vale, and the words came out with something in them that Marek Venn had not heard in her voice before and could not have named.

At the fourth bell the anteroom's terms were said, and the clerk read them, and the word was said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and it did not change, and the pump was attended, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and then she came to the fifth.

"**The air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it,**" said Dalla Sen. "**That is the fifth, and it is the fifth of six, and the sixth was added on a landing and stays where it was put.**"

She put the chalk down and looked up, which she does perhaps four times in a month.

"**Say the fifth term out loud, a second time, and say it as it is written and not as it is remembered, and then somebody who was not in this room is to read it against the book and tell me whether it is the same.**"

Bel Ordry read it against the book. It was the same, and Dalla Sen entered that it was the same.

"**Entered,**" she said. "**The anteroom's fifth term has not been able to be kept since the middle of the twenty-sixth week, because the only person in this building who could keep it has not been in this building. It is kept this morning. It is the first morning since the middle of the twenty-sixth week that it has been able to be kept at all, and it has been kept out loud in a room with a person in it.**"

"**Say how long,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Since the middle of the twenty-sixth week. In a book. In the shape this boundary keeps its worst facts in, which is a finding and not an accusation.**"

"**Say the second half of that,**" said Dalla Sen, to herself, in the voice she uses when she is reading back what she has written, and the room listened the way it always listens to that voice. "**A finding about a room and not about a man. I will not write that anybody failed, because nobody in this room failed. I will not write that anybody was owed, because I do not know that and it is not a clerk's call. And I am not going to write down how long he has been gone. That decision was this room's and not mine, and it stands in the same book on the same morning. I have been asked twice this week whether I will put the two next to each other, and my answer is that the two are not the same thing, and I am putting them next to each other anyway.**"

And Marek Venn, standing at the end of the anteroom table with his own book shut on his forearm, looked at the page she had written and did the arithmetic he had not wanted to do.

Since the middle of the twenty-sixth week. Seven weeks and change, and the room had twice refused to write that figure down about a man, and the clerk had just written it down about a room, in the same book, on the same morning, in the same hand.

They were the same figure. He was the only person in that anteroom who could see that they were the same figure, and he did not say so.

He did not say so because a man who points at two numbers in a clerk's book and says *those are the same* is a man making a claim, and this boundary has spent thirty-three weeks refusing to make claims. He was going to be the last one in the room to start.

Then a road hand said a fact from the bottom of the nine steps without coming in, because a road hand does not come in.

"**At the market this morning, four days up the road, a man was asking after this Hearthhold. Not after the road. After the terms. He had a sheet with him and he had a column on the sheet and he was reading the column out to two people at a table and one of them said this is the one with the schoolroom in it, and the man said the schoolroom is a room, and the other one wrote something down.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything for a moment, and the pump got two strokes in.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen. "**Entered in a road hand's words, at the sixth bell, and entered that it is a fact about a market and not about this boundary. And entered that the road is four days, and that the market is on the sixth day, and that no market is inside this building and nobody in this room is going anywhere to look.**"

Then the woman who cooks asked to be left alone for a moment at the kitchen-line end and the schoolteacher said the thing she says on shut days, which is that the room at the kitchen-line end has nine hearth stones in it and that the fire has now not been lit for eleven weeks, and nobody asked about the fire and nobody asked her about anything else.

At the sixth bell the clerk gave the room the day's number and the total, and gave both of them out loud, and did not walk the board.

"**Two today and two hundred and twenty-three since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and forty and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile. The counter has been at sixteen for a fortnight and I am carrying it and not re-deriving it, because a clerk who re-derives a counter every week is a clerk who would like the week to be a different number.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one, and have not moved since the second day of last week, and the reason is in a room on a Monday this week and not today. A man walking into a room is not a work anybody can be asked five people for, and I am not going to make a sixth asking out of a man's boots.**"

"**The pot is nine days. Jo says the seam gave two yesterday and one on Wednesday and four threads and eight in the nineteenth week, and I have said in five weeks running that the number for how fast that is going is not mine to make, and it is still not mine to make, and I am saying it in my own hand and not in a column.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and six days and one hundred and six nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and seven. And I have noticed the shape of that number this morning before I read it, and I am entering the noticing and not the number, and I would like it recorded that nobody in this room said one word about it at the sixth bell, including me.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is that a keeper is in this room and a board is a column with a heading on it, and I am not bringing the two into the same ten minutes on the day a man comes up nine steps.**"

Two sound wedges are in a brace, one is under the ninth hearth stone's bed, one is in the school corner's door and one is in the basket, and the dead stick has been missing since the twenty-sixth week. The school door at the far end of the steps is shut and there is no wedge in it and it is a sixth door and it is not one of those five, and a woman who keeps it is one person, and the page where a person's hours would go is blank, and nobody was sent anywhere.

The ninth room has no row, is not in the charter, is not in the second sheet, and gets no row this morning. Nobody went down the four treads, and no mark was cut in the stone anywhere in this building. Under a lip on the far side of this corridor there is still a person, packed and not held, with two timber shores above them and a post that has never been built.

**A man of thirty-four came up nine steps on the sixth day of the thirty-third week with his hands loose and nobody had sent for him, and he said two things in a room.**

**The first of them was that the room kept itself and that he was not going to be given the week. The second of them was that a keeper's work had been done in this building every single day by somebody nobody elected.**

**The anteroom's fifth term has been able to be kept for the first time since the middle of the twenty-sixth week, and it was kept out loud on a Saturday morning.**

**A clerk entered that in a book, in the shape this boundary keeps its worst facts in, which is a finding and not an accusation. She entered that the finding is about a room and not about a man, in the same book, on the same morning, next to a refusal that stands.**

**And a man who keeps a page said out loud that he keeps a list, and that his list is of rooms, and that a plan is not a person, and that this has cost him nothing to say for thirty-three weeks.**
