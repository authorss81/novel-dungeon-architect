# Chapter 162 — The Fifth Asking

**Shut for the corridor on the second day of the thirty-second week, a Tuesday, and a clerk asked five people for four and a half hours of a work in a room, with the terms out loud, and named the three she was not going to ask with the reason for each before she asked anybody anything, and the reasons were a fourth set and the names were not the names she used last week.**

**The work is to make a shape out of six pieces of a school's own beech. Four people hold the shape and one person cuts it, and the fourth pair of hands is not yet found, and the terms that are being agreed contain a sentence about a day nobody has told her how to write.**

"**The crew have been asked four times in thirty-two weeks,**" said Dalla Sen, with the book open on the anteroom table and the chalk in her right hand and nothing else on the table, which she had cleared herself and said why. "**Four times a work, and four times the terms were said out loud in a room and a person was named who could be told no. This is the fifth, and I am going to name the three I am not going to ask, and the reason for each, before I ask anybody anything.**"

"**And say before you ask why they are not the same three as last week,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Because the reasons have changed three times in a month and I am not going to use a reason that has stopped being true, and because two of the three last time are not the three this time, and a person who hears the same name come up five weeks running stops hearing the reason.**"

She said the first one without looking at her book, which Renn Vale noticed from the landing and mentioned afterwards.

"**Leena Orr. Her shoulder is in a sling and it takes no weight and that has not changed, and the reason is not the shoulder. The reason is that she is the only person in this building whose whole trade is reading a thing that is already built, and this work is a thing that is not built yet, and the one person in this building who checks my work does not go on the inside of the work.**"

"**Jo Verrick. She reads that wall at a first bell every morning and she reads it before she has done anything else, and the day this brace goes in is the day the wall she reads is a wall with a push coming out of it, and I am not going to have the woman who takes the first reading of a thing have also been one of the four who made it.**"

"**Pell Roke. The reason is not his age and I want that in the book, because his age is the reason everybody expects. The reason is that the work is holding. Four people hold and one cuts. A boy of fourteen on a crutch is not going to be one of the four that hold, and the clerk does not ask a boy on a crutch to watch instead.**"

None of the three reasons was argued with. They went in as they were given, and then she asked the two, and a third person in the room found out where he stood before she got to him.

"**Say whether I am on the list or off it,**" said Tams Quill, "**because you have said the three and I am not one of them and that is the first time in five askings, and I would rather find out from you than work it out.**"

"**You are on it.**"

"**Then say my hours before somebody says them, and say the split, because a clerk who decides a man's hours in a room after the man has not spoken is a clerk who has decided a man's hours.**"

"**Four and a half hours, and I am naming the split. Two of Iven Tallo's, two of Marek Venn's, and half of yours.**"

"**Say why the half is mine.**"

"**Because you are going to be the person who reads the wall and the person who reads the bench, and because the practice is that the man who enters what a thing turned out to be is not one of the four that make it, and you have told that room you would rather be asked than volunteer, so I am asking.**"

Tams Quill did not say thank you, and Dalla Sen entered that he had not, and entered that she had not expected him to.

Then the terms, out loud, with Renn Vale named from the landing as the person who could be told no, and who said yes to that in one word and then told the clerk to come and hear the rest of it, because she wanted to be told no-able in the same breath.

There were nine people in that anteroom and by the time the terms had been read three times there were eleven. Mikka Hale had come through from the kitchen-line with a board under her arm, to hear the anteroom's number spoken out loud in a room instead of read off a pot. A boy had come down two of the nine steps on a crutch and sat on the bottom one with his ear against the stone, which is where he sits, and nobody said his name, because nobody in that room uses a boy's name when there is a number in the air.

"**The work is this. Six cut pieces of beech become one length long enough to go from a stone bed on this side of the floor to the lintel over that school corner's mouth, and a head cut to fit the stone, and a foot cut flat. Nothing else. The work is not repairing the frame. The work is not fixing the door. The work is not opening anything and it is not moving anything, and I want that in the terms in those words, because a brace is a thing that gets confused with a repair by anybody who is tired.**"

"**Say what it is not again, slower, and say the one that is going to be argued about.**"

"**It is not a route. It does not decide where anybody goes and it will not make a room hold more people than it holds now. Ten, including one litter, for no more than two hours, and that is the anteroom's number this morning and it is the anteroom's number after the brace is in, and I would like a person who cooks in this building to say that out loud, and not me, because I am the man who would like a number to go up.**"

"**The pot is nine and I will say it out loud and I will not like saying it,**" said Mikka Hale from the kitchen-line, and that was the whole of her contribution to that morning and she went back to her board.

"**The joint is tied and not pegged. That is the carpenter's decision and it is in the terms, and the terms do not say he is right. They say that is what is going to happen and that if it goes wrong somebody who is not him is going to write down that it went wrong.**"

Then came the part of the terms that the room had not expected, and it came out of Renn Vale on the landing rather than out of the clerk, which is how the book records it.

"**You have said the terms of the fourth asking twice in the last month,**" she said, "**and one of them is that you are not to open anything and one of them is that you are not to move anything and one of them is that you take no wedge and no bar and one of them is that you do not go near the waist's third wedge. Read them again in this room, and then say which of them you are standing behind this morning, because I have a brace coming and two of those four are about a brace.**"

Dalla Sen read them again, word for word, and Iven Tallo read them a second time against what she had read, and the two were the same. She entered that they were the same, and she entered that this was the second time this month a set of terms in this building had been put against an original. She entered that the object this time was not a condition but four sentences a clerk wrote, and she said she was not going to number them again, because the last clerk who numbered them got it right and she did not want to be that clerk twice in one month.

"**All four stand,**" said Dalla Sen. "**All four are in these terms as well, and I want the room to hear me say them a fifth time and then hear me say what happens when they cannot be kept.**"

"**Say that part before you say the rest of the terms,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because the rest of the terms are ordinary and this part is not.**"

"**I have a sentence to add and I wrote it at the second bell and I am going to read it as I wrote it, because I have watched two people in this month improve on a thing in a room and I am not one of them.**"

"**Read it.**"

"**If a day comes when these four cannot be kept, then the day it comes is a day in a room, in front of the people they were written for, and it is not a hole in a floor. Nothing in this asking may be quietly stopped. A work that stops is a work that was stopped in a room by a person who said so.**"

Nobody in that anteroom improved on it, and Marek Venn, who had been listening with his own book shut on his knee, found that he had been holding his breath at the end of it and let it out without meaning to.

Bel Ordry came down two steps, which is as far as he ever came into that room on a working morning, and put the half of the argument against the terms where the room could not miss it.

"**There are two sound wedges lying on that bench and they came out of a school door in this building, and the fourth of these terms says that the five of you take no wedge and no bar.**"

"**Say which of those two things wins,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it before I read the terms a third time, because the third reading is going to be exact and I would rather not have an exact reading of a contradiction.**"

"**Neither wins. The terms bind the five of them and the wedges are this boundary's, and the boundary is not one of the five, and the boundary may do what it likes with its own wedges.**"

"**That is a clerk's answer and it is true and I do not like it,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing. "**You have taken a sentence four people agreed to and found a person it does not apply to, and the person is the one that wants the thing. I want to say what that is, because a room is going to have to look at it one day.**"

"**Say it as the half against, then, because that is what the practice is.**"

"**A boundary that is not one of the five can do what it likes with its own wedges, and can take two of them out of a door it keeps, and can write terms for five other people that forbid it, and can then do the thing itself. That is not a contradiction. That is a door.**"

Dalla Sen wrote for a while and did not read back what she had written, which is the thing she did four times in thirty-two weeks and never on purpose.

"**Entered, and entered that a woman on a landing called it a door, and entered that I am not going to improve on the word, and entered that the terms were written at the second bell and read three times and that the third reading was exact and that I read it anyway.**"

Then Marek Venn said the thing that made a sentence into a term, and it took him longer to get to than anything else he said that week.

"**I have been in this building thirty-two weeks and I have never once asked what happens to a thing I built after I am not here, and I have not asked because I have never been in a room with a thing I built that was still standing after me.**"

"**Say the rest of it, Marek, because you have stopped at the interesting part twice this month.**"

"**The rest is that if this brace is standing in ten years then the five of you are not, and the terms we are agreeing to this morning say nothing whatever about that.**"

"**And say the part you do not like.**"

"**I do not like that I do not want to agree to it, and the reason I do not want to agree to it is that I would be agreeing to it in a room where the only people who can object are people who will be gone.**"

"**Say the other half of that and then sit down.**"

"**The other half is that I refused in nine seconds to put my name on that plan, and nobody in this building has asked me for anything since except three hours of looking at air.**"

"**This morning I have agreed to six sentences in a room with a chalk and no signature at the bottom of them. Agreeing is not signing. I did not know that the difference costs something until this morning.**"

The room let that sit, and then Renn Vale said the answer from the landing in the flat voice she uses for a term.

"**Then write it in the terms, and I will say the rest of what goes with it, and the rest is that somebody who is not one of the five has to be able to stop it, and that is the whole of what a brace is, and there is no way to make a post out of that in a fortnight, and anybody who tries will have a post that is a person they found standing near it.**"

"**Do not want it,**" said Dalla Sen, when he said something too quietly for the room to hold.

"**I did not say that. I am saying the clerk may put it in, because the clerk is right, and because the person who should be frightened of what he wrote is me and not the clerk.**"

"**Enter it in the terms and in the second column of the row it belongs to, in his words and not mine,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And enter that a man asked for a sentence to be made into a term and then said he did not want it.**"

"**And enter that wanting a term made is the most ordinary thing in this book and does not count for anything, and that I am entering his want anyway, because a clerk who enters only the noble half is a clerk who is editing.**"

"**Read the terms one more time and compare them,**" said Renn Vale. "**All of it. And then somebody say out loud what the five of them have agreed to, in one sentence, so a person in a year can read it and know whether it was the same morning.**"

On the third reading the clerk read them and the carpenter compared, and the two were the same, and she entered that they were the same. She entered that this was the fifth asking of this boundary, that it had six sentences in it, that three of the six belonged to a man on a bench and one of the six had been added on a landing by a woman who keeps a corridor, and that she was not going to number the readings again, because the last clerk who numbered them got it right and she does not want to be that clerk twice in one month.

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the board was not walked, and she gave a reason for each in her own hand, and the count was read at ninety-seven.**

"**Two today and a hundred and ninety-nine since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and sixteen and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for fifteen weeks.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one, up four and a half, and that is the fifth time in thirty-two weeks that the crew's column has moved, and I am going to say the split out loud because the split is the part that matters and nobody reads the total. Two hours of Iven Tallo's, two of Marek Venn's, and half an hour of Tams Quill's.**"

"**I entered those and I did not read them, and the reason is not dignity and it is not that a clerk does not read a number out loud. The reason is that I wrote a sentence this morning that I have never written before and I want to read it once on its own before I read anything else, and the number is not what I want to read once on its own.**"

"**The pot is nine and it has not gone up since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and the seam gave one jar on Tuesday, and Jo is four threads and has not measured it in between. A crew of five has been in this building for two days and a pot at nine is being held up by a seam, and I am not going to say that in the same sentence as anything else.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is ninety-seven days and ninety-seven nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be ninety-eight, and I have said in eight weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and I am writing it.**"

"**Nobody has thanked me, and a clerk who is disappointed about that is a clerk who has begun to want something. I am entering that I have noticed it and am not going to do anything about it.**"

"**And I did not walk the board. The reason is that a set of terms was read three times in a room today, and every one of those three readings made somebody's hands into something the board has a column for, and I would rather not have a room holding a board in the same hour it is holding a set of terms that tell four people where their hands go.**"

---

**Four and a half hours were asked of five people on the second day of the thirty-second week and the asking was a room with the terms in it, and the terms are the terms of the fourth asking plus one sentence, and the sentence is that a work that stops is a work that was stopped in a room by a person who said so.**

**A man asked for it to be made into a term and then said he did not want it, and the clerk entered the want and entered that wanting is the most ordinary thing in this book and does not count for anything.**

**A door at the school end of this corridor has no wedge in it and two sound wedges are lying on a bench in this anteroom waiting for a brace, and the terms that are being agreed this morning say that the five of them take no wedge and no bar, and nobody in the room has yet said out loud which of those two things wins.**
