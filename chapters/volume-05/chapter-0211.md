# Chapter 211 — A Term Re-Set And A Second Watch

The anteroom had a term changed in it before the pump was started, and the reason it was before the pump was started is that on a shut day the six terms are said at the second bell and the pump is started at the second bell every day of the week, and the keeper had asked for the one before the other and had given no reason for asking and was not going to be asked for one.

The first day of the forty-first week is a Monday. The corridor was shut. The school did not run and lost nothing. Along the common landing the wall with five and a half shores in it stood on its side of the join doing the only thing it has ever done, and from the second bell to the fifth there was a head of water on the other side of it, three days in seven, and nobody in that room had yet found out which three.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the thirty-first reading, and the same as thirty, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked her, and Dalla Sen entered all four as four refusals and not as one, and the count of mornings is thirty-one and is the same count as the wall's, and the folding rule is in a belt and has been on a belt for thirty-one mornings.

Renn Vale put her ear to the party wall at the first bell for about as long as a sentence takes to say, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and it went into the book, and the run of mornings out of mornings is sixteen.

Then the terms were said, and the third of them was not the one it had been on Friday. The first of them was said and did not change: ten including one litter for no more than two hours. The second was the outer service door, which is the one a body went out of, and it was said in the same tone as it has been said for forty-one weeks. The fifth was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing, and the clerk did not ask him to. And the sixth was the pump, attended, two people, second bell to fifth, stopped on a rising water line, and the rising water line has two lines in it and one of them is nine hundred years old, and that was said, and it was the only one of the six that anybody in the room argued about, and it was argued about two days ago.

"**Six pails a day, every day in seven, in two crossings, four and then two,**" Renn Vale said, and stopped, and the room did not fill in after it, because a keeper stopping in the middle of a term is a keeper who has something after it, and what she had after it took her about four seconds to get to.

"**I set that in the twenty-second week and it was a good term and I have said it to a gate every working morning for nineteen weeks and I have said it in this room every morning for the same nineteen weeks, and it has been false since the fifth day of the thirty-ninth week and I told this room it was false on that morning and then I went on saying it every morning afterwards, and a term that is false and still being said is not a term. It is a thing a keeper says.**"

"**Then what is the third term now,**" said Bel Ordry, from the top of the nine steps, where he stands on a shut morning.

"**The water-carrying is the water the cook says this boundary uses in a day,**" Renn Vale said. "**Four pails and whatever the seam gives, and on the mornings the seam gives nothing then it is four, and the cook holds the number and the cook is the only person in this building who can arrive at it, and the number dates nothing and it is not a count of anybody and it is not a figure of what is in this anteroom. That is the third term. That is what I have got.**"

Nobody in that room improved on it, and the pump was between strokes, and the clerk had her hand near the book and did not use it for about a minute.

"**Say the half against,**" said Bel Ordry, and came down three of the nine steps, which is as far as he came on a shut day.

"**A term that names a person is a term that puts a person in it,**" he said. "**I have sat at the top of these steps for forty weeks and watched this room refuse six times to put a heading over a column, and the reason every one of those six was refused is in this clerk's book, and the reason in every one of them is a person. A term that says *the cook says* is a term with a cook standing at the end of it, and a person who stands at the end of a term is a person who can be found, and a person who can be found is a person who gets a name written beside them by somebody in a bad month. That is the half against and I am not going to soften it because the woman who set the term is standing four feet away and did not ask me to soften it.**"

"**You are right,**" Renn Vale said. "**And I have thought about it for two days and I am setting it anyway, and I would like the clerk to enter that I set a term I was told was the wrong shape, and that the reason is that a keeper's instrument is a sentence said to a gate, and a gate cannot be asked whether six pails a day is true, and this room can, and has been not asking for a fortnight, and a room that is not asking is a room with a false term in it.**"

Then Mikka Hale came out of the kitchen-line doorway with the cloth over her arm, and she did not go to her own door, which is what she does.

"**I have one condition and I am going to say it before anybody offers me a chair,**" she said.

"**Say it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The third term names me and so it has to be said in a room without asking me in front of the room whether it is right,**" said Mikka Hale. "**Because the day somebody asks me in front of nine people and a crew whether the number I hold is the right number, the number stops being a number I hold and starts being a number I have to defend, and a number a cook has to defend in a room is a number a room owns. So the term may be said. The term may not be checked out loud. And if I am in this room and the term is said then the clerk is to enter that I was in the room, and not one word about whether it is true.**"

"**Entered, in the cook's words, and entered that she gave it before anybody asked her and not after,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Entered, and now I am going to say the part of it that is mine,**" the clerk said, and she did not look up. "**A clerk's business is what has happened. A term that says the water-carrying is the water the cook says it is cannot be entered as true and cannot be entered as false, and there is no third thing to enter, and a term a clerk cannot check is a term a clerk is carrying for somebody else. I have done it. I am carrying a sentence I cannot check because a cook in a doorway will not have it checked in a room, and I want it in this book in my own hand that I know exactly what I have done and that I would do it again, and that the price of it is that from this morning the third of my six terms is a sentence I read out without knowing whether it is so, and there are five others.**"

"**Then the clerk is carrying five,**" said Iven Tallo, from the anvil, without looking up. "**I have been in this building forty weeks and I have been trying to work out what a boundary actually holds, and I have got to the same place three different ways and it is a bench with five men on it and a man who reads numbers at the end of it, and neither of those is in anybody's column, and I have never been able to work out whether that is a decision or an accident.**"

"**It is an accident,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and it has been an accident for forty weeks and I have entered it as a fact about this room rather than a decision by it, on purpose, because if I enter it as a decision then somebody in a year goes looking for the person who made it.**"

Then the pump was started, two people turning it, Renn Vale and Emrys Callow, neither of whom asked the other, and the head came up in about a minute and a half.

At about the fourth bell two of the six who are not this building's came out of the second of the two rooms off the far end, and stood in the doorway, and one of them was Bram Sallow and the other one the clerk had no name for and did not ask for.

"**There are two of us awake now,**" Bram Sallow said. "**There was one. I am not asking for anything. I am telling you there are two because you will find out in about a week by not finding me, and I would rather you knew on a Monday.**"

"**Then say why there are two,**" said Renn Vale, from the middle of the floor.

"**Because there is a noise in that floor again and it comes up on the side four of us sleep against, and the one that sleeps against it is me,**" Bram Sallow said. "**And Sohan has the far end and I have this end, and between us the room has somebody awake in it, and that is a thing we did and not a thing anybody gave us.**"

"**And what do you want from this room,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Nothing,**" Bram Sallow said. "**And I would like it entered that there are two of us awake and that it is ours, and I would like the clerk to enter the other half of it, which is that if you ask us for a watch we will not be able to say no, and you have never asked us for anything, and if you start now then in six months there is a rota on that door with two names on it and neither of the names is a keeper of anything.**"

Dalla Sen entered both halves in the man's own words and entered that he was not asked and that the clerk did not ask either.

"**Then say the rest of it,**" said Mero Senn, from the doorway with his bag against the wall, "**because you have been standing in that door for two minutes looking like a man with a second question and the second question is the one this room has not got an instrument for.**"

Bram Sallow did not go back in.

"**There are six of us in two rooms,**" he said. "**There is one door and it latches. There is no window in either room and I have looked and I looked on the first day and I have not stopped looking. And there is no air in this building that anybody has ever certified for the two of those rooms, and the pump has been running three mornings a week for nineteen weeks in a corner four feet from where we sleep, and nobody has ever asked whether that is a good thing to do to a room with one latching door in it.**"

"**Then I will answer it,**" said Dalla Sen, and she had the book shut, which she does not do.

"**You will not,**" said Renn Vale.

"**I was going to say that the air in those two rooms is not certified and has not been asked about, and that the medical limit on it is a physician's and the working limit on it is the air engineer's, and that neither of them has been asked by anybody in this room in nineteen weeks, and that a boundary which has just re-set a third term cannot in the same breath go and ask a woman who has two conditions unmet and unasked for a fifth thing.**" She put her hand flat on the shut book. "**So there is your answer and it is that this room has no instrument for what you have just asked it, and there are exactly two people in this building who could make one, and the reason this room has not asked either of them is on the previous forty pages and I am not going to read it out.**"

"**Then say the thing that is actually true,**" said Marek Venn, from the end of the trestle, and both of them turned, because he had not spoken for four days.

"**The pump's question has an instrument,**" he said. "**A chalk line and a groove and a rule with two lines in it and a clerk who reads the sixth of them out loud at a bell, and we have known for a fortnight that the second line is older than the first one and nobody knows who cut it, and that is a bad instrument and it is still an instrument. What you have just asked about has nothing. There is no rule for the air in those two rooms. There is no figure for the people in them. There is a term about carrying water and a flow about how much of it and neither of those has one thing to do with a person breathing in a room with one door. So the answer to your question is a rule that does not exist yet, and I have been in this building nine weeks and I have never once seen this room write a rule down before somebody broke it.**"

"**And a rule that does not exist yet is a seat,**" said Mero Senn, without moving away from the frame.

"**It is a seat,**" Marek Venn agreed, "**and I have been called that by better people than you this week, and I have not argued, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said so before being asked and did not enjoy it.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything for a moment, and outside it the wall with five and a half shores in it went on having a head of water on the far side from the second bell to the fifth, and the pump was between strokes.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a room which has changed one of its own six terms before it started a pump is owed three numbers before the day is over and is owed them read out, because a number you can hear is a number you can check and this morning is the first morning in six weeks when there is something in this room to check.

"**Five today and three hundred and thirty-five since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and fifty-two and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty-six days and one hundred and fifty-six nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and fifty-seven, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for forty-five weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in forty-five weeks, and this morning I read it out because a woman in a doorway has just told this room that she will not have a number of hers checked in a room, and the only number in this building that nobody has ever asked anybody to check is that one.**"

The board was walked, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven that are not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it and had nothing over the top of it before she read it out.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman has said out loud in a room that the water is going to take four and a half hours off him on Wednesday and has not yet said why, and that a crew's column is a quantity of asking and does not move on a Monday morning on the strength of a sentence that is going to be finished on a Wednesday.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning and is at four threads, and nobody has measured it and nobody is going to, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for seventeen weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a woman came up nine steps a fortnight ago and asked this building to keep a room full of water and went back down them with nothing.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a pump that had run, and a third term that had been a keeper's sentence for nineteen weeks and was now a cook's number carried by a clerk who could not check it, and two men awake at either end of a room with one latching door, and a rule that everybody in that room agreed did not exist yet.
