# Batch Summary — Volume 01, Batch 0005

## Outcome

**Volume 01 is complete.** Chapters 41–50 exist as finished prose scenes, reviewed and repaired against `reviews/volume-01/batch-0005.md`, in close third person on Marek Venn, with **no** viewpoint shift at all — the batch did not need one, and the one piece of new interior knowledge it contains (Nera's private count of forty-one turns and the refusal to publish a rate) was already established in Chapter 36 and is now *used* rather than revealed again.

The six-person opening party entered this batch whole and leaves it with **five**. Oren Sill dies in Chapter 48. His death is permanent, he is not recovered, he is not converted into anything, and the volume does not pretend otherwise.

The batch turns a plan with no material into a maintained, handed-over route. It does not resolve the stair's history, the plate, the guild's claim, the Lattice, or the stair's long-term condition, and it says so in its own unresolved list on the last page.

## Movement

- **Chapter 41** opens the school corner floor on a shut-corridor day and traces the void. The grit does not go to the anteroom or the corridor — it goes back into the hole it came out of, packed hard, because the room's own cost is two school days and Mikka's pot. At the bottom of two feet of grit is a dressed floor with a **running groove two fingers wide, a hundred years deep**, running toward the common landing and down. That is the same wear as the line-block's, and the line-block's three sections were cut out in the nineteenth week of the long cold year and given to a school. Renn's account of her mother's repair is why the line cannot be followed under the wash room floor, and the void can only be looked at from the school side. Ilsa decides the shut in three parts, Nera adds the fall-before-the-floor order, and the finding is reported to the people who keep the floor in their own words, which fires the batch's named review trigger.
- **Chapter 42** opens Tams Quill's hand on the day the nine days end. It does not open. He says what he said he would say, at the time: *a month and not nine days*. Oren keeps the lever for three more weeks. Then Nera writes the air plan for the lower pump gallery, because a person asked and said what he was going in for, and the answer is the jack, and the jack is not a *work* until Jo makes Marek name the work. The plan is nine lines, it is a **method and not a rating**, it says *once* on purpose, and the seventh line — who goes down — is fought and left open. The receiver of anything that comes up out of that room is the boundary's keeper and cannot be one of the six.
- **Chapter 43** pays the draw. Tams's arithmetic says the bay's lip is four feet under the well's lip at a hand's breadth every five minutes of pump, so it takes two cycles and a check that packs. Jo names the price before she starts: a day and a half of a route that pays her, and the fifth cleaning, and the seat after it. A turn comes at the second minute of the six and Oren is out of the well in one minute and eleven seconds with the jack in his left hand because he would not leave it. The tools come up over four trips and are **received by the boundary's keeper, item by item, with times**, and the crew move them and own nothing. One length — five foot nine — stays on the bay's bottom shelf with its inventory number chalked on it. The crew's frayed nine-metre line is tied round the ladder as a lashing and does not come up. The estimate drops from *four or fewer* to **two or fewer**.
- **Chapter 44** is the crew's reckoning. Tams's finding is inverted: the crew's hours came **down** (forty-eight to forty-one and a half) and the boundary's went to forty-six, and the crew's share is the half that gets counted. Then Pell Roke says the thing nobody has said: the record has six names in a column and has never had a line saying what happens to the six of them. Marek says it badly, Jo makes him say it again, and Sefu refuses the sentence that came *after* it — *some day they will be worse* — and makes him write the leaving as a spring conversation with a named air hand-over. Iven then names the whole material problem: three lengths, five wedges, a jack and a pry bar, **and no anchor**.
- **Chapter 45** builds the shore. Iven states the geography of that end of the boundary in one sentence so it is on the record, and prices the work out loud. Leena takes the survey marks on the common landing because Marek's hand cannot make a mark; Jo carries them down to the stair slate in her own hand; a chalk line is a reference and does not move. The terms are read for a fifth time and Renn's exception is written **refused as a clause, granted as a fact**. The black cord — forty feet, released by nobody since the eighteenth day — is released by Sefu for a named work, to the boundary and not to the crew, on a price, and Renn will re-tie it and nobody else may touch it. The cord bears on the **open face** of the wall, four feet up, behind the shore's head, so that a child can put a thumb on it and anybody can count the wear. Oren packs the foot to bearing with sized wood and his knee. A turn comes and the near side is as found **except for the shore**, and a thing that stands needs a keeper, and Renn Vale takes it — the shore, the road, the lead, and a line nobody has yet been found to hold.
- **Chapter 46** makes the line and changes the load. Three turns and a seizing in the hollow, beside Renn's mother's piece, which is not taken out. Iven asks for a hand on the head when the line first comes tight, and asks **Oren**, by craft and not by size, and Oren holds it for the count of thirty-one. The return comes to a stop and *arrives* instead of being beaten. The price is that it is now quicker — a second where it was a second and a half — and Nera will not publish a rate, so the ledger says *the dead window after the lever comes off is shorter, the air engineer holds the number, and no person is to plan a crossing to a number they have been given by nobody*. Iven explains the band as line wear and stops, and Renn does not answer, and the band is not explained.
- **Chapter 47** runs the first crossing, chosen by the room and not by the plan. The woman from the wash rooms goes and her mother goes because she was **not asked** and insisted, and that is in Renn's hand. Behind the rail on the far side there are **four treads going down**, and the lower receiving landing is gone but the steps are not, and the stair was a terminus for nine weeks because the slab kept bringing everybody back. Then the arithmetic: four at a time, four minutes, thirty-four minutes of pump, and Mikka's ordering with the slow group in the middle and the fast ones at the edges. Ilsa lifts her own term for one day. The count is twenty-nine and the room records, on Pell's instruction, that a count is a photograph. And the room does **not** answer the collision: the crew's last work and the boundary's crossing want the same near landing on the same day, and Nera says out loud that the lip is taking the line and a man and she does not know what happens to a person on the far side.
- **Chapter 48** is the cost. The count is thirty-one, and the two extras are named and the reason is nobody's fault. The pump is slow and the interval comes off four minutes early. The plan is a plan for **stopping**, not for timing, because Nera would not publish a rate for a turn she had seen twice during pump movement — and a turn comes two minutes into the nineteenth strokes. The four on the far side are the slow group, and the slab goes, and when it comes back they have to step off a lip that is one stone, is the edge of an eleven-foot shaft, and is carrying the line at its base. **Oren Sill is under the lip** because he said on Wednesday night, in front of eleven people, *"Then somebody is under the lip,"* and all four come across and past him, and the lip goes about two inches, which is the figure he produced himself in Chapter 36. Nobody was on the far side. Nobody went down the shaft. Mikka refuses to write *missing* and says the word *dead* in that order, because a missing man in a building like this is a debt somebody else pays, and it is always a woman with a door. The crew abandons the lamp, the pry bar, the draw cord, the jack, and both salvage refusals, and Iven says the thing that is in the ledger for good: **there needed to be a post under that lip, and there is no timber in this building for a post.**
- **Chapter 49** finds what the work bought. The stair is a vent and the boundary now breathes through it. The anteroom's rating is unchanged, deliberately: the route changes the escape, not the occupancy. A cart is refused for three reasons, and Jo's is the best: every number in the route is a number about bodies. Iven puts the guild's future on the table and says plainly that the page is **a position, not a shield**. The plinth rota is people, Isel and the woman from the wash rooms, and Jo lets the crew go without argument. The woman from the wash rooms says eleven words about a shut carrying a reason, and asks for them to be entered in **her** hand and not Renn's, because Renn has everything.
- **Chapter 50** is the hand-over. Nera's air is four conditions and no certificate, and the four room-keepers stand within reach of the cloth because they are the stop. The occupancy check is walked room by room by the boundary, at the door, on the day, and comes to thirty. The terms are on Renn's slate and agreed out loud one at a time, and **eleven people said the word and none of the crew's names are on that slate**. The route is shut once, mid-check, by the person nobody spoke to for seven weeks, for a reason nobody would have thought of, and it costs a half-day and a school day and it is right. The crew's hours are thirty-eight and a half, given, owed to nobody, and the maintenance obligation is bounded and written. The plate travels, unexplained, on a knotted bundle, because evidence goes with the people who found it. The volume ends with the first useful route in the Hearthhold, four people wide, with a woman under it — and the first route anybody there can close.

## Material and labor handoff

- **Water ledger untouched.** Five liters from the collapse plus one separately sealed liter of verified condensation; the sealed liter did not open. The kitchen's **nineteen days** are given, not waived, not reduced, and the kitchen does not feed the crew and is not fed by it.
- **Pump:** a **fifth cleaning**, paid for out of Jo's shutter route, declared before it was started at a day and a half. The seat's estimate moves from *four or fewer* to **two or fewer**, in Jo's own hand with a time on it, read back before the ledger closed.
- **Wedges: seven sound and two dead sticks, unchanged.** Three at the school-door head — which wants four — three at the waist in washed-out fill, one in the basket. The **five public-works wedges** from the cache are new to the building and go into the shore's head and foot; the school-door head does not get its fourth, and **Renn Vale refuses it in her own words** and says the reason is the length of the queue, not hardness. The head is still one wedge short at the end of the volume.
- **Timber:** no sound timber over six feet, no lath, no bronze. The cache's four lengths came up except one: **five foot nine, on the bay's bottom shelf of the flooded pump gallery, inventory number chalked on it, line still open.** The three that came up are three foot four, two foot nine and a stub, which is six foot one scarfed, and the shore is a **built-up head of three pieces**, which Iven says is the reason it has to be tended and tending it is a person. **Four foot eleven and a bit of offcut** is what is left in the anteroom. The shore's foot stands **four foot seven** back from the wall, which is where six foot one of timber stands a four-foot head and nowhere else.
- **The screw jack is under the shore's foot and stays there.** The **pry bar is in the haunch of the third step and stays there.** The **cracked lamp is on the near landing's step and stays there.** Nera's **draw cord went back onto the valve board**, marked with the two extra periods it was released for after the thirty-seventh day, making four in total.
- **The crew's frayed nine-metre line is tied four times round a ladder in six weeks of standing water** and is recorded as *spent*, not *lost*, and is going to be part of that ladder by the end of the month.
- **Salvage refused twice on the day and entered with a name against it:** the wedge-shaped stop piece in the hollow (kept at Renn's word) and the fallen parapet slab (asked for by Iven Tallo by name, refused by Dalla Sen with the lien reason written under his request).
- The school corner's mouth is **shut** — void cleared to dressed stone, packed hard, floored on the existing cover and kerb, Ilsa's fall cut first — and the grit has not come up in a fortnight. It is **Ilsa's repair and not the crew's**, and the school lost two days and Mikka's pot was on both.

## Character and labor changes

- **Oren Sill dies** at the near lip, bearing, with four people behind him and the stair turning. Not recovered. Not converted. His name is in the ledger in three hands and in the tally's *spent* column is a line about the shore's foot.
- **Tams Quill's hand is a month and not nine days**, said at the time as he promised, and the crew's whole labour figure comes down because of it.
- **Isel Ferrow** is, as of the fourth of this month, the first reader of a stair in the Hearthhold, and the ledger's plinth rota carries Pell Roke's name in the margin in Tams's hand as a debt and not a duty.
- **The woman from the wash rooms** says nine words, then eleven, then shuts a route, and the eleventh are in her own hand and not Renn's, and she asked to have them read back twice.
- **Renn Vale** becomes a keeper: a shore, a road, a lead line, and an unclaimed future. Her stair-watch refusal is **not** withdrawn and she distinguishes it out loud from counting a crossing, twice.
- **Ilsa Norr** lifts her own school-corner term for one day and says it is the first thing she has ever unsaid in this building and that it was in the direction of *yes*.
- **Mikka Hale** orders the relay with the slow in the middle and says out loud what happens if the window comes early, and the room does not write it down until the night.
- **Sefu Aran** adds to his boundary paragraph that the day the route is first used for anything but air, it goes on the clerk's page with the names and the date.
- Marek is corrected by other people seven more times and makes one correction himself, in the fortieth day, when he reads out that he has stopped trying to hold the uncountable part of it.

## Open handoff

The route is open for people and air. It is not for a cart. It is not a charter and the crew hold no document, seal, receipt or claim in it. It carries four at a time, in the pump's window, with a **person under the near landing's lip**, and it is kept by the people whose rooms it is under and can be shut by any of four of them or by the cord or by the cloth.

Still open, named, and deliberately unresolved: the post under the lip and the timber for it; the fourth wedge for the school-door head; the bronze delivery seat; the **guild's claim**, which Iven says is a position and not a shield; **Form 7-B**; Iven's suspended bond; the kitchen's nineteen days; the sealed liter; the plate, unexplained, travelling; the stair's history and the band; the far wall of the throat; the woman's eleven words about a shut; and the two questions the last page carries without answering — **who keeps the Hearthhold rooms and why do their oldest plans show homes and not treasure**, and **whether a route built by a rescue crew and kept by the people who sleep under it is a public thing or a road with a name and a lock on the end.**

## Relationship and thematic change

The batch's argument is that a route is not a hole with a rail on it, and that the difference is decided by who is under the load when the load changes. Marek's Chapter 40 refusal to mark a stone in a mechanism he does not own becomes, in Chapter 46, a line made fast in that mechanism on a boundary's own rope with a boundary keeper's name under it; and in Chapter 48 the load he changed is carried by a man, and the man is not recovered, and the honest sentence in the ledger is about timber that is under water.

Nera's refusal to publish a rate is the batch's most expensive good decision and it is what kills Oren Sill. Nobody revokes it. The volume ends with her still refusing, and with the dead window written into the ledger as *shorter, and the air engineer holds the number, and no person is to plan a crossing to a number they have been given by nobody* — which is the volume's whole ethics in one line, and which Volume 02's charter argument will have to live inside.
