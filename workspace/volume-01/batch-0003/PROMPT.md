## Required movement and chapter cards

### Chapter 21 — The Next Work-Bell

Open at the named review after the next complete pressure turn. The scored pump seat, Jo’s deferred route work, the wet seam, and the still-closed guild line create a practical question: should the crew keep the small conditional route or try to make a larger Hearthhold refuge? Marek wants a larger plan because a room for six is not a neighborhood refuge. Resistance comes from the pump’s limited life, the residents’ different needs, and the fact that a public room creates a permanent maintenance burden. Show a review of water, air, labor, and consent through work rather than exposition. End with the Hearthholds asking whether the crew will help design a room that ordinary residents—not only the six outsiders—can use.

### Chapter 22 — A Room the Hearthholds Want

Let residents describe the missing shared room in practical terms: a clinic waiting corner, a school or meal shelter, a place for families during ordinary maintenance, or a room that can receive a stretcher without blocking a kitchen. Do not grant a new blanket admission. A resident may refuse one proposed use. Marek’s goal is to turn needs into a bearing, breath, drainage, escape, and change problem. Nera should challenge any air assumption before the design is built. End with a limited site visit or work request that gives the crew access to a proposed upper/lower room, not ownership of the neighborhood.

### Chapter 23 — The Upper Gallery

Explore the old school-gallery level as a possible second tier for the proposed refuge. Its domestic or school history should be visible in proportions, hooks, old arrows, and worn floor paths, not treasure. Marek and Iven disagree about whether the old beam and floor can be reused; Nera identifies a ventilation problem that an empty inspection would miss. Pell’s ankle and Leena’s shoulder affect the survey and evacuation plan. Resistance includes a missing material, a room that needs repair before occupancy, and residents who will not let a stranger decide what their rooms are for. End with a declared, unbuilt design and a cost that must be paid before the full-room test.

### Chapter 24 — Before the Test

Make preparation ordinary and difficult. The crew and residents organize tools, meals, water, watch rotations, and a full-room drill plan. The proposed test must name occupancy, duration, gear, a stretcher route, exits, stop authority, and what happens if the upper gallery crowds into the lower corridor. Iven translates the craft work for Jo and other maintainers without claiming authorship; Dalla records labor; Mikka and the clinic/school users explain what ordinary interruptions look like. Resistance comes from conflicting schedules, the scored pump seat, the guild order, and disagreement over who may refuse the test. End with enough named people and supplies for a credible full-room test, but no guarantee that the upper gallery can be used.

### Chapter 25 — When the Gallery Loads

This is the Volume 01 midpoint reversal. The stronger two-level shelter passes an empty inspection and then fails under a realistic full-room drill. An old school-gallery floor and an ordinary crowd surge redirect load into the lower corridor. A visible part of the proposed room shifts, the shared lower exit narrows, or the old floor/beam makes the crowd’s normal movement transfer weight into the corridor below. No monster or new magical attack appears. Keep everyone alive, but make the failure physically and emotionally costly. Marek must recognize that the upper gallery cannot remain open merely because it looks like useful public space. He orders the upper wing decommissioned or opened only as a separated, low-use area, changes the escape plan, and uses or loses remaining spare materials to keep the lower residents and injured crew safe. End with the upper room closed and the lower corridor still needing a new plan.

### Chapter 26 — The Shared Corridor

After the reversal, work through the lower corridor rather than immediately designing a grand replacement. The revised idea is a shared, lower, attended corridor or refuge that keeps the clinic, kitchen, school corner, and stretcher route from sharing one weak throat. Show the physical labor: moving baskets, bracing a safe strip, protecting a drain, marking a new escape path, and coordinating Oren’s bearing work with Tams’s test loads. Nera and Marek must disagree in public about what counts as a safe air path or occupancy. The disagreement should produce a better plan, not a personality rupture. End with a lower-corridor sketch, named limits, and a new material/labor cost.

### Chapter 27 — Who Pays for the Room

Make the civic cost visible. Jo’s deferred tool-route work, Iven’s unpaid or suspended guild labor, the residents’ meal and watch time, and the loss of remaining spare materials cannot be called community favor. Dalla and Mikka argue over whether meals are wages, compensation, or simple hospitality. Sefu makes clear that no resident can bind people who did not agree, and Nera makes clear that air maintenance is not a guild or architect fee. Marek wants a clean fund or ownership solution; the room needs a public maintenance obligation instead. Include a resident refusal and a practical choice about who may stop the work. End with an interim cost agreement or a clear list of unresolved costs, not a final charter.

### Chapter 28 — The Empty Upper Wing

Physically decommission the failed upper gallery. Remove or isolate the unsafe floor, preserve the school history without turning it into salvage, and decide what can be reused without endangering the lower corridor. Iven may argue for keeping a replaceable brace; Dalla may argue that the record must show why it was removed. Marek’s control flaw appears as the urge to salvage the project’s appearance by keeping a piece of the upper wing alive. Residents need the rooms and materials for ordinary purposes. End with the upper level explicitly closed or limited, the lower route still conditional, and a clear maintenance consequence from losing the spare materials.

### Chapter 29 — A Full Room, Again

Run a revised full-room or shared-corridor test under a new declared occupancy, duration, gear load, stretcher condition, and exit plan. This test should be materially different from Chapter 25: it must test the lower shared corridor, ordinary interruption, a wet or dirty floor, and a family/stretcher/crowd mix without reopening the failed upper gallery. Do not make the second test effortless. Sediment, the wet seam, a pressure turn, a delayed resident, or a narrow passage should force a real stop and revision. Nera and Marek again contribute different expertise. End with a bounded pass or a safer limited use, plus one unresolved condition that prevents any claim of permanence.
