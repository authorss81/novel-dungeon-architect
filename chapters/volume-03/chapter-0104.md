# Chapter 104 — The Cheapest Answer

On the fourth day of the twentieth week the corridor was shut, and the cheapest possible answer to the school corner was said out loud in the anteroom at the third bell by the person whose job it was, in the form it takes, with a condition and not a date, and it was correct.

"**Take it out of use as a room and shut it,**" said Renn Vale.

She had it on her slate already, which Dalla Sen entered and then entered again underneath, because a woman who arrives with a thing written down has been thinking about it at a time when nobody was watching her, and this room has learned that is the only warning it gets.

"**Shut it with a door and a wedge and buy nothing, and put a condition on my slate in my own hand, and no day on it, because my terms do not take a number where a condition will do.**"

Dalla Sen read the condition out and it was this: *the school corner is shut as a room and not as a route, and it is opened again when the floor at the low corner has been dry under a lamp through a night and a morning, and the person who can stop that is the woman who keeps the room and not the man who wrote it.*

"**That is mine to stop,**" said Renn Vale, "**and it is mine to be stopped by as well, and I want both of those on the page.**"

---

**Then the room did the thing it does, which is object, out loud, to a specific line, before anybody picks up the chalk.**

Mikka Hale objected to the object, not the closure.

"**Then say what shutting it does. You are all sitting there pleased with yourselves and I want to know what we have done to a bed joint.**"

"**Nothing,**" said Jo Verrick, who had the lamp and the rule and had been down there at the first bell. "**The patch in the course is under the wall between that room and this one and shutting a door does not touch a bed joint and does not touch the fill and does not touch anything at all. I have been down there with my knee on the flag for an hour. The water is in the floor. It is not in the door.**"

"**Then we are not shutting the water out,**" said Mikka Hale.

"**No. We are shutting the floor out of use,**" said Renn Vale. "**And here is the whole of the argument and I would like it written down before I say the second half, because the second half is the part I am not proud of.**

"**We are not fixing anything. We are taking a room that nine children have worn into a slope, and a lamp, and a low corner with a wet floor in it, and we are putting a door on it so that no one stands in that corner again. That is all. And I have been sitting here for two days with a slate and a wedge in my hand looking for a reason it is not the right thing, and I have not found one. That is why I am saying it. I have not found one and I have been looking hard, and if any of you find one, say it now, because I will take it back.**"

**Not one of them did,** and Renn Vale looked slowly round the room to check, and Dalla Sen entered that: *the closure was proposed by the person whose terms govern it, the objection was made and answered, no objection was found, and the clerk records that she asked the room to find one and it did not.*

---

**And then Marek Venn asked the question that nobody had asked, and he asked it because he was the only person in that room who could see what it was, and being the only one had been a thing he wanted for twenty weeks and had stopped wanting somewhere around the second day of the eighteenth.**

"**What did we just stop being able to do.**"

Renn Vale answered it about the room.

"**We stopped being able to use a room that is dry when the corridor is shut. The corridor is shut on the first, second and fourth days, and that is three days in seven, and those are the days the school does not run, and the one room in this building that has been dry and empty and ours on those three days for twenty weeks is that room. We have just given that up. So the school has a room three days in seven on the far side of a fill, and three days in seven nowhere at all.**"

Then Ilsa Norr answered it about the building, and she answered it from the school door, and she was not defending the room.

"**We have stopped being able to be wrong in a room with children in it.**

"**Every argument this boundary has had for three weeks was easier because nine people were going to be in it. The water. The anteroom. The word door. The plan. You could say a thing in front of nine people and one of them would ask the next question, and the next question was always better than the one you had asked, and I have been that question for twenty weeks and I have never once been thanked for it and I have never once been sorry for it.**

"**And that room is now shut, and I am not defending it, and it is a wet floor and it is a worn floor and it should be shut. But I want the room to hear what it has just done, because in a month somebody is going to be frightened of something and there is going to be nobody in here to ask the next question, and the argument is going to get quieter, and quieter is what it was before I got here.**"

Renn Vale wrote nothing down and said, "**That is the best thing anybody has said in this room this month and I am not putting it on my slate, and I will tell you why I am not putting it on my slate: my slate is a room with a floor and a fill and seven terms, and what she has said is about the building.**"

---

**They shut it at the fourth bell, and it took eleven minutes, and nothing was bought.**

Jo Verrick put the wedge in herself and wanted the whole of it said plainly afterwards, so she said it plainly.

"**One of the three at the school-door head is in that door. So the school door has two wedges where it had three, and the school door is on the corridor and there is nobody on the far side of that door to be pushed against anything. Seven sound and two dead sticks and I have not added to either number and I have moved one, and I want that written down as a move and not as a use.**

"**And it holds. And it is a wedge in a door head in a frame I did not build, and if somebody comes in a bad month and wants that room, the answer is a man with his shoulder to a door and not a mason.**"

Bel Ordry read out the empty fourth column again, because a room had been shut and the plan did not carry it, and he read out the reason in the fourth part in two hands and then said:

"**It is not on the plan. It is on my slate, because the plan's second column already has a condition on it with no number and no day, and a room does not need the same instrument twice. The plan has nine rows and the school corner is one of them and its third column says it ran, and the row does not know about today and I am not going to let it know in a hurry, because a row that changes every time a room shuts is a row that is a mood and not a plan.**

"**So a clerk is going to write down, separately, on the slate's page and not on the plan's page, that the school corner is shut as a room, with a condition, with a person who can stop it.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it on a separate page and the two pages now disagreed on purpose, and that is the second time two documents in this building have been made to disagree on purpose, and she entered that too.

---

**And then Renn Vale went and did the thing her terms have made her do every day since the seventh term, which is walk the corridor and count what is shut, and tonight she had two on the slate, and she came back up the nine steps and stood on the landing and said it out loud to a room that had not asked her.**

"**A stair, a lip, a valve, the corridor, the dip, the low end, the waist, the school door, the ninth room, the four treads, the wash rooms, the mouth, the blank line, and now a room with a wedge in it.** That is the slate tonight, and the slate has a heading on it that says *terms of the route*, and I did not write that heading and I am not going to strike it, because it is true, and because I have found that a slate with nothing on the top of it is a slate a person can put anything on.

"**And I want the room to hear the sound of it getting longer, because that is what is happening and nobody in here is going to notice it happening. Every one of these is somebody standing somewhere being careful. There is a day when that slate is a list of rooms and there is nobody being careful in any of them, and I do not know what that day looks like from the inside of it, and I said once in this building in the fourteenth week that I would rather be the woman who is inconvenient than the woman who is careful, and I am saying it again at the fourth bell on the fourth day of the twentieth week, and I would like it noticed that it has taken me twenty weeks to be able to say a thing twice, and that I said it in the fourteenth week and somebody wrote it down and nobody has read it since.**"

**Dalla Sen wrote it and did not improve it and did not put it in a column, because it was not a column, it was a woman on a landing.**

**And at the fifth bell, with the door shut and the room empty and nobody in it,** two people stood in front of the shut mouth above the door and read the stone over it, and they read it differently, and neither of them would say what it was.

**Nera Oduya and Renn Vale, both of them with a lamp, both of them refusing to touch the stone.**

"**There are marks on the lintel,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and they are old, and they are not a surveyor's, and there are four of them and one is broken through and one is a groove about two inches long that has had something put in it and taken out.**"

"**I read the broken one as two lines and a gap,**" said Renn Vale, "**and you read it as a mark and a number.**"

"**I read the gap as a gap. I am not going to tell you what I think a mark and a number is, because a Thursday hand and a lamp is not a place to tell anybody.**

"**And I will say one thing and no more. It does not tell me one single thing I need, and I have looked at it for a quarter of an hour and it has not told me how that floor dries.**"

"**Then it is a mark and it is no use,**" said Renn Vale, "**and it is a shame, because I have wanted to say for twenty weeks that there was a word in this building older than me and there is not, and there is a groove.**"

Dalla Sen entered: *marks on the lintel above the shut mouth, four, one broken through, one a groove; read two different ways by two people; neither will say; no mark has been cut in this stone by anybody in this room and none will be.* And she entered that last part in her own hand and underlined it, which she does not do, and Bel Ordry asked her not to.

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the six things and the corridor was shut today, which the arrangement says and which is not the news and the school was at the far end of the corridor in a room with a hearth corner and a dished floor and a ceiling a fourteen-year-old cannot stand up under, and the pot was ten days, and a man of twenty-two was thirty-nine days and thirty-nine nights into a count that was his own.**

**The arrangement was untouched. Shut on the first, second and fourth, open on the third, fifth and sixth, the school on the three open days. The school corner's closure is a room and it closes no corridor on any day of the week, and Dalla Sen entered that in the ledger, in her own hand, with the reason, in this form: *I am putting this next to the closure because in six weeks somebody is going to say the school lost a day here, and it did not, and if the sentence is on the page then the argument does not have to be had again.**

**And nobody in the anteroom said the word *first*, and one man in it thought it and wrote it in a book of his own that night, in one line, and the line was this: *a room kept safe by being a room nobody is in is a thing this building now knows how to do, and I do not think anybody above the Cinder Steps has worked out yet that this is cheap.**
