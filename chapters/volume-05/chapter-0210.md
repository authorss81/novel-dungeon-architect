# Chapter 210 — What The Water Took

The seventh day of the fortieth week is a Sunday, and a seventh day is not a crossing day, so nobody came up the nine steps this morning and nobody went down them. The school did not run and lost nothing. The pump ran at the second bell and stopped at the fifth, because a seventh day is not a shut day and is not an open day and the pump has never known the difference, and the head of water was on that party wall for three hours on a Sunday in a week when it is only supposed to be on it three days in seven, and no rule in this building has ever said a pump does not run on a Sunday, and Dalla Sen entered that nobody had thought of it until it had happened and that she was not going to make a rule about it now.

So the morning had nothing come into it again, and the room looked at what it keeps for the second Sunday in a row, and this time there was something on the trestle to look at, which was a week.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the thirtieth reading, and the same as twenty-nine, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked, and the clerk entered all four as four refusals and not as one, and the count of mornings is thirty and is the same count as the wall's, and Jo Verrick said that next Sunday she was going to stop saying them and give a reason, and the clerk entered that too, and did not ask for it.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and entered it, and the count of mornings out of mornings is fifteen.

The anteroom's terms were said at the second bell, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk, and they are said on a seventh day as well, and they were said at the second bell because that is when they are said on a shut day and because a seventh day is neither. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The third was six pails a day and it was false and it was said. The sixth was the pump, and the pump was attended and running, and it had been read back out loud with two lines in it and one of the lines belongs to a person who is not in this building.

Then nothing came into the room for about two hours, and at about the fourth bell four people in it described what the water had taken, and it took until the sixth bell, and no two of them said the same size of thing, and the one instrument everybody in that room trusted was a number a cook had said out loud on Saturday that was about water and not about people.

"**I will go first because I am the person who has been counting it wrong all week,**" said Mikka Hale, from the kitchen-line doorway, "**and because if I go last I will let one of you say something about water that is not about a pot and I would rather it was me that said the small thing first so that the rest of you had a small thing to be embarrassed by.**"

"**Say the small thing,**" said Dalla Sen, and did not look up.

"**The pot was one day on Friday of last week and it is three days this morning, and the seam gave two on the Sunday, nothing on four mornings, one on Friday, and nothing since, and the head came back on Saturday morning, and nobody has measured whether that is why the seam went quiet, and I am not going to.**"

"**Say the number of days,**" said Renn Vale, from the middle of the floor, and she had not moved since the second bell.

"**The pump was off for seven days,**" said Mikka Hale. "**Four days of that I was frightened. Three days of that I was working. And this morning it is on and I am the only person in this room who has nothing to do at the second bell, and I have been standing in that doorway for a quarter of an hour deciding whether to say that, and I am going to say it, and I would like the clerk to enter that a cook in this building has nothing to do at the second bell for the first time since the second week and is not happy about it.**"

Dalla Sen entered it in the cook's words, and entered that she was not asked.

"**That is the water's,**" said Mikka Hale. "**That is what the water took. Not the pot. The pot was at one and now it is at three and the pot is a pot. The water took my work out of my morning and I do not know how to give it back and I do not want it back, and both of those are true and I would like a person in a year to know that a cook said both of them in the same breath and did not cry about it, because the clerk has entered that I did not cry and I want the no-crying entered as a fact and not as a virtue.**"

She entered it as a fact and not as a virtue, and entered that the clerk noticed, and entered that too.

Then Jo Verrick, from the wall, with her hand not on the string.

"**Then I will go next because I have got a figure and it is a figure about a bed under a stone and it is a third of an inch and it went up between the twenty-ninth week and Friday, and I have said all week that I would not write what caused it, and a cause is not going in a book either, and I am going to tell this room what I have instead, which is a date.**"

"**You are not going to write a date,**" said Bel Ordry, from the top of the nine steps, and he had not come down them all week.

"**I am not going to write a date,**" Jo Verrick said, "**and I am going to say a date out loud in a room, which is a different instrument from writing one down, and which I have not done in forty weeks, and which I am doing because a child of nine said on Friday that it is all the same water and it is not anywhere anybody put it, and she is right about the second half of that and I would like somebody to be right about the first half of it.**"

"**Say it,**" said Dalla Sen.

Jo Verrick put her thumb under the string and took it out again and it came out right, and then she said it.

"**The bed went up between the twenty-ninth week and Friday, and the frame is a finger and two-thirds and it has been a finger and two-thirds for thirty mornings, and the wall is a quarter of an inch and the same as twenty-nine, and if the water in this building had come up through the floor of that schoolroom and pushed that bed, then this room's own two numbers would be different this morning. They are not different. So the water did not come up through the floor of that schoolroom. And the water is not anywhere anybody put it. So it came in from somewhere, and it came in from somewhere in about four days, and this building has four days of that in it and I am the only person in this room who has read a number in a room all week and I have got a date and no cause. And a date without a cause is a Monday, and I will not have both by Sunday.**"

Dalla Sen entered it in the words of the woman who reads it: that a date was said out loud in a room and not written down, that the clerk did not write it down, and that the space where a reason goes beside a figure is empty and was left empty on purpose.

"**That is the second half,**" said Jo Verrick. "**Here is the first half, and it is mine and it is the worst thing I have said in this room. If the water did not come up through that floor and it is not anywhere anybody put it, then it came in through a wall, and there is one wall in this building with a bad repair on one face only, and nine children lean on the face it was not put on.**"

"**Then take them out of the room,**" said Bel Ordry, and he came down two of the nine steps, which is as far as he came in a fortnight.

"**No,**" said Jo Verrick, "**and I have thought about it for two days and the answer is no, and here is why, and I want it in the book in my words. If I take nine children out of that room this morning then this building has done the thing it has been refusing to do for forty weeks, which is shut a room where people are. And if I shut it, then somebody is going to ask me why, and I am going to have to say *because a figure*, and a figure is not a reason, and I have refused four things a day for thirty mornings and I am not going to be the woman who finally gives a room a reason that is a number.**"

The anteroom was very quiet, and the pump did not run, and it was between strokes.

"**And I am going to say the part that goes with it,**" Jo Verrick said, "**and it is worse than the other part. I have a figure. It is a third of an inch. It went up. And there is a wall and a repair and a head of water on that wall from the second bell to the fifth three days in seven, and I have not been to look at that repair, and not this week, and the reason is the same reason I have not looked at anything for thirty mornings, and it is a good reason, and it is the reason this building does not send people to places where a person can be killed, and a woman with a rule on a wall is a person.**"

The clerk entered all of it in the words of the woman who reads it: that a date was said out loud and not a cause, that she declined to shut a room, that she gave the reason for declining, that the reason is a rule in this building and not a preference of hers, and that the clerk did not ask her for a second reason and would not have accepted one.

Then Emrys Callow, from the anvil end, with the cup in his hand that had been empty for an hour.

"**Then the fourth thing is mine and it is not a number and I am going to say it in one sentence and then I am going to go and stand at the other end of this room.**"

"**Say it,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step.

"**The water did not take anything off me, and it is going to take four and a half hours off me on Wednesday, and I have not asked my five for it yet, and I have not said the reason out loud in a room yet, and I have had a fortnight and a half of reasons and none of them is good.**"

"**That is not a sentence,**" said Mero Senn, from the doorway with his bag against the wall, and he did not move away from the frame of it.

"**It is a sentence,**" Emrys Callow said, "**and it has got a shape in it, and I have put it there on purpose, because I have been in this building nine weeks and I have watched this room do a thing four times where somebody says a thing that is not a sentence and a physician takes it apart, and I am not having mine taken apart before Wednesday.**"

The physician put his bag down on the floor, which he has not done in this room in nine weeks.

Then Bram Sallow came out of the second of the two rooms off the far end, past the pump, which he had not done since the Sunday, and stood in the doorway, and nobody in that anteroom had asked him to and Dalla Sen had her hand near the book before he had said anything.

"**I said on Sunday last week that I would go back in,**" said Bram Sallow, and stayed in the doorway. "**I am not going in yet. I want to say one thing and it is a small thing and it is about a man who is not in this room.**"

"**Say it,**" said Dalla Sen, and she wrote that down before he had finished standing still, "**and I am not going to ask you to come further in.**"

"**It came back this morning,**" said Bram Sallow, and he had one hand on the frame of the door, "**about the second bell and it stopped about the fifth, and four of us have had that noise in the floor under our heads for sixteen weeks and had it taken away for seven, and I am not saying I missed it, and I am not saying I am glad. I am saying it came back and I did not notice until the fourth bell and then I noticed all at once.**"

"**Then say the other thing,**" said Dalla Sen, and turned a page over and did not write on it.

"**The other thing is that the woman who put the chalk on that frame came into that room and put it there with her own right arm strapped to her chest, and I was awake, and it was a Friday, and she was in a hurry and she did not explain it to anybody including the five of us, and I have not said so for nineteen weeks,**" said Bram Sallow, "**and I am saying it now because there is a rule on that frame and it belongs to somebody, and a rule is a person, and I have been sleeping eight feet under a person's rule since a Friday in the seventeenth week and I did not know whose it was until Saturday.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything for a moment.

"**Say the half against,**" said Dalla Sen, and she said it before Renn Vale could, and nobody had asked her to, and she entered afterwards that nobody had asked her.

Renn Vale looked at the man in the doorway and then at the frame.

"**The half against is that he is describing an accident,**" said Renn Vale, and she had not gone near the frame since the second bell, "**and that a wall making a noise at the hours a pump runs is a wall doing what a wall does when water goes past it, and that I have heard that noise myself in this room for nineteen weeks and put it down to the pump because the pump was running, and that a person can be eight feet under a rule for nineteen weeks and not know whose it is and that is not anybody's fault and is not a thing anybody has to answer for.**"

"**Then nobody has to answer for it,**" said Bel Ordry, and went back up the two steps.

"**Nobody has to answer for it,**" Renn Vale agreed, "**and the reason I am saying it out loud is that I have been carrying a rule this week with two lines in it and I have been treating the second line as a problem in this room, and it is not a problem in this room. It is a groove in a stone that somebody cut in about the year this building was made, and the woman who put the chalk on top of it did not know it was there, and the man who slept under it did not know whose it was, and none of the three of them is going to be asked about it.**"

She entered it, and entered that nobody was asked, and entered that the reason was given in a room and not at a gate.

Then Dalla Sen turned the book round on the trestle, which she does not do.

"**I am going to ask a person a question and I am going to ask it in front of this room because the last two times I have been asked something in a room I have said no afterwards, and I would like this one to be asked in the open,**" she said. "**Can a flow go in this book as a count of what is in this building.**"

She did not say it to Mikka Hale. She said it to the room.

"**No,**" said Mikka Hale, and she did not come out of the doorway, and she did not put the cloth over her arm either, because it was already over the other one. "**A flow is how much water this boundary uses in a day. A count is how many people are in it. I said that on Saturday in my own words and the clerk entered it and it has been true for three days and it is going to be true in a year, and if it goes in this book as a count then somebody in a year can divide one by the other and get a figure of how many people sleep under this floor, and that figure will be about eleven years old, and it will have come out of a kitchen, and I will be dead.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything.

"**That is the same reason I gave in the twenty-ninth week,**" said Mikka Hale, and Dalla Sen had her hand near the book and did not use it, "**and I want the clerk to enter that it is the same reason and not a new one, and that I have given it twice and it is the same sentence both times and it is going to stay the same sentence, and I would like it entered that a cook has now been asked twice and has refused twice and that the second refusal was easier and that I do not know why, and that that is as far as it goes.**"

Dalla Sen entered it in the cook's words, and entered that it is the second and not a new one, and entered that the clerk asked and was refused and did not ask a third time, and turned the book back the right way up and did not write it in the space where a reason goes.

"**And now the three things I have got my own to say,**" she said, "**and they are in the wrong order and I am entering them in the wrong order on purpose.**"

The room waited.

"**One. A pump stopped for seven days and it started again this morning on a rule with two lines in it and one of the lines belongs to a woman who is not in this building and one of them belongs to nobody, and the clerk entered that the sixth term has not been changed in forty weeks and the thing it describes has.**"

"**Two. A wall has a head of water on it three days in seven and that wall has five and a half shores and a spreader and a sixth place with nothing in it, and a foreman has said that somebody with a better eye than his has to look at it before Wednesday, and no name has been attached to the somebody.**"

"**Three. A bed under a hearth stone went up a third of an inch between the twenty-ninth week and Friday, and the room behind that bed has nine children in it four mornings in seven, and a woman who reads walls has said out loud in a room that the water did not come up through that floor and therefore came in through a wall, and she has declined to shut the room, and I have entered the declining and the reason and I have not entered a cause.**"

"**Those are my three and I have entered them one at a time and no reason goes beside any of them, and they do not go next to each other either, and I am aware that I have said that before, in the thirty-seventh week, with three facts, and I have thought about it for three days and I am going to do it again and I would like a clerk who is not me to tell me whether that is a rule or a habit.**"

"**You are the clerk,**" said Bel Ordry, and he had not come down the last seven steps.

"**I am the clerk,**" said Dalla Sen. "**So here is the half against, and it is mine, and it is the only one in this morning. Three true things with nothing between them is the only shape a person can pick up later and use without a motive. Any other shape hands whoever finds it a motive to go looking for a fourth, and there is a fourth in this building and I know roughly where it is, and a person in a year with a pen and three facts and three empty reasons is going to fill them in in the order I wrote them, and they are going to write down a pump, and then a wall, and then a school, and they are going to think they have found out something, and they will have found out that this boundary had three things it could not explain in the same week, and the four of those is in here somewhere and I am not putting it in and I am not letting it get there by being tidy.**"

While the four of them were saying it, the building went on being a building. The nine had been and gone and the flags by the kitchen-line door were cold again the way they are cold on a seventh day, and there was a man eight feet under a rule in a room off the far end who had come out of it twice in a fortnight, and the guild's claim was squared on the trestle with a name in its second line, and above the bench a plate wrapped in work cloth had not been opened for forty weeks, and in the pocket of a coat on a hook a brass rule that had said nothing for thirty-one weeks went on saying nothing, which is a thing a room learns to carry after a while and then finds heavier, and which nobody in that anteroom had said out loud and which was therefore not in the book.

Then the sixth bell came, and the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a seventh day is a day nothing crosses on and a room that has just entered three things it cannot join is owed the morning's own numbers before the day is over.

"**One today and three hundred and thirty since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and forty-seven and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty-five days and one hundred and fifty-five nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and fifty-six, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for forty-five weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in forty-five weeks. That is not stopping, and a cook in this building has refused this book twice, and I will not be the third time somebody in this room tries to make me put a number in it that a person put there on purpose.**"

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a clerk had put three facts in a book with three empty reasons and had said out loud that she knew roughly where the fourth one was, and a board is a column, and a clerk does not walk a column on the morning she has told a room there is a fourth thing.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman said on Saturday morning that the water did not take anything off him and that it was going to take four and a half hours on Wednesday, and that he has not said the reason out loud yet, and that the crew's column is a quantity of asking and it does not move on a Saturday, or on a Sunday, and not on a Monday either unless a reason has been said in a room the day before.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave one on Friday and nothing on Saturday and nothing this morning, and nobody has measured why, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for sixteen weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a woman came up nine steps on Wednesday and asked this building to do nothing to a room full of water and went down them on Friday and it is not known whether she will come back.

Then Marek Venn was asked, and he was asked by a woman with a slate, and the question was not a question anybody had put in the book, and the clerk entered the asking and the fact that she had not asked it in a corridor.

"**Are you going to say what the water took,**" said Dalla Sen, with her hand near the book and the book shut.

"**No,**" said Marek Venn, without looking up from the trestle.

She entered it, and entered that he was asked and refused, and entered that he gave a reason.

"**Saying it in a room is making a claim,**" he said, with his hand flat on the shut book the whole time, "**and a claim in this building is a drawing, and a drawing is a plan, and a plan has a person in it. I said that on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week with my hand on a book and I have not said it since because I said it would not be given again this month, and it has been a week, and I am going to hold to the week I set myself in a room, because if I do not then nothing I say in here is worth anything and there is a foreman in this building who has just told a physician that his sentence is a sentence and is not to be taken apart before Wednesday, and I have watched that man build a practice out of not being taken apart, and that is not a thing I am going to do in a week.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that the reason is the same reason he gave on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week and that the clerk has noticed it is the same reason and has entered that it is the same reason, and entered that a man who has set himself a week in a room is either keeping a term or building a seat and that this room has recently had a conversation about seats,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**And the other thing, and there is a second half to it, and I am going to say it, because there is a woman at a table who has now asked me twice for the same thing,**" Marek Venn said. "**On Monday a foreman stood at a trestle and read a guild's second line out loud and said a name was on it that he had not put there, and I was in this room, and I have known since the Friday before that the answer to that was in my mouth, and I have had it for four days and I have not given it up and nobody in this room has asked me for it once.**"

"**Why not,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Because the answer is a question and I have not asked it,**" he said, and did not look at the guild's claim on the trestle while he was saying it, and everybody in that room noticed that he did not look at it. "**Whether the hand that filled in that second line is the hand at the counter this boundary has been dealing with for eight weeks, or another hand in the same office four days away, is a question, and the answer is a person's name either way, and if I say the name in this room then a claim in a file acquires a person in it who did not know they were in it, and I would rather somebody who is responsible for that file be the one to put that name down.**"

"**Then I will say the thing nobody has said,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I will say it in the space where a finding goes, and it is a finding, and it is the fourth one this week and it is the one I said I knew roughly where it was. A name is in a second line on a form in this building and it is not ours and it is not anybody's who is in this room, and it will be in a letter within a month, and the boundary knew that on the first morning and put it in a book and has had no way to put it anywhere else, and a man at the end of a trestle has had the other half of it for four days and has kept it, and I am entering both of those and I am not joining them.**"

Nobody joined them.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with a book on it with three pages turned forward in it and three spaces left empty, and a pump that had run, and a guild's claim squared on the table with a name in its second line and a line of chalk through its fourth, and a man in a doorway who had slept eight feet under a rule for nineteen weeks and had come out twice in a fortnight to say true things and had asked for nothing, and a book in a coat on a hook with a brass rule in the pocket of it that had said nothing for thirty-one weeks, and a plate on a shelf above the bench that had not been opened for forty weeks, and a wall with five and a half shores and a spreader and a sixth place with nothing in it and a head of water on it three days in seven, and a schoolroom with nine hearth stones in it where a bed had gone up a third of an inch, and a corridor that opens on Wednesday, and a foreman who has four and a half hours of work coming and has not said the reason out loud yet, and a gallery at the bottom of a passage off the second gate that nobody in this building has been in and that a line ran through three rooms, one of which had nine children in it four mornings in seven.
