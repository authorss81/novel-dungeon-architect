# Chapter 155 — What A Keeper Can Say No To

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the thirtieth week, a Friday, and the school ran for the twenty-fourth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and a man of forty-nine came down the middle passage from a breaking survey camp and said what he was and what he kept before anybody asked him, and he did not ask anybody for anything, and the room discovered a third thing that was neither a refusal nor a purchase.**

**A store is a room, and a room has a keeper in it, and a keeper can be asked in a room with the terms out loud, and a keeper can say no, and the price is not money because there is no money in this Hearthhold and there has not been for thirty weeks.**

At the fourth bell the nine went in and one of them went in sitting down, and the strip under the ninth hearth stone was holding and the two flags had not moved, and Jo Verrick said the standing term out loud in the doorway and Mero Senn said it out of his own hand against hers and Ilsa Norr said yes to it, and Dalla Sen entered that the term is the same in two hands, and entered that she had been entering that sentence for five weeks and was not going to stop entering it on the morning a man came down four days of road with nothing to carry.

Ormer Hatch came up the nine steps at the second bell on his own, with nothing in his hands, and he was not carrying a tally and he did not have a book, and he stood in the anteroom and looked at the plan on the table in four parts and did not touch it.

"**Ormer Hatch. Survey camp. Forty-nine. Thirty-one years on and off. I keep a store, and the store is a lean-to of dressed stone with a timber frame against the cut face, and there is eleven years of beech in it and a quantity of fir, and there is no roof on the north side worth the name, and I have never once been asked for any of it by anybody and I am not here to ask for it.**"

"**Say the other half,**" said Bel Ordry, "**which is why you are here.**"

"**Because your carpenter came up my passage on Wednesday with a carpenter's questions and he asked me the first question you ask a man and I told him no, and then I sent him down here with a message that was not mine and was my keeper's, and the message was that a store is a room and I keep it, and I would like to hear the terms from whoever keeps whatever is asking for it.**"

He said the reason for the message out loud, in a room, without being asked, and the reason was the best thing anybody said in that anteroom all week and it was said by a man who had come four days to say it.

"**Eleven years is a long time to be the reason a thing is in a place. I have said no to a camp man and to a guild man and to a man from a store two valleys over, and every one of those three said no would be the same as a man who is difficult about his own work. And you have built something in here in thirty weeks that I have not seen anywhere, which is that a person can be asked for something in a room and can say no without it being a thing about them.**"

"**I want to be asked. That is why I came. I have not been asked in eleven years and I did not know that was a thing I wanted until your carpenter asked me the wrong question on Wednesday and I found out I was still standing here twenty minutes after he had gone.**"

Dalla Sen entered that a man had come down four days of passage with nothing in his hands in order to be asked for something, and entered that the clerk had no column for that, and entered that she was not going to invent one, and that it was going in the book in the form of the sentence he had said.

Then the room did the thing, and the thing was not clever and it was thirty weeks old.

"**Say the terms,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, and she came down three steps to say them, which she does not do.

"**The practice is this and it is not new and I am not dressing it up because a man came four days. When this boundary asks one of its own five for a work, the asking is in a room and the terms are said out loud, and a person is named who can be told no, and the three who are not going to be asked are named with the reason for each before anybody is asked anything. It has been run three times in thirty weeks and it has moved a column of hours by eleven and a half and it has not once been a favour.**"

"**And the reason I am standing on these steps is that the reason it works is that a keeper can be asked. A corridor keeper. A room keeper. A store keeper.**"

"**Say the two available actions back,**" said Hatch, **"because I have been in a room with two of them before and I want to hear whether yours are the same two.**"

Bel Ordry said them, and he said them standing, and Dalla Sen had her chalk out before he had finished the first one.

"**One: this boundary fills in lines five and six of a carrier's paper, names a person, and the beech comes up the middle passage as a load that this boundary is responsible for. Two: this boundary does not, the beech stays in a lean-to four days up a road, and there is no wall in lime in this Hearthhold before the weather turns, or ever.**"

"**Third,**" said Ormer Hatch, and he said it in a room with nine people in it and he was not asking permission, "**and I have not got a reason why you would want it, so I am going to put it to you and you can say no to it, which is apparently what happens down here.**"

"**The beech is not mine. I have told you that. The camp owns it until the camp closes and the camp closes when the last of the season's work is done and nobody above me will tell me the day and I have stopped asking twice. So I cannot give you a load and I cannot promise you a load and if I promised you one I would be a man who has promised something that is not his, and I have watched that happen to other men and it ends with a name on a piece of paper forty days away.**"

"**But I keep the store. The store has a list. And the list is fourteen lines long and it is true of a morning, and I can walk you up it in a room and say what is in there and how much of it and what condition it is in, and I can do that today, and I can do it on any morning you ask me for, and I can stop.**"

"**And that is the whole of what I have got and I would like somebody in this room to tell me what a person does with it, because I have been standing in my own store for eleven years giving things away to men who could carry them and this is the first time anybody has suggested that the giving was the part I was doing wrong.**"

Nobody had an answer ready, and Talla Roke had one, and she said it from the step with her three behind her and her hands loose, and she said it in the shape she uses in this anteroom now, which is the shape of a person saying the half of a thing that is against her own position before anybody asks her for it.

Marek Venn had been at the far end of the anteroom with his left hand open against the back of the bench for the better part of an hour, and what he had been doing was listening to a man describe a practice this building has been running on five people for thirty weeks and had never once thought of running on anybody with a pile of timber behind them.

"**It is not a free thing. I want that said in a room and I want it said in the first minute, and I have said that a road is a public good twice in this building in a month and nobody answered me either time and I am not going to be coy now.**"

"**A thing given by a person and not carried by a person is not given. It is a favour with a keeper. And a keeper who is owed a favour has less room to say no the second time, and less again the third, and this boundary has spent thirty weeks learning that a favour is a loan with no date on it and that a wage that can be pointed at is a wage that can be stopped.**"

"**I am not saying do not do it. I am saying that if you do it then the terms have to be in your clerk's book in his words and in yours, and one of the terms has to be that he can stop, and one of them has to be that nobody in this Hearthhold's name goes on anything of his, and one of them has to be that he can read the terms back to a room and say no to any of them, and not to me, and not to a carrier, and not to a guild.**"

"**And I am going to say the other half, which is against the whole of what I have just said, because I would be no use to anybody in this building if I only ever said the half against things.** A man with eleven years in a store who is being asked for his list is not a carrier and this boundary is not buying timber. **And a boundary that has spent thirty weeks refusing to fill in two lines on a stranger's paper has just worked out that there is a third thing, and the third thing is that a person who keeps something can be asked instead of an office that carries something, and that is not a favour, that is a keeper.**"

"**Enter that in two halves and in my words and do not improve on either,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Now the price,**" said Hatch. "**You have all been waiting for me to say it and I am going to say it and you are not going to like the first half of it.**"

"**There is no money in this building. I have been told that by your carpenter on Wednesday in a corridor and I have believed it because he was not carrying a book either. So the price is not coin and I am not going to insult you by pretending otherwise.**"

"**The price is two things and I have thought about them for four days.**"

"**One: I will not be named on a paper of this boundary's. Not on the plan, not in the third column, not in a letter, not on a list anybody carries up a passage. If that is the price then I will walk up the road tonight and I will not think about it again.**"

"**And the two is this. I want my list read out in a room. Here. With me in it. And I want the whole of it, and I want it read the whole way through including the lines that are nearly out, and I want a person in this room to write down that they have heard it. And I am not going to be in a room where a man reads a summary of it, because I have been a man whose store was summarised for thirty-one years.**"

"**There is a third thing and it is not a price and I am going to say it because a room that has been built on people saying the half that is against them should get a third thing when one is offered.**"

"**I want the fourth paragraph of a guild notice named in that room, out loud, as the thing it is. It is a paragraph that says a person under a suspended master may go on in the trade for a period not exceeding one year under the supervision of a named holder entered against a named route, in a register kept by an office, on payment of an annual supervision fee. And I have read it three times in thirty-one years and I have never once had anybody say it out loud in a room where a person was about to be asked for something.**"

There was no argument about the two and none about the third, and Bel Ordry said the reason out loud before he answered it, which is what he does.

"**The fourth paragraph has been refused three times in this building. By the man it would have named, by the boy it would have paid, and by both of them again for this boundary's charter. And it is not going to be named in this room this morning, and I am going to give you the reason and then the sentence, and the sentence is the one you asked for.**"

"**I am not going to be the man who opens that paragraph in a room on a Friday morning to persuade a man with eleven years of beech in a store to part with it. That is a room with something in it in it and you would be the something. So it goes in the book on a day when there is nothing on the table, and you will be in the room, and I will read it, and I will read the three refusals after it, and I will read them in order.**"

"**Say when.**"

"**Say when the list is read. It goes in the same morning. That is my price and I am not going to haggle on the parchment with a man who came four days on his own feet.**"

Then the list was read, and it was fourteen lines, and Dalla Sen said what she was going to do before she did it, and the man who keeps the store said it again in his own words so that there would be two.

"**I am going to read all fourteen lines and then a second person is going to read all fourteen lines again against what he said, and the two are compared, and that is the practice in this building and it does not matter that it is a stranger's list. It is a list of what is in a room. The room is four days up a road and the man who keeps it is standing in mine, and he has asked for the whole of it read, and I am going to read the whole of it.**"

"**Do that,**" said Ormer Hatch, "**and read the ninth one twice, because the ninth one is the one I am ashamed of and I want it in a book that is not mine.**"

She read it, and a second person read it, and the two were the same, and Dalla Sen entered that they were the same and entered that the store's list carries no date on it and entered the reason, which the schoolteacher gave in her own mouth and did not know she was giving.

"**Why is there no date on your list?**"

"**Because it was true this morning and it will not be true this afternoon, and a date on it would be a claim that it was true on a day. That is the shape of a count. I have been telling a room the shape of a count since the twenty-ninth week and I did not know I was going to be told it by a man with a tally of beech.**"

"**Say the shape of a count again in your own words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**into the book, for me.**"

"**A count of that kind is only true of a moment, and the moment is the only thing about a room that is worth a number. A list with a date on it is a moment pretending to be a day. I would rather it were a moment and said so.**"

"**Entered in the schoolteacher's words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a store's list four days up this passage has the same shape as a count taken out of a room at the strokes and that neither of them is a number anybody can carry up a road and be right about, and I have entered those two facts next to each other with a reason and nothing between them, on purpose, and I have not been asked to and I am not going to be.**"

---

And a man on the anteroom step, in the hour before the bell, said one sentence about the week he had had, and it is in his own book and not in this one, and Dalla Sen entered that he had said it and did not write it down and that she was not going to ask him for it.

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and the board was not walked, and the count was read, and the pot was read, and everything in that anteroom on the fifth day of the thirtieth week was said out loud.**

"**Two today and a hundred and seventy-nine since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and ninety-six and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks. The crew are at sixty-six and a half.**"

"**I read those out because a man read fourteen lines of his own store out loud in this room and asked for them to be taken down, and it seemed to me that a room where a stranger's whole inventory is on the table and nobody will say a number out loud is a room that has learned something the wrong way round.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two today and one on Tuesday and the seam gave four threads at the first bell and Jo says she has not measured it since the nineteenth week and is not going to say what eight was in a corridor two months ago.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is ninety days and ninety nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be ninety-one, and I have said in five weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and I am writing it, and a man came four days down a passage today and I did not tell him a kind thing about it because he did not ask and I am glad and that is the third time this month I have entered that I am glad.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the count did not walk it, and the reason is in his words and it is: a man from a store is standing in this room who asked to be asked for something, and a board is the thing in this building a person asks for when they want to know what a person has been doing, and he has not asked, and I am not going to put it in front of him.**"

---

**On the first day of next week an air engineer is going to ask a man who keeps no room and stands no watch for a piece of work, and she is going to do it in a room with the terms out loud, and she is going to name the three she is not going to ask and the reason for each before she asks him anything.**

**Four days up the middle passage there is a lean-to of dressed stone and a timber frame with no roof on the north side worth the name, and eleven years of beech and a quantity of fir in it, and fourteen lines in a book with no date on it, and a man in it who would rather be asked than be right, and who has not been asked in eleven years and found out this month that he still wanted to be.**

**This Hearthhold has no money, a post with a name in a register is on its way up a passage, and the fifth day of a week is nearly over.**
