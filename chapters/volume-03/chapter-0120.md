# Chapter 120 — The Survey Is Right

**The fifth day of the twenty-third week was a Friday and the corridor was open and the school ran at the far end of it for the ninth morning running, and two men came up the middle passage with a sheet of drawing, and the sheet said this boundary's sector was stable, and the sheet was right, and being right was the worst thing that had happened to it in a month.**

Harlan Merrow introduced himself the way he had the last time, which was to say his name and the office and then say the thing he did not expect to be received well.

"**I recommended your charter up a road and I told you I did not expect it to be granted. I have since surveyed your sector with a crew of eleven and I am bringing you the finding and the finding is that your sector is stable, and I want to say the next part in a room and not on a landing, because I would like a clerk to have it.**

"**I would rather it was not.**"

There was nothing for a moment after that.

"**That is not a favour. That is my own trade telling me that a sector with nothing in it does not get a crew put in it again, and there are nineteen sectors on this line and eleven of them have something in them and I have spent four weeks in a camp eating my own food and looking at your waist.**"

Then he unrolled the sheet on the anteroom table, next to the market form that was still lying face up where a clerk had chosen to put it, and Dalla Sen did not ask him to move it, and entered that she had not asked him to move it.

**To a scale, competently drawn, four days of work and eleven men,** the sheet had a legend, and the legend was the ordinary kind.

"**Four things,**" said Harlan Merrow. "**I will give you the four and I will give you the measurements and I am not going to be asked for them twice and I would like them entered in a clerk's hand as I say them, because I have been on a road for four weeks and I would like one thing I have said to be in a book in the place it is about.**

"**One. The narrowing between the dip and the school door. Moved three times since the middle of the last month, which I know because there are three wedge holes in it and I have dated all three and they are not the same date, which I did not expect. The movement is between a finger and a hand in total, it is seasonal, and it is going and coming with the cold, and it is not a fault.**"

Dalla Sen wrote it and read it back, and nobody filled the space after it.

"**Two,**" said Harlan Merrow. "**The doorframe at the head of your outer service door. A finger and a bit out of true, packed in stone and clay, and the clay is the whole of the story because clay shrinks. It is not a fault. It is a bad repair on a sound wall and it will go on being a bad repair at a finger a week until somebody puts stone in lime there, and there is no stone in lime in this Hearthhold and no timber and no bond.**"

"**Say the timber part again,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The timber you were promised for the month after next is in the store the survey is camped in,**" said Harlan Merrow. "**I put it there. That is a decision I made in the second week of the camp and I will make it again and I would like it written down that I made it and that I have not pretended for a moment that I did not enjoy making it.**"

"**Write all of it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**including the second part, and including that he said he enjoyed it, because a clerk who leaves out the second part is a clerk writing a different man's speech.**"

"**Three. The floor of the room at the school-door end, which is shut and now is not shut. There is a bed joint under it and a stone has been taken out of a course and put back in lime, and the joint ran to the haunch behind the service door and the water came up under the low corner because nine children had worn the flags dished toward the door. **It is not a fault. It is a use.** And I am aware that a surveyor is supposed to be able to tell a use from a fault and I have been in this trade for twenty-two years and I am telling you that in a building this old, the difference is a matter of whose floor it is.**"

Nera Oduya said, from three steps, "**That is the first sentence anybody from your trade has said in this building that I have written down, and I would like the clerk to write that a survey said *a use* and that the air engineer heard it.**

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, and wrote it, and said it out, and Harlan Merrow said yes.

"**Four,**" said Harlan Merrow. "**And this is the one I came up the road for and it is the only one of the four that is a finding and the other three are a list.**

"**Your sector is stable. Bearing, drainage and load are within what I would have expected of a building of this age in this ground, the two rooms that are rated are rated for what they say and not for more, and there is no condition in anything I have surveyed that I would put a closure on, and if I were asked to recommend that this boundary be left alone for a season I would recommend it in writing.**"

He put his hand flat on the sheet, the way everybody in this building puts a hand flat on things, and he had noticed that they did it and he did not comment.

"**And that is where I want the room to have me, so I will say it. Your sector is stable, and the timber for a wall in lime is in my store, and a stable sector is a sector that nobody has a reason to fund, and the two of those facts are in the same sheet and I put them in the same sheet on purpose, and I am not going to pretend to you that I have not thought about it, and I am telling you that I thought about it and put them together anyway, because the finding is the finding.**"

---

**And the answer came from the page, and Bel Ordry's answer was the answer he had given at the fourth bell of the second day of the twentieth week, and he gave it before anybody prompted him, and Dalla Sen entered that he had not been prompted.**

"**A record written by the people who live in a sector is not a survey.**

"**And I said that once at the fourth bell on the second day of this month and I said it once and did not say it twice and I am not going to say it twice now. Here is the rest of it, and I have had four weeks to find the rest of it and here it is, and it is not a defence.**

"**Our record has an unmeasured fill. It has a hair laid across a chalk line where a rule should be, because a hair is what we have and a rule is what we do not, and I have said out loud in this room that a hair is not a measurement. Our record has a wedge up a quarter of an inch at a waist that has moved three times and that three people have agreed on purpose never to measure, and one of those three is Renn Vale and she has given the reason every time and I have believed her every time and I have never once been able to argue with it.**

"**And you have measured it. And your movement is between a finger and a hand, and it is seasonal, and it is not a fault. Which means our record is not a worse version of your record. It is a different thing, and the thing it is is the only thing anybody in this building has got, and it is worth exactly as much as the man in the coat, and I said that in this room three weeks ago and I am not going to take it back because a competent man has come up a road with a piece of drawing.**"

"**Then what do you want me to do with the sheet,**" said Harlan Merrow.

"**Put it on the wall,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I will not put a date on it, and you may put whatever date you like on it in your own hand, and the two of those are the same disagreement and I am not going to have it in a room with a clerk in it.**

"**And I would like one thing put on the wall beside it, in this boundary's own hand, which is that this boundary was not asked to be surveyed, was not asked whether it would agree, and has been told that its sector is stable, and has entered that it is glad, and is not able to do anything with the gladness.**"

"**You are glad,**" said Harlan Merrow.

"**I am glad that nothing in this Hearthhold is a fault. I have been in a building for twenty-three weeks where every single thing that has gone wrong has gone wrong inside it, and a man with a sheet has told me that the ground is behaving itself, and I am glad, and I have no use for it at all, and I would like it noted that those two are in the same man and not in two men.**"

---

**At the fourth bell the market route's answer went out, on a market day, carried by a man who was going that way anyway and by nobody else,** and it went out with two sentences on it and no name and no signature, and Bel Ordry did not put his ring on it, and Dalla Sen put a clerk's routing mark on it and nothing else, and she told him she was going to tell him about his ring before he decided and she did.**

Pettin Rahl was in the anteroom with his own business on his back, on a market day, going to the gate at the bottom of the road, and he said he would carry a sheet and that he would not charge for it and that he was going there anyway.

"**Say the charter's part out loud,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because I want it in a room and I want it before the sheet leaves anybody's hand.**

"**The road up the middle passage is not shut and will not be shut for a delivery and nobody may be charged for it. **This is not a delivery. That is the whole reason it is going today and on no other day, and if I hand it to a man on a carrying day it is a delivery and the charter does not cover it and this boundary is not going to be the first instrument in this story to bend a rule it wrote itself.**"

"**Say that again slower,**" said Pettin Rahl, "**because I have carried a great many things on carrying days and I never once thought about it.**

"**You are telling me that if it went today it would be the wrong day to send it.**

"**I am telling you that it would be a different kind of act, and that this boundary is careful about the kind of act, and that it has four instruments and a hole in the middle of them and it is not going to add a fifth kind of act to the pile on a Friday.**"

Then she read the two sentences out, twice, and the cook said yes to hers again and Bel Ordry said yes to the shape of his, and there was no date on the sheet, and it went into a bag with three other people's goods in it and down nine steps and along a corridor two foot eight wide and past a waist with a wedge up a quarter of an inch, at a walk, on a market day, for nothing, by a man nobody in this building would ever be able to find.

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked for the fourth time in three weeks, the six things were read, and the room filled in after the last one the way it does.**

"**The crew are at fifty-three and a half and they moved once in three weeks and the reason is in the book in a clerk's hand and I am not giving it.**"

"**The boundary is at a hundred and eighty-eight and a quarter, and that is thirty-five in these ten chapters and seventy-one since the keeper signed, and it adds by the day the work was done, and both columns reconcile and they have reconciled for ten weeks.**"

"**Thirteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, and one mark. Nine in the ordinary column and four in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim is still a claim and its condition is still not met. The mark came off a jamb on Wednesday at the sixth bell and it is still on this board and I have given the reason and it is a good one and it is the fifth week of refusing to head that column and I have not got a reason and I have said so out loud and nobody has asked me for one.**"

A man of twenty-two was fifty-five days and fifty-five nights into a count that is his own and that is in the column with nothing over the top of it and in nothing else. The pot was ten days, and had been ten days for twenty-six days, and had never once gone up in that time. The shut school corner was open at the end of the corridor with a dry floor in it and no shelf and no nine children in it and four marks and a groove on the lintel above the door that two people read two ways. The anteroom held ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and its head was a finger and a bit out of true, and its second way out was a word.

**And the third column of the plan left this building on a Friday in the twentieth week in a clerk's hand** and had never come back, and the plan was in four parts in a coat and did not leave the building, and the charter was on a scrub board with an office's number and no name, and the second sheet was under it in two hands with no date, and four sentences lay face up on the anteroom table with a market form beside them that a stranger had read in four seconds.

**And a survey party's finding went on a wall in this boundary's own hand and undated, and it said that this sector is stable, and it was correct, and it was the first correct thing anybody had said about this building from outside it in twenty-three weeks.**

Marek Venn stood at the foot of the nine steps at the seventh bell with a sheet of drawing in his hand that he had read four times and a left hand in his pocket that had burned nine days ago and does not close at night, and the pump's check came off, and the sixth minute of the low end of the corridor came and went with nobody standing at the low end of it, and the room above him was full and rated for ten and could be emptied in the strokes by anybody who asked, and when it was empty the word did not need saying.

He had said in this room three weeks ago that he was not going to do the one thing he had left. He had done it on a Tuesday for an afternoon and a hand, and it had held a bit of nothing for nine days, and the woman who kept the terms had taken it off with a cloth, and the head was where it had been, and the sound was still in the room, and he was a man who could not leave and was not needed, and both of those had been true for three weeks and one of them had stopped being true on Tuesday and neither of them had been a relief.

**And the survey was right.**

He stood there and understood, four days too late, in the way he had understood a rule being portable once before, that **stable is what a survey says about a thing it is not going to come back to.** That the crew would go. That the finding would go into a file. That a sector found sound is a sector nobody has any reason to look at again until something in it changes, and that the only thing anybody in this building had to do about that was to be glad, and he was glad, and he had no use for it at all, and there was no chalk in his hand, and there was nobody in the room he was in who had said one word about any of it.
