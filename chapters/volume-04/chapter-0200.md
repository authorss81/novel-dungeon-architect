# Chapter 200 — Who Is Keeping Them

The first day of the thirty-eighth week is a Monday, the corridor is shut, and the school did not run and lost nothing. That is two mornings running on which nothing has crossed this building in either direction, and the passage at the top of the nine steps opened at the first bell and shut again at the second, and a foreman who went up it on Saturday has not come back down it, and nobody in this building has said anything about that in nine hours.

Bel Ordry put the plate and the coat and the rule where they go, and the plate on the shelf above the bench is wrapped in work cloth and has been unopened for thirty-eight weeks, and the brass survey rule in the pocket of the coat on the hook has said nothing for twenty-nine.

"**And that is out of a calendar this building does not keep,**" he said, "**and I am the only person in this room with that number, and it is a number about a coat, and a person in a year who finds it in a book of mine is going to have to decide for himself whether a number about a coat is a number about anything.**"

Dalla Sen entered both in the state they were found and entered that neither came into a room.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twentieth reading, and the same as nineteen, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and the line along the join is still in two places, and she gave no figure for either of them and put the rule away.

Renn Vale came down the four steps at the second bell, which is her bell on a shut morning, and was there about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times. The clerk entered a figure of nothing against it and entered that she was not going to give the run a number on the last morning of a week in which the run had been seven out of seven.

"**The five and a half is still five and a half,**" Jo Verrick said, from where she was standing, with nobody having asked her. "**The sixth place is empty and the iron for it is four days up a passage under an office that has just shut two gates I have never been through, and those two facts are not going to be joined together in a room by me, and I would like a clerk to enter that I said that out loud before somebody did it for me.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it,**" said Dalla Sen.

The anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, all six, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and the pump was attended and stopped on the rising water line that has still not moved.

The two networks were in the room and were not in each other. Renn Vale had a slate in her apron and Nella Bray had one in her belt, and neither of them took hers out, and the clerk entered that two conditions were in the building and entered neither of them and entered that she had entered that twice now and would go on entering it.

The cook was in the kitchen-line doorway with the cloth over her arm, and she stood in it rather than on the step, and she stayed there for the whole of the question and the whole of the not-answering of it.

"**Two days,**" said Mikka Hale. "**The seam gave nothing on Friday and nothing yesterday and nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and I have been asked four times this week to be careful with that seam and four times the answer is the same, which is that I do not know. And six people who are not this building's ate here yesterday and the day before, and there is a seventh mouth in this building this week than there was on Friday, and I have said nothing about that, and I would like it entered that I noticed it and did not say it.**"

"**Entered, and entered that she was not asked about it,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then the room got to the thing the day was for, and nobody had called it and nobody arranged it, and it happened at the fourth bell with the pump between strokes.

Iven Tallo set the chisel down on the anvil and left it there, and kept his left hand open and flat on the iron where it has lain all month, and looked at the wall and not at anybody in the room.

"**On Friday I counted four steps in a wall,**" he said. "**I have been in front of that join every working morning for four weeks with my hands on the stone at about the height of my chest, and on Friday I found four places along the length of it where the face on the kitchen-line side steps back about a hand's width and then comes out again. Four. And a wall that steps back four times is not a wall. It is four rooms with about nine feet of stone between them, and I have counted them twice since Friday and I got four both times, and how I count them is mine, and if somebody asks me I will say that it is my trade and that it is a carpenter's answer and not a survey.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And I am not asking what is in them,**" he said. "**I have not asked this room what is in that gap once in four weeks and I am not starting this morning, and I have watched four people in this building come close to asking it and go somewhere else, and the fifth of those four people will not be me, and I would like it entered that I have said so before I asked what I did ask.**"

Then he gave the reason he was asking, in his own mouth, which nobody had asked him for and which he had plainly been carrying since Thursday evening.

"**I was handed a reason on Thursday for turning down a wage on the first and the fifteenth and a boy off a roll,**" he said. "**And I have had four days with it and I have not worked out what it is. That is the truth and it is four days old. And the part of it I have not worked out is whether a thing can be kept by somebody who is not the person it belongs to, and that is not a question about a wage and it is not a question about a boy, and it is a question about a wall, and I am the only man in this room who has spent his life on walls and has never once owned one.**"

He put his hand flat on the anvil.

"**So here is the question, and I am asking it out loud in a room with people in it, and it is said once. Who is keeping them.**"

The pump started and got four strokes and stopped, and the room did not use one of them.

Nobody answered it.

Bel Ordry came down the step, all the way, which he does perhaps twice in a week, and stood at the end of the trestle with his hands behind his back.

"**I am going to say the half against the question and not an answer to it,**" he said, "**and I want that distinction entered before I say anything else, because in this building the two get confused about once a week and this is the week it would have been expensive.**"

"**A man asked this room a question in the thirtieth week,**" he said, "**and it was four words, and it was *who owns a wall*, and it came up nine steps in a survey camp foreman's own mouth because he was the only person in this district whose whole trade is keeping a thing that is not his, and nobody answered him, and he said that was all right, and he went up the passage on the Monday with three of his own and a load of fir.**"

"**That was six weeks ago and nobody has answered it, and nobody in this room should pretend that the question a carpenter asked this morning is a different question because it has four rooms in it. It is the same question with a carpenter in it. I will not answer it either, and I am the man in this building most likely to be able to, and I have been asked twice this month by people in offices whether this boundary owns its own walls and I have said no both times and both times it was true and neither time it was the question.**"

Then Mero Senn said the sentence, once, out loud, and then said it again in a different shape, which is what he does.

"**A plan with a named injured person in it is not a plan,**" he said, from the doorway with his bag against the wall. "**And a question with a named room in it is not a question, it is the first line of a plan, and the first line of that plan is written by the man who found out how many rooms are behind a wall, and every person in this room knows how his voice sounds when he has found out something, and it is not the voice he is using now.**"

Then Renn Vale came down all four steps and stood in the middle of the floor with the hole in the corner at her feet, and she did not go near the wall.

"**I am the person in this room whose whole job is a question about whether a place is a room,**" she said, "**and I have been asked about that place four times in four weeks and I have said nothing four times, and this morning I am going to say one thing and then stop.**"

"**If there are four rooms on the other side of that join then this building has five ways out of itself and not two, and I said the number out loud in this room nine days ago and put it on nothing, and a number of ways out of a room that a room has heard and not objected to is a drawing. So the first cost of that carpenter's question is that somebody in this room is going to have to draw something, and I have spent thirty-eight weeks making sure that nobody in this room is anybody's draughtsman.**"

Then Tams Quill, from the end of the bench, with the piece of chalk he has not used.

"**A nineteenth entry,**" he said, unprompted, and then, before the clerk had the chalk up, "**No. The seventh reason is not for this morning, and she does not get to ask me for it in the same breath as a question this room has not answered. A room that has not answered a question is not going to get a column out of me on the same morning.**"

Dalla Sen put the chalk down.

"**Entered, and entered that I had it up,**" she said, "**and entered that I put it down, and entered that a man who has refused six things on six grounds said no to a seventh before I was able to ask and gave no reason and that nothing was manufactured here in front of me, and entered that the two of those facts came within about four seconds of each other on a Monday morning and that I would like a person in a year to know that.**"

Then Ilsa Norr came up the nine steps from the kitchen-line end with her slate in both hands, and she stopped at the fourth one and did not come further.

"**The condition stands,**" she said. "**The first of us to stop agreeing takes it out, and neither of us has to give a reason first, and it has been unexamined since the fourth day of the thirty-sixth week, and the man who put the brace there is in this room and he is the man who asked the question, and I am not asking him anything and nobody in this room is going to ask him anything, and I would like the clerk to enter that the two of those facts are next to each other on purpose.**"

Nobody asked him anything.

Then Sefu Aran came down two of the nine steps, which is as far as he has come in a fortnight, and stood there.

"**I am the elected keeper of this boundary and my instrument is admission and a crossing,**" he said, "**and a question about who keeps four rooms on the other side of a wall is neither of those, and the answer is not coming out of me, and I will not pretend I could not have given it, because I could. I have been the second person to hear that sound and I did not find out what it was and I entered that I did not.**"

"**And the half against me not answering,**" he said, "**is that a keeper who declines to spend his instrument on a question asked in a room is a keeper who is waiting for a better room, and there is no better room coming, and I have been on a road for most of two months and I will not pretend that is a reason for anything except that I know what it is to be away when a thing is decided.**"

Then Nera Oduya came down all three steps, which she does for air and for nothing else, and stood at the bottom of them with the dyed cloth folded in her hand and not out.

"**I do not have half of it this time,**" she said.

Nobody filled in the rest of the sentence for her and nobody asked her to.

"**I have said *I have half of it* in this building three times in two volumes and I gave half of it once and kept the other half twice, and this morning I have none of it and not a part of it, and that is the first time it has happened and I am telling the room because somebody in it will otherwise assume I am about to, and I would rather be known to have nothing than be left holding something in front of eleven people.**"

"**I have four numbers and not a fifth this morning,**" she said, "**and the fifth stays in my mouth, and my first condition is not met and my second condition is not met and nobody has asked me about either of them and nobody is going to this morning, and I am not going anywhere, and I have not said any of those five things to make anybody feel better about the sixth.**"

Nobody asked her.

Then the man with the shut book under his forearm spoke, and he had not spoken all morning, and what he said was the shortest thing anybody said in that anteroom and it took the room about four strokes of nothing to get used to it.

"**I know it for two of them,**" Marek Venn said.

The anteroom did not fill in after it.

"**Which two of them is mine and not this room's, what I know is mine, and how I know it is mine, and all three of those refusals are the same refusal and I am going to give the reason for it in one sentence and then that reason is not given again this month.**"

He put the hand that does not close flat on the shut book.

"**Saying it in a room is making a claim. A claim in this building is a drawing, and a drawing is a plan, and a plan has a person in it, and I have spent thirty-eight weeks refusing plans and the first one will not be mine.**"

"**The half against that,**" he said, before anybody could, "**and it is the only part of this morning that is mine. I have wanted to be the man who found out since the second week of this building, and the whole of what I have wanted since the second week is to be a man in a room who says a true thing and is believed, and this is the first morning in thirty-eight weeks where saying it would have worked, and it would have worked because a carpenter asked the question and not me, and a man's question is not the place for my answer, and I would like a clerk to enter that I noticed that the reason I was not going to answer was the best possible reason and that I am not sure it was the true one.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he did not soften the second half,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then the clerk was asked, and she had been waiting for it since the fourth bell, and she said so before she answered it.

"**Somebody asked me at the fourth bell to enter the question and the three things in my book from yesterday in the same place on the same page,**" she said, "**and I said no, and I am going to say why and then I am going to stop.**"

"**A question is not a finding and a finding is not a question, and putting them on one page makes a third thing out of them that is neither, and a third thing is the only kind of thing anybody has ever sent up a passage to an office. A week ago I was asked to put two questions next to each other and I would not, and they are next to each other in this book by accident and not by hand, and this morning I have been asked a third time in a fortnight and I have said no, and I am entering that I have been asked twice in a month to put two things next to each other and have refused twice, and that the two refusals are mine, and that they are not the same refusal, and that the second one was easier, and I do not know why, and that is as far as it goes.**"

"**And entered,**" she said, "**that a carpenter asked a question in this room at the fourth bell on the first day of the thirty-eighth week and that eleven people were in it, and that nobody answered it, and that I am entering it in the shape of a question and not in the shape of a finding, and that this is the last morning of the thirty-seventh week and it is not the last morning this building has, and that a person in a year is welcome to read this line and work out for themselves whether anything was answered.**"

Nobody said a word about the man who had asked it.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a man at the end of the bench had refused a seventh thing four hours ago and unprompted and a clerk does not walk a column in the same afternoon as that.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and she read them by themselves, with nothing else in the sentence and nothing else in the room.

"**Two today and three hundred since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and seventeen and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

She gave the reason out loud, once. A room had a question in it that nobody had answered, and a total read over an unanswered question is a clerk telling the room that the morning is over, and this morning is not over, and there is a rule about not putting a day on a morning and the rule applies to this one too.

And then she read the count, and she read it by itself, after a pause, out loud, and nobody in that room had asked her to.

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and thirty-five days and one hundred and thirty-five nights. It is not going to be one hundred and thirty-six. I have been promising this room for thirty-nine weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in thirty-nine weeks, and I have said out loud in most of those weeks that it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and this morning she left it out, because a carpenter has asked a question in this room and I would rather my last sentence of that kind belonged to somebody who had said four words and got nothing.**"

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book. The reason is that a room asked a question at the fourth bell and got no answer, and a crew that is owed nothing is not the instrument for answering a question. Asking five men to go and look at something nobody can name would be putting the question somewhere it can be found, and this boundary has spent thirty-eight weeks refusing to do that with anything.

The pot is two days and the seam is at four threads and gave nothing on three mornings running, and the four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and there is no figure on this building's anteroom either and there is not going to be one.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor, and a trestle with a book on it that had three pages turned forward in it and three empty spaces in it. There was a row of packs along the bench belonging to five men, and at the end of the row a gap of about a foot where a sixth pack had stood on Friday. The corridor opens tomorrow. Nine children will cross it and go round the corner of the floor without being told to.

Behind the party wall there were five and a half shores, a spreader under the head of the sill, a sixth place with nothing in it, a brace of a school's own beech, a bad repair on one face only, and a keeper's nine. On the other side of that join, if a carpenter was right, there were four rooms, and nothing else anybody in this building could say about them. On the far side of the two rooms off the far end of this anteroom past the pump there were six people who are not this building's and a door that latches. In the pocket of a woman on the fourth step there was a piece of slate with a condition on it and no number and no day, and in the belt of a woman from a counter there was another one.

A man at the anvil end was putting his chisel away and would not be thanked, and nobody was going to try. A man with a shut book under his forearm knew the answer for two of the four and had given a reason in one sentence and was not going to give it again this month. And the room went on being the room it is: a hole in the corner of its floor, a corridor that opens tomorrow, and a question somebody asked out loud in it in the fourth bell of a Monday that nobody in it has answered.
