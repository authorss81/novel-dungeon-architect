# Chapter 158 — Joinwood Out Of A Door

**Open for the corridor on the third day of the thirty-first week, a Wednesday, and the school ran for the twenty-fifth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and four men came down the middle passage with beech on their backs in pieces a cart could not have carried and a person could, and a woman who keeps a road gave away two wedges and two lengths of a school's own store and said the half of it that was against her in the same breath.**

**Every piece of it was entered in a clerk's book with no name against it and no seal and no form, and the store's list was read against what arrived and the two of them agreed, and that is the first time in thirty weeks that a document and a fact have agreed.**

The nine went in at the fourth bell, one of them sitting down, the strip of crate board holding under the ninth hearth stone, the two flags unmoved, Jo Verrick's standing term said out loud in the doorway, Mero Senn's out of his own hand against hers, and Ilsa Norr's yes. Dalla Sen entered that the term is the same in two hands, and that the count is to be read today whether or not anybody can bear it, because it has to be read.

Then the beech came up, and it came up in pieces, and four men carried it who are not named in this book and who are not going to be, and the fourth piece was longer than any of the others and two of them carried it between them without speaking.

Dalla Sen said what she was going to do before she did it, and it was the sentence she says when a stranger has put a thing in the room, and this time the thing was a pile of wood on a floor.

"**Every piece of this comes into this anteroom with no seal on it and no form and no paper, and it is entered in this book in its own lengths and its own condition and with no name against any line of it, and the man who kept it is not named on the book, and I am entering that he was in this room on Friday and asked to be asked, and that is the whole of what a clerk is entitled to put in a column about a piece of beech.**"

Then Pell Roke read the store's list, and he read it because he reads, and Dalla Sen said what she was going to do before she did it, in the shape she has used for six weeks.

"**He is going to read all fourteen lines and a second person is going to read all fourteen lines again against what the man said on Friday, and the two are compared. And the ninth one is going to be read twice, because the man who keeps the store asked for it to be read twice and told her which one it was.**"

"**And the numbers in it are not counts of anything, and I want that said out loud before he starts, and I would like it said by the clerk and not by the man, because a clerk is a person whose business is a figure and a boy of fourteen is a person whose business is reading.**"

"**A store's list is not a count of a room. It is a list of a room, at a moment, by a man who is in it.**"

Pell Roke read it, and he read the numbers in the same voice he reads the plan's first column, which is the voice this building has come to expect and which he has said out loud once is not a voice and is what he does.

"**Fifteen lengths. Four of them are out of the roof, and I am not going to say anything about them except that he said they were out of the roof.**"

"**Two hundred and six pieces of fir, in three grades. The third grade is for shuttering and he says so on the line. The second grade is for joists. The top grade is beech, and there are forty-one of them, and he has had them eleven years.**"

"**And eleven of them are better than the rest, and he has told me which eleven, and I am reading them as a number because that is what I do.**"

"**Four. Two. Eight. Six. Nine.**"

He stopped, and looked at the list, and did not go on for about the length of two strokes of the pump.

"**And a dash.**"

"**Say what the dash is,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**He says the dash is a beam that was in the store when he took it and has been in the store since and he is not going to sell it and does not want it read as a number. He says a dash is a thing a man does not promise and I am not going to argue with him because I read numbers and that is the end of what I do.**"

Nothing in that anteroom filled in after it, and nobody asked him about any of the fourteen lines, and the second person read all fourteen again against what the man had said on Friday, and the two were the same, and Dalla Sen entered that they were the same and entered that the dash was the ninth line and had been read twice.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the list carries no date on it and I have entered the reason the schoolteacher gave for that on Friday, and the reason is her own and she did not know she was giving it, and I am not going to improve on it, and the reason is that a list with a date on it is a moment pretending to be a day.**"

"**And I read the fourteen against what came down the passage this morning, line by line, and the lengths agree and the counts agree and the condition is his word and not mine, and the dash is still a dash.**"

"**That is the first time in thirty weeks that a document in this book and a fact in this room have been the same thing. And I am entering that, and I am entering the date on it, which is that it happened, and not a day, and I want the word *happened* in the book, and I am going to argue about it if anybody objects.**"

Then the part of the morning that was hard happened, and it happened in the anteroom with nine people in it and it was not about beech.

Renn Vale had the slate on her knee and she did not have it on her knee for the beech, and she said what she was going to say before she said it, which is the practice of every person in this building who keeps anything.

"**I am the person who keeps that school door. I have never once said that out loud in a room in those words, and I am saying it now because I am about to take two wedges out of it and I want a room to know which of my jobs I am doing it in.**"

"**Seven sound wedges and one dead stick, and I know where the seven are, and two of them are at that door's head and have been since the eleventh week, and that door has been held by those two and by the frame and by a word said to a page, and I am taking the two out of it this morning and putting them into a brace across a doorframe at the other end of this building.**"

"**Say the reason.**"

"**The reason is that a wedge in a door is a thing a person can be stopped with, and a brace across a frame is a thing that stops the frame moving, and if the frame does not move then the door does not need the wedge, and that is the whole of the arithmetic and I have had it since Tuesday and I have been sitting on it since Tuesday.**"

"**Say the half of it that is against you.**"

And she said it, and she said it in the flat voice she uses for a term, and the clerk's chalk was moving before the sentence finished.

"**A door with no wedge in it is a door that has to be watched.**"

"**That is the half against it and I am not going to soften it and I am not going to give you the rest of it in a better shape. The answer to a door that has to be watched is a person who watches it, and the person is me, and I am one person, and I keep a shore and a road and the lead terms and a corridor and a landing and this Hearthhold's second way out of its busiest room.**"

"**And the second half of the half against it, which is worse. The answer to a door that has to be watched is a person, and there is not a person available, and the place in this building where a person would go to watch a door is the low end of that corridor, and there is a page in Dalla's book that says somebody stands there, and it is blank, and the third of the three conditions on it says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true.**"

"**So I am not going to use that page. I am not going to put a person near it, I am not going to ask the woman who wrote the two sentences on it whether her conditions are met, and I am not going to let a wedge come out of a school door and a man come down a passage and be sent to hold a thing in a hole in a corridor.**"

"**I have said *I have better answers and I have looked and I have not found one* in this room before and I am not going to say it again this week because a wedge is cheaper than that sentence and I have got two.**"

Nine steps away a door that had been held by two wedges and a frame and a word said to a page was being held by one of those three things less, and the frame did not move, and nothing happened, and nobody in that anteroom said anything about it, and that was the whole of what the morning had cost.

Then Iven Tallo said what joinwood is, because nobody had said it in a room in thirty weeks and it is his trade and he asked for it.

"**Joinwood is not timber. Timber is what you build with. Joinwood is what you cut to make two pieces of a building agree with each other where they meet, and it is short, and it is square, and it is dry, and it is the piece that takes the load and hands it over.**"

"**And a wedge is not joinwood. A wedge is a thing that stops a thing moving by being driven into a gap and being left there, and if you take it out the gap is the gap again. I have been holding two of them in a school door for a month and neither of them has ever been a piece of anything.**"

"**What a brace needs at the joint is a short square piece of dry beech with the grain running across the joint, and a peg through it, and something on the far side of the peg to take the pin. I have got no pegs. I have got two wedges and no pegs, and there is no bronze in this Hearthhold and no iron I have seen in a month, and a wooden pin driven into green beech will split it in a year and I would not put a pin into this brace that I would have to argue about in a year.**"

"**So say the thing, Marek, because you have had it since Tuesday and I have been waiting for you to say it.**"

"**Two beech lengths out of the school store,**" said Marek Venn. "**They are the school's own and they have been under that shelf since the twenty-first week and nobody has asked for them. They are dry, they are short, and one of them is four feet and one of them is six, and cut into four they are eight pieces and a brace across a frame with a stone bed wants six.**"

"**Say the rest.**"

"**The rest is that the school corner's mouth has not been used since the twenty-second week, and that mouth is what that store's shelf is against, and the two lengths have been lying in a school store for four weeks with a fire not lit in the room in front of them.**"

"**I have been in this building thirty weeks, and I did not know they were the joinwood for a brace until the third day of this week at about the second bell.**"

"**And the reason I did not know is that I have spent a month thinking about a piece of paper on a cart. That is the whole reason and I am going to say it in a room rather than have it found in a month.**"

Then the beech was cut, and it was cut in the anteroom on a trestle that has not been used in a month, and four men from a breaking camp and a woman who keeps a road held the pieces while a carpenter with a hand that has not closed since the twenty-second week cut them, and nobody stood in the anteroom and watched a man do a thing he was good at, and the clerk noticed and entered it.

Nothing in that anteroom had been that quiet in a month, and the noise in it was a chisel in green beech and four people breathing at the rate of a man who is working, and a girl of nine sat on the fourth step with her foot up on a crate and watched all of it without being asked to and without anyone in the room noticing that she was.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I want the whole of it in the book because it is the first time in a month that something in this anteroom went the way it was supposed to and not the way it was frightened, and it went that way because a man was handed a piece of wood and a chisel and four people to hold it, and not one person in this room had to be talked into anything.**"

"**And I entered that the man who cut it has a hand that has not closed since the twenty-second week, and cut eleven pieces with it, and put the offcuts in a heap instead of a basket.**"

"**Because a basket is a thing a person is asked to keep, and I am not going to put that beside anything. That is a fact about a basket and it is the whole of my morning.**"

And Marek Venn was on the anteroom step for the rest of it, and he had the six cut pieces laid out in a row on the bench beside him where anybody could see them, and nobody came near them, and what he said was said to the floor.

"**Thirty-one weeks I have been in this building. And the first material anybody in here has ever been given by somebody who was able to keep it is on this bench in six pieces, and it is beech out of a school's own store.**"

"**It came down four days of road on four men's backs, and it did not come with a seal on it and it did not come with a form and it did not come with a name, and it is not going to be entered in this book's column with a person's name against it.**"

And then he said the thing that the whole of the fourth bell had been walking toward, and he said it to the floor and not to the room.

"**I do not know what to do with a gift that has a person in it. I have had things given to me in my life that had nothing in them and I could spend them. I have had things given to me that had a price on them and I could weigh the price. And I have got a heap of beech in this anteroom that a man of forty-nine will be able to take back on any morning I ask him for it, and I have not got anywhere to put that.**"

"**Say the thing you are actually saying,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The thing I am actually saying is that I have spent a month refusing to fill in two lines and I was right, and that being right has cost this building a wall in lime for a fortnight, and that the wall in lime is not going to arrive by me being right. It is going to arrive because a woman on a landing gave away two wedges and a school's own timber and a man four days up a road said *bring your keeper*, and none of those three is me.**"

"**Enter that in his words and do not put a reason beside it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that I have had that sentence available to me for four days and did not write it, and that the reason I did not write it is that writing it makes me the fourth name in a list of five, and I have never in my life wanted to be the fourth name in a list of five.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and the board was walked, and the count read both columns and named no day, and a seventeenth thing went on the board and the count gave the reason himself in his own words.**

"**Two today and a hundred and eighty-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and five and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for thirteen weeks. The crew are at sixty-six and a half and have not moved since Wednesday of last week.**"

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two and the seam gave two today and four threads at the first bell, and Jo says four and not eight and has not measured it since the nineteenth week, and I am entering that the seam is going and that nobody in this building has a number for how fast, and I am not going to write one.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is ninety-three days and ninety-three nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be ninety-four, and I have said in six weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and a boy of fourteen read fourteen lines of a stranger's list out loud in this room today and said the ninth one was a dash and did not argue with the man about it.**"

"**And the board is walked, and I read sixteen entries, and a seventeenth went on it in the last minute of the hour and the count put it on himself and I am going to read it the way he gave it and not the way I would have given it.**"

Chalk in the left hand and the right one open and flat in his lap, and he did not improve on the ninth reason, and he did not give a tenth.

"**Sixteen, and then seventeen. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, a plan, a condition, and a store. Nine in the ordinary column and eight in the one with nothing over the top of it.**"

"**The store is not a null and I want that said because it is the first time in thirty weeks that one of these has been a thing that was not in this building. A null is a reading. The claim is a reading that failed. The mark was a mark. The row was a page. The plan was a page. The condition was a sentence.**"

"**A store is a man keeping a room four days up a road, and I have never entered one and I am not going to pretend I know what he does all day.**"

"**It goes in the column with nothing over the top of it, and the reason is a courier's, and I am entering it as hers because I would have given a worse one and I heard hers first.**"

"**A board that only holds things in one building cannot be read by a person in another one. That is her rule and it has been in this clerk's book since the twenty-sixth week in her own words, and it was said about a road, and it is true about a board, and I have been walking a board for thirty weeks and I have never once thought about who is not in the room.**"

"**And the ninth reason stands and I am not giving a tenth. A heading is a place to keep the person who works under it, and a board with a heading is a board a person can be asked about, and I have just put a man four days up a road on this one, and the last thing I want is for a heading to be a reason somebody can send for him.**"

"**Enter all three of those,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that the column is unheaded for the ninth consecutive batch, and enter that a man has now said out loud that a heading on this board would be a reason somebody could send for, and enter that I am not going to let the second one become the tenth reason and I am going to say so in the book so that a person in a year can see I knew.**"

---

**Six cut pieces of a school's own beech are on a bench in an anteroom, and a school door at the other end of this building has no wedge in it, and a woman who keeps that door has said out loud that the answer to a door that has to be watched is a person and that there is not one available and that the page where a person would go is blank with three conditions on it.**

**On the fourth day of this week the wall that is going to take the push is going to be measured, and a physician has said in a room that he is going to name the person in it, the count is at ninety-three, and the board has seventeen entries on it with eight of them under a column with nothing over the top of it.**
