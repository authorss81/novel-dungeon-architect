# Chapter 98 — What She Is Asked

On the day the plan was shown to a stranger the air engineer was not asked for anything, and that was the point, and it was arranged, and it was not an accident and it was not a kindness.

Nera Oduya had gone to the clinic return channel at the first bell with a lamp and her own board and had stayed there four hours, and the anteroom had filled to its number with the school at the far end of the corridor and the outer service door packed behind stone and clay, and the pump had run its window with two people on the frame, and the wheel had gone round under the floor eleven times, and nobody had said the word *air* at all.

At the second bell the anteroom went to eleven. Renn Vale said the number out loud from the landing, and eleven became ten, and the two people who were not staying went down nine steps to the lower level without being asked, and the corridor was open and nine children passed it at the first bell in twos at a walk with their teacher behind them, and the anteroom's rating said ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it was ten.

Bel Ordry read the six things at the fifth bell, and one of them was the air valve, asked and not answered, and the spring, and he read that one exactly as he had been reading it for a fortnight, and nobody in the room looked at the ladder off the corridor.

---

"**I would like that said out loud,**" said Nera Oduya, at the fourth bell, from the clinic return, "**before the stranger comes, because it is the whole of what happened today and nobody is going to put it in a book as a thing that went well.**

"This room ran for four hours today with the door packed, the anteroom at its number, the school on the corridor, and the pump on its window, **and it did not need me once. Not for the count, not for the frame, not for the shaft, not for the six minutes, not for the school corner's mouth. It did everything and it did it right, and I was two rooms away with a lamp.**

"And I am not going to be thanked for that, because I arranged it, and I would like the arrangement entered: **I arranged for this room to find out that it can do without me, in a cold month, with a full anteroom and a packed door, so that when I go in the spring there is something here that is not waiting for me.**

"That is the good half. Here is the other half and I am saying it in the same breath because I have claimed four costs in this building and I am not going to claim a fifth and hide it.

"**The cost is that this room now knows it does not need to ask. And the day a room finds out it can do without the person, it stops asking, and it is not a fault and it is not ingratitude — it is arithmetic. And by the first of the month there will be a question about the air in this building that nobody has put to anybody, and it will be a real one, and I will be four hundred miles from it with a bundle.

"So the arrangement has a price and I have paid it and I am telling you the price and I am not asking you to be frightened of it, because being frightened of it is how a boundary keeps an air engineer for four extra months by not telling her the truth. I am the woman who read the geometry of a nine-hundred-year-old table and wanted it to mean something. I know what it is to be kept because you need me. I am not going to be that."

---

Then she was asked, and the asking was done properly, by the keeper, at the fifth bell, in a room, with the terms said out loud first, and it is the first thing in nineteen weeks that anybody in this building has asked the air engineer for a piece of work rather than a decision.

"**I am not going to ask you what the air will do,**" said Bel Ordry, "because if I ask you that you will tell me what the air will do and I will put it on the page, and a page with the air on it that a stranger can read is a page about a room.

"**I am going to ask you to write one thing, and it is not the air. It is this: you are leaving in the spring and there is a plan with two empty lines in it and *neither of the two is the anteroom*, and that is the trouble, because the anteroom is the row everybody looks at and it is a door and a word and a rating in somebody else's hand. I am asking you to write down, in your own hand, and for nobody, what you think is most likely to go wrong in that room before you go.

"**Not what is wrong with it now. What goes wrong. And I want it in the third column, because the third column is the one that says what is done, and a thing that is going to happen is work somebody will have to do, and that is the only reason it belongs on that page.**

"And I want it read out, and I want somebody who is not you to object to a line of it."

---

She wrote it in nine minutes and read it out, and it is in the plan in her own hand, and it is the only line on the page that anybody in this building has written about a future and not about a day.

"**The anteroom holds ten including one litter for no more than two hours, on a door that is packed with rubble and not repaired, with a rule that the word *door* has to be said out loud in a room to bring it back into the rating. That is the whole of the danger and it is not an air danger. Air in that room is not the problem and never has been.**

"**The problem is that the rating depends on a word. A rating that depends on a word is a rating that a tired person at the fourth bell in a bad month will say the word to, because the word is a way of making the room legal, and the day somebody says the word to a door that is packed and holding on rubble, the room is legal and the door is not a door.**

"**So: either the door is made good, or the word stops working.** Those are the two. I do not know how to make a door good in a Hearthhold with no mortar of the right kind in it, and the carpenter will not have my opinion about that, and the cook knows more about it than I do."

"That is the whole of it," said Bel Ordry. "**And I am going to say the other half in a room, because a page with only the bad half on it is a page that frightens people.

"It is not *either*. The third way is the one this boundary has been running for nineteen weeks, and it is the ugliest one and it is the only one that has not cost anybody anything. **The word is said in a room, by the person who is going to be in the room, and the clerk repeats it, and the door is on the fourth line of the plan with a keeper's hand and a carpenter's hand on it, and nobody says it to a door. It gets said to a page.**

"And the carpenter's objection is entered, and the air engineer's objection is entered, and there is now a rule in this building about a word in a packed doorframe and it is the ugliest rule anybody here has ever made and I made it and I would like it read out at the fifth bell every day with the six others.**"

Dalla Sen read it back, and the clerk read back the corrections as well, which she now does without being asked and which nobody has ever once thanked her for.

---

Then the board, and the air record, and the thirteenth line.

She put it on the table in front of Bel Ordry and did not let go of it for a moment before she did, and everybody in the room noticed both halves of that.

"**I am not giving it to a man,**" she said. "**I am putting it on the page, and the page is in a room, and a stranger is going to be asked to come and stand in it. And the reason I will not put it in a coat is the reason I have had for nineteen weeks and it is not modesty and it is not the fourth column: a board in a hand is an office, and an office in this Hearthhold is a person, and the day a person holds a board this building has got a keeper of the air, and there is exactly one road up this passage and I am not going to be a person standing in it with a ledger.**

"And I keep the authority and I keep the watch and I still refuse to certify the stair's air, and none of that moves, and nobody should write in a book this week that the air has been given away."

Then the thirteenth line, and she said it out loud, and she had been carrying it for six weeks and it was the closest thing to an unkind thing anybody said in this batch.

"**The thirteenth line stays unwritten, and I am writing the reason on the page, in my hand, in the third column, because it is a work and it is the last one I have.

"*The column it wants is the column for an owner. A person is not an owner. I have been the air engineer of this Hearthhold for nineteen weeks and I have never owned a draught of air in it, and if anybody in this building wants to know what the air is they can come to the valve at the second bell and hold the release themselves, and there is a man on the threshold who will not stop them.*"

Bel Ordry looked at the page for a while, and then said:

"**That is the eighth line in the third column and it is the only one on the page that is a refusal to write something, and I want the room to notice that I have just been handed a refusal by a person who is leaving and that I have put it on a page that goes up a road.

"And I am keeping it, and I want the price entered, because I have held one page for five days and I have discovered what it is. **Holding a page means every refusal anybody hands me becomes a line in a thing I carry, and I am going to be the man who is asked in four years why the air engineer's refusal is in a boundary's maintenance plan, and the answer is going to be that she wrote it in her own hand and I would not leave it out, and that is going to be a good answer for about four seconds and then it is going to be the reason somebody takes this page off a man in a coat.**"

"Entered in the keeper's own words and read out," said Dalla Sen, "**and I have to add the clerk's half and it is short. That is the fourth time a keeper in this building has said out loud that he is doing the thing he is frightened of. The first time he said it, the room did not stop him, and I entered that the fright would look like the virtue. It is fifteen days later and the fear looks nothing like the virtue, and the keeper is frightened of something else now and has not said what, and I would like the room to have heard that I noticed the difference.**"

---

Marek Venn had watched the whole of it from the bench and had said nothing for most of it, which is what he does, and which two people in this building have now made a rule about, and at the sixth bell he said the one thing he had been holding since the second bell and which he had written in a book of his own and shown to nobody, which is now the third time he has done that in this building.

"I have been in this building nineteen weeks," he said, "**and I have been waiting for a piece of work and it has just been given to the one person in this room who is leaving.**

"And the thing about it is that it is a piece of *work* and not a decision, and it went into the third column, and the third column is the column that says what is done, and **I wrote the reason that column exists.** I said *a column you fill in before you know is a promise you have not made yet*, and I said it about a kitchen table, and I was right, and I did not know I was right about this.

"And she is going to go in the spring, and in the spring this page will have a line in it that says *the anteroom's word is said to a page and not to a door*, **in her hand, written by the person who is leaving, kept by a man who is staying, and nobody here will be able to say whether that is a rule or a favour, and that is the best thing this Hearthhold has produced since the pump and it is the eighth line.**

"And I would like the clerk to enter that the man who said a column is a promise and the man who said a page is a road and the man who said a name on a head of a column is a holder were all standing in this room, and that I have no idea which of the three of us was right, and that I have spent nineteen weeks in a building teaching a cook to slow down and I do not think I have taught anybody anything at all."
