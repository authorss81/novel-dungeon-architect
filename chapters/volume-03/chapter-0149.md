# Chapter 149 — A Regime Is Not A Threat

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the twenty-ninth week, a Thursday, and the school did not run, and two people came up the middle passage with a document that one of them wrote and the other one did not, and the argument for it was good, and nobody in that anteroom was defeated and nobody conceded in a word, and an air engineer struck a room out of a printed schedule in nine seconds before anybody had asked her and gave no reason at all, and a man said out loud that a regime is not a threat and had said it in this building a fortnight ago before anybody else did.**

Pettin Rahl came up the middle passage with a document in a stiff cover and Hollis Ashe came up with nothing, and he said what he was before anybody asked him, and he said the second half of it before he sat down.

"**Pettin Rahl. Stonelight Compact, continuity. Fifty-two. Twenty-six years. And I am carrying this and I did not write it and I do not agree with all of it, and I would like that said before anybody in this room thanks me for bringing it up a passage in a bad month."

"**I told a room on the fifth day of the twenty-sixth week, before anybody else in that room had said it, that my office would want a regime for a boundary that keeps a permitted occupancy nobody has counted, and that a regime is not a threat. And I said I wanted that said in this building by me before anybody else said it here. And nobody has said it, and I am going to have to say it again this morning in a room, and I am not going to pretend that is the same as the first time."

"**This is six pages. It is the whole of it. And the fifth of the six is a clause about rooms whose condition is not being checked, and I am carrying it and I am not going to argue for that clause, and I am also not going to pretend to you that it is not in there, because a person who brings a document up a passage and only tells you the parts she is comfortable with is the thing this whole business is about."

He put it on the anteroom table and did not take his hand off it, and Dalla Sen said that she was going to read all six pages out loud in this room and that the reason was that everything a stranger puts on that table gets read out in this anteroom within a day, and she would rather it got read out by the people who keep the rooms.

Then she read it out, and it was a regime, and it was not a seal and it was not a closure and it was not a hand on anybody's shoulder, and it was worse.

"**One: a boundary under a street of this federation shall hold a stated occupancy for each of its rooms, and shall publish that stated occupancy to the office of the federation on request."

**Two: a person sent by the office of the federation may be present while a condition on a room is being checked, and may ask, and may be told no.

**Three: a boundary shall answer in a room, to a person, any question the office asks about the holding of a room, and may decline to answer, and declining is not a refusal.

**Four: the office may hold a copy of anything published under this, and a copy published under this is not a copy of the document that produced it.

**Five: a room within a boundary whose condition is not being checked at the time the room is in use may be entered by a person sent by the office of the federation, and the boundary may not refuse, and the refusal of the person who keeps the room is not a ground.

**Six is the reason the document exists, and I am going to read it because it is the sentence I would take up the passage in a cell and put in front of a man who has never met any of you: where a boundary cannot show that a room's condition is being kept, the office shall not treat the room as kept.

Renn Vale said the sentence that nobody in that anteroom had said out loud, and she said it from the landing and she was not coming down.

"**Five. A room whose condition is not being checked at the time the room is in use may be entered, and the person who keeps the room cannot refuse, and their refusal is not a ground."

"**That is not an inspection. I want to say that in this room and I want the word used correctly. A woman from a surface federation has written six pages in which the word *entered* is used once, in the fifth clause, and the fifth clause is the only clause in the document that is not about a room, and it is about a person in a room being unable to say no."

"**And I have spent twenty-eight weeks in a building teaching people that their refusal is a ground, and I have said out loud in this room that the whole worth of the practice of a person writing the change to their own room is that she is not the one a doctor looks at, and I would like the woman who wrote clause five to be told in her own office what she has written and by whom."

"**And I am not going to say that the federation is wicked, because it is not, and I have been in two rooms with people from it this month and one of them put a note about my own sentence in her own book in my own words."

Pettin Rahl said the half that was against him and then said it in the other direction, and it took him about a minute, and nobody in that anteroom interrupted him.

"**Here is my case and it is one sentence and I said it in this room a fortnight ago and I am going to say it again in a longer form because you have all had a fortnight to think about it and I would like to know whether any of you have found the hole in it."

"**A number in a column that nobody has counted is not a safety measure. It is a number that has to be believed. And the day it is believed instead of counted is the day a room fills, and a room that fills past what it may hold does not fail loudly. It fails with a child."

"**And I have been on the other side of that. I have stood in a room on the Rim side of this federation whose page said six, and there were thirty-one people in it, and there was a stamped and dated and correctly certified clearance on that page from a competent man, and the clearance was right and the survey was right, and I was the reason a room with thirty-one people in it kept a number on it. There were four dead. I was not the cause and I have never said that I was, and I have not been able to walk into a room like that for twenty-six years without looking at the first column first."

"**And I have read your first column. I have been in this building four hours and I have looked at it, and it is a number, and it is a permission and not a count, and I believe you. I have believed you for a fortnight and I believed you this morning when I read it out loud to a room that could have lied to me about it and did not."

"**And I am going to tell you exactly what that belief is worth. It is worth nothing. Not a small thing. Nothing. Because I am going to carry six pages up that passage and put them in front of a man in a cell who has never been to this building and who is going to look at the first column and see a building with a number in it, and your schoolteacher's mouth, and a document she will not give me, and that is going to be enough for him. Not because he is a monster. Because he is competent and tired and it is a Tuesday."

"**And that is the whole of my case, and I have never in my life had a better one, and I have never had one that I liked less."

Ilsa Norr came down one step and gave him the thing nobody else in that room was going to be able to give him, which was a count, and she gave it in a form that was not the number.

"**I have a count. It is in a clerk's book, and it was taken by me out of my own room on a morning at the strokes, and it is read out at both ends of a corridor, and a clerk wrote it down and I said yes to the reading. And I told you on the fifth day of the twenty-sixth week that I was not going to tell you what it was and I am not going to tell you now, and you did not ask me, and I have noticed that you did not ask me and I am not going to be glad about it, because a clerk who is glad about not being asked is a clerk who has stopped expecting to be."

"**And what I can give you is the shape of it, and the shape is the whole of my trade. A count of that kind is only true of a moment, and the moment is the only thing about a room that is worth a number, and a number that can be carried up a passage and put in a cell is a number that will be true of a moment that is the wrong one. So I am not refusing you a count. I am telling you that the thing you want is a moment and the thing you can have is a morning."

"**And you have been in a room that killed four people over a number, and I am telling you that a building that writes a count down in a book and says out loud that it is only true of a moment is doing the one thing that room did not do. And that the reason it is not enough is that a moment does not keep anybody safe on a Tuesday in a cell four days up a road, and I know that, and I would say it to you if you asked me, and I am saying it to you now because you have been honest with me for a fortnight and I have not been honest with you at all."

Pettin Rahl did not say a kind thing about it, and Dalla Sen entered that he had not, and entered that she was glad about it for the fourth time this month and that this was the first time she had entered that.

Then Nera Oduya struck a room out of a printed schedule in nine seconds, from the second step, without coming down, and gave no reason at all, and the schedule was on the anteroom table face up and there was no heading over the column she wrote in and nothing in the space where a reason would go, and nobody asked her.

"**Say what you struck," said Dalla Sen.

"**A room," said Nera Oduya. "**And I have said that a room is what I struck and I am not going to say which room, and you did not ask me, and I am going back up the steps."

From the man who keeps the page, standing, with his hand on the date he does not say, came the boundary's answer, and it was three sentences long.

"**We will publish the statement. A person asks in a room and we read it out and no copy goes anywhere and the document does not leave this building, and that is what clause one, two and three are asking for and we are giving you all three of them and we gave them to a stranger in this anteroom yesterday with a woman from your office sitting in the room."

"**And I am going to say the other three clauses out loud so that nobody in this room is surprised in a month. Clause four, we will not give a copy of anything, and your document does not entitle you to one, and that is not a refusal, it is the third sentence of your own procedure."

"**Clause five, a room whose condition is not being checked may be entered, and the person who keeps the room cannot refuse, and there are four people in this building who have spent a month being told that their refusal is a ground, and I would like that clause in a file with a date on it, and I am not going to give you a date, and I would like the file to have one."

"**And clause six. Where a boundary cannot show that a room's condition is being kept, the office shall not treat the room as kept. That one is correct. I have said in this room on the fifth day of the twenty-sixth week that I would rather a federation want to look at this boundary than be able to take it, and clause six is the reason I was right, and I have been right about one thing in twenty-eight weeks and I would like the room to mark the date."

Pettin Rahl said what he was going to do with it, and he said it standing, and nobody in that anteroom filled in after him.

"**I am going to carry all six pages up that passage. Not the four that agree with me. All six, including the fifth, and I am going to say in the room I take them to that a woman wrote a page in this building that could be shown and not taken and that the boundary used it, and that the boundary is the only one of four I have visited this year that used it, and that clause five is the price of that and the price is the price and I would not give it up either."

"**And I said on the fifth day of the twenty-sixth week that a regime is not a threat, and I am going to say it again this morning because a person from a compact office said it to me in a corridor in the fourteenth week and I did not listen to her properly then and I have thought about it since. A regime is not a threat. It is a schedule. It is somebody writing down what will be looked at and when, and it is the only thing that has ever worked on me, and I would like the boundary to understand that a thing which is scheduled is not a thing that is refused."

"**And I am not defeated and this boundary is not safe and neither of those is on the front of this document, and I am going up a passage with six pages and I am not going to say a word on the way."

Hollis Ashe said the one thing she came up the passage for, and she said it in her own office's direction, and nobody in that anteroom had asked her for it.

"**I am going to say a thing that is against me and that nobody at the end of my passage is going to thank me for. The instrument that made this boundary arguable is mine. The first of my three things is the only reason a page in a coat got read out loud in this anteroom yesterday with a stranger sitting in it, and I wrote it, and I gave it to them for nothing, and I have been told by two competent people in four weeks that a procedure a body writes for a stranger is a document the stranger can use, and both of those people were right, and I knew the second one when I wrote it."

"**And I am still glad I wrote it. And I am not going to stand in this room and pretend that I did not know the price, because the price is what a room like this one is worth and the alternative is a building that cannot be looked at, and I would rather be used than hidden, and I would rather be used by a boundary that has decided the price itself than by an office that has not asked anybody."

"**And I would like a row on that page for my four pages, and I know what a row is now, and I am not going to ask for it, and a woman on a landing can tell you why and she would rather not."

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and said neither of them out loud, and the count was read out, and the board was not walked, and the reason she gave for all three of those was that a man was going to carry a number out of this building in a book and she would not be the one to say it out loud to him.**

"**Three today and a hundred and sixty-three since the keeper signed, and the boundary is at two hundred and eighty and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at sixty-three and a half."

"**And I entered those and I did not read them and the reason is not dignity. A man is going to carry six pages up that passage and he is going to write this boundary down in a book, and there is not one figure in this building that he cannot get from me in a room if he asks in a room, and I am not going to read the day's arithmetic out loud in front of him on the last morning he is in this anteroom. He can have it on Friday if he comes back and asks. That is clause three working and it is working in the direction it was written and I did not think about that when I agreed to it."

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is on the fill at the waist and it has not been off it and it is not moving and there is no number for that fill in this building, and the water came up the channel at the low end on Monday and it went down the channel, and there is a man in this anteroom who is not going to be asked to go and make that happen again."

"**The count. Wen Pask is eighty-four days and eighty-four nights into a count that is his own, and I am writing it, and a man from a surface federation read it in the book this afternoon because I read it to him, and I entered that he read it and entered that he did not say a kind thing about it, and entered that I have said out loud in four weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and that nobody in this building has thanked me and a man from another office has now also not."

"**And I did not walk the board. A man wrote three lines in a book in this room this afternoon and I entered them in mine, and a board is walked by a person who has nothing else on them, and I have had a regime and a strike-out and a refusal and I am not going to improve on that by saying it was busy."

**Six pages went up the middle passage with a man who is competent and tired and is going to be right about the first column on a Tuesday in a cell, and a clause in them says that a room whose condition is not being checked may be entered and that the person who keeps it cannot refuse, and there is no date on any of the six and none is going to be put there by this building.**

**In nine seconds, from a printed schedule, with nobody asked: a room was struck out, and the sixth clause of a regime is a sentence a man on a landing said was the reason he had been right about one thing in twenty-eight weeks, and a federation's route inspector sat in an anteroom and said out loud that the instrument that made this boundary arguable was hers and that she knew the price of it when she wrote it.

**Sixteen rows on a page, and the low end of a corridor has none of them, and a clerk's book has the refusal of it in three hands and in the order it was given, and the order is the finding, and a man from another office asked what the low end of the corridor was on Thursday afternoon and was told, in a room, by a clerk reading a refusal out of her own book, and he wrote it down and did not say a kind thing about it either. Marek Venn sat on the anteroom step through all of it with his left hand open on his knee and said nothing, because on the second day of this week he said out loud in a room that he cannot go down there again and that he does not know why, and because a man who has said that in a room is not to be asked to say it twice.
