### 230 — What This Room Has

- **POV:** close third person on Marek Venn, interior life present.
- **Location/time:** the anteroom, the room at the kitchen-line end, and the far door. Forty-third week, sixth day, a Saturday. Corridor open; the school ran for the fifty-fifth morning. The batch's second market Saturday, a mouth-fact about a counter only.
- **Immediate goal:** count what the room has, instead of what it owes, for the first time in five volumes.
- **Resistance:** **a list of what a room has is a column, and this boundary has refused six headings, and the day a room counts its own holdings it can be asked what it is short of — and it is short, and four households at a second gate are how it is short.** A count is not a victory and the batch must not let it read as one.
- **Information revealed:** the count, entered as a finding and not as a plan, and the two things in the room that are not in it. **And the audience, at its exact and most terrible: the school ran this morning, nine children went out at the far door at the fourth bell, and a child of nine says in a room where the counting is happening that nine children from this room go to a school where there are children who have not had water, and that they have been told.**
- **Action or decision:** **a clerk decides to count what her room has**, and the cost of the deciding is that a room which has counted can now be asked a question by anybody, and the first question is already on its way up nine steps.
- **Emotional change:** the man at the end of the trestle is not the man who counts, and the one item he would add to the count is the one item that is not a have, and he does not add it, and he knows that not adding it is the correct thing to do and it is going to cost him something in four months.
- **Power or resource change:** **the pot is three days, the seam gave nothing on all ten mornings, the fire is thirty-six weeks unlit, the crew is at eighty, the board is at eighteen, and the four households are the four households.**
- **Ending type:** reckoning, and a new question in the room rather than a settlement.
- **Next-chapter pull:** the count is the first instrument in this building that is not a rule, a term, a refusal or a debt, and nobody in the room has decided what to do with it, and the next thing this room is going to be asked is what it is short of.
