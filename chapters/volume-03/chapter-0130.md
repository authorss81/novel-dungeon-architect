# Chapter 130 — A Reason Is Legible

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the twenty-fifth week, a Friday, and the school did not run, and a room read two sheets of cheap paper aloud, line by line, and the reason on them is legible, and a man of twenty-two is dead, and neither of those things took the other one away.**

At the second bell the sheets were read in the anteroom, in a room at its number, with the pump running, by Dalla Sen, standing, twice each, and after each line somebody had to object to it or agree with it before the next one was read, and four people objected and one person agreed and one person said a thing that was both.

"**It is not a letter,**" said Bel Ordry, standing at the end of the table with his hands behind his back. "**There is no office on it and no permit and no tally and no clerk's mark and no date. It is written on both sides in one hand by somebody who is not careful and is not frightened, which I did not expect and am not going to pretend to like. It was folded twice, pressed with a thumb, and weighted with a dead stick out of this building's own wall. The weight is the polite part of it. The paper is the part I would like explained.**"

"**So: read it out, in the room, and I will stop anybody who starts arguing about it instead of hearing it.**"

Dalla Sen read it out.

**The first sheet had five lines and a heading, and it is a copy, and the copy is wrong, and somebody in the room noticed the copy was wrong before the reading was finished.**

"**One. A printed form on a board at a gate on a road between here and a middle gate carries a line, and the line is for *the person responsible for the load*.**

"**Two. A form carried up this passage by a road hand carries a line, and the line is for *the person who keeps it*, and it was filled in by a clerk in an office with a small competent hand.**

"**Three. Both lines have a space in them. In one, four weeks ago, the space is empty. In the other, a fortnight ago, the space has a title in it: *the person who keeps the anteroom's terms*.**"

"**Object,**" said Bel Ordry. "**Not to the reading. To the third line. There was no date on that form. The form has a line for a date and the clerk filled it in and told this room it was the only lie on it, and the person who wrote that line did not know the date was a lie and could not have used it.**

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a man who was told out loud in this room that a line was a lie made that line the index of somebody's reasoning, and that this boundary cannot appeal it and is not going to try.**"

"**Four,**" said Dalla Sen, and read on.

"**Four. Two offices, inside a month, have printed a line with a space in it for a person in a boundary that has no name on any of its documents. A line with a space in it is a post being cut. A post is cut once, and it is cut by an office, and the office does not have to be here.**"

The room did not fill in after it.

"**Five,**" said Bel Ordry. "**Say the fifth, Dalla, and say it exactly.**"

"**Five. We are not against the school and we are not against the charter and we are not against a woman who keeps air. We are against a line with a space in it. There have been four of them inside a month and there will be a fifth, and a body four days up the middle passage has already put a title where there was nothing, and a title is not a name. A name is refused. A title is sent for.**"

---

**A column of rooms ran down the second sheet, and one name on it was struck out in nine seconds by a woman three steps up who had not come down, and nobody asked her why, for the fourth time in this Hearthhold, and she did not give it.**

"**Say the whole column,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it at the speed it is written at, and do not let me put a pause in it.**"

She read down a list of rooms in this building — the anteroom, its outer service door, its frame, the school corner, the nine steps, the common landing, the stair, the pump, the kitchen, the wash rooms, the school store, the low end of the corridor, and two rooms with nothing at all in front of them, which are the ninth room and the blank line.

At *the wash rooms* the clerk said, "**Out,**" and stopped, and Nera Oduya had the clerk's chalk in her hand, and there was no sound in that room at all, and nine seconds later she gave the chalk back and went up three steps and said nothing.

"**Enter it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that it is the fourth time, and enter that she was not asked and gave no reason, and enter that nobody in this room asked her, and enter that I have stopped writing the next part of that sentence because there is no next part and I have written it three times.**

"**And enter that the two rooms with no name against them are on it. Whoever wrote that sheet has been in this building, or has been told about it by somebody who has, and they have put two rooms on a page with nothing in front of them, and I am not going to say whose rooms they are, and I am not going to be asked.**"

---

**And then it was argued in a room, and it was not close, and three people said out loud that they understood why, and the boundary did not recruit one of them and did not argue them out of it either, and the difference was entered.**

Renn Vale said it first, and she said it in the shape of a woman holding a term.

"**It is a good argument. I am going to say that first and I am not going to soften it, and I would like a clerk to write *good argument* in capitals if this hand had capitals. Two offices have printed a line with a space in it for a person in here, and one of them has put a title in the space, and a title is a box a letter goes into, and a person who has been given a box gets a letter whether she is ready for it or not. I have said that in a room twice in a month and been right twice, and I did not know either time that I was right about a thing that was going to catch up with me from four days up a road.**"

"**And I am not going to pretend the sheet is wrong because I do not like it. If I were a person in that office I would have written the same two lines, for the reason on the fifth line, and I would have gone on writing them after I had been told no, because I have been told no eleven times in five weeks and every one of the eleven was a good no.**"

Mikka Hale said the fourth thing, and she said it from her own board.

"**The fourth line is about this boundary and it is true, and I am not going to let a room that has just read a dead man's name treat the fourth line as the villain of it. An office has certified this sector sound, and a sound sector does not get a crew put in it again, and the man who certified it said so out loud in this room and said he would have rather it was not the finding. They read that sheet. Of course they did. It is the second document anybody from outside has ever given us and the only one that says a thing about this ground that is about the future.

"**And I will say the part that makes me the worst person in this room. I put a ten in a column yesterday and did not count anybody, and a boundary that writes down a number about a room instead of counting the people in it is one document away from a form with a space in it, and I do not know how many documents away, and that is why they came.**"

Iven Tallo said the thing nobody else had said, and he said it from the bench, and he did not stand up.

"**They levered a grate off a stone frame with a dead stick and put the stick on a piece of paper. That is a person who has been in this building, in the dark, alone, at the one place everybody here has said out loud that nobody ever goes.

"**And the low end of this corridor has a line on a page in a clerk's hand that says somebody stands there, and has said so for twenty-five weeks with no name on it, and three conditions under it, and the third is that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true. That line is the only thing on that sheet a person wrote themselves.

"**I am not going to say they are right. I am going to say that the only sentence in this building they have not had to invent is a sentence about a hole, and I would like somebody to sit with that before we decide what we think of them.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that three people in this room have said out loud that they understand why those two sheets were written, and that one of them has said that one sentence on them was written by somebody in this building, and that the room has not asked which sentence and I have not been asked and I am not going to say.

"**And entered the difference, because it is the only thing on this page worth anything to me: understanding a reason is not agreeing with it, and I have been writing things next to each other on purpose for twenty-five weeks and this is the first time I have wanted to write one next to something that is not a fact.

"**And I did not do it. I have not put the two sheets and the low end and the condition next to each other with a reason beside them and nothing between them, which is what I have done eleven times. I did not do it and I am not going to help a reader in a year do it, and I am not going to stop them either.**"

---

**Mero Senn said the sentence that stopped the room, and he was not going to be argued off it, and he was the only person in the anteroom who had not lived in it before a fortnight, and he had said on Tuesday that he was not here to be forgiven.**

"**It is a good argument,**" he said. "**I have read a hundred good arguments about why somebody should not build a thing and this is one of the better ones, and I want that said out loud in a room where a dead man's name is on a piece of paper, because the next thing that is going to happen is that somebody is going to use it.

"**So here is the sentence, and I am not going to be improved out of it.

"**A reason can be legible and a person can still be dead. I have spent twenty-two years watching good arguments do arithmetic on people, and the arithmetic always comes out, and the number that comes out is never a body, it is a cost, and a cost is a thing a room with nine children in it can carry and gets lighter when one of them is not there.

"**So I am not going to tell this boundary that those two sheets are wrong. They are not wrong. I am going to tell this boundary that the fourth column of its own plan has been empty for twenty-five weeks with a reason on it in two hands, and that a stranger has just said the good half about one of this building's own documents without knowing what it was, and that the good half is the only reason I can stand in this anteroom without a warrant.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in a physician's words, and read back twice, and entered that he has said he is not going to be argued off it, and entered that the fourth column of this boundary's plan is empty and that he has said the good half about it in a room and had not read it, and entered that I did not tell him that.**

---

**And the low end of the corridor was a hole all day, and nobody stood in it, and the review page was blank, and the school did not run.**

A dressed stone slab about the size of two hands put together, the grate, and there is no second one in this Hearthhold and no timber and no mason and no bond, and the timber for a wall in lime is in a camp store four days up the middle passage with a man in it who said he would do it again.

"**Two flags and a length of the crew's frayed nine-metre line,**" said Jo Verrick, at the third bell, "**and I have put them over it and stood on both of them with my own weight and it holds, and I am telling the room exactly what it is: two flags and a spent line, and I have been under this building for twenty-five weeks and that is the whole of what I have got for a hole over a shaft.

"**And the crew's line is the crew's. That is a sentence I have to say in a room. It has been round the ladder since the first week of the nineteenth week and it is spent and it is the crew's, and I am not taking it without somebody standing here saying it, and I have been waiting all morning and nobody has.**"

"**I am not saying it,**" said Iven Tallo, from the bench. "**You ask me. I have not got standing to give the crew a piece of rope.**

"**I am not asking you to stand there,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I am asking the person who keeps the line.**

"**That is me, and I have been at the kitchen line all day, and yes, and I would like it entered that I was asked in a room with the terms out loud and a person named who could be told no, and that I said yes before the terms were finished, and that Dalla read the rest of them out anyway and I did not object to any of them.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the crew's column is at fifty-six and a half and has not moved, and this is not an hour and not work, it is a piece of rope, and it went to a hole on a Friday and nobody counted it, and the man who gave it said he was asked, and that is all of it.**"

Then Ilsa Norr said the day's cost, in her own words, and it went in the book in a clerk's hand and was not shortened.

"**The school does not run today. That is the second open day this boundary has lost since we moved and I am not having it paid back, and I am not having anybody tell me it is the same as the one in the twenty-first week, because it is not. The one in the twenty-first week was a shelf. This one is a man.

"**And here is what I have been not saying since the second day of this week: I taught nine children in a room yesterday with a hole in the floor under a stone, and I knew, and I went in anyway, and I would go in again, and both of those are true, and the second is not a comfort and the first is not an excuse. I want both of them on the same line and I want this room to have to look at them.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the six things, and the day's number, and the count in the column with nothing over the top of it, and she read the last one twice, and the room did not fill in after it, and nobody asked her anything, and that was the whole of the week.**

"**Two today and a hundred and six since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and twenty-three and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks.

"**The pot is ten. It has not gone up since the fifth day of the nineteenth week. I am going to say it one more way and then never again: it did not move on the night a man died in the low end of this corridor, and there were eleven people behind a shut door in the same week, and neither of those things changed a number on a cook's board, and I do not know what that is worth and I am not going to be told.

"**And the count.**

"**Wen Pask is sixty-five days and sixty-five nights into a count that is his own.**

"**And I am going to say the rest of it, because I said on Monday that I would decide at the sixth bell and I have decided and I did not sleep and I am saying it in a room and not in a book.

"**And I am going to keep writing it. That is my decision and I am not going to be argued out of it and I am not going to be thanked for it, because a number that stops on the day a man is found is a record of when this boundary noticed him, and I have noticed eleven times in twenty-five weeks that a number on a page is a thing that was permitted rather than a thing that happened, and I am not going to be the clerk that wrote the last one.

"**So there will be a line tomorrow, in this hand, at this bell, and it will say a number, and nobody in this building is entitled to be angry with me about it except the man it is for, and he is not able to hear it, and I have thought about that for a day and a half and I am going to do it anyway.**"

**And in the anteroom, on the anteroom table, in front of a room that was full and that had said the word and had had it repeated, there were two sheets of cheap paper with a wash room struck out of one of them in an air engineer's hand. They did not go down the nine steps and along the corridor, and they did not go out of this building, and nothing on either of them had a date on it or a name on it, and the reason on the fifth line was true.

**A man of twenty-two was dead in a hole at the low end of a corridor that nobody has ever stood in, and the review page was still blank, and the woman who wrote the two sentences on it has never been asked and will never be sent for, and her three conditions stand in a clerk's hand without a name against them.**

**A packed sill was holding in a room nine children will be in on the third day of next week, and a hearth stone was propped on a strip of crate board, and a cook's board said ten, and a plan in a coat had a row in it that had not been there a week ago, and the number in that row was a permission and not a count of anybody, and a boy of fourteen had read it out loud in a room and said it was ten, and a woman in a room had said in the same week that eleven is not a number anybody may use for anything else ever.**

Marek Venn was at the foot of the nine steps with his left hand open on his knee, and the count was in the room again under the pump's check, low and uneven and counted, like a thumb run along the edge of a plate, and he had listened to it every week since the fourth day of the nineteenth week and treated it as a thing this building does to him. On the last day of a week in which he had been right five times about water, and once out loud in a corridor about a man of twenty-two, he understood that he had no standing whatever to say what a sound in a house is.

"**It is not the anteroom,**" he said, to nobody.

"**Say it in a room or do not say it,**" said Dalla Sen, from the top of the steps.

"**Then I will say it in a room,**" he said, "**and the whole of it, which is that a man who has spent five weeks being right about every wall in a building has stopped being a person who can be argued with about a floor, and that I said that sentence this week out loud to nine people and to a physician and to a schoolteacher, and I said it because a room needed it and not because I had arrived at it.

"**And I want it entered that five is a number I chose and not a number I counted, and that nobody is going to give me a condition for saying a number out loud, because the one condition I have written this month is on a slate in another woman's hand and it is about clay.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a man in this boundary has put a number on a page that is not a count of anything, and that it is the first such number anybody has entered here, and that it is his and not a room's, and that the room does not get to check it.**"

"**The room does not get to check it,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am not going to let anybody in this building build a system out of it tonight, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said so.**"

**And the anteroom above them was the room it had been for twenty-five weeks: ten including one litter for no more than two hours, a packed frame, a head a finger and a bit out of true and going a finger a week in this cold, and a second way out that was a word, said in a room, repeated by the clerk, and not said to a door.**

**And a person was still under a lip, and the lip was packed and not held, and the post under it had never been built.**
