# Chapter 108 — Nine Children In A Numbered Room

The third day of the twenty-first week was a day the arrangement had open, and this batch shut it, and the school ran two bells at the kitchen-line end and then did not run again, and nobody paid that day back.

The reason it had to be that day is that the pump's window is the only window the waist can be crossed in, and the pump's window is the only window the water-carrying happens in, and nine register boxes and a shelf's worth of school cannot cross a fill that has moved three times in front of nobody.

---

Renn Vale set it out at the first bell on the corridor floor, in the open, with the nine children and the schoolteacher standing there, because she has never in five months set out a term indoors where it could be agreed with.

"**The water goes up first, four and then two, at a walk, on the terms. The boxes go in the same window and under the same terms, because the alternative is a second crossing and there is not a second window in a day.** Four carriers at a time, at a walk. The bearer is at the near lip and the bearer is Jo Verrick. Two readers, one at each end. **The stop is the cloth, the cord, and any of the four people who keep a room.**

"**And I am going to say the thing I said on the third day and I am going to say it with the boxes in front of me, because the last time I said it nine children were walking and this time it is nine children carrying, and carrying is worse, and everybody in this corridor knows it.**

"**I am not going to put a hand on that fill and I am not going to let a box be set down on it and rested on, because a box resting on a fill is a load on a fill and nobody in this building knows what that fill will take.**"

"And I will not have it read as a timetable," said Dalla Sen. "**The corridor is open on the third day and this batch is shutting it, and both of those are going on the same line, and the clerk is going to put them next to each other with nothing between them.**"

---

The nine register boxes went across in two parties and a fifth, and the fifth was the same girl of nine who got counted twice on the third day, and she was not counted twice today, and she said, *I know, I said it last time, put me down twice so nobody loses me,* and Dalla Sen put her down twice.

They are the boundary's record. Each one is a child's reading book and the register and the two terms' attendance and, in the bottom of four of them, something a person has written about that child in a hand that is not the clerk's. **They are heavier than nine boxes have any right to be, and nine children carried them up a corridor at a walk past a dip and a waist in a pump's window, and it took an hour and twenty minutes and the fifth was carried by two adults with a rope.**

Dalla Sen carried a box herself. That is the part that went in the book, because the clerk's book has never once contained a record of the clerk carrying anything.

"**I have written every entry in this building for five months,**" she said, at the fifth bell, with the box on her shoulder at the far end, "**and I have got a name against a room and a name against a condition and a name against a closure and I have never once carried a thing across a fill in my life, because the fill is not in my column.**

"**I have moved my own record to the far side of the ground that three people in this building will not measure. I did not decide to. It was on a list with eight other boxes and it was that box or nothing, and I am not going to stand here and pretend I chose it, and I am not going to pretend I would have chosen the other one either.**"

---

Then the props, and it is in the book because it is the reason the school has no shelf.

Bel Ordry asked at the second bell, in a room, with the terms out loud, which is what this boundary does and which he has learned.

"**Ilsa Norr. I am asking you for the two beech lengths under the shelf in the school store. I am asking for them to make a bench in the kitchen-line room, because nine children and a teacher are going to sit on a floor in a room with a low ceiling, and a bench is not a luxury, it is a floor that is easier to get off.**

"**Terms: the shelf stays, the props come, and I will find something for the shelf. And I am not asking you to move them yourself, and I am not asking a child to move one.**"

"**No,**" said Ilsa Norr.

"Then I would like the reason on the page, because I have asked you for something in a room and you have said no in a room and the clerk is going to write down why."

"**The beech lengths are the shelf's legs,**" she said. "**I said that on the second day and I am saying it again and I would like it entered that I have said the same sentence twice and that it is still the same sentence.**

"And I will say the rest, because I have thought about it since Monday. **A prop under a shelf is a leg. It is not a length of timber that happens to be under a shelf. It is the thing that is holding nine boxes of this boundary's record off a floor that we have just found wet, and if the two lengths go to the kitchen-line end then the shelf has nothing under it and the plan's two parts come down onto the ground of a room whose floor we have not looked at since the eleventh week, and nobody is going to look at it, because there is nothing to look at and no instrument to look with.**"

Iven Tallo backed her with a craft fact and did it in one sentence, which is how he does it.

"**A prop is a leg. Put it under a bench and it is still a leg. You have not moved timber, you have moved what the timber is for, and the shelf is now a shelf on nothing.**"

"Then the school has no shelf," said Bel Ordry.

"**The school has no shelf,**" said Ilsa Norr, "**and the school has no box-stand either, and nine boxes are going to stand on a floor in two rows against a wall, and that is what it is, and I am not going to ask you twice and you are not going to ask me again.**"

He did not ask again, and Dalla Sen entered that he had asked in a room and been refused in a room and had not asked twice, and entered that the school is going without a shelf, and entered that the plan's two parts are still on that shelf on those two lengths, and that the two facts are on the same line in her hand on purpose and not because she thought they belonged together.

---

At the fifth bell, in the middle of a crossing, with a box on a child's shoulder at one end and a box on the clerk's at the other, the count was read out, because the count gets read on a crossing and it has always got read on a crossing.

Pell Roke was on the bench at the top of the nine steps with his ear against the stone and the middle part of the plan propped where he could see it, and Isel Ferrow took the far end at the kitchen line, and a boy who reads numbers and does not stop anybody read a column of numbers down a two-foot-eight corridor to a woman holding a box.

"**One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight,**" he said. "**The ninth is a dash.**"

"**Ten,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, in the anteroom's voice, and the anteroom was shut and empty and had been since the first bell. "**And that is the anteroom.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment, and then Dalla Sen said it from the far end of a shut corridor with a box on her shoulder, and she said it in the tone of a woman who has just found a sum that does not come out.

"**Ten. The anteroom holds ten. And I have been writing that number into a column in my own hand since the sixth week, and I transcribe it every fifth bell, and I have never once been in that room with ten people in it, and I have never once asked what the number was a number of, and I have known for five months that this page had a count with no name on it and I did not put those two facts next to each other because I did not think of it.**"

"Entered," said Bel Ordry, "**and I am going to say one thing about it and then I am going to leave it alone. The first column has been on that page since the sixth week and every number in it was said out loud in a room by a person before it was written down. If they are not numbers of people then they are numbers of something else, and this building has nine rows and no heading anywhere, and I am not going to invent a heading at the fifth bell of a day we are shutting a corridor.**"

And then a child asked the question, at the far end, in the middle of a crossing, in the voice of somebody who is eleven and has been walking a corridor with a box since the second bell.

"**Teacher — where does the ten come from?**"

The teacher did not answer it.

"**Teacher.**"

"**We are crossing, and it is a box, and put it down at the wall, not on the floor — not on the floor, on the wall.**"

Nobody in the corridor followed it up. Dalla Sen wrote nothing, and the boy's ear was against the stone at the top of the nine steps and he did not hear it and would not have asked it twice.

---

The corridor was shut at the fifth bell and stayed shut, and the school had run two bells at the kitchen-line end, and it did not run for the rest of the day.

That is the cost and it belongs in the plainest words this building has got.

"**The school ran at the fourth bell and it did not run at the fifth,**" said Ilsa Norr, at the sixth bell, standing in a room with a low ceiling with nine register boxes in two rows against a wall. "**The corridor was open and we shut it. That is one of the three days it runs and it is gone and it is not coming back in the week, and I am not going to be told it was moved. It was not moved. It was lost. It is a lost day and I would like it entered as a lost day and I would like the word *moved* struck if anybody has written it anywhere.**

"And I am not going to pretend the two things are the same. **I have not asked whether the closing and the losing are two facts or one fact, and I am not going to ask in a room, and if anybody in this building knows the answer I would like them to keep it, because the first time somebody in this Hearthhold knows the answer to that, it will be because they decided it, and I do not want it decided by anybody but the work.**"

And Dalla Sen did the thing she has done twice in this book, and entered it in her own hand and read it out.

"**The corridor on the third day of the twenty-first week: open by the standing arrangement, shut by this boundary's own decision, for the moving of the boundary's own record.**

"**The school on the third day of the twenty-first week: run for two bells, not run for the rest of the day, one of the three open days lost, not paid back, not restored, and no word on any page saying it was paid back.**

"**I am putting these two facts on the same line and I am putting them next to each other with nothing between them, and the reason I am doing it is not a reason, and I am going to say what it is: I am doing it so that a reader in a year cannot make it into a finding.**"

"That is the third time," said Renn Vale.

"**It is the third time and I am going to keep doing it until somebody tells me the two things are one thing, and nobody in this building is going to tell me that, and the day somebody does, it will not be because a clerk put two facts next to each other on purpose.**"

---

The third column was filled at the sixth bell, in a schoolteacher's own words, and it is the ninth line of a column that has eight filled in four hands, and Bel Ordry read it out with the rule.

"**The school is at the kitchen-line end. It is in a room with a dished floor, a hearth corner that takes the warmth, no mouth, and a ceiling a fourteen-year-old cannot stand up under. It is a worse room than the school corner. It is dry. It has no shelf.**

"**The nine register boxes stand on the floor in two rows against a wall and the boundary's record is on the far side of a fill from the clerk who keeps it, and the school is on the far side of a fill from the clinic and from me, and I am not going to pretend I have agreed with that and I am going to make it happen anyway, and both of those are true and I want them on the same line.**

"**And I am writing down in my own hand that the corridor was open and we shut it, and that the school had two bells and then did not run, and that nobody has paid that day back, and I am not going to have the word *moved* used for it in this building in any document in any month.**"

"That is mine," said Ilsa Norr, and she stood up when it was read, which is the rule.

"**Read and said yes in her own hand,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and the clerk has entered the word *moved* as struck and has entered the strike, and I am not going to fill a tenth row, and there are nine rows on this page and one of the third columns was open when the charter went on the board and I have said in a room that I am not going to say which one it is, and I am not saying it now either.**"

---

The pot was ten days at the fifth bell and ten days at the seventh and it did not go up, and the boundary's column moved by a day of moving, and the crew's did not move at all for the eleventh day, and the anteroom was shut and empty with ten on it and a frame a finger and a bit out of true, and the two jars came out of the haunch in the afternoon, maybe three, and a man of twenty-two was forty-three days and forty-three nights into a count in a column with nothing over the top of it, and a person was still under a lip.
