# Chapter 136 — A Plan Nobody Will Sign

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the twenty-seventh week, a Monday, and the school did not run, and the boundary extended its own public plan by two rows on the back of a day-board in four hands, and refused a third, and asked five people to sign it and five people said no for five different reasons, all of them good.**

**One document in four parts, in a coat, is what this boundary's plan is: a date in the top corner and no name anywhere with a date in the top corner and no name anywhere, and it does not leave this building, and nobody in this Hearthhold has signed it, and the boundary has never in twenty-seven weeks had a document it could not show anybody.**

Bel Ordry brought it up at the second bell in four parts and put it on the anteroom table, which he has never done, and stood over it with his hand flat on the top corner where the date is and did not say the date.

"**Before I open it. A plan in a coat is not a system, that is a man, and I have said that sentence in this room for six weeks and I am going to keep saying it while I do this, and I would like whoever writes it down to write that the man said it on the same afternoon he made the thing."

"**Here is what this is. This boundary's plan has ten rows and it has been a record of what has been done. From this afternoon it is going to say what each room may hold, and what condition each of those rooms is under, and who keeps each of them, and the fourth column stays empty and stays without a heading."

"**It is the same page. It is not a new document. It is a change of what a document is for, and I am the man who has to say that out loud because I am the one who asked for it and I could not tell anybody in this room what the difference is."

"**And the reason I have asked is a corridor with water in it and a room that held more than anybody has admitted and a woman from a surface federation who came up this passage on Friday and told me what she has seen a room do with a number in a column. She was right. I have been right five times in a fortnight about a floor and I got a girl of nine a broken foot, and I am not going to be the man who is right about a plan that nobody can be shown.**"

"**Then say what you want it for,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, "**because you have said the reason and the reason is the bad reason, and I want the other one.**"

"**I want it so that a room is a promise instead of a hope. And I have not got a better sentence and I am not going to improve on it in front of a room.**"

---

**First the first column went in, in the cook's hand, and she put a ten in it for the second time in twenty-seven weeks and said out loud that she had not counted anybody for the second time either.**

"**Ten,**" said Mikka Hale. "**And I want the room to hear the whole of it the second time as well as the first, because the first time I said it and one person heard it properly and this time everybody in here is going to hear it and I am not going to say it smaller."

"**Ten is what this page says a room like that is allowed to hold. I did not look in a room. I did not ask a child. I copied a rule off nine hundred years of somebody else's arithmetic and I have now done it twice, and both times the page got a permission and no room got counted."

"**A page that says ten is a building saying *up to here*. A page that says a number of people is a building saying *this many*. And the second one is a thing you can be held to and the first one is a thing you can be argued with, and I would rather be argued with for the rest of my life than be held to."

"**And I am the worst person in this room and I said so last time and I am saying it again in the same words, and the difference is that last week I was the worst person in a room about one row and this week I am the worst person in a room about two.**"

Dalla Sen read the first column back against the day-board and the cook said yes to the reading, and then the second column went in, in Renn Vale's hand, with no number and no day on either of the two new rows, and she read it out and said what the absence of a day was for.

"**A row with a day in its condition is a row that gets met on a day by a man with a book, whether or not anybody is ready, and this boundary has been taught that the hard way by a mark on a jamb and a floor and a sill, and I am not writing another one. So there is no day. And there is no number either, and the reason there is no number is that a number in a condition is a number somebody can satisfy, and a condition you can satisfy on a schedule is a date with a number on it, and that is the sentence this page is."

"**What it does have is a person. Two, and they are in the third column, and they are the two who can say this row is wrong.**"

In the way Renn Vale had said it would, the third column went in: in the hand of the person who keeps the row, and they stood up.

---

**Somebody wanted a third row, and the row was for the place where a man is dead, and the room refused it, and the refusal is the reason nobody was watching that hole.**

One row went in for the common landing. Another went in for the anteroom step, and Bel Ordry wrote his own row's third column and stood up to say it, which nobody had expected and Dalla Sen entered.

And then the low end of the corridor.

"**I want a row for the low end,**" said Bel Ordry. "**And I want to be the one who asks for it because I am the keeper of the page and it is my job to know what is on it. There is a place in this building where water stands in a channel for six minutes after the lever comes off, and where a man of twenty-two is dead under where the grate is, and where a length of the crew's own line is holding water over a hole with no grate in it. There is not one word about that place on the only public page this Hearthhold keeps. I do not care that the page is for rooms. There is a person under a slab at the near landing that is on neither this page nor the charter nor the second sheet, and I have never asked for a row for it and I am not going to. But that is a different argument and I am not making it."

"**So say no to me. Somebody in this room is going to, and I would rather it was said at the second bell with a reason than discovered in a month.**"

Renn Vale said no, and she said the reason before she said the no, and Leena Orr said the shape of it from the lower level where she had been listening with her good arm through the doorway.

"**Say her shape first, because she found it and I am borrowing it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Every row on that page is a place somebody is going to be,**" said Leena Orr, "**and I have been on the other side of this waist for five weeks and there is a plan in a coat with a row for every place anybody in this building sleeps, works, eats, washes, or teaches, and there is not one row for this side of the floor I am sitting on, and I am not asking for one and nobody is going to give me one and that is correct."

"**And the shape of it is this. A row is not a description. A row is a claim. You put a number in a column and a condition in a column and a person's hand in a column, and a person in a year picks that page up and says *this room may hold this many, and this is who keeps it, and this is the condition*, and that is not a description of a place. That is a claim on a place, made in advance, by people who are not going to be in it."

"**And the low end of that corridor is the only place in this building nobody is ever going to be in. So a row on it says one thing and one thing only, and the thing it says is *this place has an owner and here is the owner and here is what the owner will do about it*. And a man of twenty-two is dead in it.**"

"**I will support the refusal and I will not support it for that reason,**" said Mero Senn. "**I will support it because a row is a promise, and a promise about a place where nobody can stand is a lie told in a column."

"**And I want the half of it that is against me entered, because nobody in this room is going to say it and I am the only man here who is paid to say the half that is against the people in the room. A refusal to put a row on the low end means there is no plan for it, and a place with no plan is a place nobody is watching, and a place nobody is watching is how a man of twenty-two comes out of a channel. So the refusal is correct and the refusal is the reason, and both of those are in this room at the same time and I am not going to be asked to pick one.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**the refusal and the reason for it and the reason against it, in two hands and in a third, and entered that the refusal is a decision and not an oversight, and entered that the plan has no row for the low end of the corridor, and entered that the plan has no row for the place where a man is dead, and entered that the plan has twelve rows and one of the things anybody will want to write on it later is that sentence."

"**And I am entering it in that order and the order is the finding. I have been putting a reason beside two facts and nothing between them for twenty-seven weeks and this is the first time I have had to put a decision beside them, and a decision is a different animal and I do not have a category for it either.**"

Marek Venn had been in the room for all of it and had said none of it, and when a physician said that a promise about a place where nobody can stand is a lie told in a column, he put his right hand flat on the back of the bench, which is what that hand does now when it will not close, and he was doing an arithmetic he has been avoiding for a fortnight. The only advice anybody in this Hearthhold has acted on this month got a girl of nine a broken foot. The advice given in this room a minute ago is a row nobody is going to write. He could not work out which of the two was the more dangerous and he could not work out whether it was his business to find out, and he has never once in twenty-seven weeks not found out.

---

**At the fourth bell the plan went round the room and five people were asked to sign it, and five people said no, and Dalla Sen entered each reason as it was given and did not improve one of them.**

"**Who signs,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Not me,**" said Renn Vale. "**I have been the one a doctor looks at for a fortnight and I have said out loud what that is worth, and I am not going to be the one a clerk looks at instead. A signature on that page puts a woman next to a number and the number is in the row I keep, and I have spent twenty-seven weeks learning that a name next to a number is a handle.**"

"**Not me,**" said Mikka Hale. "**I am the worst person in this room and I have written two thirds of the numbers on it and I have not been in either of the rooms.**"

"**Not me,**" said Ilsa Norr. "**I will write the third column for my room and I will stand up and say yes to it in a room, and that is the closest this boundary gets to a signature and I am going to say why it is not one. A signature says *I agree this is what the room is*. A third column says *this is what I do*. I do the second one every day and I have never once done the first one about a room, and I am not starting on a Monday.**"

"**Not me,**" said Mero Senn. "**I said on Friday what I would sign and I have not been persuaded since by a good argument, which is a sentence I would like on the page, because people assume that a man can be moved by a better reason and it is not usually true of anybody and it was certainly not true of me today.**"

"**And not me,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am the keeper of it and that is the one that ought to be impossible. A keeper of a page is not a holder of a route, and that is in my own hand on the second sheet and it has not been withdrawn and the bad reason I gave for it has not been withdrawn either. If I sign this page then this boundary has a signed instrument with a name on it, and a signed instrument is a thing a person four days up a road can hold up, and I have spent six weeks telling this room that everything we write is portable and nothing we build is, and I am not going to be the first one to hand somebody a page to carry.**"

There was no signature on it. Dalla Sen wrote *nobody in this boundary will sign this plan* in the book, and read it out, and asked whether anybody wished to correct it, and nobody did, and she read it out again.

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked and a fifteenth thing went on it, and it was the first thing on that board that anybody in this building has written and then refused to sign.**

Tams Quill had the chalk in his left hand and his right one open and flat in his lap. He read both columns, and named no day, and gave no reason for not naming one, and he did not give a seventh reason for refusing to head the column because he had given one on Tuesday and had said he would not improve on it.

"**Four today and a hundred and twenty-five since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and forty-two and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half and have not moved since the second day of this month."

"**Fifteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, and a plan. Nine in the ordinary column and six in the one with nothing over the top of it."

"**The plan goes in with nothing over the top of it and I am going to say why and it is not the reason anybody in this room thinks. It is not in the ordinary column because a plan in the ordinary column is a plan somebody can point at, and this one cannot be pointed at, and that is not a flaw in the plan, it is the entire achievement of it. Twelve rows and no name and no signature and it does not leave the building, and I have never in twenty-seven weeks been in a room where the best thing anybody could do with a document was that it could not be carried out of the room."

"**And it is the second thing on this board that is not about a wall. The row was the first. And it is the first thing on this board that a person in this building wrote and then would not sign, and I want both of those in the book in that order and I am not going to be asked to improve on either."

"**A man of twenty-two is seventy-one days and seventy-one nights into a count that is his own, and I have not walked this board on a day when a clerk is standing at a sixth bell with that number, and I want that entered by her and not by me.**"

Dalla Sen read the fifteen entries out and read her own reading of them back and entered that the count had said the thing about her, which is true, and entered that the board was walked, which is also true, and entered the difference and did not resolve it.

---

**At the seventh bell the plan was in four parts in a coat, with twelve rows, and a ten in the first column of the newest two that nobody had counted, and a condition in the second column of each with no number and no day on it, and a third column in the hands of two people who stood up, and a fourth column empty and unheaded with a reason in it in two hands, and no name anywhere, and no signature anywhere at all, and a refusal in a clerk's book about the one place in this building where a man is dead.**

**At the waist the water was standing and the waist was holding, and there is no timber in this Hearthhold and no second grate and no wall in lime until the month after next, and a clerk read a count of seventy-one at a bell and said it out loud and was not consoled by anybody, because nobody in the anteroom thought there was anything to say.**
