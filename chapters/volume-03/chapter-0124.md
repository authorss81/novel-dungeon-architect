# Chapter 124 — The Terms Of The Packing

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the twenty-fourth week, and the room at the kitchen-line end stood with the door open and a lamp in it and nine register boxes on its hearth stones, and the packing of that sill took most of the day, and two people were asked for it in a room with the terms out loud, and a clerk wrote down the three people who could not be asked and the reason for each.**

"**Before anybody asks,**" said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up, "**I am going to say the three that cannot be asked, because I have been clerk in this building for twenty-four weeks and I have learned that the asking is half of the terms and the not-asking is the other half and this room has only ever written down the first.**

"**Leena Orr is on the lower level, which is this side of the waist, and the room is on the other side of the waist, and she cannot get there and I am not going to ask her and I am not going to send anybody to say I could not ask her.**

"**Pell Roke is fourteen with a splint and a crutch and he does not go down the steps and does not go into the corridor and does not go over the waist, and nobody has asked him to since the eighth day of the twentieth week and nobody is going to start on a Thursday with a good reason.**

"**And Tams Quill's right hand is open and flat in his lap and is not a working hand, and he has never been allowed a bar or a chisel or a lever or a packing in this Hearthhold and I am not going to ask him for one on the day we are short.**"

"**Enter that,**" said Renn Vale, "**all three, in that order, and enter that she offered them to me before she offered them to anybody and that I had not thought of the third one and that I am the woman who has a slate.**"

"**I had thought of it,**" said Renn Vale, "**and I did not say it, and you have said it for me, and that is twice in five weeks and I would like it entered as a habit and not as a virtue.**"

---

**Then the asking, and it is now the way this room asks anybody for anything, and Dalla Sen read the whole of it out before the question was put.**

"**Two people are asked for a piece of work. To pack the sill of the room at the kitchen-line end in stone in clay, in a room with the door open, in a shut corridor, on a Thursday, and to be out of it before the fifth bell so that the door is shut at the fifth bell and the room is shut for the weekend.

"**Two hours of Iven Tallo's and one hour of Marek Venn's. It goes in the crew's column and not in the boundary's, because the crew's column is a quantity of asking and this is the asking, and the boundary's hours are what this building does to itself and the crew's are what this building asked for, and those are two different numbers and I have watched this room confuse them twice.

"**The person who can be told no is Renn Vale, because she keeps the corridor and the terms and that sill is on her road. Either of them may say no and if either of them says no the asking is over and nothing is owed and it goes in the book as a no.**"

"**Say what the clay is,**" said Iven Tallo, and he had his sleeve back already.

"**There is no clay in this Hearthhold that is bought. There is a heap behind the kitchen line that came out of a wall and a heap at the stair foot that came out of a wall, and they are both already mixed and both of them are nine hundred years old in the making of them, which I know because Jo Verrick has put a finger in each of them and told me so.**"

"**Then the number I want is the number of hours and not the number of stones,**" said Iven Tallo. "**I have done a day of this trade in clay and I know what a day does to a shoulder and I am not being proud of anything and I want two hours and no more, and I want the third hour that is in this asking to be his,**" he said, "**and I want it written that I said that, because if he does not do that hour it is going to be me and the kitchen line by the end of the week and the kitchen line has six pails a day in it.**"

"**I will do the hour,**" said Marek Venn. "**And I will say the thing that makes it an hour and not four, which is that I cannot kneel. I can work a sill standing and I can work a sill on one knee for about a quarter of an hour and then I cannot get up without two hands and one of them does not close. So Jo Verrick's knee is in this and I am the man who holds the stones and I am not the man who is on the floor.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a carpenter named the split and a man agreed to it in a room, and entered that neither of them was asked to be brave about it.**"

---

**Packing a sill is the ordinary thing, and it was the best piece of work anybody has done in this building in five weeks, and that is exactly why the book has it.**

Jo Verrick had the stones out by the second bell and stacked in the order they came out, because she always does, and they went back in the same order with clay beaten between them, and there is no mortar in this Hearthhold and there is no timber and there are seven sound wedges and two dead sticks and **not one of either number moved.**

Marek Venn held the stones and got the joints tight and Iven Tallo beat the clay with a piece of the pump's own bed-plate and said one thing about the work out loud, once, in the middle of it.

"**This is a repair, and a repair is a thing you can come back to, and I want the room to know that there is exactly one of these in this building and it is nine hundred years old and it is in a sill at the kitchen-line end and nobody has been able to make another one since.**

"**And the reason nobody has been able to make another one is not the clay. It is that it takes a person who has done it, and there are four of us in this region and I am one of them and I have a suspended bond and an apprentice off the roll, and on a Tuesday the month after next a man in a survey camp is going to offer a piece of timber to a clerk at a counter and he is not going to offer anybody a mason.**"

Not one person answered him. Jo Verrick, on her knee with her hand in the joint, said, "**Four inches of it done. Get the next stone.**"

---

**At the third bell Renn Vale wrote the condition, in her own hand, on her slate, and read it out twice, and the clerk copied it and said her copy out against the original, which takes four minutes and is the way this room checks anything.**

"**The room at the kitchen-line end is closed with the room in use when it was closed. Eleven were in it and the number is eleven and eleven is not a capacity and is not a permission and is not a number anybody may use for anything else ever, and I have written that clause in and I have never written a clause like it before and I am writing it because a teacher stood in a room and asked for it.**

"**And the condition it is opened on is this, and there is no number on it and no day on it, and I have thought about a number all week and I am not going to use one.**

"**It is opened when the clay under that sill has stopped shrinking, and the person who decides that is the person who laid it, and the person who laid it is the man who held the stones and the man who beat the clay, and they have to be in a room together and both of them have to say it, and if one of them is not here then it is not opened.**

"**And I can stop it, and Nera Oduya can stop it, and the two of us can be stopped by each other, and neither of them can be stopped by a person four days up a road, and if a person four days up a road asks me for this I will read them the condition and I will not write it out.**"

"**Say why there is no number,**" said Bel Ordry, and he asked it because he has asked it every week since the seventh term.

"**Because clay does not tell you when it has stopped, and a number would be a day, and a day can be waited for, and this room has just been shut over a weekend with a condition on it and a word in it, and I am not going to hand a stranger a figure that says how long the whole of this took.**

"**It did not take a week. It took five weeks of this boundary and a mark that came off a wall and a word that nine children said in a corridor, and the reason I have not got a number is that the number would be a lie, and this is the one page in this building where a lie would be load bearing.**"

Dalla Sen read it out twice and the cook said yes to the copy and Renn Vale said yes to the original and Nera Oduya, from three steps, said, "**I have not been asked and I am saying yes to being in it, and I am saying yes in my own words and not by nodding, and Dalla is going to write that down and I would like it written that I was not asked.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the air engineer put herself in a condition in a room without being asked, for the second time in twenty-four weeks, and that the first time was a stop on a cloth in a doorway.**"

---

**And the last hour of the fourth day was the two people who were not going to be blamed finding out whether that was true, and it was, and it cost them the thing they had.**

Bel Ordry said it first, standing on the landing, because he is the man who writes the things and he had worked out that nobody was going to ask.

"**The rule says the person who writes a change to a room is the person who keeps it, and it is a good rule and it is five weeks old, and it has just done something this building has never had done: it has put the whole of this on a schoolteacher and on a woman with a slate, and neither of them asked for it, and the reason neither of them is to be blamed is the rule and not the two of them.**

"**And I want it said in a room by the two of them, and not by me, and I am going to ask.**"

Ilsa Norr said it for herself and did not do it well and Renn Vale said the half she had left out.

"**Nobody is to be blamed,**" said Ilsa Norr, "**and I am not going to argue about that, and I am not going to be comforted by it either, because the rule does not say the person it puts things on gets to be comfortable and I have been told that in a room by the woman who keeps the terms and I am writing it down as what I have been told.**

"**And I am not going to be forgiven either, and I would like that said by somebody who is not me, because I have been in this building five months and I have watched this room forgive people and it takes the whole thing out of it.**"

"**I am not going to forgive you,**" said Mikka Hale, "**and I am not going to blame you, and I want it entered that I said both of those in that order, and I would like it entered that a cook is the worst person in this building to say it to and that I said it anyway.**"

Marek Venn said the other half, and it was nine words, and he said it to the room and not to her, and Dalla Sen read it back.

"**I recommended it and I was right and it is going to cost a child.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in his words, and entered that he said it before anybody asked him to and that it is the shortest thing anybody has put in this book in twenty-four weeks and that the clerk is not going to improve it and does not know how.**"

---

**And at the sixth bell the board was walked, because it is walked on the days there is a person in the room who can be argued with about it, and today there were two.**

"**The crew are at fifty-six and a half,**" said Tams Quill, with the chalk in his left hand and his right one open and flat in his lap, and he read it twice. "**Fifty-three and a half on Monday of the twenty-second week, and three since the fourth day of this week, and it is three and not a gift, and the reason is in the clerk's hand in the clerk's words and I am not going to give it, because a clerk gave it better than I give it, and that is now the second week running that a man has said that in this room and I would like somebody to notice it and not to make anything of it.**

"**The boundary is at two hundred and four and a quarter. Four on the first day, three on the second, two on the third, three today, and eighty-seven since the keeper signed. It adds by the day the work was done and both columns reconcile and they have reconciled for eleven weeks.**

"**And a man of twenty-two is fifty-nine days and fifty-nine nights into a count that is his own, and it is in the column with nothing over the top of it, and it is not a debt and it is not a wage, and I am reading it because the sixth bell reads what is on the board and not what a room is feeling.**"

Then he got his chalk to the side of the board with nothing over the top of it and stopped with it a finger off the wood, which he has done a number of times.

"**Thirteen entries,**" he said. "**Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark. Nine in the ordinary column and four in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim is a claim and its condition is not met. The mark came off a jamb and is still on this board and I gave the reason for that and the reason holds. I am not going to walk that again this week because I said it once and it does not improve.**

"**And the column is not headed. It is the sixth batch running and I am not going to give you a reason this week.**"

"**You have never once not given a reason,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I have never once not given a reason,**" said Tams Quill, "**and I am not going to start on a Thursday when I have three reasons and I have not chosen between them, and a man who gives you the first of three is worse than a man who gives you none. I am going to choose one. I will say it when I have chosen it and not one day before, and I am not going to be asked for it in the meantime, and if I am asked for it in the meantime then I will say I have not chosen, and that is going to be the answer.**"

Nobody asked him. Dalla Sen entered the refusal and entered the offer and entered that he had three and had not chosen, and entered that the first time a person in this building has ever entered that a man had three reasons and was choosing was on the fourth day of the twenty-fourth week, and that nobody in the room had understood at the time that it was a different kind of sentence from the five before it.

**The room at the kitchen-line end of the corridor stood with its sill packed and its door open and nine register boxes on nine hearth stones, and the bed of the second hearth stone had a hole in it the width of two fingers, and nobody had said anything about the hearth stones since the second bell, and the school was at the far end of the corridor not running and would be in that room in the morning for the twelfth time, and the door was going to be shut at the fifth bell, and the person who was going to shut it had said in a room on Wednesday that she was not going to be able to say afterwards that she did not know, and she was right about that, and it had cost the room nothing at all to be certain of it.**
