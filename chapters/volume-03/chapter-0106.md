# Chapter 106 — The Wheel Goes With The Keeper

The first day of the twenty-first week was shut for the corridor, which was a Monday, and the boundary ran all day without its elected keeper in it, and it ran perfectly well, and that is the finding and it is not a comfort.

Sefu Aran had gone up the middle passage at the first bell the day before with two true sentences in the clerk's book and nothing else in his hands, and nobody had walked him out past the waist because the corridor was open on a Friday and four pails were going over it in the pump's window, and **that was an accident of the calendar and not a decision, and Dalla Sen entered it as an accident of the calendar, and entered that she would remember it in a month.**

---

**The first thing anybody said about it came at the second bell, from Renn Vale, on the landing, and it was not about the water or the school or the shut room.**

"**Clause four says the keeper will show the fourth column,**" she said, "**and I have been up here for two hours working out whether that is Bel Ordry or Sefu Aran, and I have worked out that it is both, and that the two of them have never been the same man and I have always known it and never once said it in a room.**

"**The plan's keeper is Bel Ordry and he has been the keeper of it in every room that has ever read it. The civic keeper is Sefu Aran and he signs first on everything. And clause four is a sentence with one noun in it and two men in this building who could be it, and until a week ago the two men agreed without ever having to say so, and that is not a rule. That is a thing two men happened to be doing.**"

Bel Ordry said, from the anteroom table, "**Then it is a hole in the charter, and the charter is a week old, and I am not going to reopen a signed instrument over a noun.**"

"**I am not asking you to reopen it. I am telling you that for five weeks this building has had two men in every sentence it has written, and one of them went up four days of road yesterday morning, and I do not know one single thing in this Hearthhold that has a name on it that is not his or mine or both.**

"**And I am not frightened of the boundary stopping. I am frightened of it being the only thing that stopped because he was in it.**"

---

It did not stop. That is what the day was for.

**The pump ran attended** — second bell to fifth, two people, twenty-six strokes, six minutes off, nineteen — **and the water came up the middle passage** in two crossings, four and then two, at a walk, over a waist that moved three times and is not measured by three people on purpose. The six minutes after the lever came off came and went, and the water stood in the dip where it stands, two foot eight wide on the edge of eleven feet of shaft, and **nobody stood at the low end and watched it, because nobody has stood there since the sixteenth week and nobody ever will, and the blank on the review page was blank at the sixth bell and it was blank at the seventh bell.**

**The school was at the kitchen-line end** and the corridor was shut, so the school did not run, and that is the arrangement and it has not changed in five months. The shut school corner was shut and **nobody in this building went and opened it to look, on the fourth day of a week in which a stranger had stood in it with a permit.**

**The cook's number was ten days**, and it had held at ten for longer than it had ever held, and it did not go up, and it was read out at the fifth bell in her words on her own board.

**And at the sixth bell, at the anteroom, on the wheel, which nobody was standing at,** Dalla Sen read the six things, and the six were the same six with two more on them, and the two more were the keeper's absence and the second sheet to be written on the second day, and nobody filled the space after the last one.

**Everything that had a person in it had a substitute in it, and not one of the substitutes was a keeper, and the reason every one of them existed is that Sefu Aran had said the words that made them, nineteen weeks ago, in a room, and had then gone.**

---

**Dalla Sen read the two sentences at the third bell, standing, in the anteroom, with the room at its number,** and she read them because the man who left them had asked her to read them to whoever would have to act on them, and there is a clerk in this building who is the person who has to act on them, and she did not think about that and she did it anyway.

"**These are not mine. They are his, and he wrote them in the nineteenth week and he read them out in this room, and he left them with me and said read them to anybody who has to act on them. So I am reading them to you, and I am reading them standing up because I do not think a thing like this should be read sitting down, and I have never done that before in this room and I am not going to pretend I have.**

"**One. Nothing comes. This boundary is not sending anybody and has not sent anybody and will not. There is a water-lock at the second gate with a sill and a bed and a wedge of hardwood and two hands a day for four days, and that timber is the office's and the office has no carpenter with a bond, and this boundary released it in writing and the release stands and the office is not to blame. The four households have their own water and their own four sets of steps and they have had them since the cold closed, and they did not ask this boundary for any of this.**

"**Two. This boundary has found two jars a day out of its own wall and is using them and is not sending them, because it has two jars a day and a man of twenty-two and no second pair of hands, and if it gave them away it would have none. And the true sentence is that it looked after itself and four people carried water up four sets of steps, and those two things happened at the same time and one of them was this boundary's choice.**"

For a while nobody said anything, and then the pump's check came off, and six of the twenty-six went by, and the water in the low end of the corridor stood where it stands, and the sixth minute went, and nobody was standing at the low end of the corridor watching it, and the blank on the review page was still blank, and nobody was asked about it, and nobody was sent for, and the blank on the review page is still blank and is going to stay blank.

Then Mikka Hale said one thing and it was the only thing anybody said about the sentences for the rest of the day.

"**Read them again at the fifth bell.**"

"**They are read at the fifth bell,**" said Dalla Sen. "**That is what the six things are for.**

"**Then read those two at the fifth bell as well,**" said the cook, "**and I am not going to argue for it and I am not going to explain why. If I explain why it will sound like I have an argument and I have not got one. I want them read because a thing that is true and heard once is a thing that gets repeated wrong by the fourth person who hears it, and a thing that is true and read every day is a thing that four people say the same way.**"

**At the fifth bell Dalla Sen read them again, and at the fifth bell every day while the keeper is gone,** and the room decided that by Renn Vale, out loud, on the landing, in the same breath as the reading, and what she decided was narrow and she said so.

"**I decide that they stand and that they are read. I do not decide that anybody in this building is to do anything about them, because there is nothing in this building that reaches four days up the middle passage, and a room that decides what to do about four households' water is a room that has started sending somebody, and we are not sending anybody.**

"**And I decide it, and not the clerk and not the man going up the road, because it is my page and I can stop it and I can be stopped by it. Write all of that down and do not shorten it.**"

"Then what is the point of reading it," said Wen Pask, from the fourth step, who has no standing in this room and has had it anyway for five months.

"**That is not a question I can answer and I am not going to try,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I will tell you what I can answer. It is not for them. It is for the fourth person in this room who has not been here for all of it.**"

---

Then Marek Venn did the thing nobody in this building has done since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, which is to go and find out what would happen, and he did it with a piece of chalk and it took him the whole of the fourth bell and the whole of the fifth.

He went round the building asking one question of everybody, in their own work, at their own station, and the question was what a man had been doing here for twenty weeks that wanted a keeper's word.

**The first thing was the wheel.** Renn Vale stood at it for a bell and a half and then came off it.

"**I can hold it,**" she said, "**and I am not going to pretend I cannot, because I can, and because refusing a thing I can do is a vanity and this building has four of those already.**

"**I can hold it because of what he said when he signed the charter, and I am going to read his words out because they are the only reason I can stand here, and they are in the book in his hand.** *The road is not the paper, the road is a way up a passage and I hold the cord and the wheel and the bell and none of those are paper.* **He said that in a room and I wrote it down, and it is the reason I have a wheel today, and it has been the reason for three weeks and I did not think about it once until this morning.**"

"Nobody thought about it once," said Bel Ordry, "**and that is not a fault. That is what it is for.**"

"**Then I am not a keeper,**" she said. "**I am a woman holding a wheel because a man said out loud that a wheel is not paper, and those are different, and the difference is going to matter the first time somebody wants me to do something I do not want to do, and on that day there is nobody above me to say no and there is nobody above him either, because he is on a road.**"

Dalla Sen entered that, in her own hand, and entered that it was the first entry in the book that named a gap rather than a work, and that she had a column for it and was not going to use one, because *a gap with a heading on it is a job, and a job is what a person gets given at the fourth bell in a bad month.*

**The second thing was the water-carrying.** Six pails a day, every day in seven, two crossings, four and then two, at a walk, in the pump's window, on a fill that has moved three times.

**The room read the seventh term out** in Renn Vale's own words from her own slate, at the fifth bell, with her standing on the landing and nobody else in the room doing it, and it was right, and every figure on it was hers, and it did not want a keeper at all, and **she read it and then she said the thing that made the day.**

"**It is right and it is mine,**" she said, "**and I have been saying it is the boundary's for five months because the boundary is a nicer word than mine. The clerk writes it down as the boundary's because the charter is the boundary's.** **I am not the boundary. I am a woman with seven terms and an eighth and a slate, and when this boundary stops, what stops first is me, and not because anybody took anything off me.**"

**The third thing was a signature**, and Dalla Sen found it at the fourth bell, and it was the smallest and it was the worst.

"**The second sheet,**" she said. "**The charter has seven hands on it and one of the seven is a man who went up the middle passage yesterday morning. The boundary is not reopening the charter, so the sheet goes under it, and the practice in this building for nineteen weeks has been that a thing under the charter carries the boundary's hands. We cannot get his.**

"**So we have to work out who signs it instead, and I have asked three people in the last hour and I have got three answers and I am not going to decide this and I am going to write down all three.**

"**Bel Ordry: he says a thing under the charter does not need the boundary's hands, it needs the hands of the people whose objects of care are in it, and that is a better rule than the one we have been using and he is right and I hate it.**

"**Mikka Hale: she says the air engineer does not sign anything in this Hearthhold and has said so out loud, and that therefore the sheet will have one signature on it and a second hand on it, and that a document with one signature and two hands is better than a document with seven signatures that means nothing.**

"**And Isel Ferrow, who I did not ask and who was standing there, said that a sheet with two names on it is a sheet two people can be sent to.**"

**The space after that went unfilled too.**

Then Marek Venn said the fourth thing, which was not a gap, and which he had been carrying since the seventh bell on the day before.

"**The plan is a page in a coat in a room with a man in it,**" he said, "**and I have said in this room that a page in a coat is worth having exactly as much as the man in the coat, and I said it out loud in front of eleven people and it got written down, and I meant it.**

"**And on the second day that man emptied his own school store and gave away the room, and on the third day he stood on the common landing and watched nine children walk over ground that three people will not measure, and on Friday he read the standing arrangement out to a stranger in a room and then he did not ask for anything when it was finished. I have been in this building five months and I have watched him do less and less of the loud things for three weeks, and I do not know what that is, and I am not going to stand in a room and be the man who asks him why in front of the clerk.**"

Renn Vale said, "**That is the first thing you have said in a month that I did not already know.**"

He came back down the nine steps at the sixth bell and said out loud that all three of the things had a substitute in them and not one of the substitutes was a keeper, and he said the part that was for the room and the part that was for himself, and Dalla Sen wrote both.

"**Then here is what I have found and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have got. This boundary does not need a keeper. It has three things that want a keeper's word and every one of them has somebody else in it, and the wheel is not the wheel, the wheel is a man who comes off a step and says a thing out loud, and everything he says can be said by other people, and they mostly do.**

"**I have wanted to be needed in this building for twenty weeks. I have been needed twice last week and both times it was a floor and a wet joint and a man with a rule who knew where to put his hand, and neither of those needed me, they needed a woman who had been kneeling on that flag since the eleventh week.**"

Nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him, and Dalla Sen entered that the room heard it and entered that nobody was asked to agree, which is a thing this room has had to learn twice.

---

**And at the sixth bell Tams Quill walked the board twice and read both columns and named no day and gave no reason, and put a number in the column with no heading, and did not head the column.**

"**The crew are at fifty-two and a half, and they have been at fifty-two and a half since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and they did not move today, and I am going to give the reason out loud because Renn Vale gave hers out loud and because two people in this room have now said the same thing about the same figure in two different words and it is time somebody put it in a column.**

"**The figure is not a debt and it is not a rota and it is not going to move by itself, and it moves when a person in a room asks the five of them for a work, with the terms said out loud and a person named who can be told no. Nobody has asked them for anything. That is the whole reason. It is not that they are tired and it is not that they are angry and it is not that they have offered twice last week and been stopped twice, which they have, and which is in the book.**"

"**The boundary's column,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**A hundred and forty and a quarter.** A hundred and seventeen and a quarter when the keeper signed, and nineteen in the week he went, and four today: Dalla Sen one, a reading and a reading again and a copy for a courier. Bel Ordry one, a page in a coat carried past three things that wanted a keeper's word. Renn Vale one, the road held for a week with a substitute named in her own hand. Me, one, a board, walked twice. **Four, and it adds, and I have done it twice, and both columns reconcile and they have not stopped.**"

Then he read the column with nothing over the top of it, the way he reads it: a leg, a bundle, **forty-one day and forty-one nights**, a half hinge, a line refused, a page gone out of sight, and a wage.

"**And it has no heading and I am not going to give it one, and nobody has asked me to today, and I would like it noticed that it is the fourth batch running and that I have a better reason each time and that a man with a better reason each time is a man who is running out of reasons, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that and say something about it before I have to.**"

Nobody in the room said anything about it, and he had asked them to.

---

**At the seventh bell Marek Venn went up the nine steps to the anteroom bench and sat down, and there was nothing for him to do, and that was the second time in twenty weeks, and it was worse than the first.**

He had a book of his own and he wrote in it, and this is the first entry in that book about him and not about a plan.

> *Twenty-one weeks. I have been the man who goes in this building since the first week. He goes up the road this morning and the boundary carried on and the pump ran and the six minutes came and went and the pot was read at the fifth bell and the low end of the corridor stood in water with nobody watching it, exactly as it does when I am on a bench being useless with a cloth round my hand.*
>
> *And the second thing, which is worse. A room can hear the true thing about itself and go on to the fifth bell. It is not comfort and it is not all right. It is the boundary working. It is the thing we have been arguing for in nineteen weeks — that a room does not stop when a man goes up a road — and it worked, and I was the last man in this building that needed it to and I am the one it left standing on a stair with nothing to carry, and I do not know what to do with an evening.*

He read it back at the seventh bell to nobody, because that is the rule, and put the book away, and went down to the kitchen and ate, and the pot was ten days and had not gone up and was not going to.

**The shut school corner was still shut, and nobody opened it to look, and nobody has opened it since the day it was shut, and the marks on the lintel over the mouth are still there being four marks and one groove that two people read two ways.**
