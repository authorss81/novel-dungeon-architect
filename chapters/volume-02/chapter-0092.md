# Chapter 92 — The Door Off Its Hinges

Bel Ordry asked her at the first bell on the second morning of the eighteenth week, in the anteroom, in front of the clerk, and he asked her properly, which in this building means he said the thing that would cost him and then stopped talking.

"I am asking you to stop the outer service door being used," he said. "**Three days. From now until the third of them is read out and the room says the word.** Not the bell, not the wheel, not the throat. The door.

"And I have no standing to ask you and that is why I am asking you and not telling you, and I will take no."

Renn Vale looked at him for long enough that the anteroom got used to it, which it had, by then, and which was not a small thing about a woman who says no before the argument starts.

"**No,**" she said, and then she did not stop, and what followed took four minutes and Dalla Sen wrote all of it and read it out twice.

"I will not hold that door shut for three days while the anteroom is over its number, and here is why, and it is the whole of why.

"**My terms are the lead line and the shore and the road, and the outer service door is in the anteroom's rating and not in my terms, and I have said that in this room twice and neither time did anybody hear it.** If I hold that door for three days then there are two sets of terms on one room in two hands and no rule anywhere in this building that says which of them wins, and in a month somebody will lean on the one that is convenient and the other one will be a sentence in a book that nobody is reading.

"And I will not do it quietly either, which is the part that matters, because I have watched a man in this building hold a door shut for thirteen weeks by never saying what was behind it and I thought that was admirable until he stood in a room and told us what it was for. **I do not want a second one of those in here and I would rather be the woman who is inconvenient than the woman who is careful.**

"So: no, and I am not going to be asked twice, and it does not need to be a person who can be refused. It needs to be the pump, and it needs to be the water, and it needs to be the air, and the door has to go because a woman with a jar and a man with a frame have said the water is not for drinking until it has been read out three times. **You did not have to ask me. You had to ask them.**"

---

Marek Venn had his own hand up before anybody had finished refusing, and it was the fifth time in eighteen weeks, and it was the thing he was worst at and the only thing he had ever been fast at.

It went up to about a third of the way, the way his father's hand went up in a room, the way a hand goes up when a man has already costed the job and knows the sequence and has decided that the trouble with asking is that it takes a minute. It went up for a second and a half. It came down before anybody noticed it, which was deliberate, and it was the first time in his life he had brought it down before anybody noticed.

What stopped it was not the woman on the landing. It was her sentence, *two sets of terms on one room in two hands and no rule anywhere in this building that says which of them wins*, which is a thing he has written on a form in front of a client, with a date on it, in a city four hundred miles and a year away, and which he had never once had to fix from the outside.

He had opened two of those. He remembered the second one in a way he had not been expecting to. There had been a hatch cut in a slab for a services run and two authorities with different ideas about who owned the floor it came out on, and he had put a cover on it and signed it and gone up to a room, and the room had gone on living on the cover for two years and nobody had ever asked which authority the cover belonged to, because the cover worked and a working thing is not asked about. He had thought about it once in eighteen weeks. It had come back unbidden on a Tuesday in a kitchen while a woman refused to do a job he could have done in four minutes, and it had come back with the date on it, which was the part that hurt, because he had not known the date and his hand did.

He did not say any of that in the room. He said one sentence, and he said it to Renn Vale and not to the clerk, and Dalla Sen entered it because she enters things.

"I have taken a door off a frame in a slab before and signed a cover over the hole," Marek Venn said, "and never once found out whose the cover was. I am not doing that here. I am asking her the same way I would have to ask in a building with a client in it, which is the way she just told me to."

They did not get the door off until the third bell, because Jo Verrick would not take the frame off anything with a lever in it standing over eleven feet of shaft, and the pump's window is the only window there is, and the wheel went round under the floor twice while they waited and nobody minded, because a boundary that minds the road for twenty minutes over a door is a boundary that has started to count its own costs in the wrong place.

Then Bel Ordry and Isel Ferrow and Wen Pask took the outer service door off its hinges, and it took forty minutes, and it was four iron pins and a haunch of dressed stone behind each one, and the pins are rusted into the stone and came out whole, which is the only good luck in the whole of the day and it is not much.

Renn Vale stood in the corridor on the anteroom's side and watched the whole of it and did not help and was asked not to, and the reason she gave was that a woman on a landing who has been asked to hold a room and then stands in the middle of the work is a woman who has become the work.

Behind the frame there was a joint of dressed stone about four inches deep, and the mortar in it was lime mortar, and it was a joint anybody in a town would have pointed at.

Four threads came out of it and none of them was a drip. They were about the thickness of a bootlace and they had been running for months, and down the face of the stone there was a stain shaped like a leaf, and the leaf was white.

Nobody in the room had looked at it, and every one of them had walked past it, and Jo Verrick put her hand on it now and stood with her hand on it and did not say anything for a long time.

"**Every day,**" she said at last. "Every day for fifteen weeks, into a wall, in a building with a fire in it, and I called it warm."

---

Nera Oduya came down four steps, which is one more than she has ever come into anything in this volume, and she did not touch the water and she did not touch the stone, and she said the thing that turned the morning into the week.

"**Where is the air going?**

"Nobody has asked it and everybody in this room has been assuming the answer, and I have been assuming it harder than anybody, and I have been in this building eighteen weeks and I have never once written a line about a wet wall, because I do not have a column for a wet wall.

"A wall that has been taking water for months is a wall with four threads in it, and a wall with a hole in it is not a wall as far as my work is concerned, it is a **route.** Air and water go the same way and one of them is quieter about it. That stone behind that door is now a passage between whatever is under this level and this anteroom, and the air that has been coming up out of the school corner's mouth since the collapse has been going **somewhere**, and there is nowhere for it to go but down, and the thing at the bottom of that is the shaft that stopped being a chimney the week the cold came.

"So I am not looking at a leak. I am looking at the place where the unaccounted air in this building has been arriving, and it is in the wall of the room with the highest count on the plan, and the room whose second way out is the door we have just taken off, and the room I have certified as fit to hold ten people including one litter for no more than two hours for five weeks running on a test I did in the first fortnight and have not repeated because the terms have not changed."

**It went quiet, and it stayed quiet,**

"Nera," said Mikka Hale. "Say the part about the door."

"**I am not saying it now and I am not going to let anybody use me for it.** In five minutes this room is going to be full of people who need somebody to tell them a wet wall cannot be in the wall of a room, and I am an air engineer and I do not hold the anteroom and I have never held the anteroom. You want the whole of it in a room, you ask me again in a room, and I will give you the whole of it in a room, and I will not give you it on a stair with my hand on a stone."

---

The anteroom was emptied before the lever came off, and that was the only way it could be emptied, because a pump standing off in this building puts water over a floor two foot eight wide on the edge of an eleven-foot shaft, and eleven people cannot be standing in that corridor at the fifth bell on the strength of a door frame.

So they emptied it in the pump's window, which is twenty-three minutes, and Renn Vale stood at the school door and counted the corridor because that is her work and she was not in the anteroom, and the count went eleven, and the last of the eleven was a woman on a litter who came down the nine steps backwards under her own direction.

**And the pot was running that whole time off a joint in a wall with the door off it.** Nobody said that out loud until the air engineer said it out loud, and she said it while watching eleven people go down nine steps she had just said were not in a room anybody could stop:

"That is a pump running on the evidence. Not on a joint. **On the evidence.** And if the water in that wall does what water does, then the pump has been running for five weeks on a thing that four people have been eating and drinking and nine have been taught in, and I have certified a room on the strength of a test I did in the first fortnight, and I have not repeated it, and the reason I have not repeated it is that I did not want to find anything."

"You still have not found anything," said Bel Ordry.

"No," said Nera Oduya. "I have found **four threads of water in a dressed stone haunch,** and I have not found the end of it, and I am not going to call four threads an emergency in a room with a pump on it, and I am not going to call four threads nothing. I am going to say the only thing I will say about it, and then I am going to go and stand on the landing, and it is this:

"**That is nineteen weeks of a building's water in a wall, and nobody in this building wrote it down, and the reason nobody wrote it down is that there is no column for a wet wall, and a wet wall is not a fault, it is a route, and nobody in this building owns a route they did not build.**"

**Renn Vale suspended the anteroom's rating in her own hand before anybody used the room again, and it was read out, and the suspension said three things:** the anteroom holds **what a shut anteroom holds**, the outer service door is out of the rating entirely until a person says the word *door* out loud in a room and the clerk repeats it, and the second way out of the anteroom during those three days is **six people down nine steps and a waist that is shut.**

Then Mikka Hale made the announcement she had said she would make on the Monday, and made it to the anteroom, and made it twice, in the same words, in case anybody thought they had missed the first.

"**The anteroom is not available to the school for three days.** Nine of you at the school end of the corridor with the door at your back and the teacher, and that is worse than the room and I am sorry about it, and I am not going to pretend the two are the same, because they are not, and I have taught nine children for a week and I know the difference between a bad room and a room with a door in it."

Ilsa Norr said, "It runs. That is all I am going to say about it and I would like it written down that I said it once and did not argue."

"Entered in her words and read out," said Dalla Sen.

---

**Leena Orr's litter had to go down the nine steps to the lower level,** and Mikka Hale carried the good end and Bel Ordry carried the bad end and Leena Orr told them both where to put their feet, and the whole operation took eleven minutes and was done without one word of complaint from anybody including the woman on it.

"Twenty-seven days," said Wen Pask at the fourth bell, to nobody, in the doorway, and Dalla Sen entered it because she has entered everything he says in a doorway for a fortnight and nobody has ever asked her to stop.

At the fifth bell the reading went on the pot and the corridor, and the pot was **eleven days** and did not move, and the frame was at the pump, and the door was leaning against a wall in the anteroom with its four pins in it, and the hole behind it was four inches deep and full of lime and running water, and Jo Verrick said out loud, twice, to two different people, that there was no mortar in this Hearthhold of the right kind and the wall would be packed with stone and clay and **would hold, and would not be a repair, and would have to be said in a book as a thing that is holding and not as a thing that is fixed.**

**The door stayed leaning against a wall in the anteroom with its four pins in it, and the pump was at its window, and the jar was on the bench under Renn Vale's eye and it was going to be there three days.**

**Nobody drank anything,** and the jar stood on the bench under Renn Vale's eye, and it was going to stand there three days.
