# Chapter 169 — The Bed Under The Ninth Stone

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the thirty-third week, a Thursday, and the school did not run, and the brace stood through the night over the head of the anteroom's outer service door with a shim in it, and a woman who keeps the room went in on her own at the second bell and knelt down beside the ninth hearth stone and found out what it was doing.**

**Nobody was sent to look. Nobody had to be. The condition said that the first of two people to stop agreeing takes the brace out, and that neither has to give a reason first, and that the second to find out is the one who acts, and she was the only one in that room who had a reason to kneel down.**

The brace had been standing for about nineteen hours. It had been standing through a shut corridor and a night with nobody in the anteroom. The two sound wedges that had come out of a school door three weeks ago were in it, and the shim was in it, and the frame was a finger and two-thirds out of true instead of a finger and three-quarters.

Jo Verrick read the wall at the first bell and read the frame at the same bell, because she now read both, and the two readings were the same as Wednesday's, and she entered them and went to the pump.

A shut Thursday in this building is quiet in a way the other days are not. The room at the kitchen-line end is not being entered or left by anybody. It has nine hearth stones in it, a fire-frame with the fire out of it, and a strip of crate board under the ninth one. A person who keeps it goes in on shut days and looks at all nine in order with her hand on each, which she has done since the twenty-fourth week, and it takes about a minute and a half when she is not looking for anything.

Then, at the second bell, the room at the kitchen-line end was empty and the school was not running and the woman who keeps it went in to do the thing she does at the start of a shut day, which is to look at the nine hearth stones, in order, with her hand on each one, and to say the room to herself the way her own trade taught her.

She was on her knees at the ninth one for longer than the other eight took together.

The strip of crate board was still under it. Both flags were still in place. The stone had not moved. What had moved was the bed, which is the hole the stone sits in. It had been a hand and a half below the stone since the twenty-fourth week. It was now a hand and a little under it, and the difference was small enough that a person who had not been looking at it every shut day for nine weeks would not have seen it at all. She had been looking at it. She saw it. She did not stand up.

She put two fingers in the gap under the ninth stone and felt the cold coming off the clay, and the clay was wet, and the clay has been wet in a building with a pot at nine for a week.

Then she stood up and went and got the slate, and she did not write anything on it, and she came down four steps into the anteroom and stood in the doorway of the room where four people had held a piece of beech the day before, and said the thing out loud, and she said it to the room and not to the man who built it.

"**The bed under the ninth stone has come up about half an inch since Friday morning and the stone has not moved and the flags have not moved and the board is where it was.**"

Nobody in that anteroom spoke, and a pump maintainer was at the frame, and a physician was standing by his bag, and a man on a step had a book shut on his knee, and a clerk had a piece of chalk in her hand and had not put it down.

"**Say what you want done,**" said Dalla Sen, and her voice did not change and nobody in the room thought it had until afterwards.

"**I want the brace out.**"

"**Say the rest of it, because the condition has three clauses and you have said one of them, and I have read the condition to you twice and I would rather you said all three than have me fill them in for you.**"

"**The first of us to stop agreeing takes the brace out. Neither of us has to give a reason to the other one first. And the second of us to find out is the one who acts.**" She put the slate down on the anvil end of the trestle. "**I have stopped agreeing. I found out at the second bell and I am the second, and I did not have to give anybody a reason first, and I am not going to give anybody a reason now, and I am not going to be asked for one in a room by anybody, including the two of you.**"

"**Then it comes out,**" said Dalla Sen, and put the chalk down, and said the rest of it out loud so that the room had it in a voice and not only in a book.

"**Entered: the condition was met on the second day of this week and it is broken on the fourth day, in a room, by the person it names, and the brace is to be taken out, and the person who has to act is the person who found out, and that person is not the man who built it, and nobody in this building has sent anybody anywhere to find out.**"

Mero Senn came off the wall in about a second and a half, which was the fastest anybody in that room had ever seen him move, and then stopped. He had begun to reach for the wall with two fingers of his right hand, in the habit of a man who reads a building by touching it. This morning the wall was not the thing he had been sent for.

"**I am not going to touch that wall,**" he said. "**I have spent eleven days telling this room that I will not read my own readings, and I am not going to walk in and put my fingers in a gap in a floor to find out whether I was right. Ask somebody who has not been in this room.**"

"**Then ask one,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**No. Ask her.**" He moved his chin a half inch towards the man on the step, and then stopped doing that too. "**I am not going to name the man. He cut it and he is in the room and a man who has been asked to be the person who checks his own work has not been asked, he has been nominated by a physician who is frightened of his own hands, and I am not doing it.**"

Renn Vale came down the nine steps, all nine of them, which she has not done in a fortnight, and she stopped at the bottom.

"**Then I will ask one,**" she said, "**and I will ask it of the person the room has not been thinking of, and the reason is that a person who keeps a room is the person who is allowed to be wrong in public about that room, and a man on a step and a physician in a doorway are both people who are professionally not wrong.**"

She said it to the schoolteacher.

"**You have been in that room every morning for thirty-three weeks. Was the bed a hand and a half under that stone on Friday, or was it a hand and a bit, and did you know which, and who told you.**"

"**I have not measured it since the twenty-fourth week,**" said Ilsa Norr. "**I have looked at it every morning and I have not measured it, because a person who is going to be frightened of a number stops measuring and I did that in the fourth week of the twenty-fourth and I have not gone back.**"

"**Then the number on your slate this morning came out of somewhere,**" said Renn Vale.

"**It came out of two fingers and a habit, and the habit is thirty-three weeks of my life, and I am not going to put a number on it in a room and I am not going to be told I did.**"

"**Then say what it is, and say it in the shape it is actually in,**" said Renn Vale, "**and then say what you are going to do about it, and then four people are going to take a piece of beech out of a wall and everybody who is not going to help with that is going to be standing somewhere else by the second bell.**"

"**It is a thing I have been looking at every morning for a week and a bit and a half, and I am the woman who keeps the room, and I am not going to pretend I put a rule on it. And I want it out.**"

That is how the brace came out of that wall. It took eleven minutes. It was not a thing four people did together because the shape had been agreed; it was a thing four people did because one of them was a woman who keeps the room and had said so in a room.

The anteroom had a sound in it during those eleven minutes that Marek Venn had not heard in it before. There was the crack in the beam under the table, and there was the shim coming out, and there was four sets of boots on a stone floor, and none of it was loud.

Iven Tallo took the wedges out first, and he did not do it in a hurry and he did not do it in a way anybody would have called skilled, and after the first wedge he sat down on the anteroom floor for about ten seconds and then got up again, and nobody in that room said anything about it and Dalla Sen entered it.

Marek Venn took the end that went in, with his forearm under it and his right hand doing nothing at all, and it came out about half an inch at a time. The shim came out with it, and the shim had a bruise in it on one corner that had not been there on Wednesday. The carpenter held it up in the lamplight for a long time and then put it on the trestle rather than in the heap, and said the word *tomorrow* out loud to nobody in particular, and said nothing else about it for the rest of the morning.

Bel Ordry was the fourth of the four, and the knee went.

It has gone that way since the twenty-eighth week. Past about half of the bend it stops being a knee and becomes something he negotiates with, and he got two-thirds of the way down to the anvil end of the trestle and then had to put a hand flat on the floor and finish the last third on his hands, and he did it. Nobody helped, because he had not asked, and because the man who might have asked was underneath the piece of wood at the time.

"**Say your knee out loud,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have been in this building thirty-three weeks and you have never once said it in a room, and a clerk cannot enter a man's knee that he has not heard the man say.**"

"**It does not bend past about half and it is not going to,**" said Bel Ordry, from the floor. "**It has not been going to since the twenty-eighth week. That is a fact and it is not this morning's fact and I would like it written down separately from this morning, because this morning I put a page down on a floor and everybody in the room watched me do it and I would like a person in a year to be able to tell the two things apart.**"

"**Entered, separately, in his words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that he did not ask for help and that nobody offered and that the reason nobody offered is that a man who does not ask in a room is a man who is being left alone and not a man who has been forgotten, and that this building has got that right twice in thirty-three weeks and I am entering both of them.**"

Then the anteroom's terms were said, and the word was said, and the clerk repeated it, and the room's count was restored with a piece of beech standing on a trestle four feet away from the door and not over it.

"**Say the figure,**" said Marek Venn.

Jo Verrick had been on the frame since the brace was clear of it.

"**A finger and three-quarters,**" she said. "**Which is what it was on Monday morning, and what it was for four days before that, and what it will be tomorrow. Say that out loud, all of it, including the part where nothing was gained, because a room is going to be told this afternoon that a brace was built and taken down in three days and that is not a story, it is two figures and a condition.**"

"**Then say what the two figures are, in one sentence, and do not put a word in front of them,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**A brace went into a wall on Wednesday and came out of it on Thursday, and the frame is a finger and three-quarters out of true.**"

"**And a bed under a ninth hearth stone is about a third of an inch higher than it was on Friday morning, and I have got the second of those from two fingers and a habit and not from a rule.**"

"**And I am going to write both of those things in my own book tonight, and I am not going to write what caused either of them.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said *the place where it comes out*, and Dalla Sen did not write it, and it stayed in her book from Monday in a physician's own words with a name that was a phrase and not a term.

Marek Venn stood in the middle of that room with a piece of beech four feet away from him and found that he had nothing to add. He had said on Tuesday that the two people talking about a wall would be a physician and a schoolteacher, and the clerk had entered it as a change and not as a loss, and he had said nothing about it then. He said nothing about it now. The brace was off the wall. The frame was exactly where it had been. The thing he had built in two days out of a school's own store had cost that room half an inch of floor and had cost the anteroom nothing at all, and he was going to have to be the man in this building who says both of those sentences in one breath, and there was no version of him that got to say only one of them.

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and the count was read at one hundred and four, and the board was not walked, and nothing in that anteroom was made into a lesson by anybody in it, including the man whose name is not on a page and never will be.**

"**Two today and two hundred and eighteen since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and thirty-five and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for sixteen weeks. The crew are at seventy-one.**"

"**I read those out because a brace was taken out of a wall today and I am not going to let a room put a brace in a column on the same afternoon that a woman in a room said *I want it out* and nobody argued with her, and the two of those are the whole of what happened and neither of them is a number.**"

"**The pot is nine. The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and four days and one hundred and four nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and five, and I have said in thirteen weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and I have written it, and nobody has thanked me and nobody was going to and I have stopped noticing, which is a change and not a virtue.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is that a condition was broken today and I am the clerk who wrote the condition down, and a board is the last thing a person should be holding on a day when a thing she wrote was used.**"

---

**A woman who keeps a room knelt at the ninth hearth stone on the fourth day of the thirty-third week and found its bed about a third of an inch higher than it was on Friday morning, and she said in a room that she wanted the brace out, and she said the three clauses of a condition she had written a fortnight ago, and nobody argued with her, and nobody was sent to look.**

**A physician would not put his fingers in the gap in the floor because he has spent eleven days asking this room to read a wall and not themselves, and a woman on a landing refused to ask the man who built it and asked the woman it could hurt instead, and that is the first time in thirty-three weeks that this building has got the right person to ask in the right order.**

**The frame is a finger and three-quarters out of true, exactly as it was on Monday, and a bed under a stone is a third of an inch higher than it was on Friday, and a clerk read both figures out in a room and said she was not going to write what caused either of them.**
