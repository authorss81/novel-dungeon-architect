# Chapter 206 — Four Households And A Handful

The third day of the fortieth week is a Wednesday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the forty-fourth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door. The pump was not running and had not been for four days. There is a difference between a building with a pump and a building without one, and after four days it is not in the noise any more, it is in the way nine children come down nine steps, which is that four of them came down them more slowly than they did on Friday and one of them came down them holding the rail.

Nobody in the anteroom had asked the school to do anything about that and nobody in the anteroom was going to.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twenty-sixth reading, and the same as twenty-fifth, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right. She gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked, and the clerk entered all four as four refusals and not as one, and Jo Verrick said that the reason she was still saying them after twenty-five mornings was that she had not yet found a morning where not saying one of them would be safe, and that was a thing about her and not about the wall.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell for about as long as a sentence takes to say, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and entered it.

The anteroom's terms were said at the first bell, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it. The third was six pails a day and it was false and it was said. The pump was attended, and stopped, and had been for four days.

At about half past the tenth bell a woman came up the nine steps.

She was about forty and she was not in a hurry, which took work, and she stood on the top step for a moment and looked at the anteroom floor the way a person looks at a floor they have heard a great deal about, and then she came the last three steps without being asked to and without being helped, and she stopped at the end of the trestle and she did not look at the book and she did not look at the guild's claim and she looked at Mikka Hale, who was in the kitchen-line doorway with the cloth over her arm.

"**I am not going to take a chair,**" she said. "**I have come up four sets of steps and I am going to stand, and if that is an insult in this room then it is an insult and I will go down again and say it to the gate.**"

"**Sit or stand, and the room has no opinion,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the clerk would like it entered that a person came up nine steps on a Wednesday morning on the third day of a week and did not ask for a chair, and that she said the reason out loud before anybody could have made a thing of it.**"

"**Petra Wend,**" the woman said. "**Second gate. Two brothers, their sister, and a house that only carries. I have come because there is water in rooms.**"

"**Entered, in her own words, and entered that she gave the name of the household in the order the household stands in and not in the order the people stand in,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Renn Vale, from the middle of the floor.

"**There is water in rooms,**" Petra Wend said, "**and I do not know in which rooms, and neither does anybody at the bottom of my steps, and the reason we know there is water in rooms is that a man with a wooden tally came down our steps on Friday and said nine words and went back up, and nine words is not a thing you can argue with.**"

"**What were the nine words,**" said Bel Ordry, from the top of the nine steps.

"**Water in the low rooms,**" said Petra Wend. "**That is eight. And the ninth was *yet* and I have not been able to decide all week whether it was a comfort or a threat, and I have decided it was a comfort, because a man with a tally who says *yet* is a man who thinks it stops, and if he did not think it stopped he would have said *coming*.**"

Nobody in that anteroom improved on that.

"**Then tell the room what you came for,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have come up four sets of steps and the clerk would rather have the thing than the road.**"

"**I have come up four sets of steps to ask this building to keep the water,**" said Petra Wend.

The pump did not run, and the corridor was open, and nine children had just gone out at the far door, and the room did what it does about four times in a week, which was nothing, for about two seconds.

"**The boundary cannot do that,**" said Dalla Sen, and she said it flatly and without any of the three ways she has for saying a hard thing, and she had the book in her hand and did not open it. "**And I am going to tell you why, and I am going to tell you why in the room and not at a gate, because you have come up the steps and I owe you the room.**"

"**Go on,**" said Petra Wend.

"**Keeping water is a post,**" said Dalla Sen. "**A thing that is kept has a person responsible for it, and a person responsible for a thing is entered in a column, and a column has a heading over it, and this boundary has spent thirty-nine weeks refusing to put a heading over one of its two columns and has had seven reasons given to it for refusing and has not yet accepted any of them. The first column of our plan is a permission and has been a permission since the second week. Our hours go on a line with nothing over the top of it and nobody adds them up. And a boundary that keeps water is a boundary that has a keeper of water, and a keeper of water is a post, and a post is a thing that can be found, and this building has spent five volumes explaining to offices that a person who can be found is not a keeper.**"

"**That is a very good reason,**" said Petra Wend, "**and I have four sets of steps and no water and I am not going to pretend it is a bad one. I want to say the next thing and I want to say it exactly.**"

"**Say it exactly,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Water is not a thing anybody keeps,**" said Petra Wend. "**That is the whole of what I came up here to say and I have been holding it since Friday and I did not know I was holding it until I was standing on your top step. Water is not a thing anybody keeps. It goes somewhere. It goes down a drain and it goes up a pipe and it goes through a floor into a room where four people are asleep, and it stood in a gallery in this district for nine hundred years and it is standing in a gallery now, and nobody in this building put it there and nobody at the bottom of my steps can make it go anywhere else. So the question is not whether somebody keeps it. The question is where it goes when it goes.**"

The room was quiet, and the two women in it who had said the most in the last five days had both said it in a doorway.

"**And you are right,**" said Renn Vale, from the middle of the floor, "**and I am going to say the rest of it because you have made it a room's problem and it is not a room's problem and I would rather say that here than have you say it somewhere else.**"

"**I am not going to argue with a keeper,**" said Petra Wend.

"**I know you are not,**" said Renn Vale. "**I went down to that gate on Saturday and told four people that I cannot keep six pails a day, and I have not reported what they said back to me, and the clerk entered on Sunday that I had declined to report it, and she was right to, and I was right to, and I would like it entered this morning that the person who said it to me on Saturday is standing in this room and has said it to a better audience than I am, and that she did not know it was the same sentence until about a minute ago, and that neither of us did that on purpose.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Petra Wend, "**because you have started it twice now.**"

"**The rest of it is that you are right about where it goes and you are wrong about the keeping, and both of those are true at once, and you cannot have one without the other, and I have not got a better sentence than that and I am a keeper and I set terms for a living.**"

"**I did not ask for it to be kept by anybody,**" said Petra Wend. "**I asked for it to be kept where it is. I have four households and two of them have a child in each and one of them has a person who cannot get up a stair, and I am not asking this building for a post. I am asking it not to do something. Send it away, drain it to the surface, put a door on it and call it shut, and I will not say a word. Keep it here, and do not do anything to it, and it will be a thing my steps have got instead of nothing.**"

"**That is the half of it I can give you,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the half I cannot, and I am going to say both and I am not going to be asked for the second one again.**"

"**Say it,**" said Petra Wend.

"**The half I cannot give you is that nobody in this building knows where the water is,**" said Dalla Sen. "**Not me, not him, not the woman who reads that wall every morning, not the man with the shut book, not the carpenter, and not the elected keeper. There is a gallery at the bottom of a passage off your gate and there is a claim on it lying on this table with a seal on it and the claim does not say how deep it is and does not say which floor. If I could tell you where it went to, then I would be able to tell you what I am about to ask you to do, and I am not going to be able to.**"

"**Then what are you about to ask me to do,**" said Petra Wend.

"**Nothing yet,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I have said nothing yet out loud, and I want it entered that I said *yet* and that I did not mean it kindly and that I have noticed I have said it, and that a man with a wooden tally said it on Friday and it was a comfort and it has turned into a different word in a room in three days.**"

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell with his shut book under his forearm and he had done the thing he does on about four mornings in ten, which is to listen to two people describe the same fact in two instruments and find that the two instruments agree, and to be the only person in the room who can see that they agree, and to get nothing whatever out of it.

Because he could describe it exactly. That was the whole of his trade and the whole of what was left of it: he could stand in a room and say precisely where the water was going and what it was going to do to the ninth hearth stone's bed and to the frame of a door on the common landing and to a floor with a hole in the corner of it, and he could say all of it correctly, and it would not be worth anything at all, because a woman standing at the top of his own building's steps had just been told by a clerk that this building does not put a person's name in a column, and she had said that was a good reason, and she was right, and it was a good reason, and there was water nine hundred years old standing at the bottom of a passage four days away and he could not go and look at it because there was a crew of five men on a bench and a woman at his table and a school that ran on three days in seven, and because he had said out loud in a room on Friday that he was not going to say a thing he knew until he had looked at it, and there was nothing to look at.

That was the worst part of it, and it was new. For thirty-nine weeks the thing that had been wrong with him had been that he wanted to be the man who found out. It had not previously occurred to him that there could come a morning on which he found out nothing, and could describe everything perfectly, and that the perfection would be the part that nobody could use.

Then Mikka Hale came out of the kitchen-line doorway with the cloth over her arm and did not stop at the end of the trestle.

"**I have been standing in that doorway for nine minutes listening to a woman ask this building for water,**" she said, "**and I am going to answer her, and not because I am the person who should, and I would like the clerk to enter that I answered her and that a person who is not a keeper and not a clerk answered a woman from a second gate on a Wednesday morning because there was nobody else in the room who had any of it in their hands.**"

"**Entered, in the cook's words,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**You have four households and a person who cannot get up a stair,**" said Mikka Hale. "**I have six people at my pot who are not this building's and I have had them since the thirty-fifth week and I decided that on a Friday night by myself and told this room and asked never to be asked about it, and I have not been asked about it once in a fortnight. You have been up here four minutes and you have told a room about your own and you did not ask for mine, and I want to say to you that the pot is two days and there are six people at it who are not mine either, and I have not been able to give any of it away for a fortnight because there has been nothing to give.**"

"**I am not asking for yours,**" said Petra Wend.

"**I know you are not,**" said Mikka Hale, "**and I told you anyway, and I would like it entered that I told a stranger something in a doorway that I have not told this room, and that the reason I told her is that she came four sets of steps and asked properly, and that is the whole reason, and there is no other reason and I am not going to be given credit for a better one.**"

Then Nella Bray, who had been at the anvil end with her slate under her arm the whole time and had not said anything since the foreman left on Monday:

"**I want to put a fact in this room and I want to put it in before the day is over, and it is a fact about a form and it is available to no other instrument, and it is this. That claim on that table has a second line on it and the second line has a name in it. On Friday a woman in this room said that a form which cannot be answered correctly is a form which cannot be used, and that the sheet stays on the board. That is true and it is in a clerk's book. And on Friday morning a woman in that office answered it correctly, about a room in a gallery that nobody in this building has been in, in a hand that fills them in correctly, because that is what she is for.**"

"**What are you telling us,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I am telling you that the objection stopped holding on Saturday and I entered that in the book on the day I said it, and I said the day it would stop holding was the day a piece of timber went out on a cart, and it went out on the first and the cart is four days up a passage, and I did not know that then and I have known it since Monday and I have not been asked and I am telling you now because there is a stranger at that table who has a claim with a name on it and she is owed the fact before she goes down four sets of steps.**"

"**Say the fact,**" said Petra Wend.

"**The fact is that somebody competent at a counter four days up a passage has written down a person's name against a room full of water that nobody in this building has been in,**" said Nella Bray, "**and that person is not in this building and has not agreed to it, and the person does not know yet, and when that person finds out, the letter about it is going to have his name at the top of it and this room is not going to be able to put it anywhere.**"

The anteroom was very quiet, and nine children were on the other side of nine steps, and the pot was two days, and the pump had not run for four days.

At the sixth bell Dalla Sen read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a room which has been asked for something it cannot give is owed its own three numbers before the day is over, and that the numbers are not a comfort and she was going to say so.

"**Two today and three hundred and seventeen since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and thirty-four and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty-one days and one hundred and fifty-one nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and fifty-two, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I read it out on a Wednesday because a woman came up nine steps this morning and I wanted the room to have three things of its own before she went back down them.**"

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a person from a second gate was standing in the room and a board is a column and a column is a thing this boundary has spent thirty-nine weeks not doing in front of anybody it has not asked.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman rose on Tuesday and named three people he could not ask and did not ask, and a question about a wall is still owed to those five men and the reason has to be said in a room before the asking and not on the morning of it.

The pot is two days, the seam gave nothing, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a woman from that gate was standing in this anteroom at the seventh bell with four sets of steps in her legs and a sentence in her mouth that two people in this room had said first in their own ways, and this boundary had given her nothing and told her why in a room instead of at a gate, and the reason it had told her in a room was that she had come up the steps.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and a trestle with a book on it and a guild's claim on it with a line of chalk through its fourth line and a name in its second line, and a pump that had not run for four days, and a pot that was two days, and a woman from the second gate at the end of the trestle who had asked this building to do nothing to a room full of water and had been told that this building could not promise to do nothing about a thing it did not know the address of, and who was going to go back down nine steps that afternoon and would not be thanked for having come and had not expected to be.
