# Chapter 135 — Two Good Cases

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the twenty-sixth week, a Friday, and the school ran for the sixteenth morning, and two people came up the middle passage from the same office and made the same case about the same room from opposite ends of it, and neither of them was a villain and one of them was right.**

**Both of them sat through the charter being read out against the original, clause by clause, because that is the practice in this building, and clause four is about one office, and the two of them are not that office.**

They came up together, which was itself the thing nobody had planned, because a woman had said in a room six weeks ago that she was going to carry this boundary's hole up the passage and that there was no version of that which did not end with her writing a procedure of her own.

Hollis Ashe put her tally on the table and left it there, and said the thing she had said the first time before she said anything else, and then Pettin Rahl said his, and then they stood on opposite sides of the anteroom like two men who had been sent to look at the same wall by an office that had not told them there was another man coming.

"**Hollis Ashe. Stonelight Compact, route inspection. Forty-one. Sixteen years. My permit covers a line of stations on the surface above the Cinder Steps and the approach to it.**"

"**Pettin Rahl. Stonelight Compact, continuity. Fifty-two. Twenty-six years. I am not on that line of stations and I have not been on it for eleven years and I came up this passage because I was told there is a boundary under it with a room in it that was shut and opened and a floor with a hole in it, and I am here about the floor.**"

Bel Ordry did not put the charter on the table. He read it out of his coat, standing, and he said the reason first because that is what he does.

"**Say the reason out loud before you open it. The reason is that this boundary has a charter and no name on it and eight clauses, and clause four says this office may see this boundary's plan when this office asks, in a room, to a person. And clause four says the Cartwright office."

"**And neither of you is the Cartwright office, and this boundary has no procedure for you, and it has not written one, and I am not going to write one for a man standing in my anteroom in a bad month, and I would like all three of those said out loud before anybody in this room starts being clever about it.**"

"**Then we will take the room,**" said Hollis Ashe, "**and the answer to the third thing is correct and I would like it on the record that I heard it said and did not argue with it.**"

"**Clause four,**" said Renn Vale.

---

**She read it out of the keeper's hand and Bel Ordry read the copy out after her and they were the same, and it took two minutes, and neither of the two people from the surface asked to see the page.**

"**The office may see the plan and the boundary's own record when the office asks, in a room, to a person, and not otherwise, and the office may not hold a copy, and the office may not name a holder of a route in this Hearthhold, and any question the office has is asked of a person and answered in a room.**"

"**That is a good clause,**" said Hollis Ashe. "**It is genuinely a good clause and I want to say so before I say anything else about it, because in nine years I have read four charters written by people under a floor and three of them were a sentence with a name in it."

"**And it names one office. And I am not that office. And my office is the one that has a line on a form with a space in it for the person who keeps a room, and the space has had something in it since before I came up this passage."

"**I have written the procedure. I told this room I would and I have. It is four pages and it does not name a person and it does not name a route, and it is the only instrument any body has produced in six weeks that this boundary could put on a table without a person getting picked up by it.**"

"**Say what is in it,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Three things. One: that a boundary may publish, for anybody who asks in a room, a statement of what each of its rooms is permitted to hold and what condition each of those rooms is under, in a form with no name on it. Two: that the boundary may decline to show the document itself while showing the statement, and may say no to a person who wants the document, and that saying no is not a refusal to answer. Three: that a person from another office may be present while the statement is read, and may ask, and may be told no, and that the whole of it is not a thing that is given to the office and kept."

"**And I want to say the fourth thing and it is the one I have not written down. I have written a procedure that assumes the boundary wants to be looked at. I could not write a procedure for a boundary that does not want to be looked at, because there is nothing in one.**"

---

**A man with a floor in his head said the thing this boundary has not got an answer to, and it was true, and nobody in the anteroom argued with him, and a schoolteacher told a room from another office that it had a count and that the count was not for them.**

"**I am going to be quick and I am going to be plain and I am not going to be talked out of it and I am not going to be rude, because the woman standing next to me is not rude and I have worked with her for eleven years off and on and I do not think she would be here if I were going to be."

"**Your page has a number in the first column. It has had one for twenty-six weeks. I have seen your clerk's own book and I am told the number is a permitted occupancy and not a count of anybody, and I believe that, and it does not help."

"**Because a permitted occupancy is a number that says how many bodies the room may hold, and every office that has ever looked at a room has looked at that number as though it said how many bodies are in it, and it is going to keep happening, and it is going to happen the first time somebody from my side writes it in a cell in the wrong column."

"**And I have been on the other side of that. I have stood in a room on the Rim side of this federation whose page said six, and there were thirty-one people in it, and there was a stamped and dated and correctly certified clearance on that page from a competent man, and the clearance was right, and the survey was right, and I have been the reason a room with thirty-one people in it kept a number on it. There were four dead. I was not the cause and I have never said I was, and I have not been able to walk into a room like that for twenty-six years without looking at the first column first."

"**So here is my case and it is one sentence and it is the only thing I have come up this passage to say."

"**A number in a column that nobody has counted is not a safety measure. It is a number that has to be believed, and the day it is believed instead of counted is the day a room fills, and a room that fills past what it may hold does not fail loudly. It fails with a child.**"

For a while the anteroom said nothing, and the pump got three strokes in, and a schoolteacher on the fourth step said the answer and it was the best answer anybody in that room had.

"**I have a count,**" said Ilsa Norr.

She came down one step, which she does not do, and she stood in a room's width of nothing between the school door and the kitchen line, and she said it to Pettin Rahl and not to anybody else.

"**I counted my own children out of that room on the Monday morning at the strokes, and I read the number out at both ends of a corridor, and a clerk wrote it in a book in her own hand and I said yes to the reading, and the number and the page are the same number and they are not the same kind of thing and I did that on purpose on the Friday before it and I would do it again."

"**And it is a count. It is not a permission. It is not on the page, it is not for a page, and I wrote a clause in the teacher's own hand on the day I closed that room which says the number is not a capacity and not a permission and not a number anybody may use for anything else ever."

"**So the thing you want is in this building. It is in a clerk's book, and the person who made it is standing in front of you, and it cannot be given to you, and I would like you to hear the reason, and the reason is not caution. The reason is that a count of that kind is only true of a moment, and a moment is the only thing about a room that is worth a number, and if a number of that kind can be carried up a passage and put in a cell then in a year somebody will carry a number of that kind up a passage to say that a room was safe, and it will be true of a moment, and the moment will be the wrong one."

"**And I am not going to tell you what the number was. You did not ask me. I want that entered, because a man came up a passage to find out what was in a room and this room did not tell him, and I would like that in the book in the shape of a refusal and not in the shape of a courtesy.**"

"**Then that is my problem,**" said Pettin Rahl, and he said it without heat, and he wrote for about a minute and then read what he had written out loud in the room, which was the thing that made him a person and not an office.

"**I have written: the boundary keeps a permitted occupancy on a public plan and will not give a count, and gives a count when the room is emptying and calls it a count and not a permission, and the count is not on the plan. That is what I have. And I am going to carry that up the passage, and I am going to say that I have read a plan with twelve numbers in it and not one of them counted by anybody, and my office will want a regime for that, and a regime is not a threat, and I want that said in this room by me before anybody else says it here.**"

---

**One more time the physician was asked, in front of two people from a surface federation, whether a plan with a number in it was acceptable, and he gave the same answer he gave on Monday and added one sentence to it.**

"**The same answer, and I am not going to improve it because I said on Monday that I would not answer that with a feeling and I am a man who keeps his own sentences."

"**And I will add one thing in front of strangers, because you are two strangers and this is a room and a room is where a thing like this goes. A plan with a number in it and nobody's name against it is a plan about a building. A plan with a number in it and a name against it is a claim about a person. I will not sign either, and I will sign a condition, and I would like it entered that a physician has now been asked that question twice in four days by his own building and once by a federation and has given the same answer three times.**"

---

**By the sixth bell the clerk had read the six things and the day's number and the count, and the corridor was open behind her and the school had run a bell at the far end of it, and two people from a surface federation were in the room.**

"**Three today and a hundred and twenty-one since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and thirty-eight and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half."

"**The pot is ten. The jars are two. The water is at the waist and the waist is holding and has never been measured."

"**The count. Wen Pask is seventy days and seventy nights into a count that is his own, and I said on the second day of this month that I would write it every day and I am writing it, and I am going to enter that two people from an office four days up a road have now heard me say it, and that neither of them said a kind thing about it, and that I am glad, and that is a strange thing for a clerk to be glad about and I am entering it because I said I would not improve my own arithmetic.**"

Bel Ordry said the last of it to two people from another office at the seventh bell, and he said it standing, with the plan still in his coat where it had been all day.

"**You have both been in this room all day and the answer to what you came for is on my chest."

"**The plan does not leave this building. The charter does not leave this building. The first column stays in the coat and the fourth column stays empty and there is a reason in two hands on the page that I have read out to you this afternoon, and the reason is that a column with a heading on it is a column a man at a counter can ask for, and I would like a federation to want to look at this boundary more than I would like a federation to be able to take it."

"**I have said that out loud and I have said it to two people from an office I have no procedure for, and if that is a hole then it is the hole and you have now seen it and you may go up the passage and put it in a file."

"**And the other half, which I am going to say because a man who only says the half that flatters him is the man who recommended a room be shut a fortnight ago. The one document in this Hearthhold that says what a room may hold is in a coat, and a coat is not a system, and I have said that sentence out loud in this room and I have been saying it for six weeks and I am still the only person who has to say it.**"

"**That is a bad answer,**" said Hollis Ashe, "**and it is the correct one, and I am going to write it in my four pages in those words, and I would like the clerk to know that I am going to write it in a way that a room can be looked at without being taken.**"

Nobody asked her to.

**In a coat at the seventh bell was the plan, and the charter was on a scrub board with an office's number on it and no name anywhere, and the anteroom above all of it was the room it had been for twenty-six weeks with a broken condition in it that a clerk had entered and not repaired, and the water was at the waist on ground that three people have never measured, and a corridor full of flags and a spent line was holding at the low end, and a man of twenty-two was seventy days into a count that is his own.**
