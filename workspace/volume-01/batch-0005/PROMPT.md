## Batch goal

This batch carries the Volume 01 climax and then resolves the volume as a **second and deeper Hearthhold threshold**: not an arrival, because the crew arrived in Chapters 9–10, but a route the crew can keep, maintain, and hand over. Two jobs, in this order.

**Job one, Chapters 41–45: earn the materials and the air plan.** The next step is named and it is not the stair. It is the **lower pump-gallery cache** — the screw jack, the pry bar, and the last of the bulk tools — in a room that has been under water for six weeks, in a building whose only way to draw a sump down is a pump with a scored bronze seat and no spare, and which sits in the stair's pressure line. Nera will write an air plan for that gallery only when a person has asked to go in it and has said what they are going in there for. The school corner floor must be opened and traced first, because it is a mouth and the plan's own second step is refused. The pump seat must survive the intervals the drawing-down needs, and Jo's estimate of about four cleanings has no slack in it.

**Job two, Chapters 46–49: the stair.** An ordinary timber brace, rope anchors, Nera's air guidance, Iven's craft, and Leena's survey marks make a two-way route. Marek does not defeat the faultform by hitting it; he changes the load and gives the stair a different function to repeat. It costs a crew member, it forces the crew to abandon salvage and equipment, and Marek's hand cannot do fine work so he depends on another person's readings.

**Job three, Chapter 50: the hand-over.** The route is open for people and air, not for a cart and not for a private guild claim. Nera and the Hearthhold residents insist on an air and occupancy check before anyone crosses, and the check is passed by the people who will live with it rather than by the crew that built it. The wrapped empty-seat plate travels with them and is still unexplained. The volume ends as bounded maintainers on a maintenance obligation, not as owners of a way home.

## Required movement

Ten chapters, ending the volume. The shape below is a plan, not a cage; adjust the order if the chapters earn a better one, but every element must be present and Chapter 50 must be a hand-over rather than a victory speech.

- **Chapters 41–42 — the mouth and the air plan.** The school corner floor is opened and traced with Ilsa's and Mikka's terms in force and the school not running. The grit is cleared, the void is found, the fall or the shut is decided by the people who keep the floor, and the finding is reported to them in their own words — which is the batch's named review trigger. In parallel or immediately after, Nera writes the air plan for the lower pump gallery, and the writing of it is a scene, not a summary.
- **Chapters 42–44 — the cache.** Drawing a flooded gallery down needs intervals a scored seat may not survive, and a named air plan nobody has ever made. What comes up out of the water is the jack and the pry bar and the last of the bulk tools, and what the crew has to leave behind is the thing the batch's scarcity has been building toward. This is where a fifth cleaning is paid for, or the seat degrades visibly, and where Tams's hand and the Oren-and-Mikka lever arrangement earn their place.
- **Chapter 44 or 45 — the crew's own reckoning.** Before the climax, the crew has to say out loud what the stair project is going to cost in the currency the record uses, and one of them has to say the sentence about leaving. Nobody has said it yet and the batch needs it.
- **Chapters 46–47 — the brace.** An ordinary timber brace, rope anchors, and a load that is met from a floor that already exists. Iven's craft, Oren's bearing work, Leena's survey, Nera's air, and Jo's board under load. The change is to the load and the function the stair repeats, not a blow and not a victory. Someone has to be holding something when the function changes, and that person is chosen by craft, not by luck.
- **Chapter 48 — the cost.** The route-capacity event. Too many people, one route, a school that runs five days, a door that is two feet eight inches, four bearers, and four minutes. **Oren dies here**, permanently, doing the thing he is good at, and the crew abandons salvage and equipment. Nobody revives him, nobody converts him, and the volume does not pretend.
- **Chapter 49 — the route is open, for people and air.** Not for a cart. Not for a guild claim. The abandonment of the equipment is on the page with its reason.
- **Chapter 50 — the second threshold.** A maintained, handed-over route. Nera's air and occupancy check is passed by the people who will live with it, not by the crew. The crew ends as bounded maintainers on a maintenance obligation. The wrapped plate travels and is still unexplained. The route is also the first route somebody else can close. Resolve the volume's immediate promise — the group gets out of the Cinder Steps and reaches and then *keeps* a threshold — and do not resolve the Lattice, the stair's full history, the plate, the guild's claim, or the stair's long-term condition.
