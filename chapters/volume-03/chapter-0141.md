# Chapter 141 — A Bundle And A Name In A Space

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the twenty-eighth week, a Monday, and the school did not run, and the market day was the sixth day of the week before and it came and went, and a courier came back up the middle passage with what it had cost, and a condition on a room is checked three mornings in seven and four mornings in seven it is not checked at all.**

**Nothing in this Hearthhold got out of it. One bundle did not. And a plan of rooms is not a network of rooms, and the first thing that is missing from one is not a design.**

Talla Roke came up at the second bell with her three behind her and the tally in her hand, and she said what she was before anybody asked her, because that is now what she does in this anteroom and she has stopped apologising for it.

"**I was on that road on the sixth day and I did not go through the gate and I stood at it for most of the afternoon with a woman who is competent and is doing her job, and I am going to tell you what happened in the order it happened, and the clerk can put it in her book in that order because the order is the whole of it."

"**There is a board at that gate and there has been a board at that gate for longer than anybody can remember, and it has seven lines on it, and two of them are for a name and for the person responsible for the load, and until there was water on a road I do not go along, nobody at that gate ever had a reason to touch it."

"**And then there was water, and a clerk put a hand on it, and the hand says that if you want to go along that road with more than what you are carrying then those two lines get filled in. No order. No notice. Nothing with a date on it. I have said all of that in this room before and I am not going to say it smaller."

"**And here is the part I came up this passage to say. On the sixth day I watched a man from this Hearthhold go up to that counter with a bundle on his shoulder, and he is one of ours, and he is not one of the five of you, and the clerk asked him for his name."

"**And he did not give it. Not because he is brave. Because a woman at this end told him what a name on a page about a load is for, and he believed her, and he is not a courier and he has no mark and he has nothing of mine to stand on. So he stood at a counter on a market day with a bundle on his shoulder and did not say his name, and the clerk waited, and he did not say it, and then she said the road was closed to him, and he came home."

"**And the bundle is still in your anteroom. And I want somebody in this room to understand that that is not a story about a man being stopped at a gate. It is a bundle, and it is the only thing in this building that belongs to nobody, and it is going to be in that anteroom at the end of this month, and at the end of the next one."

Nobody in that anteroom said a kind thing about it, and the cook, who has been in this building twenty-eight weeks and has a pot at ten days, said the sentence that made it a fact instead of a grievance.

"**It is a bundle and a bedroll and a belt and a half of bread he did not eat," said Mikka Hale. "**And I have been putting his share of the pot back into the pot since the fourth day of the twenty-fifth week and I would like that entered, because I did it on purpose and I did not ask anybody, and it is the reason the pot is at ten and not at eleven."

---

**Then the woman with the rule was asked a question in a room and she answered it before anybody had asked it, and it was the same rule and it was still right and it had got worse.**

Renn Vale had the slate on her knee on the landing and she was not reading it. She said it into the room and Dalla Sen's chalk was already moving.

"**I wrote a condition on the room at the kitchen-line end in my own hand in the last week of the twenty-seventh and it is on the tenth row of a public plan with an objection written above it in a different hand and a man's proposal under both, and the condition is that the strip under that ninth hearth stone is said out loud, in a room, every morning this room is used, by a person who is not named on that page and is not named anywhere in this building's record of who keeps what."

"**And I am going to say the half of it against myself before anybody else in this room finds it, because I found it on Monday morning and I have been sitting on this landing since the first bell."

"**A room is used three days in seven. The condition is checked every morning the room is used. So the condition is checked three mornings in seven, and there are four mornings in seven when nobody walks into that room and nobody puts a hand on that bed and nobody says the sentence out loud in a room, and a strip of crate board under a stone that has gone over a hand's width and has a hole under it the width of Jo Verrick's hand does not know what day it is."

"**I wrote the condition. I am the person who writes conditions on that room. And I wrote one with a hole in it, in my own hand, in a room with nine children in it three days in seven, and I did not see the hole in it for three weeks."

"**And I am not going to fix it by adding a day, because a day is a number and I have told this room for twenty-eight weeks that a condition you can satisfy on a schedule is a date with a number on it, and I am not going to be caught out of my own sentence in the month I say it."

"**So the answer is this and I would like the clerk to read it against the original and compare the two. The strip is said out loud at the first bell every morning the room is used, by a person, in a room, in front of nobody at all if there is nobody in it. And on the four mornings a week when that room is shut, the person who keeps that room says the same sentence out loud in a room in this building with the room empty."

"**That costs nothing and it is the answer, and I know what it is and I am going to say what it is out loud, because a condition that costs nothing is a condition that a person in a bad month will decide is not worth saying. It is four sentences a week, in an empty room, to a building, about a stone nobody is standing near. That is the whole of it. It is stupid and it is cheap and it is the only version of this that does not put a day of that school on a ledger and take it off again."

"**And I am not writing it as a post, because I have spent four weeks watching a man in this building refuse to head a column and I am not going to hand him a reason to head this one."

Ilsa Norr came down one step from the fourth, which she does not do, and she did not agree to it and did not refuse it.

"**Say the second half of it back to me," she said, "**and then say this. My four children crossed a waist on the Friday before last on ground that three people have agreed for twenty-eight weeks not to measure, and one of them has a bone that has moved since a physician put it there, and two people in this building have not said out loud that it has not moved. So do not tell me the room should be shut for four mornings in seven as though the room were the thing I am frightened of."

"**The room has nine hearth stones in it and one of them is propped on a piece of board and it is the only room in this building with light in it, and I am not going to have it shut for four days because a woman on a landing found a hole in her own sentence and would like it mended before she is the one who has to say so."

"**Put it in front of me on Thursday with the number of mornings in it and I will answer it. Not today, and not because I am refusing it."

Dalla Sen read the condition back out of the book, twice, and the two readings were the same, and the clerk entered that the woman who wrote it had found a hole in it herself and had said so before anybody asked, and entered that the person who keeps the room has not answered it and will be asked on the fourth day of this week.

---

**Then somebody said the thing the whole morning had been walking toward, and it was said as an ordinary sentence and it was the largest thing said in this building in four weeks.**

Bel Ordry had the plan in four parts on the anteroom table and nobody had touched it, and Marek Venn stood at the step with his right hand flat on the back of the bench, which is what that hand does now, and he said the thing first, which he has not done in this room in three weeks.

"**You keep saying rooms. Twelve of them, and I have been in this building twenty-eight weeks and I have never once heard the word *network* said out loud by anybody in it, and I have been waiting to see whether it was going to be somebody else's word or ours, and it turns out it is going to be mine, so I would rather say it here than have it arrive in a month from a woman from a federation."

"**A plan of rooms is a list of places. You can stand in a corridor and empty a room into the room next to it, and then that room is full, and the room next to it is a room with a person in charge of a number that nobody counted, and I have watched that happen in fifty minutes on a Thursday in this building and it was the best thing anybody in this Hearthhold has done in four weeks."

"**And it is not a network. It is one room with a queue. A network is several rooms, each of which can be emptied into the next, so that the fourth one is not full and the third one is not full and the second one is not full, and each of them has its own count and its own person who keeps it, and the person who keeps each one of them writes the change to their own room and nobody else writes it for them."

"**That is the whole of the design. It is four rooms and a floor between them, and it is not a route and it is not a hole, and it does not need a mark, and it does not need me. It needs a person in each room who can say how many that room is holding and who writes down what they do."

"**And the first thing that is missing from it is not the design. It is a pair of hands and a crew that is not on a rota."

Renn Vale said the half of that which was against it, and she said it before she agreed with any of it.

"**You have described a thing with four rooms in it and one crossing in the middle of it, and the crossing is me, and the crossing is four at a time for four minutes in the pump's window, and the crossing is a waist with water standing on the fill on it, and the fill has moved three times and has never been measured by three people on purpose."

"**So the network you have just described is four rooms and a bottleneck, and the bottleneck is me, and I am not going to be a bottleneck for thirty people, and I will tell you now what I will do and what I will not do so that nobody has to find it out in a corridor."

"**I will open the waist four at a time, in the pump's window, with a bearer at the near lip, for as long as the pump runs. I will not open it with a full corridor of people in it at one time while there is water on that fill. And I will not open it at all on a day anybody at this end tells me somebody is going to be at the low end of that corridor, because the low end is where the water comes up first and I have got a page in a clerk's book that has been blank for twenty-eight weeks about exactly that and I am not the woman who is going to put a person in a blank space to make it true."

"**And the anteroom takes ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and the fifth of its five terms has been broken since the middle of last month, and if your network is going to end up in my anteroom then the network has a number in it and I want to see the number and I want to see who wrote it."

Bel Ordry put his hand flat on the date in the top corner of the plan and did not say the date.

"**Then say what you want on it," he said, "**and I will tell you now that nobody in this building is going to sign it, and I told the room that on the Monday of the week before last and it is still true, and a plan that cannot be signed is a plan that can be argued with, and I would rather be argued with than sent for."

---

**At the sixth bell the board was walked, because the count was in the room and the clerk had read a bundle out of a courier's mouth and both of those had happened in the same hour and a board is walked by a person who has nothing else on them.**

Tams Quill had the chalk in his left hand and his right one open and flat in his lap, and he read both columns, and named no day, and gave no reason for not naming one.

"**Three today and a hundred and forty since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and fifty-seven and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half and have not moved since the second day of the twenty-sixth week."

"**Fifteen entries. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row and a plan. Nine in the ordinary column and six in the one with nothing over the top of it."

"**And the column is not headed. Eighth batch. I am not going to give the reason today because I have not got a better one than the one I gave on Tuesday and I would like somebody in this room to understand that a man who has eight reasons and gives the seventh one is not being careful, he is being lazy, and the difference has not cost anybody anything yet."

"**And the day it does is the day a heading is a thing you can be judged by. A null is a reading that found nothing. There are eleven of them in that column and every one of them is a thing somebody in this building was paid to go and look for and did not find, and under a heading they stop being eleven nothings and they become eleven findings. And a finding is a thing a person is responsible for. So the day this column has a heading on it is the day somebody in this room can be called in front of a clerk and asked what they found, and what they did not find, and why they did not write it down."

"**That is the reason. It is worse than the last one and it is not about a word and it is not about a doorway. It is that a heading is a place to keep the person who works under it."

Dalla Sen read the fifteen entries out and read her own reading of them back and entered that the count had given the eighth reason and had said he had seven better ones, and entered that she was not going to improve on it either.

"**And the count. Wen Pask is seventy-six days and seventy-six nights into a count that is his own, and I said on the second day of last month that it was not going to stop and I have written it every day since and I am writing it now, and I am entering that a bundle is in this anteroom that has been in it since the fourth day of the twenty-fifth week, and I am not joining the two numbers, and I have been asked not to by nobody, which is the first time I have been able to say that."

---

**In the anteroom, on the anteroom table, in a coat four hands down a wall in a room nobody was in, was a plan on butcher's paper in four parts with a date in the top corner and no name anywhere, and twelve rows, and a first column that had never held a count of anybody, and a second column with no number and no day on any of the twelve, and a third column with eleven lines filled and one empty that the man who keeps it will not name, and a fourth column empty and unheaded with a reason on it in two hands and a modal struck out of one of them; and one row with three hands on it and an objection written above a proposal.**

**A corridor lay shut for the second time in seven days, and a room at the far end of it was standing open with a stone propped on a piece of board and a condition on it that nobody had looked at for four mornings, and a woman who had found the hole in her own sentence had asked a schoolteacher a question she did not intend to answer this week.

**At the low end of that corridor there were two flags and a length of the crew's own spent line over a hole with no grate in it, and a page in a clerk's book about that place had been blank for twenty-eight weeks with three conditions on it, and the third of them says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true, and nobody has been asked and nobody is going to be.

**A woman who runs a road for people who do not have a permit sat on an anteroom step with a wooden tally in her hand and three people of her own behind her and said that the first thing this Hearthhold is short of is not a plan.
