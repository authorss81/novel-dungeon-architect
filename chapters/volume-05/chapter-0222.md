# Chapter 222 — The Door, And Two Answers

The fifth day of the forty-second week is a Friday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the fifty-first morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door, and at the second bell two people in this anteroom picked up the ends of a bar and put their shoulders under a stone drum that had been lying on a trestle since Thursday.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the forty-second reading, and the same as forty-one, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, in the same words for the sixth time, and the clerk entered it and entered that it was the same sentence.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is twenty-seven.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it is right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and on the morning a nine-hundred-year-old door is shut in this building the count of the people it may hold at one time did not move by a quarter of an inch, and the clerk entered that because she wanted it on the page next to the door. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The sixth was the pump, and it ran, and there is a door in this building that is shut for the first time since anybody alive was born.

Four things were closed in about nine minutes by four people who were not asked to do any of them, and the broken louvre over the kitchen line was stuffed with a sailcloth and a wedge, and the low vent off the corner was closed with a board and a piece of the crew's spent line, and the seam vent off the far end was closed with a flag out of a floor that has a hole in it, and the riser at the top of the nine steps was closed and held, and the clerk entered all four as four decisions and entered that this room had not been asked for any of them and had not stopped any of them, which is the second time in a week this room has not stopped a competent person and the first time it has watched itself do it.

Then two people went down the wet stair and came up with the windlass between them and set it in the channel, and it went across in about four minutes, and the man at the end of the trestle was right about the third minute.

Nobody in the anteroom said anything at all while it happened, and the pump was between strokes, and at about the fourth minute of the four there was a sound in the room that was not the door and was not the pump.

"**Do not tell me that,**" said Nera Oduya, from the second of her three steps, and she was not looking at the door.

"**I have not said it,**" said Marek Venn, and she turned round.

"**You have written it on your face and it is a face I have been reading for six weeks,**" Nera Oduya said, "**and I am telling you not to tell me because of what I am about to have to say, and not because of you.**"

"**Then say it,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**That is not my plan working,**", from the second of her three steps. "**That is my plan not being able to tell me whether it is working. A chamber with four fathoms of water in it pushes the air in front of it, and if the far side of that door is shut then everything it pushes has one way to go, and the one way it goes is up the shaft that serves this anteroom and the room at the kitchen-line end, and those two rooms should be breathing harder than they have breathed in nineteen weeks.**"

She came down all three of her steps, which she does perhaps twice in five volumes.

"**They are not,**" she said. "**They are breathing exactly as they were breathing an hour ago. I have been down on that stair and put my hand on the shaft mouth and it is dead. Nothing is coming up it. My plan is either the best plan anybody in this building has heard in forty-one weeks or it is doing nothing whatsoever, and I cannot tell you which, and I have told you both of those out loud in a room, and I would like the clerk to enter the second one as a finding and not as a reason.**"

"**Entered as a finding,**", and she did not look up.

"**Then say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I have told you since the second day of the forty-first week that a man who stands under an air shaft with a lamp and cannot feel anything cannot certify what is coming up it, and that I was not talking about a lamp, I was talking about me,**" Nera Oduya said. "**I can tell you the air in this room is fit to be breathed. I have been certifying that every morning for six weeks and I have never once been able to tell you where it came from, and this morning I needed to know where it came from and I cannot, and I am the only person in this building who can be asked about it and I have just told you I have nothing.**"

The anteroom was very quiet, and the pump ran, and the four things that had been closed stayed closed.

"**Then this room is going to say the thing out loud,**" said Mero Senn, from the frame of the far door with his bag against the wall where it goes, "**and I am going to say it to the man at the end of the trestle and not to the room, and I am going to say why I am saying it to him and not to the room, and I have thought about that for about ten minutes and I have got it.**"

"**Say the reason first,**", and her voice was not different at all.

"**Because he is the only person in this room who can build the first answer and he is the only person in this room who has spent nine weeks in this building asking to be given something and refusing it when it was given,**" Mero Senn said. "**Everything else in this building goes through a door or a person. What I am about to say goes through him, and if I say it to the room he will hear it as the room asking him to be useful, and I have watched that man be asked to be useful four times in five volumes and take it as a wound every time.**"

He came the two steps into the room, which he does not do.

"**There are two answers and there is no third and I have been looking for one since Friday afternoon and I want the clerk to enter that I looked.**"

"**Say the two,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**One. We reopen the gallery.**" Mero Senn turned round and looked at the door, at the bottom of the wet stair, at nine steps and a passage and a chamber with four fathoms of water in it. "**Which means the far side of that pressure door is open, which means nine men who went down there at the fifth bell yesterday are on the far side of an open door in a room with four fathoms of water in it, and the passage they are in is a passage nobody in this building has ever walked, and a physician has never been in, and there is no air plan for it and no figure of anybody in it. It is cheap. It is the cheapest thing anybody in this room could do this morning, and I am telling you that first so that nobody in here tells me afterwards that I made it sound worse than it is.**"

"**And two,**", from the middle of the floor.

"**Two. We accept a controlled loss.**" Mero Senn put his hand flat on the back of a chair, which is a thing he has never done in this room. "**The turn-over is not finished. The turn-over will not be finished. That pump on the far side has not been turned over in nine years and it is not going to be turned over in this plan and this building has no authority on that line and no way to reach it. The chamber beside it keeps filling. The door stays shut, the four protected rooms go on breathing whatever they are breathing this morning, and the thing that is lost is a turn-over and the work of nine people who came four days to do it, and nobody drowns and nobody is hurt and the loss is real.**"

He turned back to the room.

"**Those are two. One of them costs a person in a passage nobody has measured. The other one costs a man his work. I have been in this building nine weeks and I have not named an injured person once, and the reason I have not is that there is not one, and that is the correct condition for me to be standing here.**"

"**Then say the half against the whole thing,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I have given you two and told you there is no third, and that a physician who says there is no third in a room is a physician who has decided for everybody in it what can be thought,**" Mero Senn said. "**So here is the rest of it. I have not decided it. I have found that the third is not available to me, and I have looked for it in one direction only, and the direction is this: a third answer is a plan with nobody named in it. A plan where the loss goes somewhere nobody can point at, or where the risk is carried by a person who has not been asked, or where we shut the door and find out later what was on the far side of it and go and look. That is the third answer. It is available at any hour of any day in this building, and it is the one everybody in every room I have worked in for nine years reaches for, and it is the one I have come to the end of not being able to recommend, because it is a plan where the man at the end of the trestle is the plan.**"

"**Entered, in the physician's words, and entered that he looked for a third and did not find one, and entered that nobody asked him to,**", with her hand near the book.

Nobody in that anteroom said anything, and the pump ran, and the four things that had been closed stayed closed, and somewhere eleven feet below a room where nine children had been taught that morning there were four fathoms of water that nobody in this building could now reach.

"**Then I am going to ask the one question in this room that nobody has asked him,**" said Seth Wray, from the kitchen-line doorway, "**and I am going to ask it before the sixth bell and not on Monday, because I have been in this building a fortnight and I am not going to be a man who waits for a room to be in the right mood.**"

"**Ask it,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**Which of the two would you do,**" said Seth Wray, "**and I am asking you as a man who has to stop work and not as a room, and if you tell me it is not my business then you are the first one in nine years who has been straight with me and I would like the clerk to hear it either way.**"

Mero Senn did not answer for about as long as the pump takes to make three strokes.

"**It is not my business,**" he said, "**and the reason is that both of those are about what a room does and one of them is about what a room is prepared to say out loud, and I am a man who can stop the work and I cannot decide what a room is prepared to say, and if I start deciding that then the first thing I lose is the only thing I have, which is that when I say stop, a room believes it.**"

"**Then that is the first straight answer I have had off a physician in twenty-three years,**" said Seth Wray, "**and I am going to give you the other half of mine so that this room has both halves and not just mine, and I would like it entered that I said it before anybody asked me whether I was going to.**"

He came the whole way to the trestle, which he has not done since the second day of the forty-first week.

"**I am not going to be the man who picks either, and I want to be allowed to say why, because if I do not say it now I will say it on Monday in a worse mood. The first of those two costs a person, and the second of those two costs me, and I can see from here that a room is going to pick the second one in about four seconds because it is the one that does not have anybody in it. And I am telling you now that if that is what happens, then the second one is not a loss that this building accepted. It is a loss that this building handed to a man who came four days for it, and those are two different instruments, and I would like somebody in a year to be able to tell which of the two we did.**"

"**Then say the half against that,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**The half against is that I have no standing here, that nine men of mine are asleep in a room at the kitchen-line end and a man I have never met wrote a paper that put them in a gallery, and that a man with a clerk's coat on has just been given the whole of this and I have been given two lines of it,**", and he did not put the slate down. "**So my half against is that I am the only man in this room with nothing at the end of it, and that I have said all of this out loud anyway, and that a foreman who says a thing out loud in a room is not a person being owed, he is a person who has spent the room's afternoon.**"

"**Entered, in a foreman's words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that the first half of what he said is a sentence this room may want to keep and the second half is why it should not,**", and she had the book shut.

"**And one more thing in my own mouth before the day closes,**" said Renn Vale, and she had not moved from the middle of the floor all afternoon. "**That door is not a way out of this building. I have been the keeper of the corridor for forty-one weeks and I know every way out of this anteroom by what it weighs and what it costs, and that door is not one of them and it is not going to become one, and if anybody in this room this evening starts calling it a second way out then I am going to stop being the keeper of it before the seventh bell and I will not give a reason and you will not get one.**"

"**Entered, in the keeper's words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that she has said that twice in five volumes about two different things and that this is the first time she has said it about a thing nobody had counted.**"

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle for the whole of it and had not moved, and what he had found out, in the order he found it out, was as follows.

First, that he had been handed two. He had been handed two and not three and not one, and the room had not asked him for anything, and the handing had been arranged so that the asking could not be mistaken for an instruction, and a woman who has spent five volumes refusing to depend on a man with a shape in his head had known enough about him to do that.

Second, and this was the part that took him about four minutes to get to, that a man who can build one of the two answers is not the man who chooses between them, and that he had spent nine weeks in a building where the two things had been the same thing. He had been the man who said no, and the saying of it had been his work, and the work had a name, and a man with a name in this building got asked things and got owed things and got put on the boundary of what was owed. He had thought he was refusing. He had not been refusing. He had been doing a trade, and the trade had a shape, and the shape had a name in it, and the name was a man with no post and a shut book.

And third, which was the one he had been turning over for the rest of the afternoon, was that he wanted it to be the other way round. He wanted the room to hand him two answers and then hand him the choosing, because a man who is handed the choosing is a man who is the man, and he had been the man for a fortnight and a batch now and it had felt like a relief and he had not once looked at the relief.

He put his hand flat on the shut book and left it there and worked out, at about the fourth bell, that a refusal is not a decision and that he had been treating them as the same instrument since the second week of this volume, and that one of them costs nothing and the other one costs a man his work, and that a person who has only ever been handed the first of those is not a person who has refused much.

At about the fourth bell a woman came up the wet stair and said that nine men had gone down at the fifth bell the previous evening and that this building had not heard one word from any of them since, and that she was not asking anybody for anything, and that she had walked down to the top of the passage at the first bell this morning and had found the line tied at the third tie and the lamp cold where it had been set down.

The clerk entered that the line was at the third tie, and entered that the boundary had given those nine a length of black cord on Saturday and no bell and no name to shout and no lamp that was any use, and had said out loud in this room on the sixth day of the forty-first week that it was not going to give them any of those, and had said it as two decisions and not as one thing, and had been right about both of them.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a room which has just been told it has two answers and no third is owed three numbers it can verify, and that a doctor who says a third answer is a plan with nobody named in it has just told a room everything about the morning except the two figures that are true.

"**Four today and three hundred and eighty-two since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and ninety-nine and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and sixty-seven days and one hundred and sixty-seven nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and sixty-eight, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for fifty-six weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in fifty-six weeks, and this morning two people in this room closed four vents in a nine-hundred-year-old building on a plan that a woman cannot check, and neither of those two things is going to be in the number.**"

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a board is a list of what this building found, and a plan is not on it, and a physician has just told a room that a third answer is a plan where one man is the plan, and a clerk is not walking that board this morning.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman has four and a half hours and has not asked, and that the work which would take those four and a half hours is on the far side of a door that was shut at the second bell this morning, and the clerk entered that she had written that sentence in the shape of a man looking at a door he cannot get through and did not write the looking down.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning for the tenth morning running, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for twenty-eight weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and four rooms in this building are breathing something at the moment and nobody in this building can say what.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a door at the bottom of a wet stair that was shut for the first time in nine hundred years and had a stone drum in it that had been lying on that table since Thursday, and a room with two answers in it and no third one, and a woman who can certify the air and not say where it came from, and a man at the end of a trestle who had been handed exactly two and had worked out, over the better part of an afternoon, that a man who is handed two is not a man who has been given anything.
