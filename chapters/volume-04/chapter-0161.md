# Chapter 161 — What A Joint Is

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the thirty-second week, a Monday, and the school did not run, and a carpenter laid out on the anteroom table what a joint is, and then named the four things this building does not have. Three of the four were metal, a kiln, and a second pair of hands.**

**Two sound wedges are on a bench waiting for a brace that has not been built. Six cut pieces of a school's own beech are beside them. A mark in a lintel has been read three ways this month by three people and has not said anybody its limit.**

The trestle was still up from the week before with the heap of offcuts at the end of it, and Iven Tallo cleared a space on the table with the back of his left hand, because the right one does not close, and laid a piece of beech flat, and laid a second across it at right angles, and knocked it with a knuckle so the room could hear that it was not fixed to anything.

"**A joint is not a pin. Everybody thinks it is a pin. A joint is two pieces agreeing to stay in the same place, and the pin is only one of the ways of making them agree.**"

"**Say the fourth way,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it before anybody in this room hears the first three and starts choosing between them.**"

"**Four ways I have used.**" He turned the crosspiece a quarter turn. "**Pegged, where you drive something through and take it out from the far side. Housed, where you cut a box in one and a tenon in the other and it holds by being the right shape. Lapped, where the two overlap and it is nailed. And tied, where you put cord round it and it holds because you made it hold.**"

The anteroom was cold at that hour in a way that had stopped being remarkable. It was the level above the waist, the corridor was shut outside, and the only air in the room came down a mouth three steps away that a woman had opened once as a vent and had never certified and would not let a child stand near. The line of chalk and the piece of string on the far wall showed at this hour, because the lamp on the ante-room table threw a shadow of the packing line that moved a finger's width across the plaster as the second bell went and the air in the building shifted. Marek had noticed it twice in an hour and had not mentioned it to anybody, and it was the first thing in ten days that had not been in his own book.

Bel Ordry said the half against it from the step without coming down. He had been on that step since the first bell with the plan shut in his coat.

"**You are describing a joint that anybody can undo.**"

"**I am describing a joint the person who put it in can undo, and nobody else, because the other three ways take a tool this building has not got.**"

"**Then say what happens when that person is asleep.**"

"**Then it stands until they are not.**" He put his palm flat on the table beside the beech. "**I want that said in this anteroom in those words before we start. A joint with no pin in it has to be a shape that stands up on its own. A shape that stands up on its own can be taken apart by a person with a knife and nothing else, and that is the reason I want to build it this way, and I am not going to be asked for it twice.**"

Marek Venn had his own book out of the inside of his coat, which he had not done in front of this room since a woman from another office read a plan out loud and got no copy of it. He opened it on his knee and held the page down with his forearm, because the other hand will not close on a spine.

"**Say the four things we do not have,**" he said, "**and then the carpenter's sentence can be argued about with a floor under it.**"

There was a stretch of the morning where the only sounds were the pump and somebody moving a trestle leg to get at a gap, and what came out of it was a list, and Dalla Sen read it back as it was given, because a list read back is the only kind this building trusts.

"**Iron. None in this Hearthhold that anybody has seen in a month, and the bar under the third step is the only thing in this building ever called iron, and it is a pry bar.**"

"**Bronze. The pump's gland seal is split and the drive key was restored, and those are the only two, and neither of them can be cut up.**"

"**A kiln. Nothing in here hot enough to draw a pin. The one fire that is not cold has not been lit in eleven weeks, and it is in a room with nine hearth stones in it and a bed a hand and a half down under one of them, and the cook has been asked about it seven times and has refused seven times.**"

"**And a second pair of hands.**" He put his right hand flat on the table, open, and left it there. "**Four things. Three are materials. One is people, and I have put that one last, because I have been in this building thirty-two weeks and I have never once put the people last.**"

"**Enter the four,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that he put the one about people last, and that the fire is the eleventh week and not the tenth, and that a fire a woman keeps refusing does not get a number of weeks put on it in my hand.**"

"**What is left is stone, clay, beech, and a line,**" said Marek Venn.

"**There is a line.**" Iven Tallo did not look at the corner where the ladder stood. "**Nine metres of it, black, and it is spent, and it is the crew's, and it has been round that ladder since the week they crossed it. I have had it in the back of my head for a fortnight and have not said it, because I said on Monday that I would not put a pin in this brace that I would have to argue about in a year, and a new cord is a pin I would have to argue about in a year.**"

"**Say what a spent line is,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have just handed it to an engineer and he is the man who was in the channel with it.**"

"**A line is spent when it has been loaded past what it will hold once,**" said Marek Venn, without looking up. "**After that it does not snap. It goes slack and keeps going slack, and it tells you first, and it tells everybody else at the same time, which is more than a pin does.**"

"**Say the other half.**"

"**The other half is that a spent line in a joint is a joint that will come apart in a bad month, and I would rather it came apart in a bad month than a good one.**"

The room went quiet at that. Nera Oduya had the valve board under her arm at the second step and had not come down all morning.

"**Four tons,**" she said. "**The physician said four tons, or thereabouts, and your clerk wrote the or thereabouts down beside it. I want to know whether a line is being asked to hold four tons or the piece of beech is.**"

"**The beech. Every time.**" Iven Tallo turned his hand over on the table. "**Cord does not hold a push down. It holds two pieces together. Cut the beech wrong and the cord will hold it wrong all day, cheerfully, and the beech will fail and not the cord.**"

Then the clerk asked for the condition to be read, because it was in the practice, and she gave her reason out loud, which was that a condition nobody has heard for six days starts to be a story.

Renn Vale came down four steps with the slate on her forearm and did not sit, because one end of the bench had six cut pieces of beech on it and a man on the other.

"**If the wall takes the push, the room is shut, the nine come out, nobody waits on a physician, nobody waits on a meeting, and nobody in this building is to be told afterwards that they were told to wait.**"

"**And it is not that, because the wall is going to take the push, and I am not arguing with that. So I am not going to write a condition that is a date with a number on it and call it a condition.**"

"**Here it is, in my own words, and it is about the brace and not about my room. The brace stands while the two of us still agree that the wall can take it, and the first of us to stop agreeing takes the brace out, and neither of us has to give a reason to the other one first, and the second of us to find out is the one who acts.**"

It was read twice, which was not the practice, and Renn Vale had asked for it. When the second reading was done, the schoolteacher stood up off the floor at the kitchen-line end where she had been sitting out of the way with her back to the wall.

"**Say whether that is what I said.**"

"**That is what you said.**"

"**Then say what you want read,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Nothing. I have been asked twice this month to be careful with words by a person who is not a lawyer, and I am going to say one thing about that and then let it go.**" Her hand went flat on the stone floor beside her knee and stayed there. "**I wrote it. I said it. It is on a slate and in your book. I do not want it read back to me twice a week to see whether the words have moved. The words are the easy part.**"

Marek Venn watched the hand on the floor and then watched the clerk not write anything, which is the thing he had learned to look for in this room, because a clerk not writing is a clerk declining to improve on something.

The rest of the morning went on time, and time was the second of the four sentences in a survey, and nobody in the anteroom had any.

"**The first sentence is a tested support, and we have it,**" said Marek Venn. "**The third is a counter-bearing, and it is the wall on the other side of that room, and it is forbidden to be anything else. The fourth is a mark, and the mark is a piece of dressed stone in a lintel that three people have read three ways, and the one of them with the most right to an opinion says his is worth nothing.**"

"**So it is the second one,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and it is the one we cannot beg.**"

Jo Verrick had been standing at the kitchen-line with a folding rule in her apron since the first bell, and she answered without moving.

"**Say it in that order and say the number in it, because a room arguing for an hour can be stopped dead by one figure.**"

"**Say it, Jo.**"

"**A day. The corridor is shut on the first, second and fourth days, and the school runs on the three the corridor is open, so a day the school does not run is a day the corridor is shut, and a shut corridor is a day nobody has to come through that frame to feed a pot or carry water. That is the time. Four days in seven, and two of them are gone already, because the fourth day of last week was a Thursday.**"

"**Four,**" said Dalla Sen, and wrote it.

"**And the pot is nine, and the seam gave two on Wednesday and one on Friday and one on Tuesday, and Jo is not going to say what that is a trend of.**" She put the chalk down on the table edge. "**A day in this building is not a day. A day in this building is about four threads of water in a lime joint, and nobody has measured it since the nineteenth week, and it is not going to have a number on how fast it is going from me.**"

"**Then nobody begs for time in this room,**" said Marek Venn. "**Because there is nothing to beg with. Time here is not a thing a person can be given out of kindness, and the terms of the fourth asking had no clause about time in them because the person who wrote them did not know it was going to be the whole of the argument.**"

"**Enter that the time is four days in seven and two of the four are this week,**" said Dalla Sen. "**Enter that I have said for three weeks running that I am not going to make a number for the seam, and that a man on the step has just told a room that the clerk's refusal is the reason the second sentence is the hard one and not the first.**"

Then the man who keeps the page said the thing he had been holding since the second bell. He said it standing, with his hand flat against his coat where the plan was.

"**A clerk's book has a new kind of line in it this month and I want a room to hear what kind, before the third column of anything else is filled in.**"

"**Plainly, Bel. Do not do the thing where you make it a preface.**"

"**On the first day of last week the woman who keeps the air asked a man for three hours of a work, in a room, with the terms out loud, and she said the cost of the asking out loud before anybody could say it after her. What she said was that a person who has been asked by the authority is a person with a standing, and a standing is the first half of a post.**"

It got the quiet it gets when a man on a step says the thing everybody has been carrying.

"**I keep the page. The page has no row for that. It has a fourth column that is empty and has no heading and is not going to get one, and a man four days up a road is on a board in a column with nothing over the top of it. I have spent thirty-two weeks writing down what this boundary will not put in a column, and something has just put itself in one without anybody's permission.**"

"**Say the half against it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because the half against is said first by whoever is going to lose this and it is not me.**"

"**The half against is that a standing is not a thing I can hold, and a person four days up that passage cannot see it, and a man who has been asked something by the air authority is not thereby somebody anybody answers to until a line is written saying so. Nobody in this building is going to write the line. Enter that the keeper of the plan has noticed this, has not written it, and is not going to be argued into it by a room.**"

"**Entered in his words. It is the first entry in this book of a shape that arrived from outside this building, and I am entering a woman's sentence from last Monday next to his, because they are about the same word, and that is not the two questions.**"

"**You are not putting the two questions next to each other,**" said Marek Venn, sharply, because that was a thing she had refused three times in a month and he knew the price of it.

"**I am putting a man's sentence next to a woman's sentence. If it were the two questions you would hear me argue before I wrote it.**"

He had not spoken for a minute, which in that anteroom was long. What he said was not to her.

"**I have had a standing for a week and I did not notice it, and I want that entered in a shape that is not a speech.**"

He put his forearm on his knee and his open hand hung off the end of it and did not close, the way it does.

"**A woman asked me for three hours of a work and said the cost of it out loud, and I said yes, and I went back to that bench, and I have been sitting on it for seven days being a man with a standing and being proud of it in a way I have not admitted to anybody, including me.**"

"**I have no post. I have refused a page with my name on it twice in a room. And the first half of a post has been handed to me by somebody who had to announce it, and I have spent a week enjoying it, and a man who keeps a page has just told a room it came from outside and that he is not putting it in a column.**"

Jo Verrick turned the folding rule over in her fingers and did not use it.

"**Say the standing out loud once,**" she said, "**because there are eight people at that table who have not heard it and one of them is fourteen and sitting on a step, and the boy reads numbers and a standing is a number nobody has read out.**"

Marek Venn heard himself say it and heard how pleased he sounded and understood, all at once, that he had been keeping the shape of that week the way a man keeps a tool he is not allowed to put down.

"**Enter all of it, including being proud,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I have nothing to add to it. A man proud of a standing is still not a post. If he is still not a post in a month then the air engineer's sentence will have cost her something and gained him nothing, and I am writing that because it is going to be true and not because I want it to be.**"

Nera Oduya came down one step, which she had not done all morning.

"**What is the brace going to do to a standing,**" she said, "**and answer it as a work and not as a favour. I did not ask a man in this building to be flattered by me, and it is not going to flatter me either.**"

"**It is going across the head of that frame. A foot on a stone bed and a head against a lintel, and the mark is four strokes and a groove in a piece of dressed stone three steps from where you are standing.**"

"**That is half an answer. What I asked is whether it changes the air.**"

"**I do not know.**"

"**Then it does not go in until somebody who does know has been asked, and I am that somebody.**" She turned the valve board over against her hip. "**That is a work and not a favour. I will want terms, and I will want a person who can be told no, and I will not stand on a step for the whole of it.**"

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and the board was walked at seventeen entries, and the count was read at ninety-six, and the reading is a working practice and not a funeral and nobody in that room made it into anything else.**

"**Four today and a hundred and ninety-seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and fourteen and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for fifteen weeks.**"

"**The counter has been thirteen for a fortnight and it is fifteen now, and I am going to write it once and stop noticing it. The crew are at sixty-six and a half and have not moved since the third day of the week before last, and I am not going to tell this room that a carpenter laying out four ways of making two pieces agree is a work anybody asked five people for.**"

"**The pot is nine and it did not go down. The seam gave two on Wednesday and one on Friday and one on Tuesday, Jo says four threads and not eight, and nobody in this building has a number for how fast that is going and I am not going to be the one who makes one.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is ninety-six days and ninety-six nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be ninety-seven. I have said in seven weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and a man with a tally, a man with a slate and a man who keeps a page have all been in this room without thanking me, and I am entering the three together and I am glad.**"

Tams Quill read the board with the chalk in his left hand and the right one open and flat in his lap, and named no day, and gave no reason for not naming one, and neither improved on the ninth reason nor produced a tenth.

"**Seventeen. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, a plan, a condition and a store. Nine in the ordinary column and eight in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim's condition has not been met and the mark is not coming off the board, and I have said both every week for six weeks.**"

"**And the column is not headed, tenth batch, and I am not giving a reason this week. I have said for a fortnight that I would say the week got away from me, and I am going to keep saying that until it stops being funny, and then I am going to say it anyway.**"

"**And the store is still on it, and nobody has done anything about that, and it is a man with a store that is going to be emptied of its fir on Monday. It is not a null and it is not a claim and it has no column, and I am not going to say anything about it.**"

Dalla Sen read the seventeen entries out, read her own reading back against them, and entered two things: that the count had given no reason this week, and that she had not asked him for one and was not going to.

---

**A carpenter said on the first day of the thirty-second week that a joint is two pieces agreeing to stay in the same place, and that the cheapest one in this building is a shape a person with a knife can take apart, and that four people have to hold a piece of beech in that shape while one person cuts it.**

**There are three of the four.**

**A man with no post has had a standing for a week, and a man who keeps a page has noticed it out loud and has refused to put it in a column, and a fire in the corner of a room with nine hearth stones in it has now not been lit for eleven weeks and has been asked about seven times and refused seven times.**

**The pot is at nine days and the seam is at four threads, and a condition in the second column of a row with no number and no day on it says that two people have to look at a wall. The condition is not met, and nothing in this building is going to say so in a room until two people have looked.**
