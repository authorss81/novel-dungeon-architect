# Chapter 165 — The Fourth Hand

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the thirty-second week, a Friday, and the school ran for the twenty-eighth morning, and a woman who keeps a corridor was asked to hold a piece of beech and said no, and a woman who cooks said yes, and a carpenter was asked to put his name on the record of a thing he built and said no as well. The count was read at one hundred, and a clerk told a room on Thursday so that nobody would thank her.**

**The brace is four hands and one cutter. It has been three since Wednesday, and the price of that is a day, and a name in a clerk's book, and two people who would rather not be in it than be good at it.**

At the fourth bell the twenty-eighth morning went in the way the twenty-seventh had, and Mikka Hale asked to be asked about the fire for the eighth time and refused an eighth time, and asked for the children to be cold in the record in the same place as the refusal, and then she put her board down on the sill by the doorway, which is where it goes when she is going to do arithmetic instead of cooking.

"**Say it out loud,**" Dalla Sen said, "**because you have said a number out loud in this room twice this month and both times it went down, and a cook who does arithmetic by the doorway is doing the pot.**"

"**I am doing the pot.**" Mikka Hale did not pick the board up. "**The pot is nine days. It has been nine or ten for thirty-two weeks and it has not gone up once. The seam gave two on Wednesday, one on Friday, one on Tuesday and two today, and a seam that gave eight threads in the nineteenth week is giving four, and nobody has measured it in between, and I have asked for it to be measured four times and it has not been.**"

"**Say the part that is about you.**"

"**Five people have been working in this building for two days on a thing nobody asked them to be paid for in a pot. A pot at nine with nine children in it and a crew of five in it is not nine. It is going to be eight by the middle of next week, and the only reason it is not eight today is a seam in a wall behind an anteroom that gave two jars this morning instead of one.**"

She picked the board up then, and squared it, and did not say the next part in the voice she used for numbers.

"**So a brace is being paid for in that seam. I am not going to say that out loud in a room as though it were a finding, and I am not putting it in a column next to the pot, and I will not be the woman who says it. I am saying the arithmetic. The clerk can decide what the arithmetic is.**"

"**Entered, and entered that the seam is holding a pot at nine against the arithmetic of a building with a crew of five in it, and that the cook would not say it and did say the arithmetic, and that I am putting the two next to each other with nothing between them, and that I am not going to write the word brace beside either of them.**"

Then the question of the fourth hand, which had been sitting in that anteroom since Wednesday like a chair nobody would sit in.

"**Four people hold the shape and one person cuts,**" said Marek Venn. "**We have had three since Wednesday and the third has been a man who is holding two corners with one hand and his knee, and a room does not get better at that by saying so at the sixth bell.**"

"**Then ask somebody,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I am not going to ask somebody because somebody is in the room. I am going to have to ask somebody because the shape cannot be held with three, and that is a different thing and I do not want this building to find out the difference by watching a piece of beech move.**"

Renn Vale came down four steps. She had the slate on her forearm and she did not put it down, and the anteroom found out that this was the answer she had decided on in the last half hour and had not said in any of the three rooms she had been in since the first bell.

"**Ask me,**" she said. "**Ask me now, in the room, with everything said out loud, and I am going to say no, and I want it in your book in those words, and I want it read back.**"

Dalla Sen asked her, and she said the terms of the asking out loud first, and then Renn Vale said no.

"**No. And here is the reason and it is not modesty and it is not that I am not strong enough, and I would like both of those put in the book so that nobody has to be kind to me about it.**"

"**Say the reason.**"

"**I am one of the two people who can take this thing out.**" She turned the slate over once. "**A woman on a landing and a woman in a room wrote a condition a fortnight ago and it has two of them in it, and I am one of them, and the second clause is that the first of us to stop agreeing takes the brace out and neither of us has to give a reason to the other one first.**"

"**And the half of it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have taught this room to ask for that and you do not get to skip it.**"

"**The half of it is that a person who can take a thing out cannot be one of the hands that put it in. Not because it is improper. Because in two years, when that beech shim has crushed at its corners and the frame has gone a twelfth of an inch and somebody has to decide, the room will look for the person whose hand was on it, and if that person is the person who is allowed to take it out then the room has learned the wrong lesson, and it will have learned it from me.**"

"**Say the last of it, and then I will stop asking.**"

"**The last of it is that I said in this room a month ago that a person named in a room is not a post, and I meant it, and I have been waiting thirty-two weeks for a chance to find out whether I meant it about myself.**"

Nobody in that anteroom said anything for about three strokes of the pump, and then Mero Senn said one sentence from the doorway where he had been standing with his bag, and it was the rule and not a feeling.

"**A plan with a named person in it is not a plan. I have said that in this room and it does not soften because the person is the best one in it.**"

"**Enter it in her words, in that order, and enter that I asked her in a room and she said no before I finished the terms,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And enter that a physician named the rule and that I am entering his sentence directly after hers because it is hers that it applies to and I am not going to separate them.**"

Then Mikka Hale said yes, and she said it from the doorway with her board under her arm, and she said the half against it before anybody asked, in the shape of a woman who has been in this building thirty-two weeks and has learned how this room works.

"**Yes. And I am the cook, and I keep the pump frame, and I have a clinic return watch, and none of those is the room this brace pushes on, and I have thought about that for a day and a half and that is why I am saying yes and not because a room is looking at me.**"

"**Say what you are holding and what stops it, because that is the practice and it goes on the page,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I am holding a corner of a piece of beech at about the height of my chest. I have held things at that height for thirty-two years and my hands are better than most and worse than I would like. What stops it is a term in your book and the two women. And if it starts to go then I am letting go and stepping back, and I am not the person holding it on when it goes.**"

"**Say the other half, which is the half about you, and you owe this room that much because you are the second person today who has said yes.**"

"**The half is that I am the woman who said out loud a fortnight ago that she was not going to put a dead man's share into that pot for a brace. And I am going to hold a piece of wood for a brace. And those two sentences are about the same thing, and I would like a room to notice it, and I would like it noticed that I noticed it first.**"

"**Entered, in her words, and entered that a cook has said yes to a brace and has said no to a dead man's ration in the same fortnight and that I am not putting a reason beside the two of them and that anybody in this book who wants to make them one is welcome to come and argue it in a room.**"

Then the shim, which is the joint, and which took eleven minutes and is a piece of beech a finger wide and four inches long and has more to answer for than anything else in this building.

Iven Tallo cut it off the end of the fourth beech piece with a fine saw and did not put it in the heap with the offcuts, and stood with it in his hand for a moment before he squared it, which nobody in the room had seen him do before and which Dalla Sen entered without being asked.

"**Say why you did not put that in the heap,**" said Marek Venn.

"**Because the heap is what is left over and this is not what is left over.**" He held it up so the lamp came through it. "**This is the piece that touches the stone. Four tons comes through this, and it comes through the two long edges of it because that is what a shim is, and when those edges crush this thing is finished and the head settles.**"

"**And when it is finished?**"

"**And when it is finished a person takes it out and puts another one in, and the person is a term in a book, and I said that on Thursday and I have said it twice now and I am going to say it a third time when it is written down and a fourth time when somebody is looking for it in ten years.**"

"**Say the last of it as a carpenter and not as a prophecy.**"

"**Say it as this: I can tell you what this shim is made of and I cannot tell you when it dies, and anybody who tells you when a thing like this dies is selling you the certainty and not the thing.**"

He set it on the trestle in a place where three people could see it, which was not a habit of his, and then the anteroom looked at the school door for the first time in three days.

It was at the far end of nine steps and it was shut and there was no wedge in it, and there had not been a wedge in it since the Wednesday of the week before last, and a woman who keeps that door was standing at the top of the steps looking at the head of it.

"**Say the thing everybody in this room is thinking,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it before the carpenter does, because he has been thinking it since Wednesday and he is a better man with a saw than he is with a sentence.**"

"**A door with no wedge in it is a door that has to be watched,**" said Renn Vale. "**And the answer to a door that has to be watched is a person, and I am the person, and I have said that in this room and I am not going to stop saying it because I have now said no to holding a piece of beech.**"

"**Say the arithmetic of that, because you have made yourself smaller twice in two days and I would like somebody to count it.**"

"**The arithmetic is that I am one person and I keep a shore, a road, the lead terms, a corridor, a landing and this Hearthhold's second way out of its busiest room, and I have now added a door head that is not wedged, and there is no page in this building I can put a person's hours on. The page where a person would go is blank, and it is not going to be used by me.**"

"**Then say what is left, and say it as a fact and not as a plea, because the clerk will not take a plea and I will not enter one.**"

"**What is left is me, and the hours I already keep, and a door that is shut on a day the school does not run, and nobody watching it. That is the honest answer and it is a bad one, and the better version of it is not true, and I would like it entered that I said it in that order.**"

Nobody in that anteroom solved it, and nobody tried, and two wedges lay on a bench four feet from where the clerk was sitting, and the terms of the fifth asking said that the five of them take no wedge and no bar.

Marek Venn looked at the two wedges for a while, and did the thing he was told he did and could not help doing, which was to tap the surface next to them twice with the knuckle of the hand that will not close before he trusted them. Quarter-sawn, dry, and the end grain gone pale and a little furry at the edges, which is what eleven weeks in a room with no fire in it does to beech. He had driven them into that door himself in the eleventh week and had not thought about them since. That was a fact about him rather than about the door, and he wrote it in his own book on the anvil of his knee, in pencil, in two lines, with no date at the top of it, because there is no date on anything in this building except the corner of a plan.

Then, at the second bell, Dalla Sen asked Iven Tallo to sign something, and made a piece of paper for it first, and the piece of paper had a line on it and nothing else.
"**This is the record of the thing. What it is, what it is joined to, what it is joined into, what stops it, and who can take it out. It goes in the book in your own words. I have left a line at the bottom and I am asking you to put your name on it.**"

"**No.**"

"**Say why, and say the real one, because the clerk has asked that of me in a room and I am not going to be the first man in this building to give her a decent one.**"

"**The real one is that I have got my name in the second line of a piece of paper in a stiff cover with a proper seal on it, four and a half days up a road, and I have not decided about that one, and I said in this room that the date I refuse it is not today.**" He put the pen down on the table without touching the paper. "**I am not going to put a name in the second line of a second thing in this building until I have decided about the first one. And I would like that entered as the reason and not as modesty.**"

"**Enter it in his words and enter that the line at the bottom of that paper is going to stay empty, and enter that I am going to leave it on the page anyway, because a page with a space on it and a page with nothing on it are two different instruments and a person in a year needs to be able to tell them apart.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and the count was read at one hundred, and the board was not walked, and nobody in that room made one hundred into anything.**

"**Three today and two hundred and seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and twenty-four and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for fifteen weeks. The crew are at seventy-one.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is one hundred days and one hundred nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and one. I have said in eleven weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and a hundred is a figure a room will want to do something with, and I am telling you now that I am not going to let it.**"

"**I told this room on Thursday that it would be a hundred today so that nobody would be surprised, and I have now said to you that I told you, which is the one time in ten weeks I have done that, and I would like it noticed and not thanked.**"

"**A man at the back of this room said out loud this morning that a hundred is a round number and a round number is a thing people put on a page. He is right. I am not going to put a mark beside it, and if anybody finds a line drawn through that hundred they are to understand that it was a person being careful and not a person being sentimental.**"

"**The pot is nine. A cook said the arithmetic of a crew of five against a seam at four threads, and the clerk has entered it and joined it to nothing, and the pot is nine and it may be eight next week and there is nothing in this building that fixes that.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is that two people said no in this room today and one person said yes, and a board is a place where a thing gets a column, and I would not give a column to a morning in which the deciding was done by people standing up and sitting down.**"

---

**A woman who keeps a corridor said no to holding a piece of beech because she is one of the two people who can take it out, and she said that a person who can take a thing out cannot be one of the hands that put it in, and that she has been waiting thirty-two weeks to find out whether she meant a thing she said about herself.**

**A cook said yes and said the half against it first, and a carpenter said no to signing the record of a thing he built because his name is in the second line of a stiff cover four and a half days up a road and he has not decided about that one.**

**One hundred days, and the clerk told a room on Thursday so that nobody would be surprised, and nobody was. The pot is nine days, and the only reason it is not eight is a seam in a wall behind an anteroom.**
