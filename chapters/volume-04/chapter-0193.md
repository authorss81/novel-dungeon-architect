# Chapter 193 — One Way Out Of A Room

The first day of the thirty-seventh week is a Monday, and a shut Monday in this building is nine steps, one pump, and a floor that is colder in the corner where the flag stone used to be. The school did not run and lost nothing. There are eleven people in this Hearthhold and there are six who are not this building's, and the anteroom is rated ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and that figure has not moved all morning and nobody in the room has mentioned it, which is itself a kind of mentioning.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell before anything else in the room got going, and the number was a finger and two-thirds, and it has been that since the second day of the twenty-eighth week. Then she read the party wall with a piece of chalk and a piece of string, and the reading was a quarter of an inch, the sixteenth, and the same as fifteen. She put the chalk in her pocket and kept the string in her hand and did not put the folding rule anywhere near the join, and when Tams Quill asked her from the bench whether she was ever going to get a rule into that gap, and her answer was no, and he took it as one and went back to his end of the bench with a piece of chalk he has not used.

Renn Vale came down the four steps at the second bell, which is her bell on a shut morning, and put her ear against the party wall on this side of it and was there about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times. Dalla Sen entered it as a figure of nothing, and entered that it was the fourth morning she had entered that, and did not improve on it, and Renn Vale told the room, out loud, that she would go on being dull about it on purpose, because the only way to keep a promise of that shape is to be exactly as uninteresting as the truth usually is and a person who makes her watching interesting is a person who has started performing it.

Then Jo Verrick gave the whole of that wall out loud, and nobody had asked her to, and it took her about half as long as a Friday's.

Five and a half shores standing on the common landing side. A spreader under the head of the sill, which came out of this floor on the fifth day of last week and is bedded on the two stones either side of it with clay that will be dust in two years. The sixth place empty and not filled, and the iron for it four days up the middle passage, and no iron anywhere in this building that anybody has found. A brace of a school's own beech pushing on the same wall from the kitchen-line side, a foot and a half lower than the frame it is under. A bad repair on one face only, put on the face that gets looked at, because that is the face a room's people are standing on. And a keeper's nine behind that, on the other side of two feet of it.

The last part came out without stopping, and she let the room sit with the fact that she had done it on purpose, and the reason was that a reader who arrives at the nine having stopped is a reader who has stopped reading.

The anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, all six, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and the pump was attended and stopped on a rising water line chalked on the frame that has still not moved. Bel Ordry named the fifth as the fifth of six and read nothing else of it, and Dalla Sen entered that he had named it and read nothing, and entered that nobody read it, and entered that this was the fifth morning running she had entered that and that she was not going to give the run a number.

Then the cook, from the kitchen-line doorway, with the cloth over her arm, and she did not come through it.

"**The pot is three days,**" said Mikka Hale. "**And the seam gave me nothing on the seventh day and nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and six pails a day every day in seven in two crossings, four and then two, and that has not changed since the week before last. And the four households at the second gate have nothing, and no figure is on that gate, and nobody in this building is going to put one there.**"

"**Then give the clerk the other number,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step.

"**There is no other number,**" said Mikka Hale. "**Six men who are not this building's have eaten out of my pot every day since the Monday of the week before last because I decided it alone on a Friday night and told a room, and it is a fortnight now, and nobody has asked me about it once, and a fortnight is long enough that I would like it entered that a cook has been left alone with a decision for a fortnight and has not been asked and has not minded, and that both of those are still true, and that I still do not know which of them is doing the work.**"

Dalla Sen entered it, and entered that the seam gave nothing on two mornings running, and entered that the pot at three days is with six more mouths in it than the plan of this building was written for, and that the plan does not have a column for mouths and that is not the clerk's fault and is not a defence.

Then Renn Vale came down all four steps, which she does not do except when she is going to say something that belongs to the floor rather than to the landing, and stood in the middle of it with the hole in the corner at her feet and the nine going round it.

"**Then I am going to do a thing this morning that I want entered before I do it,**" she said. "**I am going to say the number of ways out of each room in this building, out loud, in this room, with everybody able to hear, and I am going to say it room by room and in the order a person walks them, and it is going to be a number said in a room and it is not going on anything.**"

"**Before anybody counts anything, the person paying for the counting states what it costs,**" said Bel Ordry, and came down one step, which is more than he does in a week.

"**The cost of it is that nobody in this building has ever said that number, and the reason nobody has said it is that a number of ways out of a room is the first thing in this Hearthhold that would have to go in a column, and a column has a keeper over it, and a keeper over a column is a post.**" She put her hands flat on her hips. "**And I have spent thirty-seven weeks not being a post, and counting my own exits out loud in a room with strangers in it is the first half of becoming one, and I am doing it anyway, and I would like that in the book in that order.**"

"**Then the half against, and it is mine and it has been mine since the third week of the thirty-fourth,**" said Bel Ordry. "**A boundary that cannot say how many ways out of a room it has is a boundary that is one bad Tuesday from having none, and it will not find out on the bad Tuesday, it will find out afterwards, in a clerk's book, with a day on it.**"

"**And the half against that is hers,**" and she came back up one step to give it, "**and she gave it to me on the third day of last week without being asked, in front of a woman from a counter: a number said out loud in this room becomes a number this room has agreed to, and a number this room has agreed to is a number somebody can be asked for. So I am putting a number in the air on purpose, on a Monday, having heard that, and if it is the wrong thing then it is mine and it is in the book as mine.**"

"**There is one of you who is going to be asked for it, and it is not going to be me, and it is not going to be you,**" from the end of the bench. "**Whoever it is gets it from me first, and not from a decision somebody else has already made about them.**"

"**Then take it,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step. "**And put the two figures back into the room in a keeper's mouth, because a number that is said once in a morning belongs to the person who said it by the second bell.**"

"**The anteroom is ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and it is a permission and it is not a number of anybody in this room,**" said Renn Vale, "**and that is the fourteenth time I have said it and I have said it eleven times with six strangers in the building.**"

The pump got three strokes in and nobody in the room put the two together.

Marek Venn had it. He had had it since Saturday morning, out of two mornings of standing at the top of nine steps and watching nine people cross a floor, and he had it to within one, and it was the number he had refused to give in this room on the third day of last week for a reason he had said out loud and had not enjoyed. He had spent four days since then telling himself that the reason had been a good one, and it had been, and it was also the reason that had let him keep it. A figure of what is in a building has a source and a source is a post. A number of ways out of a room is worse than that, because it is not a figure of what is in a room at all, it is a design, and a design that a room has heard and not objected to is a drawing, and the drawing would be his. A drawing in a man's hand on a table in a room that has agreed to it is the first standing anybody has handed him in thirty-seven weeks, and he did not have to be asked for it.

He put his hand flat on the shut book, and it was the hand that does not close, and it lay there the whole time she was counting and nobody looked at it and it did not matter that nobody looked at it.

"**The kitchen-line room is through the common landing door and the common landing is over the corridor and the corridor is over the school door,**" said Renn Vale, "**and I am going to say the rest of it in the same order, and I am not going to be asked a question in the middle of it because I will lose my place.**"

She did not lose her place.

The room at the kitchen-line end has one. One door, and it is on the common landing side, and behind that door is the common landing, and the wall with five and a half shores in it is between the common landing and that room, and the keeper's nine are on the other side of the wall. The common landing has one, and it is the school door through to the corridor. The corridor has one, and it is the same school door, and the corridor is shut on three days in seven and that includes this morning. The anteroom has two, and she gave the count and not the argument, and then gave the argument anyway. The outer service door, which a body goes out of, on four rusted pins, packed with stone and clay, holding and not fixed, with a brace of a school's own beech over its head a foot and a half lower than the frame. And the nine steps up into the middle passage, which is a crossing, and which is shut on the first, second and fourth days of every week, and this is the first day.

"**So this building has two ways out,**" she said, "**and one of them is shut three days in seven, and the room that nine children are taught in has one way out, and that one way out goes through the room the wall is in.**"

Nobody improved on that for about as long as it takes the pump to do four strokes.

Then Jo Verrick said the rest of it, from where she was standing, without being asked, and she said it flatly and she said it as a reading and not as an opinion.

"**The sixth place is empty and the iron for it is four days up a passage,**" she said. "**And a shore with no foot plate crushes its own mark into a floor and takes the floor with it when it lets go, and a carpenter said that out loud in this room on the first day of the thirty-fifth week and it has been true for two weeks and nobody has been able to act on it. So the wall is five and a half, and there is no sixth, and the wall is not the wall it was, and I have read that wall sixteen times and it has not moved once and that is not the same as the wall being all right, and I would like somebody in this room to hear the difference from me because I am the only person here who can see it.**"

Mero Senn came off the doorway where he had been standing with his bag against the wall since the first bell.

"**Then the plan has got a name in it,**" he said. "**A plan with a named injured person in it is not a plan, and I have said that in this room three times and nobody has ever disagreed with me, which is not the same as anybody having listened. So the question is not how many ways out there are. The question is who is under that wall on the morning one of those five and a half lets go, and if nobody in this room will say the name then the plan is not a plan, and if somebody says the name then the plan is not a plan either, and I would like somebody to explain to me what a plan is in this building if both of those are true.**"

"**I will say it,**" said Emrys Callow, and he came off the bench and stood in the middle of the anteroom floor the way he had stood in it on the second day of last week when he told the room he had turned down paid work four days running. "**It is one of my five and which one of them is not mine to name, and it is not the boy on the bench and it is not a child, and it is a man of thirty-odd holding the end of a bar under a stone with no spreader under the head of it, and the man holding that end is the one with the least time on a crew, because that is who you put on the end. The reason the name stays with me is that a name under that wall is a plan, and I am not handing a physician a plan, and the clerk is to enter that a foreman was asked for a name and gave the shape of one and kept the name.**"

"**Entered, in his words,**" and she laid the slate flat on the trestle, "**and entered that the man who would be under that wall is a man of thirty-odd holding a bar and is not a child, and entered that a foreman who has said the shape of a person and kept the name has done the thing this room has spent thirty-seven weeks asking for, and that nobody in this room has thanked him for it, and that the clerk has not thanked him either, and has entered that she noticed herself not doing it.**"

"**That is the whole of the morning so far and it is not enough,**" from the kitchen-line doorway, where she had been since the first bell with her own slate under her arm, and she came in for the first time, which is not a thing she does. "**You have got a wall with five and a half on it and a room with one way out of it and a fact about a piece of timber going up a passage in a foreman's hand. And I have been standing in that doorway for forty minutes doing the thing I do at a counter, which is looking at three things that cannot all be true and working out which one is a lie. And none of them is a lie. That is the difficulty and I want it in a room before anybody starts building anything.**"

"**All three of them, in the order I gave them, and nobody gets the second one without the first,**" said Renn Vale.

Nella Bray put her slate on the trestle, which she has done twice.

"**One, the timber. The district's book says six and the wall has five and a half, and the true fact about that is going up the middle passage in a foreman's hand with a day of the week on it and not a date, and it has not come back. Two, the iron. A sixth place needs a foot plate and a foot plate is iron and the iron is four days up the same passage, in the same district, under the same office. And three,**" she said, and she did not put the slate down, "**a document that has been in this building since the fourth day of the thirty-fourth week. A cleared-risk map of this district's occupied rooms, wax head at each end, a seal number under a clerk's name, and every figure on it true, and no surveyor who made it has ever been inside one of the rooms it names. Two of its six rooms are this anteroom and that schoolroom. Both of them are a ten. And the ten is what each of those rooms is permitted to hold.**"

"**And the three cannot all be true,**" from the step, and he did not come down this time.

"**They can all be true and that is the problem,**" and she put one finger flat on the trestle beside her slate. "**At a counter, three true things that cannot all be true means somebody is about to be asked to choose and is going to choose with a pen. Three true things that can all be true means somebody is going to leave this room believing that a document is a room. And I have watched men do the first for nineteen years and I have watched exactly one building try the second, and it is this one, and it has been trying it since the third week of the twenty-ninth week and nobody has told it that the word for it is a category of error and not a virtue.**"

"**What is the word for it,**" with the chalk up and not writing on it.

"**The man who keeps the page can have it first, and if he has nothing then I will, and my word is the counter's word and the counter's word is that a permission with nothing under it is a lid on a hole,**" said Nella Bray. "**I do not know that is the right word for your trade. I know that it is the right word for mine, and I have been stamping pieces of paper with that shape since I was nineteen years old, and every one of them had a clerk's name under it, and not one of them had anybody's name over it.**"

Bel Ordry put in the one thing he had been carrying since the sixth day of the thirty-fourth week, and he put it in before anybody could get to the middle of the morning with it.

"**The two figures are not going to be read against each other in this room this week,**" he said. "**They have been entered against each other twice in a clerk's book, which is a different act from reading them out loud with a second person checking, and I am not going to do the second thing in a room that has seventeen people in it and a stranger's wax head on a table six steps away. And the room which can hear it still does not exist.**"

"**It does not exist,**" said Renn Vale, from the fourth step, "**and it existed on Saturday, when I said the same, and I would like it entered that I have now said that sentence in three days running and that a woman repeating herself in a room is a woman who is waiting for something, and I am waiting, and I have not worked out what for.**"

Nera Oduya came down one of the three steps she comes down for air and for nothing else, and stood at the bottom of them with the dyed cloth folded in her hand and not out.

"**Four numbers,**" she said. "**And not a fifth, and the fifth stays in my mouth, and nobody has asked me for it, and it is not going out on a Monday on which a room has said a number out loud twice already and thinks it has learned something.**"

"**And the other thing,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**And the other thing is that the room at the kitchen-line end is the only room in this Hearthhold I have never been able to certify, and I have not tried in six weeks and I am not trying this morning, and if a room wants a number of ways out of that room then somebody has to stand in that room and look at the air in it while it is full, and that somebody is not me on a day when the nine of them are not in it and there is one person in it.**"

Nobody asked her for the fifth and nobody asked her about the room, and Dalla Sen entered that four numbers and not a fifth had now been said in this building twelve times and that nobody had ever asked for the fifth, and entered that the air engineer had declined to go into a room and had given a reason, and that the reason was a person and not a rule.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, both of them, and read the total again afterwards, and the reason she gave for reading it twice was that a room had said a number about its own exits in the middle of the morning and had not yet been given one back, and a clerk who reads a total once on a morning like that is a clerk who thinks the morning is over.

Three today and two hundred and eighty-one since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and ninety-eight and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went into the book the way the other figures went into the book, under the same line, in the same hand, with nothing over it. In the middle of putting her slate away the clerk said out loud, to nobody in particular, that it was a number of days and not a number of anything, which is the thirty-seventh week running she has said it in a room. Then she said that she had said it, and had not been thanked for it, and was not going to stop.

One hundred and twenty-eight days and one hundred and twenty-eight nights. It is not going to be one hundred and twenty-nine. She has been promising a room she will go on writing it down for thirty-seven weeks, and in thirty-seven weeks nobody has thanked her once, and the number is at one hundred and twenty-eight, and one hundred and twenty-eight is not a round number, and nobody in the room remarked on it, which was the correct thing for every person in that room to do and was correct for the clerk as well.

Tams Quill was on his feet at the sixth bell with the board, which is what he does at the sixth bell, and there are eighteen entries on it. He gave them in the order they were in: eleven nulls, and then seven that are not nulls, which are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace. The split between the two columns is nine and nine, and the column with nothing over the top of it is the second one. He gave no reason for the column and nobody asked him for one, and she entered that he had given none and that she had not asked, and that a man who has refused four things in three weeks was on his feet at a sixth bell on a Monday and had not been asked to refuse anything, and that this was the second morning running she had entered that about him and that she was not going to keep a figure of it.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and they did not move.

"**The reason goes in the space where a reason goes,**" she said, "**and the reason is that a room which found out this morning that it has one way out of a room nine children are in is not going to be handed a second unfamiliar thing before the bells are out. And a man with no post asked me on the first day of the thirty-sixth week to write down what I was going to say before I said it, and I have never done that, and this is the second time in six weeks I have wanted to and have not.**"

The pot is three days and the seam gave nothing on two mornings running and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and six packs along a bench and a trestle with two slates on it that belonged to two people and had nothing written on either of them yet. A figure of ways out of a room was in the air in that anteroom, where anybody could pick it up and use it. A woman who keeps a corridor said at the step that she was not going to be the woman who said it was enough, and a clerk entered that she had said it, and nobody in the room improved on it, and the anteroom went on being a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and six packs along a bench.
