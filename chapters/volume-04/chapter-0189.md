# Chapter 189 — The Head Of A Sill

The fourth day of the thirty-sixth week is a Thursday, and the corridor is shut, and the school did not run and lost nothing. Jo Verrick read the frame at the first bell before she said anything else and the number was a finger and two-thirds, and she read the party wall with a piece of chalk and a piece of string and the reading was a quarter of an inch, the fourteenth of them, and the string was where the string was. She put the chalk in her pocket and did not put the rule anywhere near the join.

At the second bell Renn Vale put her ear against the party wall on this side of it and was there for about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over, and came back and said what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the clerk entered it as a figure of nothing and nobody in the room asked her a second question about it.

Then Dalla Sen read out what was on the trestle, which was the six terms, and she read them out because a room had refused a foreman on Wednesday and a clerk does not carry a thing like that into a morning without saying it out loud again.

"**Five work the common landing side. The kitchen-line side is not worked and is not entered. Every person whose hands go on that wall says out loud, in a room, what they are holding and what stops it. Nobody is sent to the low end and the page there stays as it is. Nobody goes through the wall and nothing goes into the line of nothing in it. The person who watches that line is somebody who is not the man who built anything on that wall and not the man who found it, and that person can be told to stop by a person in a room. None of it goes on the board.**"

Emrys Callow had it on the slate and read it back against what she had said with her mouth, which is the second time in six days he has done that and the first time he has been asked.

The six of them have been on the common landing for six days and they have not yet been told what they are standing next to, and this is the first morning on which anybody in this building has been in a position to tell them, and the reason it has taken six days is that the work is not described in any document in the room. It was described on the second day by a woman who refused to describe it and by a clerk who entered the refusal, and a description entered as a refusal is not a description. On Friday a week ago a man who was told a third of it gave a figure of his own out loud and got the rest of it wrong for a fortnight, and he entered that himself, in the space where a reason goes, in front of a room.

"**Same,**" he said. "**And I am going to ask the thing nobody has asked, and I am asking it because you refused me yesterday and I would rather be refused for a reason.**"

"**That is what I asked for,**" said Renn Vale, "**and I have never once in this room had to ask twice.**"

"**What is the work,**" said Callow.

Renn Vale did not answer him, and the reason she did not answer him is that on the second day of last week she had waited two days to be asked about that wall by somebody, and had been asked by the wrong man, and had refused him, and the whole of this week had been built out of that refusal. So she said nothing, and Dalla Sen said it instead, in a clerk's voice, because it is a clerk's information and it came out of a man's mouth on a Friday a week ago and she wrote it down.

"**A spreader under the head of the sill,**" she said. "**Which is a piece of stone with a flat face on it, put under the head of that sill, so that the head of a shore has something to bear on that is not the corner of a nine-hundred-year-old stone. The stone is a flag out of the corner of this floor, because it is the only flat-faced stone in this building that nobody has to break.**"

A spreader is not a repair. It is a piece of ground. A shore with nothing under its head is a piece of timber standing on the corner of a stone that is nine hundred years old, and the stone is taking the load an eighth of an inch at a time, and the timber shortens, and the timber does not complain about it. After a week the timber is half a shore, and a half shore is not half a wall. It is a shore that has been eaten and a foreman who has to write five and a half where a district book says six. The man who set six of these on the Monday of last week gave the correct answer in this room in one unnumbered sentence: two foot plates, a spreader on the head of the sill, and a shore a person could take out with a wedge in four minutes instead of forty. He was right, and nobody acted on it for four days, and a piece of timber lost a finger of its length while nobody acted, and a carpenter who had been right and had said nothing entered that himself, in front of a room, on the Friday, without being asked.

"**You have not said where the corner is,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Here,**" Dalla Sen said, and put her own foot on it, and it was the corner that is colder than the rest of the floor and that two people have stepped round without ever having discussed it. "**And a flag out of this floor comes out once and does not go back in the same bed, and I am going to say that out loud now rather than at the sixth bell.**"

Then the woman who keeps the room at the kitchen-line end came the length of the anteroom, which she does perhaps four times in a fortnight, and stopped on the anteroom side of the trestle with her slate in both hands.

"**Before the hole in the floor,**" said Ilsa Norr. "**Before any of it. I wrote a condition in this room in the thirty-second week and it is in this clerk's book and it has not been used once in six weeks, and I am going to say it in the words I wrote it in, and then I am going to say what a stone under a head of a sill does to it.**"

Dalla Sen put her chalk down.

"**The brace stands while the two of us still agree that the wall can take it. The first of us to stop agreeing takes it out. Neither of us has to give a reason to the other one first, and the second of us to find out is the one who acts.**" Ilsa Norr put the slate on the trestle. "**The two of us is me and the man who put it there. I have thought about this for a day and a night and I have not slept much of it, and I am going to say all of that, because I am about to do a thing that is not honest if I do not.**"

Nobody in the room moved.

"**A spreader under the head of that sill changes what the head of that sill is doing,**" she said. "**It does not push on it from the other side. It stops five and a half pieces of timber from being eaten a quarter of an inch at a time, and being eaten is not the same as pushing, and if I am honest about my own trade, which I have spent four days being, then this work makes that wall safer and does not load it.**"

"**And nobody is going to ask you whether it does,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Nobody is going to ask me, and I am going to agree anyway, and here is the cost of that in a room,**" said Ilsa Norr. "**I still agree that the wall can take it. I have said it now, out loud, in front of you, and the man it is half of has not been asked and is not going to be, and I am not agreeing for him. So the condition has not been examined this morning. It has been met by one of the two of us and not looked at by the other, and for six weeks that condition has been the truest thing this building owns, and this morning I am spending it, and I would like it entered that I thought about it for a day and that I spent it anyway.**"

"**Then nobody is going to ask the man who built it,**" said Renn Vale, "**because the clerk has just named him as one of the three who cannot be asked, and I want it entered that the reason she gave for not asking him is the same reason I gave for not using a stop, and neither of us is going to explain that to a room this morning.**"

Dalla Sen entered it in the keeper's words, and entered that a condition with no number and no day on it had gone into a working morning unexamined for the first time in six weeks, and entered that nobody had asked the second person it names, and that she had noticed herself not asking and was not going to give that a number either.

Marek Venn stood at the end of the trestle with four sentences in him, and all four of them were about a woman who had just spent a true thing to keep a hole out of a schoolroom floor, and he gave none of them away. What he had instead was a small, sour, entirely private arithmetic. He had asked to be asked to be told no, out loud, on the sixth day of last week, and had understood at the time that it was a request for a power. It was not a power. It was a request to be put on a list, and he was on a list, and the list had been used on him this morning without him, and the woman who had used it had done it in the same breath as agreeing to a wall on her own, and both of those things were correct, and he was the only person in that room it hurt.

"**Then that is a hole in a crossing,**" said Mero Senn from the doorway, "**and I am going to say the rest of it before anybody decides anything. A plan with a trip in it is a plan that has chosen who falls. I am not going to be the man who says *be careful by the flag* to a nine-year-old girl. So the plan names her. It is going to be Orla Kern, twice a morning, and she is the fastest of the nine and she does not slow down for a corner.**"

"**Then the nine go round,**" said Ilsa Norr from the kitchen-line doorway, where she had been standing since the first bell. "**They go round through the kitchen end and it adds four minutes each way and I will walk it with them the first four mornings myself and then I will stop, and that is my decision and not the clerk's, and Dalla is to write it in the space where a reason goes.**"

"**And the rating,**" said Bel Ordry from the step, "**which I am going to say out loud because somebody will otherwise think it has moved. That page has a permitted occupancy in the first column and it is ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it is a permission and not a count, and a permission that says ten is still ten with a hole in the floor. The hole does not change it and nobody in this room is going to pretend it does.**"

"**I will read the six terms at the fourth bell with a hole in the floor under them,**" said Renn Vale, "**and the count will be ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and a man who reads numbers can ask me whether that is honest and I will answer him.**"

Then the foreman put both hands on the edge of the trestle and asked the question he had come down the middle passage for.

"**How much of it is left,**" he said. "**You have all been talking about a spreader. There is a gap where the sixth shore was and there are five and a half on that wall and every one of them is standing on a head that has nothing spread under it. How much wood is left on five and a half shores, in days.**"

Nobody answered him, and the pump got three strokes in.

"**I am not asking for a figure about a room,**" he said. "**I am asking for a figure about five pieces of timber, and I have asked a clerk for one in this room already and was refused, and I want the refusal again so that I know it is a practice and not an accident.**"

Dalla Sen had her chalk down and kept it down.

"**I am not going to give you a figure in days,**" she said, "**and the reason is that a clerk who puts a number of days in a book about a piece of work has made a schedule, and a man who keeps a page has told this room in the last ten days what happens when a sentence arrives from outside, and I am not going to be the one who brings the next one in.**"

"**Then a foreman will put a figure on it,**" said Callow, "**and it will be a figure about five pieces of timber and nobody's children, and it will be in my own mouth, and if it turns out to be wrong then the man who is wrong is standing in this room and not four days up a passage. Your clerk is right that she will not make a schedule. She has not thought about a foreman making one, and she should, and so should I.**"

"**A foreman puts a figure on his own five or he puts nothing,**" said Renn Vale, from four steps down.

"**Four days,**" said Callow. "**On the fourth of those days one of my five is under that wall, and I have told this room on Wednesday that I cannot tell you how many of us are under it.**"

"**Entered, and entered that it is his figure and not this boundary's,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and entered that a day is not a date and I am going to write down that I wrote that.**"

Then the clerk turned her slate over and put it face down, which she does when a figure is going into the book and not onto a slate, and nobody remarked on the two figures on the other side of it from Tuesday, which were still there and stayed there.

"**Then I am going to ask,**" she said, "**and I have been not asking for six days and I am going to say out loud, before I do it, why the reason has changed, because the reason has changed and a clerk who changes her mind and does not say so is a clerk with two books.**"

She said who she was asking, which is the practice.

"**I am asking Iven Tallo, Tams Quill and Jo Verrick, for four and a half hours, on the fifth day, on the open morning, on the common landing side. Four and a half hours, and the split is named before anybody names it: two of Iven Tallo's, two of Tams Quill's, and half an hour of Jo Verrick's. And Jo named her half hour herself on Tuesday night and told one person and I am the one she told.**"

"**I named it to her,**" said Iven Tallo from the anvil end, "**and I am not going to pretend I did not, and I am also not going to pretend that a man who has just been asked for two hours is a man who is doing a favour.**"

"**Then the three who could not be asked, with the reason for each, and the reasons are not the reasons of the last five askings.**"

She read them off her own hand.

"**Marek Venn. The reason is that he is the man whose hand is on the brace, and the brace pushes on the same wall, and a man who put a thing on a wall is the man a room looks at when the wall is in question. He asked to be asked to be told no, out loud, on the sixth day of last week, and he has not been asked since, and I am not going to pretend to him this morning that this is an oversight.**"

"**It is not an oversight,**" said Marek Venn, "**and I have four sentences about it and I am not going to say them in this room.**"

"**I know you are not.**" She did not look up. "**Pell Roke. The reason is that he is on the bench at the top of the nine steps and this room is rated ten including one litter and he is inside that figure every morning of the week, and the work carries a stone down nine steps and up again, and a room cannot ask the person in the middle of its own capacity to be moved out of the middle of it. And there is a second reason and I am entering both: the last thing this building did with a page was to try to put a person in a column, and I am not starting with a boy of fourteen.**"

"**Talla Roke. The reason is that she is not in this room. And the reason she is not the reason, and I am entering the real one: the last person down nine steps with a bundle was her, and if I ask a courier to carry a piece of work then that piece of work is a road, and this boundary decided on Saturday that it will not hand anybody a bundle. And asking a person who is not in the room is not asking a person. It is asking a room, and this boundary did that with a printed sheet for six weeks.**"

"**Then Renn Vale,**" she said, "**is named as the person who can be told no, to the whole of it, and the terms are out loud: nobody is sent to the low end, the page there stays as it is, the kitchen-line side is not worked and not entered, and nobody is to be asked whether that wall can take this, including the man who put the brace on it.**"

Renn Vale took the slate out from under her arm and looked at it and put it back.

"**I have never been given that in five weeks,**" she said, "**and I want the room to hear that I am not going to use it, and I want it entered in the space where a reason goes and not in a column, and the reason is that if I use it this morning then in a month there will be a standing in this building that has my name under it in a way nobody asked for, and I would rather be the reason a thing was allowed than the thing that stopped it.**"

"**That is not nothing,**" said Bel Ordry from the step. "**That is a keeper declining a stop. In a year somebody is going to read that an anteroom's terms were never once used in a fortnight and conclude that nobody here can be stopped, and it will be the wrong conclusion, and I will not be the man who writes it.**"

"**Then write the other half,**" said Renn Vale, "**which is that a stop nobody has used is not the same as a stop that does not work, and this room has had six of those in a month.**"

"**The cost, then,**" said Dalla Sen, with the chalk up and not writing. "**In a room, before I ask. I am asking five people to carry a piece of work on the morning a foreman has told this room that it cannot say how many people are in it, and on the morning a woman at a counter asked for that figure and five people in this room refused it in five different ways, and the clerk entered the refusals. That is the cost of this asking and it is not a small one and I would like it in the book in those words.**"

"**And the half against this morning, which nobody has given,**" said Iven Tallo from the anvil end, with the chisel in his right hand and his left open and flat on the anvil. "**A room that has said no five times in a fortnight and asks on the sixth has a shape. I do not know what the shape is and neither does this clerk, and that is very nearly the definition of a schedule. I am not saying the answer is no. I am saying that a man who has watched a room refuse things for five weeks and then watch it ask on the sixth has got a picture, and I have got the picture, and I would like it entered that the carpenter has got it too.**"

"**Then let us both keep it,**" said Renn Vale, "**and let neither of us say it in a room with a printed sheet on the table, because that is where a sentence like that goes to be useful to somebody.**"

"**There is a second half of the cost,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and it is mine to say because I am the man who has been arguing since the second day that a crew with nothing to do takes the next work that comes. I was right. He turned down paid work for four days. I do not know why and neither does he, and I have stopped guessing in a room. And now this building is going to ask five of its own to do four and a half hours of work that nobody has paid anybody for, next to six people who have not been paid anything at all, and I would like a person in this room to say out loud that it is not fair, and I would like a different person to answer it.**"

"**It is not fair,**" said Iven Tallo.

"**It is not,**" said Callow, "**and my five are not on your column and are not going on it, and I am not asking for that, and I would like it written in the same week in the same book that on Wednesday I told this room my five were not going in a book that a district can read. Two facts, one morning apart, and nobody has to make anything of them.**"

Dalla Sen entered the two next to each other with nothing between them, which she has now done three times in a fortnight, and entered that the seventy-one moved by four and a half to seventy-five and a half, and that the fifth asking in thirty-five weeks became the sixth, and that the reasons have not been the same reasons twice.

At the fourth bell the anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and the pump was attended.

At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's figures and the total and left both of them unsaid, and the reason was entered too, and the reason was that a room had named a figure of days in it and a clerk had refused to give one, and a clerk who reads a total out loud on a morning when the only numbers in her head belong to a foreman is handing her morning to a foreman. Two today and two hundred and sixty-eight since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and eighty-five and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count was read at one hundred and twenty-four and it is not going to be one hundred and twenty-five, and the clerk said out loud that it was a number of days and not a number of anything, and she has said that in thirty-three weeks running, and nobody has thanked her.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a man who reads numbers had given nothing away for two days running and a board is not the place to go looking for what a man is holding.

The pot is four days and the seam gave nothing on Wednesday, and the four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody.

And in the far corner of the anteroom floor there was a flag stone with a line round it where a chisel had been once, a long time ago, by somebody who had put it back, and on the fourth day of the thirty-sixth week a foreman and a clerk and a woman who keeps a corridor stood looking at it and agreed to take it out, and none of the three of them could say in a room what would be under it.
