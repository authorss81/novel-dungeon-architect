# Chapter 204 — The Number Printed Again

The first day of the fortieth week is a Monday, and a shut Monday in this building is nine steps, one pump that is not running, and a floor that is colder at the kitchen-line end than at the top of it. The school did not run and lost nothing. Nobody crossed the corridor and nobody was going to. The flags by the kitchen-line door were cold at the first bell and cold at the ninth and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for sixteen weeks, and there is a bundle of birch bark on the hearth that Ilsa Norr has asked three times to be allowed to light and has been refused three times by a person who will not give the reason.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twenty-fourth reading, and the same as twenty-third, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked, and the clerk entered all four as four refusals and not as one, and did not say the number of the morning, because she had said on Sunday that she was going to leave that frame alone for a week and a clerk keeps to a thing she has said she will keep to.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the second bell, which on a shut day is her bell, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and entered it, and entered the eleven mornings out of eleven.

The anteroom's terms were said at the second bell, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk. The count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The third was six pails a day and it was false and it was said. The pump was attended, and stopped, and had been for two days.

Then a man with a wooden tally came up the nine steps with a folded sheet of paper under his arm and put it on the trestle and said four sentences and went back down them, and he has never once come further into this room in five volumes, and nobody in this room has ever asked him his name, and the clerk entered all four of his sentences and did not ask him either.

"**One, a salvage claim, made out in the ninth week and sent to a middle gate. Two, it came off the board at that gate on a Friday. Three, it came back up the passage on the Saturday with a seal on the corner of it. Four, that is the whole of what I have and I am not going to stand here being asked for a fifth.**"

"**Entered, in a road hand's mouth, and entered that he was not asked for a fifth,**" said Dalla Sen.

Then nobody in that anteroom touched the sheet for about a minute and a half, which is longer than it usually takes, because the first thing anybody saw on it was the number in the corner.

It was a seal number. It was under a clerk's name at a middle gate. And it was the number that had been struck through with a line of a woman's hand on a market gate form in the second week of the thirty-ninth week, and struck through again by a man of twenty-two years' standing four days later in an insurance office, and the two of those had been the whole of what this boundary had ever won.

"**It is the number,**" said Bel Ordry.

He came down the nine steps, all the way, which he does perhaps twice in a week, and he did not touch the sheet and he put one hand flat on the table an inch from it and left it there.

"**Say the half against,**" said Nella Bray, from the anvil end, with her slate under her arm. She had not said anything else. She had been at the anvil end for four days.

"**I am going to say it, and I am going to say it the way I said it on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week, because it is the same sentence and it is the sentence I have been saying for five weeks and I would like this room to hear the same words twice instead of hearing a new one.**" He did not look at the paper. "**A head struck through a number is not a rule that has gone. The rule is standing. The office is right about the rule and always was. What has happened is that one number on one sheet has a line through it, and a number is printed, and the next quarter somebody prints that number again with no line on it.**"

"**The next form comes down that passage with a fresh second line in it,**" said Nella Bray, "**and this room will be standing exactly where it is standing this morning with the same two reasons and no way to use either of them.**"

"**Nine days,**" said Bel Ordry. "**I said that on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week and it has taken nine days, and I have been in this building thirty-nine weeks explaining why sentences in rooms do not travel, and one did in four seconds at a counter, and a number came back up a passage in nine days with no line on it, and I would like a clerk to enter that I said it and said it kindly and that it was correct.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything, and the pump did not run, and the pot was two days.

"**Now the half against the half against, which is mine and which I am obliged to put in the room, and it is the reason I have not slept a whole night since Thursday,**" said Bel Ordry, "**which is that I have been right. Both halves. The number came back and I was right about the number, and in September a letter is going to come down that passage with a man's name at the top of it, and I said that on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week and I have been right about that too, and being right about both of them is the worst thing that has happened to me in this building, because the whole of what I have been doing for thirty-nine weeks is explaining to rooms that neither half is a rule, and I have been wrong twice and the room has been right and it cost a man at a counter his hands.**"

He took his hand off the table.

"**I have not forgiven anybody and I am not going to and I would like that entered. I have been out-forgiven by two instruments and one of them is a piece of printed paper on a table.**"

Then Nera Oduya came down one of her three steps, which is the second of the three and is as far as she comes, and she had the dyed cloth out and not out, and she had a piece of chalk in her other hand, and she did not ask anybody for anything.

"**The fourth line,**" she said.

"**The fourth line is a condition of entry and it is the Compact's,**" said Bel Ordry. "**You may not touch it.**"

"**The fourth line is a condition of entry and it names four sets of covers at a wash room,**" she said, "**and no such covers are on this claim and have never been on this claim, and I am striking the line out and I am doing it before anybody asks and I am giving the reason to nobody.**"

She drew the chalk through the line in one stroke, about four inches, and gave the chalk back, and went up her three steps without a word, and the clerk entered that she was not asked and gave no reason and that nobody in the room asked her, and entered that this is the fifth time.

Nobody in the anteroom said anything about the chalk, and that was deliberate, and the clerk entered that too.

Then a foreman came up the nine steps about the middle of the third bell, with two of his own behind him and no packs, which is how a crew comes when it is not going to work.

He was about fifty and had grey in the side of his head and he stood at the anvil end of the trestle and did not look at the paper until he was asked to, and then he read all of it, which took him a while, and put it down again.

"**Seth Wray,**" he said. "**Ninefold Charter, salvage and clearance, and I have been a foreman for twenty-three years and I am not the man who made this and I am the man who brought it. I want to say four things and I want them in the order I have them and I do not want the room to be kind to me about any of them.**"

"**Nobody is going to be kind to you about anything,**" said Emrys Callow.

"**Good. One. That is a claim over a flooded gallery at the bottom of a passage off the second gate, and the claim is properly made out, and the seal on the corner of it is a seal I have no objection to, and the office that put that seal on it has never once been asked to look at this building's plan and I am not going to stand here and pretend that a number being reprinted is anybody's villainy. It is a quarter's printing.**"

"**Two,**" he said. "**The claim covers salvage. Water, timber, metal, salt-crust, anything a person can take out of a room and carry, from a gallery that has been under water since before my time. I have not been in it. Four of my own have not been in it. There is a man in this district who has been in it and he is not in this room and I would rather he were.**"

"**Three,**" he said, "**and this is the one I came to say and I would like it written down in my words and not improved. I have read this claim twice on the road up here and there is a second line on it and the second line has a name in it, and I did not put that name there, and the sheet came off a board at the middle gate with the name already on it, filled in correctly, in a clerk's hand, by a person who fills them in correctly because that is what they are for.**"

Dalla Sen put her hand near the book and did not pick it up.

"**I do not know the name,**" Seth Wray said. "**I do not know whose name it is. I have carried it up four days and I have not asked, and I am telling a room that I have not asked, and I would like somebody to understand that a man who does not ask a question on a four-day walk is not a careful man, he is a man who has decided that what he finds out will be worse than what he does not know, and that is a decision and not a virtue.**"

"**Entered, in a foreman's words, and entered that he had not asked and was not asked,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Four,**" Seth Wray said. "**I have been told this boundary does not answer forms. I have read that in a file and I have been given the word *refused* three times by three different people in an office, and I have come up four days to find out whether the word was accurate, and I am going to say the thing I am owed and then I am going to stop talking. I am owed a hearing. Not an answer. A hearing, in a room, with the person who made the claim in it, and I have made the claim, and it will be made by me and not by a letter, and I will come back up these steps for it whenever this room says, and I will not come up them unasked.**"

The room did not say anything for a bit, and the man at the anvil end turned his cup round on the table a quarter of a turn.

"**Then it is heard,**" Bel Ordry said, "**and it is not answered, and I am going to say the half against that too and I am going to say it in one sentence. A claim over a room full of water is a claim over a thing that cannot be carried, and the second line asks for a person responsible for a load, and there is no load on that ground, and a form that cannot be answered correctly is a form that cannot be used, and that is the argument a woman from a counter gave on a Friday in the thirty-ninth week and it is correct and it is going to do nothing at all, because this form has been answered correctly by somebody who is not us.**"

"**Then it was answered correctly by somebody,**" said Seth Wray, "**and the somebody is a person, and I would like to know whether your counter is the one that did it, because if it was, then your argument is still true and my form is still no use to anybody, and if it was not, then somebody else's counter filled in a name for a room nobody has been in, and that is a different thing and it is worse.**"

Nobody in the room knew whether it was or not, and the clerk entered that nobody knew, and entered that the question was put to a room and the room did not have it, and entered that the room did not have it in the space where a finding goes and not in the space where a reason goes.

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle since the first bell with his shut book under his forearm, and he had understood the second line at about the second sentence Seth Wray said, and what he understood was not a fact about a guild.

It was the sentence a woman from a counter had said to him in a room in the fortieth week, three days ago, and it was that a man who reads things is a man who can be found, and a man who can be found has a name and a description and a reason to write both down, and a form which cannot be answered correctly is a form which cannot be used. She had said that her objection stopped holding the day a piece of timber went out of this building on a cart, and that had been entered in a clerk's book, and the timber had gone up the middle passage as a load, and the load had a form, and the form had a second line, and the second line had been filled in by a person who was competent and was doing their job.

And a different person from the same office, in a different building, four days away, had filled in a name.

He did not say any of it. He was the only man in that anteroom who had been in the room when the name was first spoken of, and the only man in that anteroom who could have said whether the name on that sheet was the same name, and he sat at the end of a trestle with a shut book under his arm and looked at a stranger's handwriting and let it stand.

He would be asked about it four days later by a woman with a slate, and he would find that he had had it in his mouth for four days and had not given it up, and that the reason was not modesty and was not the one he would have given.

Seth Wray put the sheet back on the trestle, squared it with two fingers, and did not take it with him.

"**Leave it,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**I will leave it,**" Seth Wray said, "**and I will come back up these steps when this room says and not before, and I want it entered that a form has been left on a table in a room by a person who made a claim, and that this boundary has not taken it and has not sent it back, and that the reason is that sending it back is a refusal and a room is not allowed to refuse on a Monday morning without a hearing and a hearing has not happened.**"

"**Entered, and entered that the claim is neither granted nor refused and that a hearing is owed and not given,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a guild's form is lying on this boundary's trestle with a line struck out of it by this boundary's air engineer and a seal number on the corner of it that this boundary's own half against predicted in advance and correctly.**"

At the sixth bell Dalla Sen read the day's figure and the total out loud, both of them, and then read the count, and gave the reason for the order she gave them in herself, and the reason was that a room which has been handed a piece of paper that costs somebody else something is owed a number that is its own before it is owed a stranger's.

"**Three today and three hundred and ten since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and twenty-seven and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

She looked at the slate for a moment and did not turn it over.

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and forty-nine days and one hundred and forty-nine nights into a count that is his own and it is not going to be one hundred and fifty,**" she said, "**and I have said that in this room forty-five weeks running and I am going to say it tomorrow as well, and I have noticed this week that I have got a way of saying it that I did not have last week, and I do not know when I got it, and I am not going to find out, because a clerk who examines how she says a number is a clerk who has started to like saying it.**"

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a claim has been left on a trestle and the trestle is not a column and a board is, and a clerk does not walk a column on a day when she has been handed a third kind of instrument by a stranger with four days of walking in his boots.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that five men have not been asked in nineteen weeks, and there is a man on the nine steps' landing who has been on a four-day walk and is owed a hearing, and a crew is not the instrument that gives a man a hearing.

The pot is two days, the seam gave two yesterday and nothing this morning and is at four threads, and the four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a keeper went down four sets of steps on Saturday and said a true thing at a gate and has not reported what she was told.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor, and a trestle with a book on it and a stranger's salvage claim lying squared on it with a line of chalk through its fourth line and a seal number in its corner, and a pump that had not run for two days, and a plate on a shelf above the bench wrapped in work cloth and forty weeks unopened, and in the pocket of a coat on a hook a brass rule that had said nothing for thirty-one weeks. Above the book, where the figures go, there was nothing at all, and the space where a reason goes beside a guild's form was empty, and a man had stood in a room and said he had not asked a question on a four-day walk, and a man at the end of a trestle had had the answer in his mouth and had kept it.
