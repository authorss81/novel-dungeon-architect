# Chapter 108 — Nine Children In A Numbered Room

On the third day of the twenty-first week this boundary shut a corridor that the arrangement said was open, and lost one of the three days the school runs, and did not get either of those things back.

Renn Vale closed it at the first bell, standing in the anteroom, with the terms in her hand, and she said the reason before anybody argued, which is her habit and which stopped working in the nineteenth week and which she does anyway.

"**The waist has moved three times and is not measured, by three people, on purpose. I have got nine register boxes and a shelf's worth of slates and nine children and a teacher, and I am not going to have a heavy crossing and a school morning in the same hour on a fill that three people have agreed not to touch, and I have not got a second window, because the low end stands in water for the six minutes after the lever comes off, twenty-six times a day, and the only window I have got is the strokes in between.**

"**So the corridor is shut today. That is a day the arrangement has open and I am closing it. Enter it as a closure and enter the day it costs and do not put one next to the other yet, because you will all do it in about an hour and I would like somebody to have refused to.**"

"**No,**" said Dalla Sen, from the fourth step.

"**I beg your pardon.**"

"**No, I will not put them next to each other yet. You have told me not to and I am a clerk and when the person who keeps a road tells me to hold a thing back for an hour I hold it back for an hour, and I am telling you now that I am going to put them next to each other in an hour, and that I am going to do it with a reason beside them and nothing between them, and that the reason is not going to be a reason.**"

Renn Vale laughed at that and said, "**Go on.**"

"**It is going to be me saying that I am putting two facts next to each other on purpose so that a reader in a year cannot make them into a finding.**"

There was no better idea in the room, and the corridor was shut, and the day was lost, and nothing was paid back and nothing was going to be.

---

**And they moved a school, at the second bell, in the pump's strokes, in three parties, with a bearer at the near lip, and nine children and a teacher carried their own record across a fill that has moved three times.**

**Before each party the count had to be read out,** because a crossing needs to know how many are on each side, and the only page in this building with a count in it is the plan.

Renn Vale read the first column out of the kitchen part of it, standing in the corridor at the school door, and she read it in the order it is written, nine rows, and the clerk heard her and wrote it down, and here is what the anteroom's row says.

"**Ten,**" said Renn Vale. "**And that is the highest number on the page and it is the anteroom's, and there is nobody in it.**"

"**Then it is not a headcount,**" said Ilsa Norr, who was at the front of the first party with a tower of boxes on her hip, "**and I am not walking across that fill on your arithmetic, Renn, and I say that with love.**

"**Ten is not how many are in that room. That room has been empty since the first bell every day for twenty weeks. So either the number is old, or the number is for something else, or the number is a number somebody said a long time ago and one of us wrote down, and I do not know which, and I am not going to guess with my hands full.**"

"**Then say what the crossing is,**" said Renn Vale.

"**Nine children, one teacher, and one tower of boxes that I can hold and a woman with a rule at the near lip and a reader at this door and a cook at the far end with her hand up, and the number for that is in the room and not on a page, and I will count it aloud at both ends and you can write that down and it will do.**"

And that is what they did, and it is in the book, and Dalla Sen entered that the room did not use the plan's first column as a headcount and entered that it did not say why not, in three lines, where three lines was the space she allowed herself.

A boy of about eleven said, from the second party, in the middle of the dip, in the middle of a crossing, at a walk, which is not the moment for a question and was in fact the moment for a question:

"**Miss. Where does the number come from.**"

"**We will find out,**" said Ilsa Norr.

"**Miss—**"

"**We will find out,**" she said, "**and I am not going to tell you a story about it while you are carrying a box, and I am not going to pretend the question is a bad one.**"

Nobody followed it up, and it was not followed up after, and that is a decision and not an oversight. It sat in the middle of the day the way an eleven-year-old's question sits in a corridor, which is to say very quietly, and it is in the clerk's book in one line with no comment, and it is the line that gets read.

---

**And then somebody asked for the beech lengths, and the request was refused, and the refusal was right, and the person who asked for them did not need to be asked twice.**

"**The shelf has to come off its props,**" said Jo Verrick. "**You cannot carry a shelf's worth of anything on two lengths laid across two pails and you cannot take the shelf with the boxes still on it, so the props have to come out from under it and the shelf has to go across on its own or not at all.**"

That is when Iven Tallo said, in the corridor, to nobody, that the props were the two beech lengths under the shelf the nine register boxes stood on, and that they were the only two beech lengths in this Hearthhold, and that if you take them out from under that shelf the shelf comes down and the nine register boxes come off it.

"**I am going to be asked for them by a woman in the next hour,**" he said, "**and I am telling the room now so that nobody can say afterwards that a carpenter in this building was slow.**

"**Say no to me when she asks and say it in the room, and do not thank me for it, because I have not done anything. The props stay. A boundary that loses its record in a week is a boundary a stranger can rewrite in a month, and I have spent eight months with my own record in a file and I know exactly what one is worth.**"

Ilsa Norr said, when the question came, in about four seconds flat, and without a word of thanks, and without any of the argument:

"**No. And I was not going to ask, and I want that written down that I was not going to ask, and that a carpenter told the room before I asked so that nobody could put it on me.**"

**The two beech lengths stayed under the shelf. The school went without a shelf.**

---

**They were in by the fourth bell, and the room was a room nobody has taught nine children in, and here is what the room is.**

It is at the kitchen-line end, past the hearth corner, past the clinic door. It has a dished floor, a hearth corner with a cold hearth in it that is somebody's job and is not the schoolteacher's job, no mouth in it, and a doorway wider than the anteroom's, and the light in it is the best light in the corridor and the floor is the worst floor in the corridor. It has a doorway that is wider than the anteroom's. It has the best light in the corridor and the worst floor in the corridor, and the light comes off the hearth stones and the floor is dished toward the doorway for the same reason the school corner's was dished toward its door, and Ilsa Norr said that out loud at the fourth bell and made the clerk write it.

Up went the nine register boxes, onto the hearth stones, in two towers, which is dry and which is higher than the floor. Wen Pask carried four of them across the waist in a party and a fifth and said nothing about it, because he had been asked in a room with the terms out loud and because being asked is a different thing from being thanked, and a cook said yes to the hearth stones before anybody asked her, and a cook who says yes to a thing before she is asked it is a cook who has been asked the same shape of question before.

**The school ran two bells. It then did not run.**

**Two bells of teaching, and then nothing.** Ilsa Norr taught a thing about the number nine to nine children on a hearth-stone floor in a room with no shelf, and the room heard it through the corridor, and then at the fourth bell she came out into the corridor and said one sentence about it, and Dalla Sen wrote it in the clerk's hand on a separate page and not on the plan's page, because the plan has no row for this room and a blank row in a new document is an omission and an omission reads as an accusation, and Ilsa Norr said that before anybody said it to her.

"**Write this in my words and write it on the room's own page and not on the plan's page, because the plan has nine rows and this room has not got one and it is not getting one today, and if I put a line in the third column for a room the plan has never heard of then in a year somebody is going to read the plan and think we forgot it, and we did not forget it, we have not written it, and those are different and one of them is worse.**

"**The school is in the worst room in this building and it is dry. I did not agree with that and I am not going to say I did. The room they took from us is shut with a wedge and I am not going to pretend that a room shut with a wedge is the same as a room taken from us, and it is not, and I would like the book to be able to say both of those sentences without one of them cancelling the other.**

"**And the day is lost. I am not calling it moved and I am not calling it paid back and if either of those words gets near this page I will take the page off the wall.**"

Bel Ordry read it out with the rule that everyone who wrote a line stands and says yes, and Ilsa Norr stood, and nobody read anything after it, which is not the rule and which she allowed and entered that she allowed.

---

**And then at the sixth bell the clerk did the thing she had said at the first bell she was going to do, and it is in the ledger in her own hand and it is the reason a book can be read in a year by somebody who was not in the room.**

"**Two facts and I am putting them next to each other with a reason beside them and nothing between them.**

"**A closure was entered on a day the arrangement had open. A school day was lost.**

"**I am putting these two facts side by side on purpose. The reason is not a reason. The reason is that I am putting them near each other so that a reader in a year cannot make them into a finding, and I have said so out loud in a room and the room let me, and that is the whole of my authority in this matter and I am not going to pretend it is more than that.**"

**At the sixth bell Tams Quill walked the board** and read the crew at fifty-two and a half, **unchanged since the fifth day of the nineteenth week, and the reason is still the one Renn Vale gave**, and the boundary's column up four today and thirty-one since the keeper signed, and in the column with no heading **forty-three days and forty-three nights**, among six other things the count will not name on a Wednesday, and no heading, and no reason given this time, and he was asked for one on Monday and gave one, and it was not a better one and he said so himself.

---

**And at the last bell a courier came up the middle passage with a folded paper, and the road was not shut and nobody was charged for it, and the paper had no date on it of any kind.**

Dalla Sen read it standing, in the anteroom, with the room at its number.

"**It is from the second gate and it is a notice, and it is written on a form, and there is a clerk's mark on the form and not a name.**

"**The water-lock at the second gate is closed for survey and is attended. A person may attend the survey on giving notice at the counter. Notice must be given in person. This notice is not dated.**"

"**The word,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Attended,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And it is a word this boundary uses every single day. Jo Verrick runs an attended pump. Renn Vale's terms say attended. It means a thing with a person on it who does not leave it, and it is the best word anybody in this building has got.**

"**And somebody four days up that passage has written it on a form and meant something else by it, and I would like the room to notice that we have read that word four hundred times and that we have never once read it the way it is written there.**"

Nothing was said about it, and the paper went into the book, and the boundary's three open days for the week were two, and one of the three that the arrangement had is a day that got shut for a school that moved a shelf, and nothing has been paid back, and Ilsa Norr has not been told which of the two things is the independent one, and she has not asked, and nobody is going to tell her.
