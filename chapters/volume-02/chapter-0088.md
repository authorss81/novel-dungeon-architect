# Chapter 88 — What He Was Asked

She said it in the anteroom at the first bell on the third morning of the seventeenth week, standing at the top of three steps with her board against her hip, and she said it before anybody had thought about asking her, which was on purpose and which she did not explain until afterwards.

"**Bel Ordry. I am going to ask you this morning, at the valve, and I am going to ask you myself, and the asking is mine, and no clerk writes the name anywhere until it is done.** If anybody in this room wants to be in the room, they can go and stand somewhere else."

"Some of us are going to want to be in the room," said Bel Ordry.

"Yes," said Nera Oduya. "**That is the whole difficulty and it is why I am doing it at the valve and not here, and I am telling you that instead of pretending it is a rule.** A person finds out about a post from the person who is handing it over, on a day when the handing is a choice, and not from a page in a week when it has already been decided. I said that in a full room eight days ago and had it entered as a refusal to be asked, and there is a hole in this book's record with my refusal in it and the clerk is not going to paper it over with a line.

"So: I am going to go and ask him, and I am going to come back and say what happened in my own words, and he is going to be able to say his own, and the two go in side by side or they do not go in at all."

"Are you asking him for the valve watch," said Dalla Sen.

"I am asking him whether he will take the air valve watch when I go in the spring, and I am telling him what it is, and I am telling him what I do not know about it, and **I am not going to ask him in a room.**"

---

Marek Venn went with her as far as the turn, and she had asked him to and had said why, which was that a man who is asked to witness an asking is a different thing from a man who is standing in it, and he said he would rather be in it, and she said yes, and then said no, and then said come, and then stopped at the turn herself and put her hand flat on the wall on the clinic-return side and said:

"From here I can hear most of it and I cannot hear the argument, and if I cannot hear the argument I will not pretend to him that I did. And if anybody asks me in a year what was said in that room, I will say what I heard and say plainly where I stopped hearing. That is the only honest version of a witness and it is the first one anybody in this building has offered anybody."

At about nine minutes past the first bell, from round the turn, in a voice that was pitched for a man who does not want to be overheard, came Bel Ordry saying no.

It took about ninety seconds and then it stopped, and then it started again, and what came over the wall in the next twenty minutes was this, and Marek Venn wrote none of it down and held all of it, and the only part of it he has ever repeated is the part at the end.

---

"No. And I am going to say all of it now, because you have told me all of it, and a man who is given the whole of a thing and then asked for it has been asked a favour, and I am not going to answer a favour with a no. **So: no, and here it is, and it is not noble.**

"**One.** I am on the threshold bell, and the bell cannot be left, and a valve cannot be given to the man who is on the bell. You have a hand-over and you have a bell and one of them is going to go and it is not going to be the bell, because the bell is a rope tied to a newel and it is in Renn Vale's building and it is Dalla's day-book, and a rope does not need a person the way a valve does, and everybody in this room knows that and nobody has said it out loud, and I am saying it out loud so that you do not waste a morning.

"**Two.** I have held nothing for fifteen weeks. Not nothing nearly. Nothing. I have a key to nothing and a bar and a wall and two rooms off a ladder nobody else has any business on, and a hinge of my mother's in a bag for seven weeks, and I have been a man standing in cold places being careful, and I have been *good* at it — not useful, good, and there is a difference and I have had it explained to me by your count for a fortnight.

"And I have been good at it *because* I have held nothing. I made the clerk look at her own hand. I wrote *measured: nothing* on purpose, in a room, in order to be caught, and being caught was the point, and a man who is holding nothing can put a sentence into the air and then stand there and let a woman be frightened by it, and that is a thing to do with your hands free.

"**The day I hold something, every one of those becomes something somebody has to check me about.** And I have watched this building for fifteen weeks and I know exactly what it does with a thing it cannot check. It asks for a name. It puts a name in a column. And then the name is in a file, and the file is forty days away, and there is a man in it who did not know when he was getting one.

"So no. And that is the whole of me saying no, and it took me about a second and a half to work out, and I have been not-saying-it since about the fourth day of the eleventh week."

---

"Then tell me what it is," he said. "**The valve.** Because you have come round here and asked me for a thing and you have not told me what the thing is, and I want to say why that is the part I object to, and it is not the asking.

"You would not do that to Jo Verrick. You would not do that to Renn Vale, and you would not do it to me about the bar, and you did it to me, and I have been on the end of this boundary's ladder for fifteen weeks and I have watched every person in it do one kind of work properly, and **all of them told me what their work was before they asked me for anything about it, and you did not, and I have been sitting on that since the eighth week and it is the reason I would have said no anyway.**"

There was a long pause, and then Nera Oduya said, "That is fair. That is entirely fair and I did it on purpose and I am not going to pretend I did not, and I will tell you why after I have told you what it is, because the why is a worse thing and it should come second."

"Go on, then."

And she told him. All of it, from the turn, in about eleven minutes, and Marek Venn heard nine-tenths of it and the anteroom later heard it in her own words, and it is in the book, and it is the longest single thing the air engineer has said in this building.

The valve is a plate on a shaft that comes up out of the wet mineral below this level and turns, and it is hers, and it is the only moving part in the Hearthhold's air that a person can reach with a hand. It has five releases on the board beside it and she has never taken a sixth. The baffle above it is the reason the school corner's mouth can be shut at all. The clinic return channel is on the same seam. **And the stair's air is on the same seam**, which is why nobody may be on that stair in the six minutes after the lever comes off, and the six minutes are not six minutes of quiet — the shaft is a sink in those six minutes, not a vent, and it has not been a chimney since the cold, and the terms were written for the worst case and the worst case is now a six minutes in which nothing moves because nothing is moving.

"And the water," she said. "In those six minutes the water in the dip comes up under a corridor two foot eight wide that stands on the edge of that shaft, and there is no post at that low end, and there is a woman who will come if the sound changes and nobody will ever know whether it changed while she was lifting a cover, and the page still says *the moment that sound changes* and the page does not say *and we will know.*"

"And the rate?"

"I have not published a rate in fifteen weeks and I am not going to in a cold month, and I have claimed three costs for that refusal and every one of them is in this book in my own hand, and the second one was wrong. **I read the geometry of a nine-hundred-year-old table and wanted it to mean something, and I was wrong about what it was for, and I did not act on being wrong, and a person who is wrong and does not act is the dangerous kind of careful, and I have said that in this room and I am saying it again with my back to you.**

"And the school corner's mouth has been shut since the collapse and the air that used to come up out of it is not accounted for, and the page on the dowel has no air on it at all, and that has made it worse rather than better, and I have been alone with that for fifteen weeks and I have told nobody, and I am telling you because you have just been handed a valve and a valve is the thing the missing air comes out of."

---

"Why you did not tell me," he said, after a while.

"Because you have held nothing for fifteen weeks and I wanted to see whether you would take something, and I have been in this building sixteen weeks and I did not know that about you until the eleventh of them, and by then it was too late to be honest about it." She said it without decoration. "**And I am going to say the rest of it, because I have decided in the last two minutes that you have earned it, and I am not going to be a woman who decides things about people in rooms.**

"I am leaving in the spring. That has been in this room since the tenth week. And a valve handed to a man in a cold month, in the seventeenth week, with a draught on a stair nobody has walked in nine hundred years, and a corridor low end with nobody at it, and a pot at twelve days, and a review set for the first day of the month it stops falling — that is a bad hand-over. I know it is a bad hand-over. **I did it in a bad month anyway, and I did it to a man who has refused to hold anything, because I have been in this building eleven weeks and I could not think of a better time and that is the actual reason and it is not a good one.**

"I am not asking you to be ready. I am asking you whether you will hold it, and I am telling you what I know and what I do not know, and I am not going to argue, and if you want to go and stand somewhere else afterwards I will not think anything of it except that I will put the fact that you went in this book, because that is a fact about you and not about me."

---

"Then here is what I am going to do," said Bel Ordry, "and I have been standing here for about four minutes working out whether I am allowed to say it, and I am.

"**I am not answering it today.** Not no and not yes. **Asked, and not answered**, and you can put that in the book in both our hands with the time on it, and the name goes against it, because a name against *asked and not answered* is a fact and not a holder, and that is the only reason I am letting my name near it.

"And I will answer in the spring. In a room, with you in it, and not before, and here is the reason and it is not modesty.

"**In the spring this boundary is going to know whether it is keeping itself or being kept.** That is the whole of it. If the pot has stopped falling, then a valve is a thing a building of eleven people runs, and I can hold it. And if the pot has not stopped falling, then a valve is a thing eleven people are holding on to, and what you would be handing me is the most frightening thing in this building, which is not a stair and is not a lip — **it is the only handle anybody has on the air.**

"And I have watched a room this week find out that a man who said *I am not coming* in a written notice had also cost nothing, and a room is going to have to find out in the spring what it is when the water is not coming. **I do not want to answer a question about a valve in the cold, in a bad month, with a number going down every day, because a man who answers then is answering a different question and he will not be able to tell which one he answered.**"

Marek Venn heard the last of it at the turn, and it was the sentence about the spring, and it was the best sentence anybody had said in this building in a fortnight, and he did not know for another two hours that it was going to decide the shape of a page.

---

She came back up the nine steps at the second bell and said it in the anteroom, in her own words, and Bel Ordry came up after her and said his in his own words, and Dalla Sen wrote both and read them out, and then stopped with the chalk up, because the clerk of this boundary had a problem and stated it before she solved it.

"Right," she said. "Then I have to say something, and I would rather say it than find it out in four months.

"In the fifteenth week I entered a **refusal to be asked** and I said in this room that it was a hole in the record and that I was not going to paper it over with a line. And I have just been handed a line. A name, a date, an hour, and two hands, and the two hands do not agree, and one of them has said *I will answer in the spring.*

"So: is this the line, and have I papered it, and is a question asked and not answered a hole or is it a hole with a rim on it?"

"A hole with a rim," said Renn Vale, from the landing, at once.

"A hole with a rim," Renn said, "**and I will tell the room why, and it is because of the other line, and everybody in this room has forgotten the other line exists because we have been looking at this one.**

"The other line is blank. It has been blank for five weeks and it has two sentences on it and not one name, and it is the low end of the corridor, and a woman on a landing is holding a room with nobody's work in it, and **if I write Bel Ordry's name against the valve I will not touch that line, and if I had ever let a clerk's pen get near that line this morning I would have said no on the anteroom floor.**

"Those are two different lines and they are not interchangeable and they are not in competition. One is a woman who will come when a sound changes and will never have a name on anything. The other is a man who has been asked to hold a valve and has said *when*. **The first is a condition and the second is a refusal, and the page has got both, and it is the only page in this building where somebody has said no to a thing and somebody else has said not yet to the same thing, on the same day, in different hands.** And I want the clerk to read the blank line out, with the two sentences, immediately after she reads Bel Ordry's, every fifth bell, from now on, and I want the two of them read together, and I want that entered as a thing I have asked for and not as a rule."

"Read together and entered in her own hand and in mine," said Dalla Sen.

---

And so Dalla Sen read out six things on one line that evening: the lip's range, and the seventh term, and the ninth line, and the pot, and the blank on the review page with the two sentences on it instead of a name, and — for the first time — **the air valve, asked and not answered, and the spring.**

Six things, and the anteroom heard all six.

Nobody filled the space after the last one, and a man who had held nothing for fifteen weeks went up a ladder off a corridor that nobody else had any business on, to two rooms of his own above the clinic return, with a hinge of his mother's in a bag for seven weeks, and did not take it out, and the shaft below the corridor went on not being a chimney, and the school corner's mouth stayed shut, and the air that used to come up out of it stayed unaccounted for, and there was no rate, and there was no plan, and the two sentences on the blank line were still two sentences and not a name, and the stair was corded and shut and stayed shut, and a person was still under that lip.
