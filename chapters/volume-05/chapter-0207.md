# Chapter 207 — Two Different Waters

The fourth day of the fortieth week is a Thursday, and a shut Thursday in this building is nine steps, one pump that has not run for five days, and a floor that is colder at the kitchen-line end than at the top of it. The school did not run and lost nothing. The weather above had turned and everybody in the anteroom could tell, not because of the weather but because the air in the room had gone different on Tuesday and had not gone back, and nobody in the room had said so, and the woman whose whole trade was air had said so on Tuesday in eight words and had said it in a way that made three other people in the room look at the floor.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the twenty-seventh reading, and the same as twenty-sixth, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she gave the four refusals in her own words before anybody asked, and the clerk entered all four as four refusals and not as one, and the count of mornings is twenty-seven and is the same count as the wall's, and it is the same count because she says both in the same breath and has never once found a reason to say one without the other.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the second bell and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and entered it, and the count of mornings out of mornings is thirteen.

The anteroom's terms were said at the second bell, all six, by a person and repeated by the clerk. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours. The outer service door was the one a body went out of. The fifth of those six terms was in a room with both of the people it names in it. The third was six pails a day and it was false and it was said. The pump was attended, and stopped.

Then the room did the thing it does on a shut morning, which is find something in the middle of it and turn it over, and what it found on Thursday was a pot.

It went on turning over for about two hours. It did not come out of anybody's mouth as an argument for the first hour and a half. It came out of the room the way things come out of rooms in this building, which is that somebody said a fact and then somebody said a fact next to it.

"**Four days,**" said Nella Bray, at the anvil end, with her slate under her arm. "**I have been in this building nine weeks and the pot has been one day since the Friday before last and it is two days since Sunday and it is going to be two days on Monday, and this room has now gone four days without a pump, and I would like to know whether anybody in it is going to say out loud what four days of that is.**"

"**I will say it,**" said Mikka Hale, from the kitchen-line doorway. "**Four days is four days and a pot is a pot and I have said all of that to this room before and nobody has improved on it, and I am not going to say it again this morning because I have said it so many times that a number said out loud in here has stopped meaning anything and I said that last week and I meant it.**"

"**Then I will say the other one,**" said Nella Bray. "**On Wednesday a woman came up nine steps and asked this building to keep the water in a gallery at the bottom of a passage off the second gate. And this room has not answered her. And I have watched this room since Wednesday, and what this room has been doing since Wednesday is talking about the pot.**"

"**That is two waters,**" said Iven Tallo.

He had not been in the conversation and he had been at the anvil end the whole time with his left hand flat on the iron, and he said it the way he says things, which is as though he had been working on it for an hour and had only now found the words.

"**Two waters,**" Iven Tallo said. "**And this room has been treating them as one water for a day and a half, and I have watched it, and I want to say why that is a thing a room can do by accident and then be wrong about for four days.**"

"**Go on,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**A joint is not a joint because of the wood. A joint is two pieces and something happening between them, and the something is either the same all the way along or it is not, and if it is not then you have not got a joint, you have got two different joints with a piece of timber in the middle of them, and you find that out on the day you cut it and not on the day you needed it.**"

"**And these are two different joints,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**These are two different waters with a piece of this building in the middle of them, and the piece in the middle is a pot, and the pot is a thing six people who are not this building's eat out of, and they have been eating out of it since the thirty-fifth week by a cook's own decision.**"

"**Say the part you are circling,**" said Renn Vale.

Iven Tallo took his hand off the iron.

"**If the water from that gallery came up here,**" he said, "**and went in that pot, then the pot stops being a thing a cook looks after and it becomes a thing a boundary keeps. And the moment it is a thing a boundary keeps, somebody in this room has to answer for it, and the person who answers for it is a person in a column. And we do not have a column for it, and we are not having one, and the clerk has been at that table for two days explaining to a woman from a second gate exactly why we are not having one, in the plainest words anybody has used in this building for five volumes.**"

"**So nobody is arguing for the pot,**" said Nella Bray.

"**Nobody is arguing for the pot,**" Iven Tallo agreed. "**And I have watched four people in this room this week get close to arguing for the pot and go somewhere else, and I am not going to be the fifth. I am the man in this room who has spent his life on walls and has never once owned one, and I said that on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week and nobody asked me about it then and nobody is going to ask me about it now, and what I have learned in the two days since is that there are two waters in this building this week and only one of them is anybody's, and the one that is anybody's is the one with six strangers eating out of it.**"

"**That is the eighth reason and not a reason for the column,**" said Tams Quill, from the end of the bench, without looking up. "**I am not giving it as one. I heard him say it and I am recording that I heard it and that it was not mine and that I am not putting it in my mouth.**"

"**Entered, and entered that he was not asked for it and did not claim it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a carpenter gave a reason in a room this morning which is the second one this week that has not been about the man who gave it.**"

Then the physician came in from the kitchen-line end with his bag against the wall, which is where he puts it, and stood with his good shoulder against the frame of the far door, and he had been in the room for the whole of it and had not said anything for the whole of it, and when he spoke it was not about water.

"**I am going to describe somebody in this room,**" said Mero Senn, "**and I am going to do it because I have watched him for two days and because nobody else in this room is going to and because I have got about four minutes before somebody asks me a medical question, and if I am honest the medical question is not going to be the one I have.**"

"**Then describe him,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**The man at the end of the trestle,**" Mero Senn said, "**has not said one word in two days about the thing he knows. He could tell you to within a hand's breadth where that water is going and what it will do to the ninth hearth stone's bed and to a door frame on the common landing and to a floor with a hole in the corner of it. He has the right hand for nothing and the left hand does not close and there is a mark on the inside of his arm, and he is the only person in this building who can say what a wall in a gallery four days away is doing, and he cannot go, because there is a school on three days in seven and a crew of five men on a bench and a woman from a second gate at a trestle, and because on Friday he said out loud in a room that he was not going to say a thing he knew until he had looked at it.**"

"**And I have watched him enjoy that,**" Mero Senn said. "**That is what I want entered, and I want the clerk to write *enjoyed* and not improve it. He has been the man who finds out for thirty-nine weeks and he has wanted it and been proud of it, and on Friday he found a morning where not saying it was the strongest thing available to him, and he has been sitting at the end of that trestle for two days like a man sitting on a warm stone.**"

"**That is not what I have been doing,**" said Marek Venn.

"**I know what you have been doing,**" Mero Senn said. "**You have been sitting on it and you have been calling it discipline, and I have watched men call a great many things discipline, and the test is simple. If you had a reason that was good enough, you would have said it in this room on Friday afternoon, because this room would have gone and looked. Nobody went. So either your reason was not good enough, or it was good enough and you were not the person who was going to act on it anyway, and in both of those cases what you have got is not a principle. It is a seat.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything for a moment, and Dalla Sen wrote for about four seconds and then stopped and looked at what she had written and did not read it back.

"**Entered, in the words of the man who said it,**" she said, "**and entered that the words were *a seat*, and entered that I wrote them down without improving them because I have spent this week watching a clerk improve two other people and I did not want to do it to him on the same morning.**"

Marek Venn did not say anything about it, and what he was thinking about was not the gallery.

He was thinking about a man with a shut book under his forearm who had been right four times in two days, and about the fact that the man had spent thirty-nine weeks explaining that sentences in rooms do not travel, and had been right about that too, and had been out-forgiven by a piece of printed paper and had said in this room on Monday that he had not forgiven anybody and was not going to and would like that entered. He thought that if he answered a physician in a room with a sentence, it would be a sentence, and the next sentence would be in a file, and there were four days between this room and a piece of paper and he had watched it happen in nine days.

"**Do not answer him,**" he said, instead. "**I am not going to answer him because he is right about the seat and I am not going to give the room a reason for the seat this morning, and the reason I am not is that the reason would be about me and this room has had four of those this week.**"

"**Entered, and entered that he was not asked and answered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that a man has now twice this week declined to explain himself in a room and has given a different reason each time, and that the second of those reasons is the better one and I do not know what to do with that.**"

The room went on being the room it is for the rest of the morning. The seam gave nothing. The pot stayed at two days. Petra Wend went down the nine steps at about the second hour and nobody watched her go and nobody asked her whether she was coming back and the clerk entered that she was not asked, and entered that she had come up four sets of steps on a Wednesday and had gone down them on a Friday, and entered that the difference between those two days was four days and no figure was attached to it.

And then, at about the ninth bell, Iven Tallo, who had gone back to the anvil and had not said anything for an hour, put his hand flat on the iron again and said a thing that has not been finished being understood, and he said it into a room that was not listening because it was a Thursday and the pump was not running.

"**A line is a line,**" he said. "**You have all been talking about one room of water and there is not one room of water. When a thing stands in more than one place it is not in the same place, it is on a line, and a line goes through as many rooms as it can get through, and we have been standing in the one we can get to.**"

Nobody asked him which rooms.

"**I said that on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week about four steps in a wall,**" Iven Tallo said, "**and nobody in this room asked me then and nobody is going to ask me now, and I am not going to say it again until somebody in this room has been to the other side of something.**"

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither was read out, and the reason went in beside them: a shut morning is a morning on which nothing crosses and on which a room turns something over, and a clerk who reads a total out loud while a room is turning something over is telling the room the turning is done.

Six today and three hundred and twenty-three since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and forty and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that on its own line, in the ordinary hand, and was not read, and no reason was given for that either, and the line has no heading over it and is not going to get one. Wen Pask is one hundred and fifty-two days and one hundred and fifty-two nights, forty-five weeks of a clerk saying out loud that she is going to keep writing it down. It is not going to be one hundred and fifty-three.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a physician had described a man in a room in four minutes and a carpenter had said a sentence that nobody in the room had finished arguing with, and neither of those things is a thing a board can hold, and a board is a column, and a clerk does not walk a column on a Thursday.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman named three people he could not ask on Tuesday and did not ask, and he has not yet said the reason he could ask the other two, and a reason that has not been said in a room is not a reason.

The pot is two days and the seam has now given nothing for five mornings running, and it is at four threads, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for sixteen weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and the woman from that gate went back down nine steps this afternoon with nothing, having been told why in a room, and a man in this room had been told to his face by a physician that what he has got is a seat.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor, and a trestle with a book on it and a guild's claim on it and nothing else anybody wanted to look at, and a pump that had not run for five days, and a carpenter at an anvil who had said a line is a line into a room that was not listening because it was a Thursday.
