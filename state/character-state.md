# Character State — End of Volume 02, Batch 0001

## Opening party

Six people came out of the Cinder Steps. **Five are here at the end of Volume 02, Batch 0001**, and all five appear in Chapters 51–60.

### Marek Venn

- **Position:** the Hearthhold air-gate anteroom and its lower level, the common landing, and — by named work period and named terms — the lower corridor, the school corner, and the stair's service bend, fixed first landing, and near landing.
- **Body:** Right hand permanently lost for fine control from the Chapter 5 jack crush; left hand usable and strained. **Seven written nulls**, the seventh in Chapter 54, on the corner of a slate, pocketed by Jo without being asked. Nothing usable has ever come back. **In Chapter 58 he puts his hand flat on the bench either side of the wrapped plate, gets nothing, and does not write *null* on anything, because there was nothing to write it on and he knew what it would say.**
- **Mind:** His control flaw appears three more times and is corrected three more times, and he makes one correction himself. He is stopped by **Nera Oduya** in Chapter 51, four words, when he is about to turn a keeper's stone and a number into a work; he is stopped by **Jo Verrick** in Chapter 55, four words, when he is about to offer the crew's hours to a stranger's project on a bench in his own head; and in Chapter 59 he **admits, in a full room, twice, on Sefu Aran's instruction, that he has been thinking about it at two in the morning with nobody's name on it.** Sefu's sentence is the standing one: *a man who will admit a two-o'clock plan in a room is worth ten men who have not got one.* He is corrected by Renn Vale in Chapter 60 about the obligation, and he concedes it out loud with the reason given.
- **Immediate choice:** the two springs. He has told a room there are two of them — the boundary's air hand-over conversation, which Sefu says must come first and must be with Nera Oduya, and Talla Roke's second gate — and he has promised neither. The leaving itself is unchanged: five of them, a spring conversation, a named air hand-over, and no hour of the maintenance owed.

### Leena Orr

- **Position:** litter in the anteroom, the lower corridor, and the service bend; limited clinic care access, not residence. The litter does not go past the service bend for anything on the returning stair.
- **Body:** Right shoulder dislocated in Chapter 1 and reduced in Chapter 2; slinged, no weight through the right arm, **not currently dislocated**. Four bearers and four minutes through a two foot eight inch gap on a wet floor. **In Chapter 53 she hauls at the well head on the draw's rope hitch with her left hand**, feet braced on a wet floor, tells the room on the Wednesday, and the room says nothing.
- **Mind:** Unchanged and still the structural critic. In Chapter 51 she stops a number until its method is stated — *a number without a method is a rumour with a decimal on it* — and the number that survives the room is a range rather than a figure. Her discipline stands: *a chalk line is a reference and does not move, and if the slate and my chalk ever disagree then the slate is a copy and mine is the original.*
- **Immediate need:** shoulder recovery, a dry place, and her four minutes kept in every page somebody writes.

### Iven Tallo

- **Position:** anteroom, lower corridor, common landing, the fixed east stair, the service bend, and the stair's near landing inside the cord.
- **Body:** uninjured; exhausted. Thirty-six. **Twenty-nine hours of uncompensated instruction** as last counted, plus two days of shore work he has asked Dalla Sen to keep off the front of the ledger. He prices work out loud as a matter of principle, and in this batch he prices two things: the shore, and the danger of a lawful paper.
- **Role:** He says the batch's two structural sentences. **This is not a post** — a post stands up and there is nowhere for it to stand up to, so it is a shore, and a shore is only as good as the packing at its head. And **the correct response to a guild page is nothing**: the boundary's own page was the answer, the guild's page is the first nail, no second page and no seventh line on the slate, because the day the slate gets a line added to answer a clerk it stops being a thing the boundary keeps and becomes a document the boundary defends. He also warns Talla Roke that her order is good and her order is the danger, and he identifies the actual shape of the crime: a claim on the boundary's material is a claim on the boundary.
- **Immediate tension:** a suspended-bond carpenter in a building that will not pay him, who has now built two shores he will never be paid for, who has put public-works equipment into a boundary work twice, and who has told a room that the wall he helped build is going to cost somebody something that is not written down anywhere.

### Pell Roke

- **Position:** the anteroom bench, the top of the nine steps, the stair head, on his crutch. Never on a step.
- **Body:** fourteen; left ankle sprained in Chapter 8, splinted, supported. Cannot run, take unsafe loads, cross the gallery deck, or take three steps and a lip.
- **Mind:** Three things this batch, all of them his. He is the **plinth reader** and calls the turn **eight seconds** before the wheel, and says *this time* and not *eight*. In Chapter 54 he asks, in front of the room, that nobody build a plan on him, because he cannot come off the bench and if he could he could not cross the waist, and *if anybody treats what I say as a stop then I have taught this room to be afraid of me, and I will not have that on a bench in a stair*. And in Chapter 53 he says **that is my sister** without moving, without standing, without going down the steps.
- **Family:** **Talla Roke is his older sister and it is in the prose.** The recognition is a fact about two people who were already in the same building: Pell is on a bench, the courier reads the room, Isel — now the clerk at the threshold — reads a name off the register, and he says it. They say one word to each other on the steps in Chapter 53 and do not speak again in the anteroom. At the top of nine steps in Chapter 60 they have a short conversation about a hand that opens in the eleventh week and about a courier's own unremarked hand, and they do not otherwise acknowledge each other, and Dalla Sen writes down the fact that they did not speak again after the one word. **The motif of *eleven words* belongs to the woman from the wash rooms and is not used for them.** **This may not be re-dramatised as a discovery.** His name is still in the margin of the plinth rota in Tams Quill's hand as a debt and not a duty.

### Tams Quill

- **Position:** the tally board, the count, the water record, the case board, the reading-back, and a sling on a right hand that does not close.
- **Body:** **Two crushed fingers from Chapter 37, one finger and a half of travel**, improving by an amount only somebody looking every day could swear to, and improving at about half the speed it needs. The date is on the board: **a month from a day in the seventh week is the eleventh week**, and he says it out loud in four separate rooms so that nobody can be kind to him about it. He is off the lever, the bar, the chisel, and the packing for the whole batch.
- **Role:** He invents two more tally columns — **received** and **held** — and reads them first. He reads the crew's and the boundary's hours together for the first time and finds the same finding one item longer. He puts the **nineteen days of meals** into arithmetic, on the grounds that a thing that is not hours can still be a debt. He puts **Renn Vale's own four hours** on a board and she says *it stays*. He reads Oren Sill's packing method out of the *carried* column longhand in his left hand, in every chapter of the batch, and has promised to keep reading it for as long as he is in the building. And in Chapter 57 he **refuses a bar** that Isel Ferrow puts in his left hand, having asked twice, and gives the best reason anybody gave: *when the interval came off early, the first thing that went was the hiss, and a man at a frame with a bar in his hand does not hear a hiss.*
- **Visibility:** named in every chapter of the batch.

### Oren Sill

- **Position:** dead.
- **Death:** Chapter 48, at the near landing's lip, bearing, with four people behind him and the stair turning. **Not recovered. Not converted. Not named as a living man anywhere in Chapters 51–60.** The record order is unchanged and the reason for it is Mikka Hale's.
- **How he is used in this batch:** only as a method. Tams carries his four sentences about packing a foot to bearing in a *carried* column in his own left hand, written longhand because he is frightened of losing it, and reads them aloud in Chapter 54 to a room that is about to pack a head the same way, with a knee, because there is no wedge. **Jo Verrick says out loud that she learned it off a man who is under a stone eleven feet down.**

## Nera Oduya

- **Position:** the valve, the stair-side grille, the clinic return channel, the school corner's floor, the stair's new air, and — once, in Chapter 58 — the fixed first landing, with the board and the cloth, declared in advance.
- **Authority:** technical authority over airflow, air testing, and safe operation of the air gate. Unchanged, independent, and never folded into a resident's consent or a keeper's veto. She **refuses to certify the stair's air**, keeps a watch on it, and **still refuses to publish a rate for the dead window and nobody has asked her to.**
- **The batch's work:** she decides that what *once* means is not hers to decide, and hands it to the room. She writes the **fourth air document** — a method for one work, with *spent* on it and the number three in her own hand — and then **applies its own term against herself** when the draw fails, because the work has not ended. She loses the delivery check to five foot nine of timber. She carries the air valve watch two floors down for the first time in eleven weeks, declares it out loud before she goes, and has it written that it is not to be done again without her in writing. She hears a **door in a shaft** and refuses four things on the strength of one method: not to go down, not to publish, not to take the watch off, not to certify anything. And she claims her own cost in Chapter 57 rather than letting the room carry it: **a watch is not a smaller certificate, it is a debt that gets paid in somebody's day.**
- **Relationship:** no intimacy, no recognition scene, no accelerated beat. Their Chapter 57 exchange is her claiming a cost, not his. **One marked viewpoint shift into her in Chapter 58** is the batch's only shift and it changes four decisions in the room.

## Sefu Aran

- **Position:** the civic wheel, the deeper gate, the threshold, the cord, the black cord's hook, and the boundary's stores.
- **Role:** He **names a work** in Volume 02, in his own words, after saying that the naming can be struck out loud by whoever he names it to — and the work he names is not the one the crew wanted, because he cannot get them a post. He **fires his own closure clause for the first time**, on the shore, and reads out what the resulting line sounds like: a Hearthhold worked on its own route and shut it and did all of it with people who live there. He **refuses to let a second page be written** against the guild's, holds that the first spring is a hand-over conversation whether or not anybody is leaving, makes Marek admit a private plan out loud twice, and asks for the leaving sentence to be read rather than paraphrased. He is the one who asks every threshold-bell holder in person what the bell is for.
- **Relationship to Nera:** deliberately separate authority, still the reason the boundary is defensible, and in this batch he says so out loud in front of a courier — the first spring is a conversation with the air engineer, and the air engineer is not going to be impressed by a man who has been talking to a courier.

## Hearthhold boundary maintainers

- **Renn Vale:** keeper of the shore, the road, and the lead line. In this batch she **refuses, in advance and in her own hand, to be on the landing while the lip is packed** and will not be asked; she **refuses a Cartwright clerk permission to observe a crossing** and refuses to withdraw either half of the answer; she gives the room the meaning of the word *once* in a sentence that turns out to be the batch's whole institutional argument; she writes the route's **seventh term**; she has her own four hours put on a board and says *it stays*; and she says she will not be the one to declare a boundary she has only just started believing she is allowed to have. Her sixth-watch position is **four days in seven, unchanged**; her Chapter 38 stair-watch refusal is a **wider** refusal of the same thing and is **not withdrawn**; she has **still refused the crew access to the woman from the wash rooms** and the batch honoured that absolutely. She has said twice, in the same words, that her mother's piece is a stone in a hole and is not a code.
- **Dalla Sen:** repair ledger, route record, work-bell relay, cost witness, and the person who asks who checks the tally. In this batch she **does not become a reason**: when the draw fails she reads the air engineer's own line back off the card rather than rewriting it, and says *I am the clerk, I do not get to be the reason, I get to be the mouth.* She records the refusal/withdrawal distinction she was asked to record and adds her own hand under the courier's. She **refuses to write *pending* on a question.** She reads the whole ledger out, item by item, to a courier who asked for it. She strikes *finished* out of her own entry at Nera's request, and strikes *noted* and *attention*. She asked for the sister fact to be entered with a time on it, and for the fact of the two of them **not** speaking to be entered with a time on it.
- **Jo Verrick:** trial pump maintainer with **five paid cleanings** and an estimate of **two or fewer** in her own hand, unmoved in eleven weeks, and now with **no check left** — a flat hiss read by ear with a two-note vocabulary. She is the instrument in this batch: she made the lip measurable, gave a **range** because she cannot tell the stone from her own string, **named the price of the timber out loud before anybody asked**, went down the well, reported the tiled floor and the peg holes, packed the shore's head with her knee, said out loud that a finger and a half is not a fix, **stopped Marek in four words**, refused to feed a courier's work, and said the seat goes before the cold and named the three things she does not have.
- **Mikka Hale:** kitchen, clinic return watch, pump frame, and meals. She refuses to feed the work and says so out loud, takes the water mark so that a bell-holder is not doing two things, and in Chapter 59 **says the cost of a courier's withdrawal out loud on the record**: four households in the second gate carrying water up four sets of steps this winter. She still will not feed stair work and still has the cracked flue wall she chose to watch.
- **Ilsa Norr (nine children and herself, which is ten, and twelve on a market day):** In this batch she **asks the volume's question out loud** — *who keeps the Hearthhold rooms* — and says why it has never been asked, and gets no answer. She makes Jo's reading a range. She insists a witness's words go on the page as a witness's words and not as a clerk's line. She **refuses the register boxes again**, in her own words, in front of the same people, and gives the reason and a **date in three weeks** and the terms, one of which is that the crew will not be in the room. She refuses a day of school a second time and says it is a day the nine children do not get back.
- **Isel Ferrow:** the first reader of a stair in the Hearthhold. In this batch he is the **bearer at the near lip** on the shore and on the Chapter 57 crossing, and his best moment is the one where **he does not leave the lip**, because a bearer is there for the whole of a crossing. He also **offers Tams a bar** twice, is refused twice, and asks for both to be written down.
- **Ruk Sella:** the plinth reader in this batch, and the threshold-bell deputy for the draw, asked in person and repeating the terms back. Her count of the threshold-bell deputy now stands at four across the manuscript.
- **Bel Ordry (new):** of the two rooms above the clinic return, thirty-five, has held nothing in eleven weeks, and is the first person in this building to **ask what a bell is for before he answers whether he wants it.** He is the threshold-bell holder for the shore and for the Chapter 57 crossing, repeating the whole of it back. He goes down to the fixed first landing as the bearer when Nera takes the air valve watch to the lip, because **a bearer at the near lip is a person of this boundary.** He then takes the bar against the throat's north-east cheek every morning at the second bell and writes down the answer whatever it is, **including nothing**, and says out loud that that is a real thing he has written down and that it is going to be on the page the day after tomorrow and the day after that, and that the day somebody hears something is the day somebody knows he has been there every morning doing nothing and doing it properly.
- **The woman from the wash rooms:** **she is asked before she is needed.** Renn Vale asks her at the top of three steps on the morning, in her own hand with a time on it, and she says yes and asks that the asking and the answer be in Renn's hand and not her own, because she would not want it in her hand where it looked like a favour. She holds the string. And she shuts the route for a reason nobody would have thought of — *Jo's hiss has gone thin and I am not a pump hand and I do not know what a thin hiss is, and that is exactly why I am shutting it* — and says it out loud instead of writing it, because she can do both and the harder one is better. It is the first use of the closure clause for a pump rather than a school bell, and it cost a half-day of Jo's shutter route and a day of Ilsa's school, and it was right.

## Dael Harrow, Corva Nye, and the Cartwright pressure

Dael Harrow is still not present. His route-custody order is unchanged and still cannot open the Hearthhold gate, order air, create consent, or establish ownership. **The guild has now been told, in writing, by somebody competent.** **Corva Nye**, a Cartwright route-custody clerk out of the Marrowgate clearance office, came down the middle passage on foot as an **inventory witness** attached to a courier's lawful written order, was entered in the boundary's ledger on the clerk's own authority as **not a person of the boundary, no watch, no rota**, and asked the only question her form allows. She wrote the boundary's answer in the margin of her own form in her own hand, said her office will read it as a route with nobody on it, and asked for that to be on the record because a form with no place to write a keeper has no place but a holder. She then **asked properly to observe a crossing and was refused**, and wrote that down too. **Her report went up.** She is **not** an antagonist, **not** Dael Harrow, and **not** Cassian Rook: she is a competent, sincere, bounded official who did her job properly and went home, and she must not be turned into one.

## Talla Roke

- **Age:** twenty-two. Courier, salvage picker, and route scout out of the middle gate. Four years on the road, eleven Hearthholds, nine of them slept in.
- **Arrival:** the third morning of Volume 02, at half past the fifth bell, down nine steps, with a satchel, a folded paper and a leather case, and a clerk eleven minutes behind her.
- **Order:** lawful, witnessed, and exactly the paper Iven Tallo asked for in Chapter 12 — the parapet slab and the bulk public-works stores to the Middle water-lock, received by a named keeper, moved by a courier's own hands, costs carried by the Middle. It carried a clerk's mark and not a name.
- **Withdrawal:** **in her own hand, in the anteroom, in the eleventh week**, after the tally, on the bench end with her back to the wall, having asked for it to be recorded in her words and not the clerk's. Her stated reason: the moment that slab carries a load in a Hearthhold work it is material in use under a guild lien, and a claim on the boundary's material is a claim on the boundary. Her second reason is about her own trade: a courier's whole trade is that a thing claimed by nobody can be carried by anybody, and writing her order into a boundary's book puts a second lock on the same door.
- **The distinction she asked for and got recorded:** a **refusal** is the clerk's and stands and can be appealed; a **withdrawal** is the courier's and can be made again by anybody with a mark.
- **The cost she named before anybody could be gentle with her:** four households in the second gate carrying water up four sets of steps this winter. She asked for somebody else to say it out loud and write it, and Mikka Hale did.
- **What she asked for instead:** the crew's hours, by name, for a named work, in the spring, for the second gate's inlet. **Denied for this year**, on Sefu Aran's grounds that the first spring is the keeper's conversation with the air engineer about a named hand-over and it happens whether or not anybody is leaving.
- **What she knows that nobody else in the room does:** she has never seen a vault in eleven Hearthholds; every plan she has carried off the middle gate's survey shelf is drawn like a house, with doors drawn on hinges and a line that means a level and not a treasure; a latch in a shaft means a doorway, and a plan with a door on hinges was drawn by somebody standing in a building like that. **She is the batch's professional witness to the volume's question and she cannot answer it either.**
- **Family:** **Pell Roke's older sister.** Stated in Chapter 53, entered in the ledger with a time, and used in Chapter 60 as one word on the steps and a short conversation at the top of nine steps. **It is not a reunion and it must not be re-dramatised as one.**

## The stair, the plate, and the evidence

The stair is **restricted, corded, and in service on its near side only**, with a line-block that is a running block, a line in it made up in three turns and a seizing beside Renn's mother's stop piece which was not taken out and not built on, and **two timber shores on the common landing**. The nearer one is five foot nine of public-works timber with its inventory number chalked on the end, packed at the head with sized wood and a knee, **with no wedge in it**, standing four foot two back from where the near landing's stone beds. **Four treads go down behind the rail on the far side and nobody has been on them and they are not walked this month.** The return is quicker. The band is named as line wear and is otherwise unexplained. The far wall of the throat is eleven feet down and has never been seen. **And a wooden knock with a slide on the end of it came out of it in Chapter 58, and an air push out of the throat's north-east cheek at knee height, and neither is explained.** The **wrapped empty-seat plate** lies in a knotted bundle of work cloth in the crew's hands, unexplained, unidentified, unused, unclaimed, and **unopened**, and in Chapter 58 Marek put his hand flat on the bench either side of it and got nothing.

## Relationship and power guardrails

- No harem structure and no instant romance. Nera remains an independent air engineer and civic adult. **One marked viewpoint shift in the batch, into Nera in Chapter 58, carrying distinct interior knowledge and changing four decisions.** No intimacy, no recognition scene, and no acceleration.
- **No formal loadcraft stage, bearing mark, repeatable reading, route change, binding, relay, or automatic repair has been granted.** Seven nulls, all written down, all giving nothing usable.
- The five Room Checks and the declared-occupancy discipline remain the growth framework. **The anteroom's rating is unchanged for the second volume running, on Nera's explicit instruction: the route changes the escape and it does not change the occupancy.**
- The working practice — the person who will keep a room writes the change to it — held under pressure again and produced the batch's best work: a keeper's seventh term, a courier's withdrawal, a clerk's margin, a teacher's dated refusal with terms, a bell-holder's record of *nothing*, and a woman's reason for a shut.
