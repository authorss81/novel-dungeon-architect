# Chapter 94 — Where The Work Is Known

Six lines of the third column were open, and the plan had been out of Bel Ordry's coat for four days, which is longer than it has ever been out of that coat, and he had it in both hands and did not put it down for a quarter of an hour, and Dalla Sen entered that he had held it for a quarter of an hour and had not asked to put it down.

"Six of the nine are open," he said, "and the fourth column is open, and I have not been able to put it down. **I have kept a page for four days and I am going to read the whole of it out in the order it is in, and the people who wrote the lines are going to stand up and say yes, and the ones who did not are going to say the word *not mine*, and I am not going to read a line and have the room nod at it.**"

---

Three lines were already filled, and he read all three, and the room heard them, and they were the same three it had heard on the Friday of the seventeenth week and nobody asked for them again and that is exactly the point of a page you can read twice.

Then he came to the open ones.

**Jo Verrick's was the first open line he filled, and she stood up to hear it, and she had clearly written it in advance because it was in her own hand and the hand was a carpenter's.**

"**Found on the second day by a man counting pails, nine days late and in his own words. Located in the haunch of the outer service door in dressed stone and lime mortar, four threads and a stain the shape of a leaf. Tested three days, three vessels, three hours, one of them taken with the pump off and the only one of the three that is a reading of the water at all. Recorded in five hands and one word struck out in a sixth. In use from the sixth bell on the third day, and not before. The page says no colour, no smell, not drunk, and not *clean*, and *clean* is struck in a cook's hand and I wrote it first.**"

"**That is mine,**" said Jo Verrick. "**And I am not going to have it on the page without the two jars a day, because a page that says *in use* and does not say how much is a page that gets over-read in a month.** Two jars. Maybe three. **And the two are not the pot and the pot is not the two.**"

"Two jars, in your words," said Bel Ordry, "and the cook's separate number, and the clerk has it."

**Second came Renn Vale's, and she read it herself from the landing without coming down, and it is the shortest line on the page.**

"**The outer service door came off its four pins at the third bell on the second day and went back on at the sixth bell on the third. Behind it is packed with stone and clay and it is holding and it is not fixed. The anteroom's rating is restored with the word *door* out loud in a room and read back by the clerk, and until that word is said the outer service door is not in the rating. Six people went down nine steps to the lower level with a litter on the second day while it was off.**"

"That is mine," said Renn Vale, "**and I am not going to have the words *the door was* anything else than what they are, because if that page ever says the door was shut and it was not, then the eighth term and the page are both lies and at least one of them will be believed.**"

**Third was Ilsa Norr's, and she had it written out, and it is the only line on the page with a school in it.**

"**The school ran on the third day of the eighteenth week at the school end of the corridor with the door shut at our backs and the anteroom shut, and nine of us and the teacher, and the wet floor, and the teacher, and no second way out. It ran. It was a worse room than the anteroom and I am not going to have it written as though we were moved.**"

"**It is written as *moved* and I would like it changed,**" said Nera Oduya.

"It is not *moved*," said Ilsa Norr. "**I have been moved four times in nine months and every time somebody wrote *moved* it meant that the room was the same and I was the problem, and this time the room was the problem and I was in it, and those are different sentences and only one of them is true.**"

Nobody argued with her, and Dalla Sen read the correction back in the clerk's hand with the line struck, because a page that strikes a teacher's word without reading back the strike has corrected nothing, and the anteroom had learned that from a cook three days ago.

**Fourth was Nera Oduya's, and she gave it from three steps, and it is the only line on the page carrying the words *I will not*, three times, and she read all three out loud because she wanted them heard.**

"**The school corner's mouth was opened a hand's width at the fourth bell on the third day with the pump stopped, and shut at the fifth. The air out of it is warm and it goes into the throat and not out of it, and I had the direction of that air wrong in every account I have given since the collapse, and that is entered. A cold moving current comes off four treads and I cannot say where it goes. I will not call either of them ventilation. I will not call either of them a shaft. I am not publishing a rate.**"

"That is mine and I am aware it is a line on a page that says *I will not* three times, and I have thought about that for two days and I am still putting it there, **because those are the only three things in this Hearthhold I can be held to, and if they are not on a page then they are on nothing, and the day I stop saying them out loud is the day they have gone.**"

---

Then the two rows that stayed as they were, and Bel Ordry read them himself and the room did not argue, which is now the finding.

On the page the **wash rooms** have a row and an empty third column and Renn Vale's reason for it, which is the woman's sentence, in the clerk's hand, with no name against it, and the woman is not in this room and was not asked.

**Ninth: no row at all,** refused by two women for two reasons in two hands, and the reasons were in the book and the row was not, and that is a document doing the hardest thing a document can do, which is *not* writing something that everybody agrees should be written.

For the **anteroom**: the highest count on the page, the condition *wet*, and a third column that was no longer empty.

"It is not a person," said Bel Ordry, before anybody could ask, and he said it to the anteroom rather than to the clerk. "**It is a door, a packing, a rating and a term about a word. And I said on the Friday of the seventeenth week that the day somebody fills that row with the word *person* the page is finished and it is wrong, and whoever does it has to be in the room. Nobody has written that word, and I have read the whole of the row out, and I would like the clerk to enter that I read it and did not find it.**"

"Entered," said Dalla Sen, "and **the count's own reason for the row is still on it: the anteroom's work is a knee, and the knee is Jo Verrick's, and the knee is not in the column either, and I do not know where a knee goes and I am not going to invent a column for it.**"

---

Then the fourth column, and it had been empty and unheaded for four days and there were now three reasons in three hands for it staying that way, and Bel Ordry put the sheet flat and put his hand flat on it and would not move his hand, which is the second time in four days that anybody in this room has laid a hand on a piece of paper like that.

"**One,**" he said. "A column with a name on the top of it is a heading, and a heading makes a thing a unit, and a unit is a thing a room can be praised in."

"**Two,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing. "A column of *will not* is a list of things this boundary has promised not to do, and a promise is a promise whatever the tense."

"**Three,**" said Nera Oduya. "In about nine days a man is going to come down four days of road with a piece of paper, and the first thing he will do with a page like this is look for the column that says what we will not do, because that is the column he can carry up a road and read out at a counter. **A headed fourth column is a road out of this building. That is all it is.**"

"And the count," said Tams Quill, from the bench, with his right hand open in his lap, "has a different reason again and has had it a fortnight, and I am not going to give it, and I would like somebody to notice that the keeper has just come round to my reason and did it in one sentence and in the wrong order, and that I am not going to be pleased about it in front of anybody."

Then Bel Ordry did the thing nobody had expected, which was to ask for a piece of chalk.

"No," said Dalla Sen. "**I am not going to give you the chalk, and I would like it entered that the keeper of this page asked the clerk for the chalk and the clerk said no in front of the room.**"

"Then read it," he said, "and then somebody else writes it, and I will hold the sheet, and I will not write on it myself, because a keeper who writes the reason for the thing he cannot do is a keeper with the reason in his own hand, and in four years that is a man who wrote himself out of a column."

He gave the reason to Renn Vale and not to the clerk, which was deliberate, and Dalla Sen let it be deliberate and entered that it was.

Renn Vale wrote it, and it is in the page in her hand, and she read it out.

"**The fourth column is empty. Nothing has been put in it. It has no heading. This is true as at the day in the top corner. The keeper of this page is permitted to put something in it and the person who does it has to be in the room, and no promise is made here by anybody that it will stay empty, and none has been asked for.**"

Nera Oduya objected to one word in it before it was accepted, and she objected from three steps, and she was right.

"**None.** Take it out. *No promise is made here by anybody* is a promise, and I have spent three weeks in this room refusing to write promises on paper because a promise on paper is an obligation somebody can call in forty days from now, and I have just written one and put my name nowhere near it. **The column is empty and nobody has promised it will stay empty. Those are two different sentences and the second one is a thing a judge can be given and the first one is a thing a person can be caught at.**"

Renn Vale looked at it for a while and then said, "**That is right and it is better and I am not going to argue with the air engineer about a modal at the fifth bell.**"

In the end the word was struck and what took its place was written in Nera Oduya's own hand, and the page carries it in two hands, and the two hands agree, and it is the first line on this page that anybody in this building has ever corrected and left corrected.

---

Tams Quill was asked at the sixth bell, by the clerk, whether the board would be read on the Friday.

"**No day,**" he said. "**I am not going to name a day this time. I have read this boundary's figures for fifteen weeks and every time one of us has put a day on a promise, the day has had to be defended like a person, and I would rather be slow in a room than defended in one. I will read it when I have walked it.**"

"And the crew's is forty-eight and a half," said Dalla Sen, "and it has been for ten days, and it is not going to move this week, and nobody should pretend to anybody that it has."

"It will move on Friday," said Marek Venn, from the bench, and then stopped, because the count looked at him and the count had said no day, and Marek Venn took it back in the same breath and in front of everybody.

"I have been in this building eighteen weeks," he said, "and I have said *on Friday* out loud, in this room, about six times, and I do not know one of those six days that anything came. **I have been putting my hands on a piece of work to see whether it would hold and then I have been putting my hands on a calendar.** That is the same hand. I would like it in the book that the man who said *on Friday* on the fourth day of the eighteenth week was the man who spent this morning reading a column that has six empty lines in it and calling it a plan."

"Entered in his own words," said Dalla Sen, "**and I would like it noted that he took it back before the count said anything, and the count had not said anything, and the count was going to let him have it.**"

"**I was going to let him have it,**" said Tams Quill, "**because I have wanted to hear a date said out loud in this room by somebody else since the twelfth week.**"
