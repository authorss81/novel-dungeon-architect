# Chapter 191 — The Day That Went By

The sixth day of the thirty-sixth week is a Saturday, and the corridor is open, and the school ran for the thirty-eighth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end. Nine children went up the nine steps at the first bell and nine came down them at the fourth, and they went round the corner of the anteroom floor, which is what they have done every morning since Friday and which is now a thing nine children do without being told.

Nothing in this building is a market on a sixth day. There is no market in it, nobody in it went anywhere to see one, and the only fact about a sixth day that reached this room came in Nella Bray's mouth at the first bell, and it was a fact about counters.

"**Counters close early on a sixth day,**" she had said, "**and they close early because there is nothing to count. I have worked six years of sixth days and I could not tell you one thing that happens in an office on a sixth day that does not happen on a fifth with less light. And I am telling you it because in about a year somebody in an office is going to look for a thing they did on a sixth day and there is going to be nothing in the tray, and that will not be because anybody refused. It will be because it was a sixth day, and nobody writes that down.**"

Dalla Sen had entered that as a fact about counters and not as a finding, on Saturday of the week before, and had written *not a finding* in the line on purpose.

Renn Vale watched the line in the party wall at the first bell and came back and said what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the clerk entered it as a figure of nothing and did not improve it.

"**A figure of nothing, three days running,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am going to say in a room that I have started to like it, and that liking it is the first thing I have liked about the work in a fortnight, and that a clerk who likes a figure of nothing is a clerk who has stopped asking her own figures to do work.**"

"**Then stop,**" said Bel Ordry from the step.

"**I am not going to,**" said Dalla Sen. "**I am going to say it out loud, which is the whole of the practice.**"

Then Iven Tallo put the chisel down, which is what he does before he says a thing he means, and asked the question nobody in the room had put in a week.

"**What does a boundary do about a day that has gone by,**" he said, "**on which a man was expected to walk in and look at three rooms and did not?**"

The anteroom was quiet for about four strokes of the pump. It was the first time in six days that a sentence in this room had begun with a question about the office rather than about the wall.

"**I will answer it and I will answer it badly, and then somebody will answer it properly and I would like mine entered first so that nobody can pretend the room went straight to the good answer,**" said Nella Bray.

She came the length of the anteroom and stood on the trestle side of it, where she has stood twice this week and not on any other day.

"**A thing this building does about that day is a counter-schedule,**" she said. "**That is the word for it and I have not invented it and I would like the clerk to write it down. A counter-schedule is a schedule. And a thing which is scheduled is not a thing which is refused, and that sentence has been in that clerk's book since the thirtieth week with a man's name against it, and I have been in this room five days and I have watched every one of you get comfortable behind it, including the woman who has been refusing to write a word for a week, and I would like the room to notice that a good sentence can be a place to stand that is not facing anything.**"

Nobody improved on that, and Renn Vale did not, which is how the room knows it landed.

"**And the rest of it,**" said Renn Vale, "**because you have said the first half and the first half is cheap.**"

"**A schedule is three things,**" said Nella Bray. "**A day. A column. And a person who will be present. If you make those three, you have lost, and you have lost in front of the only people who can read what you made. And if you make none of those three, then what have you made.**"

"**Then that is the whole of the argument and it is a good one,**" said Renn Vale, "**and I have not got an answer to it and I have been not getting an answer to it since Wednesday.**"

"**I have got the half against waiting,**" said Bel Ordry, and came down one step, "**and it is the only thing I have got, and it is not much, and I am going to say it before anybody tells me to. A day that has gone by is the only day this boundary can still do something about. Every other day is a day it has to be ready for. That day is gone and it cannot be refused and it cannot be accepted and it cannot be entered, and if a room cannot do anything at all about a day like that then the day was somebody else's all along and we only stood in it.**"

"**That is the best thing anybody has said in this room this week,**" said Renn Vale, "**and it is also the exact shape of a trap, and I would like somebody to tell me which.**"

"**They are the same shape,**" said Bel Ordry. "**That is what a good argument is.**"

Then Dalla Sen put her hand flat on the slate, which she does not do, and said the thing the room had been circling for three days.

"**Then it is not a day,**" she said. "**It is a morning. Mornings are what this building has. We have had one of those every day for thirty-five weeks and we have never once written one down, and a day of the week in a column is a thing an office can act on and a morning is not.**"

"**And the rest of it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**The rest of it is that we do not appoint anything,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I want the reason in the space where a reason goes, and the reason is not a principle. The reason is that a thing you appoint is a thing you can be late for. If we call a morning and name it, then a morning that did not happen is a morning this building failed to keep, and this building has just spent six weeks finding out that it cannot keep a promise about a day of the week, and I am not going to do it again on purpose with a day of our own.**"

"**Then nobody is told,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Nobody is told,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am entering that, and entering the cost of it, and the cost is the real one and I would like it in the same sentence. We cannot prepare for it. We cannot tell the five that asked for four and a half hours, and we cannot tell the two crews, and a thing nobody is told about is a thing anybody who was not told can refuse, and that includes a foreman who has already refused us once this week for a reason he says he cannot find.**"

"**And you cannot be ready for it,**" said Renn Vale.

"**I cannot be ready for it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I have spent thirty-five weeks in a building where being ready was the whole of my trade.**"

"**Then there is one more thing and nobody has said it,**" said Bel Ordry, and he said it to the floor rather than to the room. "**A thing that is not written down is a thing that did not happen. I have spent this week sending a true fact about a piece of timber up four days on somebody else's paper, because a fact that is held back is a thing that kills somebody in a year. And now you are proposing to do a morning and write nothing, in a building that has just learned what a fact needs in order to travel. I am not saying it cannot be right. I am saying that we will not find out for a long time, and I would like the room to know that it has chosen not to find out.**"

"**Then the half against that is mine and I am not going to soften it,**" said Renn Vale, "**and it is that this boundary has spent thirty-five weeks being the only room in this Hearthhold where a thing that happened has a place where it happened. That is the whole of what a clerk is. A kitchen is where bread happened and a schoolroom is where nine children were taught and a landing is where a man put a bar under a stone. And you are asking me to put this morning in a room with no corner of it in it, and I am going to do it, and I would like it entered that I think it is the bravest thing anybody has asked of this room and the stupidest, and I do not know which.**"

Emrys Callow had been at the end of the bench with his hands on his knees for the whole of it, and he answered the cost before the room had finished saying it.

"**Then do not tell us,**" he said. "**I have been in this room six days being told things by people who are not obliged to and I have acted on exactly one of them, which was about a piece of timber with a finger of its head gone. So I will do whatever the seventh day turns out to contain. I will not like it. I will not tell anybody in this room that I did not like it, and I would like that entered, because a crew that says afterwards what it thought of a morning it was not told about is a crew that was in the morning.**"

"**That is the first thing anybody has said today that I believe,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**It is the first thing anybody has said today that costs the person saying it,**" said Bel Ordry.

Marek Venn had spent the week learning the days of the week in a private way that nobody had asked him to learn them in, the way a man learns a timetable before he is told whether he is travelling. The first day was shut. The second was shut. The third was open and nine children went through the anteroom twice. The fourth was shut. The fifth was open and a stone came out of a floor. The sixth was open and nine children went through the anteroom twice. And the seventh day was not a crossing day, and there was nothing at all on it he could do, and he had known that since the first day of the thirty-sixth week without anybody telling him.

So there was going to be a morning in this building that he did not know about and had not been asked for and could not prepare for, and every other morning in thirty-five weeks he had wanted to be in it. He had wanted to be in this one too, and the wanting had nothing to do with the wall this time, and he had no idea what to do with a want that has no work in it.

Sefu Aran had been at the head of the nine steps through all of it and came down two, which is where he stops now.

"**Somebody is going to ask me whether an elected keeper may call a morning,**" he said, "**and I am going to answer it before it is asked properly. No. That is not a thing an elected keeper of this boundary does, and I have been here nine days and I have not done it once, and if I did it on the seventh day it would be the first time in nine days and everybody in this room would know what it meant.**"

"**It would mean you had found something,**" said Nella Bray.

"**It would mean I had found nothing and had decided to hold a morning about it,**" said Sefu Aran, "**which is what the four of us have been doing for six weeks and I am not going to be the fifth and the first one to do it on a Sunday.**"

Tams Quill had been at the end of the bench for the whole of it with his reading hand closed round a piece of chalk he had not used, and he had asked for nothing, and now he was asked.

"**Is the morning going on the board,**" said Dalla Sen, who had not wanted to ask and asked.

"**No,**" said Tams Quill.

"**Reason.**"

"**I have got one and I have been waiting all week to give it,**" he said. "**This is the fourth thing I have been asked to put on that board in a fortnight and it is the first one I have wanted to refuse.**"

"**Then put the other half on the table,**" said Dalla Sen, with the slate still turned over.

"**A board is a column.**" He put the chalk down on his knee. "**And the whole of what this building has done about that day is that it did not put it in a column. There is no day in it. There is no column over it. There is no name in it anywhere, and I have read eighteen entries on this board and not one of them is a person, and there is a reason for that and it is my reason and I have given it eleven times and I am not going to give it again. So the first time this building does a thing out loud instead of on a page, the first thing anybody could do is write it on a piece of slate, and then it is a column, and then the person who reads this board in a year finds a thing here that this building did in a room and thinks it was a plan.**"

"**And who reads it in a year,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Me,**" said Tams Quill, "**or whoever stands here after me with the same right hand.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in his words, and entered that a man who has refused three nineteenth entries with three different reasons has refused a fourth for a reason that is about himself, and that he said so before anybody asked him to, and that I am entering the shape of it and not the shape of anything else.**"

The anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and the pump was attended and stopped on a rising water line that has not moved in six weeks.

At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's figure and the total and did not read them, and the reason was entered too, and the reason was that a room had just agreed to do a morning it could not name and a clerk does not stand up and read a total in front of a thing nobody in the room can see the shape of. Two today and two hundred and seventy-six since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and ninety-three and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count was read at one hundred and twenty-six and it is not going to be one hundred and twenty-seven, and she has said in thirty-five weeks running that she is going to keep writing it, and nobody has thanked her.

The board was walked at the sixth bell at eighteen entries, nine in the ordinary column and nine in the column with nothing over the top of it, and the column is not headed, and no reason for it was given and none was asked for.

The crew are at seventy-five and a half and have not moved since the fourth day, and the reason is in the book, and the reason is that a clerk who has agreed to do something she cannot name is not going to hand five people a second unfamiliar thing on the morning after she did it.

The pot is three days and the seam gave nothing on Friday, and the four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody.

Then the six people who are not this building's went up to the common landing with their packs, and behind them the anteroom was a room with a hole in the corner of its floor and nine steps going up into a passage that is shut today and will not be a crossing day tomorrow. The wall on the far side of it had five and a half shores on one side, a brace of a school's own beech on the other, and a line of nothing about two fingers wide on that other side, and a woman who keeps a corridor had an hour of the seventh day standing in front of her like a piece of ground nobody had spread anything under, and she had not yet decided what it was for.
