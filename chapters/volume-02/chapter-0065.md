# Chapter 65 — Both Columns

The review was held at the first work-bell on the morning after the assessor went, and Dalla Sen put a chair at the end of it and wrote on the chair's back with chalk, because an empty chair in a room is a thing people fill with an opinion.

"Nobody sits there," she said. "It is here so that a person reading this in a year knows the room had a place for a Cartwright route-custody assessor in it and did not use it."

It took fifty minutes and it was the shortest review in eleven weeks, and the reason it was short was that the room had spent a fortnight learning to say a thing once.

---

"**One. The throat.** Four mornings of a bar against the north-east cheek, at the second bell, by a person, by hand. Three answers of *nothing* and one answer that the person who gave it cannot classify, and the shape of it recorded and no classification in either direction, and the temperature of the throat recorded, and no conclusion.

"I am not asking anybody to change it," said Nera Oduya. "I am asking that it is written as what it is, which is a bar and a wall and a man in the cold, and that when the word *knock* appears on that page in a year somebody will have four weeks of *nothing* in front of them and will not be able to make anything of it without asking me, and I will still be here, and I will answer."

"Seconded," said Renn Vale, "and I want one thing added and I want it in my hand. If it turns out to be a door, then it is a door in a building I keep, and nobody goes down it, and that is not a decision anybody in this room is allowed to make on the day. It is a decision that is made now, in the cold, by the people who live here, on the record, so that in the summer nobody can stand on the common landing and say the words *we did not know*."

"Written," said Dalla Sen, "in Renn Vale's hand, with the time on it."

"**Two. The check.** The vocabulary is withdrawn. A hiss is a hiss and a hiss is thinner and in this cold they are the same note, and the instrument the room has been given to read the pump is a man with nothing in his hands and a water line in a dip he can see from the frame."

"And the pump runs attended," said Jo Verrick, "two people, from the second bell to the fifth, and Isel Ferrow is one of them and he is losing a shutter route he is paid on, and I have said that in front of Dalla and I am not going to say it again, and the draw is stopped by hand when the water in that dip is at the line, and the line is chalked on the frame by Leena Orr because she does not go down three steps and a chalk line is a reference and does not move."

"Seconded," said Ilsa Norr.

"**Three. The six minutes.** The shaft has stopped being a chimney in the cold. The terms on Renn's slate are unchanged and were written for the worst case and the worst case is now a six minutes in which the water in that dip comes up under a corridor two foot eight wide that stands on the edge of the shaft, and there is nobody standing at that low end, and there is not anybody available to stand there."

He came to that and stopped, and read it out again, and the room heard it twice, and nobody filled the space after it.

"**That is one of the two things this review is not deciding,**" Nera said. "I am not deciding it. I have said for eleven weeks that I will not publish a rate and I am not going to publish a person, and if I name a person this week then in a month I will have named a person in a worse week, and the reason I will have done it is that the water came up. So: recorded, unresolved, and it is going to sit on this page with no name against it for as long as it has no name, and if somebody wants to come to that page in six weeks and put a name on it, I would like them to have to come past the line that is already on it."

"**Four. The form.** Form nine C, route disposition, three dispositions and a person to fill one, and the boundary's answer is the answer the carpenter of this building gave in this room on the fourth day, which is nothing. There is no second page. There is no seventh line on that slate. There is no letter to Marrowgate and no clerk's note in the margin of anything, and the route stands as unattended on a register forty days away, and no person may add a name to that, and I would like the last part written down as a term of this building and not as a policy."

"Who is going to say no if somebody brings a second form up those steps," said Bel Ordry.

"Dalla," said Sefu Aran. "And I will be standing next to her when she does, and I will not be the one who says it, because the day a keeper starts saying no to paper is the day a keeper starts keeping a file."

---

"**Five,**" said Mikka Hale, from the frame, and did not come to the middle of the room, and everybody let her not come to the middle of the room. "That is not on your list, and I am putting it on, and it is a day old, and it is going to be worse on Friday and it is the reason I did not sleep.

"The fill at the waist is washed-out stuff. That is what it is — it is not stone and it was never stone, and whoever packed it packed it out of what came out of the dip, and it has been taking water for nine weeks and it has been taking frost for three days, and on Tuesday the frost took a set in it about the height of a hand, and this morning it let go.

"It went down about a hand's breadth along the left side, at the shallowest point, which is the point where the corridor is two foot eight and the shaft is eleven foot underneath it and the two are not one below the other, they are eleven feet below and eight inches beside." She held her hand up flat. "Not a collapse. A slump. And it has slumped under nine children's feet and a clinic's and a household's in nine weeks, and it did not complain, and it is not going to complain.

"**And there is no packing for it, and there never will be, because the packing that would have gone into the waist went out of the anteroom's four foot eleven and into a shore's head on the common landing in the tenth week.** Both of those are in Tams Quill's column. Both of them are in his column on the same page, and I am not saying it to be clever. I am saying it because on Friday somebody is going to stand on that waist and I want it entered by the man who spent it."

Nobody in the anteroom looked at Tams Quill, which was the correct thing to do and cost everybody in the room something.

"I would like it read out of the board and into the review," said Dalla Sen, "in his own words, and with the two lines side by side and not merged, because a column that merges two spends into one spend is a column that has begun to look better than it is."

And Tams Quill read out the two lines, one in each hand, and they were in the *spent* column, six weeks apart, and they were the same wood, and he read them and put the chalk down and said, "Yes. That is mine. Put it in the review in those words and put my name under it, and do not let anybody be kind to me about it, because I have been the man with the board in this room for eleven weeks and I have been proud of the board and the board is two lines on the same page that I did not put next to each other, and that is the whole of what a board is and I am not going to pretend otherwise."

---

The tally was read at the fifth bell, and it began with the water, because it always begins with the water and because this week the water was the only line in the book that nobody had read out loud before.

"Five litres from the collapse and one sealed litre of verified condensation in a stoppered jar, and the jar has not opened, and neither of those is drinking water and neither of those is anybody's, and they are on the page so that nobody in a year thinks they are part of the kitchen's count. The kitchen's nineteen days are given and not waived and are not down by one. And the water comes up the middle passage in pails on the shoulders of whoever is coming, and it has never once been in this book, and it goes in on Monday in Mikka Hale's words with the time on it, because a thing that has never been counted is a thing that has never been argued about."

"Add it to my column with no heading," said Mikka Hale.

"It is not a day, Mikka."

"It is not a day and it is not an hour and it is a pair of shoulders, and there is no column in this book for a pair of shoulders, and that is the whole of what I have been trying to say to you for a week and you have been putting the days in your blank column and the pot in a column of its own and **not one line in eleven weeks of that board has ever had a person's back written on it.** So add it, and leave the heading off, and I will read the week out at the fifth bell every Friday and the room can add up what it costs in whatever column it likes."

---

The two columns were read next, together, for the third time, and the room found the second one harder again, the way it had twice before.

"Mine," he said. "Thirty-eight and a half, and it has gone up, twice, in a fortnight, which is the first time since the second week, and I have checked it four times. It is **forty-four and a half.**

"The search was last week and it is already in the sixty-five, and I am not reading it twice. So: three since. A period at the lip in the cold, and a board, and a column with nothing in the heading. A morning in a room with a form on a bench, and the whole of that morning was not one hour of anybody's craft. And the two reviews, the short one and the long one.

"So three, since a fortnight ago, on a lip, a form, and two reviews. **And I am not proud of any of it, and I am not going to be told to be proud of it, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that the first fortnight in eleven weeks in which our column went up at all, not one hour of the six was work anybody in this building needed done.**"

"Theirs," he said, and turned the board. "Eighty-one and three-quarters."

He read the boundary's items the way he always did, without rounding, and named the ones that were days and the ones that were hours and refused to convert either.

"The search is in the sixty-five and I am not reading it again. So, since Friday: four days of Bel Ordry at a bar, and the fourth one was in the cold and I am counting the four mornings as days because that is what they were, and the day he was going to fix his mother's door is not in this column, because he gave it to a wall and I could not put it anywhere. Three and a half for the pump attended, two people a day, and Mikka's half-day on the shutter route and her day on it. Half a day of Ilsa's school on Wednesday, because the corridor was corded for an hour and a half while the cold air came up the shaft and the nine children were in the corridor at the wrong end of it, and Ilsa said that in the room and did not say it kindly. Two hours of the clerk's evenings and one whole evening of hers, and I am not counting the hours of a woman who is reading a stranger's report in her own time and calling it work, and I am counting them anyway, and Mikka will argue with me about it and she is entitled to. Three for a day of having a stranger in the building: half a day each of Renn and Ilsa and Mikka, and a whole day of the clerk's, and a half of mine, because I was in that anteroom on the fourth day with a stranger's form on the bench in front of me and did not say one whole sentence about any of it, and I do not know what that half is and I have put it in anyway. Mikka's pot, again, twice, which is a pot and not a wage, and which is money and not a day, and which is in the kitchen's own book and not in this one, and which I have now entered seven weeks running and am not entering here, and if anybody wants the whole of it they can add it over there and they will get a better number than mine, because it will be theirs. And three days of a watched flue wall. And that is the list, and the list adds, and I have done it twice, and it is sixteen and three-quarters on the sixty-five, and there is not one hour in the sixteen that anybody in this room should be proud of, and half a day of the sixteen is me in a room saying nothing."

"And I am not counting Renn Vale's four hours," he said, "for the second week running, out loud, in this room, and she has not asked me to stop, and I am going to keep saying it until she does or until I am wrong."

"It stays," said Renn Vale, from the upper landing.

"The finding is the finding," Tams said. "The half that gets counted is the half that is ours. It has not changed in four weeks and it is not going to change in the twelfth, and if it ever does change then I will stand here and read out why, and I will not be pleased.

"And the column with no heading in it is three lines longer than it was on Monday, and they are three days, and days are not hours, and a day is not a thing anybody in this building has been counting, and I have left the column with no heading on purpose, because if I give it a name then it becomes a unit and then it becomes something a room can be praised in."

---

Mikka Hale asked the question that a fortnight ago had not been answered, and she asked it in a harder form, and she asked it as herself.

"Eleven days ago," she said, "a schoolteacher stood in this room and asked who keeps the Hearthhold rooms, and nobody answered her, and it is in the book with the week on it and no word *pending*, and I have read that entry every day since, and I have not been able to leave it alone, and I am going to say why, and then I am going to ask it again, and I am going to say the reason out loud first, because you are not going to let me put a question in this room without one and I am not going to put one without one.

"Here is what I have learned in eleven days, and it is a small thing and it is the whole of it. **A room is kept by the work in it.** That is not a feeling. That is what happened to us in the ninth week. Renn has a road and she walks it every morning. Jo has a pump and she stands at it. I have a pot and a clinic return and nineteen days of meals, and I count them, and if I did not count them this room would not eat. Mikka — " she stopped and laughed once, which nobody had heard her do, "I am the pot, and the pot is the count, and if I stopped counting then the number would be a rumour.

"And every one of those is a **job.** And a job is a reason for a person to be in a room. And so the walls are kept, because the woman with the pot is in the kitchen four times a day, and the woman with the road is on the landing every morning, and the woman with the school is on the floor under nine children and paid for it out of her own hand.

"**And when there is no work to be done, there is no reason for anybody to be in the room, and the room is not kept, and nothing is missing, and nobody notices the day it stops.** That is what happened in this boundary the week before the crew came up a shaft, and I have been here eleven weeks and I can tell you exactly which rooms have a person in them and which rooms have not, and the ones that have not are not neglected. They are simply not anybody's work. And the day the work stops, they are not anybody's work again, and the only person who ever finds out is the person who comes back to use them and finds them cold.

"So I am asking it again, and I am asking it worse. Ilsa asked **who keeps the Hearthhold rooms.** I am asking: **who keeps the Hearthhold rooms on the day there is no work left to do in them, and who notices, and how does anybody find out?**"

She put her hand flat on the frame.

"And I am not asking it as a question about the future. I am asking it about Friday, because on Friday the corridor is going to be shut for the waist, and when the corridor is shut the school does not come, and when the school does not come the pump is not attended in the way it is now, and if there is no school and no pump and no stair work and no one in the school store, then this Hearthhold has eleven people fewer in it on Friday than it has on Wednesday, and the walls on Friday are not anybody's job, and nobody will notice except me, and I will notice because I am the pot and I will have to carry the water."

Nobody in the anteroom answered her.

Then Bel Ordry said, from the bench at the side, without any particular weight: "Then the answer is you, and that is not an answer, it is a pot."

"Yes," said Mikka Hale. "That is exactly what it is, and I have got to Thursday to find out whether I can put it down, and I would rather somebody else found out first."

---

Sefu Aran, at the wheel, came to it the way he came to everything, by putting a day and an hour on it.

"The spring conversation," he said. "It is owed. I have held it since the seventh week and I put the leaving in it, and then I took the leaving out of it, and I have not been able to leave the room alone for a fortnight and I am going to do that now, and I want the room to hear the reason, which is that the hand-over is the only part of it that is not mine.

"**The second day of next week. The fourth bell. One hour. In this anteroom.** Sefu Aran and Nera Oduya, with the five of you, and Dalla Sen, and nobody else unless the keeper or the air engineer asks them by name, and the terms are named now and not on the day.

"And I am going to say the thing about it that I have not said, and I am saying it in front of the clerk so that it is in the book with the date. It is not a conversation about the crew. It is a conversation between the keeper and the air engineer about what the air of this building is and who does what to it, and **it happens whether or not anybody is leaving, and if the five of you all go north on Thursday it happens on the second day of next week with the two of us in the room and nothing to lose.** And I have made Marek Venn write the leaving as a spring conversation with a named air hand-over before he goes, and I have made him write it twice, and neither of those sentences is a condition of his leaving. It is a condition of this boundary having an owner of its air, and I want that read in front of everybody before I stop saying it."

Nobody argued with him, which is what a room does when a keeper has said the right thing in the right order.

Then Nera Oduya said, from the valve, and it was four sentences, and they were the air engineer's, and Dalla Sen read them back twice at the air engineer's own request.

"Here is what it is, and I want it in those words on that date and not before.

"I am not going to be handed a building. I am going to be handed four things. The valve, and the stair-side grille, and the bypass hatch with the terms it is latched on, and a board with four cards on it and two marks in a corner that I cannot account for. And I am going to be handed a post — the air valve watch, at the second bell and the fifth, by a person, out loud, to a person — and **the last one is a person, and the person is not me, and it is not going to be me for a year.**

"And the post is mine until I say it is not mine, and I will say it in that hour, and the person I say will not be in this room on that morning, because I have found out about that woman and that man in eleven weeks and I am not going to introduce them to a post in a room with twelve people in it. So: the second day of next week, the fourth bell, one hour, and on the third day of next week I go and stand at somebody's door."
