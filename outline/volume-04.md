# Volume 04 Outline

## Volume identity

- **Title:** Nine Roads, One Throat
- **Chapter range:** 151–200
- **Central promise:** the timber the guild promised arrives, and it arrives as a load with a person responsible for it, and a boundary that will not fill in two lines cannot take it and cannot refuse it without losing the only wall in lime in a hundred miles of passage.
- **Ending type:** two parallel routes with two keepers and no order on them, a clearance monopoly broken at a counter and not in a speech, two Hollowmarks shut, and a larger question about who owns a wall that nobody in the volume answers.
- **POV:** close third person on Marek Venn. **No viewpoint shift is scheduled in this volume.**

# Starting state — written out of what Volume 03 delivered

**The series outline assumed Volume 04 opens on one legally open route that is physically fragile and politically valuable. Volume 03 delivered something with more edges than that, and Volume 04 is written on the delivery.** None of the following is invented; each is in `state/continuity.md`, `state/character-state.md`, or Chapters 141–150.

## The instruments

- **A public maintenance plan, sixteen rows, in four parts, in a coat, with a date in the top corner, no name anywhere and no signature anywhere at all.** It was **read out loud in a room to a person from a surface federation with no copy given**, under that office's own first and third clauses, and the boundary said out loud that it was doing it on purpose and knew the price. The first column holds a **permitted occupancy** and has never held a count of anybody. The second column has **sixteen conditions with no number and no day on any of them.** The third column is what is done, in the hand of the person who keeps the row, **fifteen lines filled and one empty that the keeper will not name.** The fourth column is **empty and unheaded**, with its reason in two hands and a modal struck out of one of them.
- **A charter with a hole in it one institution wide and one institution deep.** Seven signatures, no name, an office's number, eight clauses, **clause four a right against the Cartwright office only.** Five bodies have now come to this boundary that the four sentences do not name.
- **A Compact regime of six clauses going up the passage**, of which the **fifth** says a room whose condition is not being checked at the time the room is in use may be entered, the boundary may not refuse, and the refusal of the person who keeps the room is not a ground, and the **sixth** says that where a boundary cannot show that a room's condition is being kept, the office shall not treat the room as kept, **which the boundary has said out loud in a room is the reason it has been right about one thing in twenty-nine weeks.**
- **A market road.** Thirty-one people of this Hearthhold went up it once as ordinary residents with nobody at the front of it, **a form came off a board at a counter because a courier's mark is three people's and cannot be entered in a register, the office conceded nothing in a word, and a competent person at that counter said the sheet comes back on a board in a month by a clerk who does not know about Tuesday.** A courier wrote *a week is not a month* twice in a clerk's book so that the second one is the one a person reads.
- **A network of four rooms and a door**, multi-room by construction, each room able to be emptied into the next, each with its own keeper who wrote their own condition, **with no order on it: the last one out of a room decides when that room is empty.** Three of the four second ways out are rooms and the fourth is a word, and a word is not a way out, and a woman on a landing has said so out loud against her own practice.

## The material position

- **No timber, no lath, no bronze, and no mortar of the right kind.** The packing under the anteroom's frame is stone in clay and **clay shrinks as it drys.** **Seven sound wedges and one dead stick** — two at the school-door head, one in the school corner's door, three at the waist with the third up a quarter of an inch and a bit, and one in the basket.
- **The timber for a wall in lime is in a survey camp's store four days up the middle passage**, and the man who camped it there said he would do it again. **The halfpenny sheet promised timber in the month after next, and the month after next is this volume.** Three other Hearthholds were expected to accept that sheet and **three of them will have timber and will be able to shut a route when a clerk at a counter says so and will think they are being careful.**
- **A brace is impossible and is also the only thing available.** `bible/power-system.md` is the authority: a brace is a physical support joined to a bearing mark; it requires joinwood, timber, stone, cable or another tested support, **a counter-bearing, and time to let the load settle**; **it does not increase a room's capacity; it redistributes force and creates a new failure point; and it costs material, labour, time and a clear evacuation plan.** A carpenter in this building struck the word *brace* out of a plan in his own words in the twenty-eighth week, because there is no mark in it and a thing called temporary is a thing a man in a bad month is allowed to stop maintaining. **Volume 04's first problem is that there is no material at all until the month after next, and Volume 04's first gift is that the month after next is now.**
- **A person under a lip**, packed and not held, with a post under it that has never been built. Untouched in fifty chapters and untouched in this volume's first batch.
- **Pot at ten days** and has not gone up since the fifth day of the nineteenth week. **Jars two a day, entered separately and never added to the pot.**
- **The water at the waist is on the fill**, about the height of a knee, and it is not moving, and three people have never measured it on purpose and have given that reason out loud in a room.

## The political position

- **A boundary that has decided it will not fill in two lines on somebody else's form**, and has a bundle in its anteroom that cannot leave the building because of that decision, and a cook who has been putting a dead man's share of the pot back into the pot since the fourth day of the twenty-fifth week and told nobody.
- **Nobody is hostile.** Two people from a surface federation have sat in this anteroom and one of them has said against her own office that the instrument which made this boundary arguable is hers and that she knew the price. **The escalation is therefore not attack; it is a schedule.**
- **A guild cartel that is competing and not defeated**, whose rule is that a road a person carries a load along is not a recognised road unless there is a named person responsible for the load, and which has never once had that rule tested until one Tuesday in the twenty-ninth week.

## The things Volume 04 must schedule against and not around

1. **The air valve is asked and not answered**, with Bel Ordry's name against it, and he answers in the spring, in a room, with Nera Oduya in it. **The thirtieth week is late in a long winter and the spring is coming.**
2. **The air engineer's theory**, held since the fourth day of the eleventh week, bound to *when this boundary is keeping itself*, **which Volume 04 approaches hard and does not meet.** She sets a second condition. **No room may do the asking.**
3. **The name she said in her own hand in the twelfth week** is a second, distinct thread and is still not asked.
4. **The refusal of a row for the low end of the corridor stands** and is not re-argued from scratch, and the third of its three conditions — that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true — **is the reason a brace's reaction cannot be taken at the lowest point in the building.**
5. **The ninth room, the four treads, and the ninth line** with its one and its *none.* Nobody walks them. **No mark is cut in the stone anywhere in this building.**
6. **The wrapped plate**, unopened, on a shelf above the anteroom bench, and the brass rule, silent, in the pocket of a coat on a hook.
7. **Wen Pask's count**, which is at eighty-five, is not going to be eighty-six, and does not stop.
8. **The four households at a second gate** who have nothing, behind a water-lock shut for a survey of a sill, a bed and a wedge of hardwood that nobody has said how long will take. **No figure may be attached to that gate.**
9. **Sefu Aran**, the elected route keeper, is on a road and nobody in this building knows where, and **the room has decided not to write down how long he has been gone.** His return is scheduled for Batch 0003 and is not a rescue and is not a takeover.

# Where this departs from `outline/series.md`, and why the departure serves the ending

### Departure 1 — there is no legally open route, because there is no route

**Delivered:** a network of four rooms and a door, on a page in four parts in a coat, with a person named in a room who may stop it and no name on the page. A corridor that is shut three days in seven. A market road that was used once by ordinary residents and on which a form came off a board for a week.

**Why this is better:** the series' version makes Volume 04 a defence of a thing that already exists. **The delivered version makes the volume a build.** This boundary has never had a route. It has had a stair that is not a route, a corridor that is shut, and a road four days away that a clerk controls. **The volume's question is therefore not *how do we keep this route* but *can we make a thing that is ours out of material we were not given*,** and that is a better question than defending a document.

**How it serves the ending:** `outline/ending.md` requires a city whose essential routes and records are **public without being captured**, and a route council that is independent of any single steward. A boundary that has to build its own first route, out of material offered on somebody else's form, and that *refuses the form*, arrives at self-government having already paid for it once in timber. **The refusal is the foreshadowing.**

### Departure 2 — the opposition arrives as a schedule, and it is right

**Delivered:** a regime of six clauses going up the middle passage, a hardliner who is right about a number that has to be believed instead of counted, a cooperative official who is glad her own instrument was used against her own convenience, and a guild cartel that lost a form for a week.

**Why this is better:** a cartel in competition and a federation with a clause are the right opponents, but **a schedule is a better weapon than a threat, and one of them has already said so out loud in a room: a thing which is scheduled is not a thing that is refused.** Volume 04's escalation is therefore **the boundary discovering that the dangerous document is the one with a column of times in it**, and the volume's job is to make the reader understand why that document is *reasonable* — because a boundary that has said a permitted occupancy out loud and never counted anybody is genuinely a building a competent and tired man in a cell cannot verify.

**How it serves the ending:** the ending needs a Compact that threatens intervention and a Stonelight treaty that is signed and distrusted. **A schedule in a schedule in this volume is what makes Volume 12's treaty hall possible.**

### Departure 3 — the Bracer arrives out of a refusal, not out of a gift

`outline/series.md` commits Marek reaching the Bracer stage in this volume, with Iven choosing him over a better-paid guild assignment.

**Delivered:** a carpenter has struck the word *brace* out of a plan in his own words, because a brace is a support joined to a mark, there is no mark in this building that is not off the wall, and **a thing called temporary is a thing a man in a bad month is allowed to stop maintaining.**

**Why this is better, and why it is the volume's engine:** the stage arrives as a **refusal to accept a gift on the donor's terms.** The timber comes up the middle passage as a load. A load has a person responsible for it. Filling in two lines is what the boundary has refused three times. **So the volume's central physical problem is also its central moral problem, in one object, and the answer is a third thing that is not a refusal and not a purchase: a store is a room, and a room has a keeper, and a keeper can be asked in a room with the terms out loud, and can say no.**

**How it serves the ending:** the ending's rule is that residents can withdraw a dangerous project through a council process. **A boundary that learns to ask a keeper rather than buy a carrier has already learned the ending's central civic move, one volume early, about a pile of timber.**

### Departure 4 — the volume opens away from the building, on a road and a schedule

**Delivered:** the crew are not on the rota, their obligation is discharged, their column is a quantity of asking, and the boundary's only external contacts are a courier, a road hand, a guild cart, a survey camp and a federation's continuity officer.

**Why this serves the ending:** Marek's flaw is control disguised as responsibility, and the ending requires that he learn to accept that a person who will not carry a load is not a deserter. **In this volume the news always arrives before the thing does** — the form comes back before the timber comes up, the schedule comes down before anybody knows what it is for — **and the man who has been at the bottom of a channel for two and a half days is the one person in the building with no authority and no post, and he is therefore the one who is asked to go and look at things.**

**This is the volume's growth in one mechanic: a man with no post becomes the most useful person in the building precisely because nothing can be done to him, and he has to decide what to do with that.**

### What is *not* departed from

The central pressure, the four major locations, the four major factions, the midpoint reversal, the escalation, the climax, the concrete resolution, the next-volume question, and the power/relationship change are all delivered as committed. **The departures are to the starting state, to the order in which things happen, and to the shape of the opposition. They do not touch the volume's destination.**

# Midpoint reversal — Chapters 176–179

**Committed:** *a supposedly independent map is based on a falsified survey; Marek's own route is opened by a faultform after the guilds sabotage the alternative.*

**What is delivered, and it is the book of Chapter 110 turned inside out.**

**Volume 03's discovery was that the plan's first column has been transcribing a *permitted occupancy* rather than an actual one, and that the number came off a nine-hundred-year-old dowel nobody had ever read out to it.** That fact is a bookkeeping collision in one building. **The reversal is that it is a national fact.**

1. **A guild insurance office produces a map of this district's occupied rooms and calls it independent, and it is independent, and it was made from clearance records.** A clearance record is a count of what a room was *permitted* to hold, certified by a competent man on a day, and the certification is correct.
2. **Every number in the map is true.** No figure has been altered. No surveyor has lied. The word *falsified* is a clerk's word and she enters that she is entering it against her own professional instinct, and that the falsity is not in the figures and is in the noun.
3. **The exposure is a comparison, read out loud in a room.** A guild map and this boundary's plan are put side by side and the numbers agree, and the rooms do not, and the difference is that one of them was counted and one of them was believed.
4. **Marek's own route is opened by a faultform after the guilds sabotage the alternative** — and the sabotage is not a levering of a grate with a dead stick. **It is a form, filled in correctly, by a competent clerk, at a counter, about a different road.** And the faultform opens a link in the wrong place, and **it is not killed, because killing it leaves the load**, and it is a room repeating an unresolved last action, and nobody can say which last action.

# The Bracer stage — owed here and nowhere else

`bible/power-system.md` is the authority. **Bracer: build a temporary brace, refuge, or protected work corridor with a team.** Cost: time, materials, labor, and a failure point that must be watched; people can be hurt while he is protecting them.

## What it is, and where it arrives

**Chapters 163–168, in Batch 0002.** It arrives out of the volume's refusal. The material is the timber from a store whose keeper can say no, and the joinwood is the two wedges at the school-door head and the school store's two beech lengths. **It is a brace at the anteroom's outer service door frame**, which is a finger and three-quarters out of true, packed in stone in clay, holding and not fixed, with a Marker mark off the wall and a carpenter in the room who would not put it back.

## All four of the bible's requirements are on the page

1. **A tested support.** Stone, clay, beech, and hardwood wedges, and **Iven Tallo's name against every one of them in his own words**, and he is not redeemed and he is not thanked.
