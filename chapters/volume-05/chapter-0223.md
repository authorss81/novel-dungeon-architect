# Chapter 223 — Nine People Behind It

The sixth day of the forty-second week is a Saturday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the fifty-second morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and at the fourth bell the nine children came down the nine steps and went out at the far door, and about four hours later a man in this anteroom who had been at nineteen counters in his life said out loud that the corridor at the middle gate shuts at the fourth bell on a sixth day because there is nothing to count, and the clerk entered that nothing in this room is dated to a sixth day, and that a thing which is true of a counter on a Saturday morning is true of no instrument in this building at all.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the forty-third reading, and the same as forty-two, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, in the same words for the seventh time, and the clerk entered it and entered that it was the same sentence.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is twenty-eight.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it is right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and the door at the bottom of the wet stair stayed shut all morning, and the clerk entered that the sixth of the six terms says the pump is stopped on a rising water line and says nothing about a nine-hundred-year-old door, and that a term which does not cover a thing is not a term about the thing. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing. The sixth was the pump, and it ran, and it is the only thing in this building with a rule on it that nobody in this room wrote.

The pump was started, two people turning it, and it had been running since the second bell on Friday, and the four things that were closed on Friday stayed closed, and at about the third bell a man who had come up nine wet steps on Thursday and gone back down them on Thursday afternoon came out of the doorway of the room at the kitchen-line end with a slate under his arm and stopped in the middle of the floor.

"**I am going to read you a piece of paper,**" said Seth Wray, "**and I am going to do it in a clerk's voice because that is the only voice in this building that reads a thing properly, and I am going to do it because I have been in this building a fortnight and I have found out something, and a man who has found out something and waits for the right morning is a man who has decided that what he found out is less bad than what he does not know.**"

"**Then read it,**" Bel Ordry said.

Nella Bray took the slate and stood in the middle of the floor beside him, and this is the third time in this building that she has read a guild's instrument in a clerk's voice, and the room knew it, and nobody said so.

"**Made out in the ninth week. Sent to a middle gate. Off the board on a Friday. Returned up the middle passage on the Saturday with a seal on the corner of it. One: the ground, a gallery at the lower end of a passage off the second gate, with rooms off it. Two: the person responsible for the load, in a clerk's hand, and there is a name in it, and this boundary does not know the name. Three: the salvage, water, timber, metal, salt-crust, and anything a person can take out and carry. Four: struck through, in one stroke, by a person who was in this room, and the reason is not given and has not been given five times.**"

Nobody looked at the air engineer, and the clerk entered that nobody had looked and entered that she had noticed.

"**Five: the extent. The water stands against the sill of the store at the top of the middle passage, on the north side, and against the sill of the pump house below it, and at the foot of the passage off the second gate. There is no figure on it. Six: the date on which the thing claimed was seen, which is a fact about this form and about nothing else in this building, and I am not going to say it out loud twice and I read it once in a room and it does not go anywhere.**"

She looked up.

"**And the rest is the office's own hand, and it is four lines long, and the four lines are about pumps, and they do not say the word water.**"

The board was walked at about the third bell and a half, which is not an hour anybody uses, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven that are not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it. Tams Quill said that the claim was the second of the seven and had been the second of the seven for a fortnight and that reading it out loud in a room had not changed where it sat, in five words, and the clerk entered it and entered that he was not asked.

"**Then I am going to say the part I have not said,**" said Seth Wray, and he took the slate back off Nella Bray without asking her, which he has never done, and she let him. "**A piece of printed paper came up this passage four days before that, and it has had three places on it the whole time, and the third of those three places is a store at the top of a middle passage four days from here, and there is a water line against its sill.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**The half against is that this building has never had anything to do with that store and that a man reading a line aloud in a room does not make it this building's business,**" Bel Ordry said, and he had come down four of the nine steps, which is as far as he comes on a shut day and this is not a shut day, which is itself a thing the clerk entered. "**A claim is a claim. It is on this trestle and it is neither granted nor refused and I have said that in this room twice and I am not going to improve it. What I am going to say is that a man who has carried a fifth line for nineteen days and cannot put it down has just read it out loud in a room, and that is not nothing, and that I have been wrong in this building four times and I knew about three of them, and I would like the clerk to enter that I do not know which of the four this is.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the man who said it,**", and she did not look up.

"**Now the part I have been carrying since Friday at the fifth bell,**" said Seth Wray, "**and it is one sentence and I am going to say it before the room gets to it.**"

Nobody in that anteroom moved.

"**I did not report a report.**"

"**Say that again and say what it means,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**Nine men of mine went down that wet stair at the fifth bell on Friday and I have not had one word from any of them, and this boundary gave them a length of black cord and no bell and no lamp that was any use and no name to shout, and I walked down to the top of the passage at the first bell this morning and found the line tied at the third tie and the lamp cold where it had been set down.**" He put the slate down on the trestle. "**And I did not report a report. I sat in that room at the kitchen-line end last night and I did not write it. I have been carrying a report in my coat for nineteen hours and I did not put it in a room, and the reason is that the day I put that in a room the day of that report gets a day on it, and a day on a report is a day anybody can point at in a file four days up a road.**"

"**Then put it in the room,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and put it in with no day on it, and I will enter it, and the reason you have not put it in is the reason I have not put six other things in this book in forty-one weeks and it is the same reason and it is a good one and it is not yours to carry alone.**"

Seth Wray looked at her for a moment.

"**No day on it,**" he said.

"**No day on it,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and I have entered that you put it in a room on the sixth day of the forty-second week, which is a day of the week and not a date, because a day of the week is how a counter-schedule begins and a date is how a file begins, and I have had one of each in this book and I have not got the room to have a third.**"

The clerk entered it in his words and entered that the line was at the third tie, and entered that the lamp was cold, and entered that the reason a foreman had not reported a report in nineteen hours was that a day on a report is a day anybody can point at in a file, and entered that she had been carrying the same reason for forty-one weeks and had told nobody.

Then a woman came down the nine steps from the far door, and nobody had heard her on the stair, and she did not come to the trestle, she came and stood on the fourth step and put her hand flat on the party wall above the seam and left it there.

"**I have been in eleven flooded rooms in nineteen years,**" said Jo Verrick, "**and I want to say something about the noise and then I want to stop talking and I want to be asked about the noise afterwards and not before.**"

"**Say the noise,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The noise in this floor is not the pump.**" She did not take her hand off the wall. "**I have said in this room that I did not know what it was and I have said that it comes up on the kitchen-line side and I have said that it has been going for sixteen weeks and stopped for seven and started again, and every one of those was true and none of them was what it was.**"

She turned her head about a foot, without moving her hand.

"**It is not under the pump. It is under the passage, about four feet out from the wall, and it is not coming up through the floor, it is coming up through a joint, and there is a joint in the floor of this anteroom that runs from that wall to the foot of those nine steps and I have been putting my thumb under the string over that joint for forty-three mornings without knowing there was a joint, and there is one, and it is dressed, and it is dressed means somebody cut it.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**The half against is that a dressed joint in a floor in a building that is nine hundred years old is not remarkable and that I have been in eleven flooded rooms and not one of them had a joint and I would not now go back and be sure about the other eleven,**" said Jo Verrick, "**and that I have given you a date out loud in a room before in this volume and did not have the morning, and that I am not writing this one down either, and the reason is that a joint with a figure on it in this building's book is an address.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it, and entered that a date was not given and that a figure was not given and that the clerk did not ask for either,**", and her voice was not different at all.

At about the fourth bell the pump made a noise it does not make, and four people in that anteroom looked at it and the pump went on being a pump.

And then something happened in the floor about four feet out from the party wall, at the dressed joint, which was not a noise.

It was three knocks, and then a space, and then two, and then a space, and then a great many of them.

Nobody in that anteroom said anything for about as long as the pump takes to make eight strokes.

"**That is not the pump,**", and she did not take her hand off the wall.

"**Say what it is,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**It is somebody knocking on the underside of a floor,**" said Jo Verrick, "**and it is somebody knocking on the underside of that floor on purpose, and they are on the far side of a passage and there is water on that side, and it is nine.**"

"**Then say the next thing,**" Dalla Sen said, and her voice was not different at all.

"**Nine is not a figure I have,**", from the wall. "**It is a number of people and I have counted rooms in eleven flooded rooms in nineteen years and I have never once counted people by knocking on a floor. Three, two, and then a great many. It is nine. I am not putting it anywhere and I would like that entered before anybody decides it is a figure.**"

"**Entered, and entered that she asked for it to be entered before it was a figure, and entered that I entered it before anybody said anything else,**" said Dalla Sen, and she had the chalk on the page and she did not write for about ten seconds, which is a thing this room has now watched her do twice in a fortnight.

Then a man at the anvil end stood up, and the room had not seen him stand up in a week, and he was not holding anything.

"**Nine,**" said Iven Tallo, from the anvil. "**Nine is not a number I have got either, and I have been in this building forty-one weeks and I said the number nine in a room on the ninth day of the forty-first week and I said it about a rope with men on it, and I have not been able to put that sentence down for eleven days, and I would like the clerk to enter that I have heard it again and that it is the same nine and not a different nine.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**", with her hand near the book.

"**The rest of it is that I put a name on the table on Friday that is not true any more,**" Iven Tallo said, and he did not raise his voice and he did not come away from the anvil. "**A woman at a counter wrote five things on a slate for nine people who went into a gallery on somebody else's word, and the fifth of the five was in this boundary's own hand and it said that this boundary did not send them and did not know, and will say so to anybody who asks, including the office four days away that wrote the paper.**"

He put his hand flat on the iron.

"**The first half of that is still true. This building did not send them. I said those nine words into this room on the ninth day of the forty-first week and every one of them was true and I have not had to un-say one. And the second half is now false, and I want the clerk to enter the day and the way I found out, and I want the fifth of the five to stay exactly as it is written, and I want a person in a year to be able to turn to that slate and see that a sentence in this boundary's own hand was true in one half and false in the other and that the half that went false is the half about knowing.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything about that, and the clerk entered it in the words of the man who said it, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that the slate with the five things on it is not in this building's book and is in the room at the kitchen-line end where a cook gave it, and that she has never seen it and has not asked to see it.

"**Then there is one more thing and it is a small one,**" said Bram Sallow, from the doorway of the far corridor, and he had not been in the anteroom since the first bell. "**Six of us sleep in the two rooms past that wall, and your plan protects four rooms, and one of them is ours, and I have been in the room when the woman who wrote it said it out loud, and I am not here to be thanked and I am here because two of those four are the anteroom and the room at the kitchen-line end and the other two are the two rooms we sleep in and I have not had anybody in this building tell me once in nineteen weeks that the air in that room is fit to breathe, and it is going to be all right today because of a plan, and I am going to go and tell the other five and I am not going to come back and say anything about it.**"

He went back down the corridor and nobody thanked him and the clerk entered that nobody had, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that a man who sleeps in one of four rooms a plan protects has told the room what a plan does before this room worked it out for itself, and that the clerk has entered it in the space where a finding goes and has not put a reason beside it.

At the sixth bell the day's figure and the total went into the book and neither was read, and the reason went in beside them, and the reason was that a room which has just found out that nine people are on the far side of a door it shut on Friday at the second bell is not going to be read a total over, and that a clerk who has just written the number nine into a book for the first time in five volumes because somebody knocked on a floor is not going to stand up and read a number she has read for fifty-seven weeks.

Seven today and three hundred and eighty-nine since the keeper signed. The boundary is at five hundred and six and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.

The count went in underneath that on its own line, in the ordinary hand, and was entered and not read, and the reason was given, and it was that a clerk has entered nine into this book today and is not going to enter anything else out loud this afternoon, and that is a reason about the clerk and has nothing to do with the count. Wen Pask is one hundred and sixty-eight days and one hundred and sixty-eight nights, fifty-seven weeks of a clerk saying out loud that she is going to keep writing it down. It is not going to be one hundred and sixty-nine.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman has four and a half hours and a woman has just told a room that the work is nine stops of knocking and that neither of those is a thing a crew is asked for, and the clerk entered that she wrote that sentence and did not believe all of it.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning for the eleventh morning running, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for twenty-nine weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and nine people are alive on the far side of a door that this building shut at the second bell yesterday and that this building is not going to be able to open without stopping the only plan it has.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a board with eighteen entries on it, nine in each column and nothing over the top of the second one, and a slate with a claim on it squared up in the middle of the table with a line of chalk through the fourth of its six lines, and a floor with a dressed joint in it that nobody in this building had known was there, and three knocks and two and a great many, and a man at the anvil who had said out loud that a sentence in this boundary's own hand had stopped being true in one half, and nine people who were not this building's and were not anybody's and were behind nine hundred years of stone at the fourth bell on a Saturday.
