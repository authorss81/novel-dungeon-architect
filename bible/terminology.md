# Terminology Bible

Capitalization below is the default for planning files. Dialogue can vary by region and class, but new terms should not be introduced with alternate spellings unless the variation is intentional.

## Core places and institutions

- **The Lattice:** the buried ancient refuge and self-repairing structure that outsiders call a dungeon. The name is preferred by the Hearthholds after Volume 2; early surface characters usually say “the Hollow” or “the old structure.”
- **Hollowmark:** a surface entrance or shaft that reaches the Lattice. “Opening a Hollowmark” means opening a route, not merely breaking through rock.
- **The Rim:** the inhabited surface region around the Morrow Basin, made of towns, caravan roads, farms, and city-states.
- **Marrowgate:** Marek's surface city and the first modern city to reach the Lattice through a failed tunnel.
- **The Hearthholds:** the independent underground neighborhoods, workshops, clinics, schools, and route stations formed around stable Lattice rooms.
- **Hearthhold Middle:** the oldest dense network of underground residences and public works, where Nera and Talla have family and work ties.
- **The Cinder Steps:** the unstable first sector below Marrowgate.
- **The Wet Galleries:** the flooded and vertically connected reservoir/air network.
- **The Quiet Wardens' Reach:** old administrative, burial, and record rooms maintained by the Wardens.
- **The Loom Deep:** the deep network surrounding the Civic Loom and the One-Seat.
- **Sable Cut:** a collapsed surface-and-underground passage where Alden Venn disappeared; its broken survey corridors contain early evidence of the original distributed Loom.
- **Morrowlight:** the name used by residents from Volume 11 onward for the emerging self-governing network inside the Lattice; the final resolution makes that provisional arrangement a durable civic charter. It is a city, not a new empire and not the name of the whole world.

## Historical names

- **Cairn Commons:** the collective civilization that designed the Lattice. “Commons” describes a civic society, not a modern democratic government in every respect.
- **The Long Undertow:** the continent-scale pressure event that the Cairn Commons tried to survive. Its original cause remains intentionally unresolved; its effect is known.
- **The Sundering:** the failure of the One-Seat during the Long Undertow, which fractured the Lattice and created its present unstable sectors.
- **The One-Seat:** an emergency control core that concentrates decisions and load in one living operator. It is an old protocol, a political idea, and the final structural antagonist.
- **The Civic Loom:** the Lattice's distributed control and repair network. It was designed to work through many independent anchors, not a permanent sovereign.

## Materials and craft

- **Anchorstone:** dense, heavy stone that carries stress and holds a bearing mark.
- **Joinwood:** fungal timber grown in Lattice seams; light, flexible, combustible, and useful for temporary braces.
- **Bellows moss:** lichen that filters dust and helps maintain breathable air in narrow passages. It is living infrastructure and must be tended.
- **Remembering glass:** mineral panes that preserve a limited physical pattern—pressure, heat, footsteps, or repeated gestures—not a complete memory of a person.
- **Siltglass:** transparent, brittle mineral formed by heat and pressure; useful for windows, dangerous as a load-bearing wall.
- **Spore salt:** mineral-preserve crust harvested from dry galleries; medicinal and food resource.
- **Black cord:** strong cave fiber used for temporary braces, warning lines, climbing supports, and rescue hauling.
- **Rule-line:** a mineral or structural seam that carries load, air, heat, or civic function farther than ordinary matter.
- **Load path:** the route force takes from a structure into stable support. A safe structure normally has more than one available load path.
- **Counter-support:** the support that receives force when a wall, route, or load mark is changed. A change without a counter-support is an unsafe impulse, not a shortcut.
- **Bearing mark:** a limited inscription that directs a rule-line's force. It has one direction, one condition, and one material limit.
- **Brace:** a physical support joined to a bearing mark to redistribute force.
- **Route change:** a deliberate alteration to a door, stair, shaft, vent, drain, or boundary so people or resources can move differently.
- **Binding:** a structure made compatible with the Lattice's memory of use. Binding creates maintenance obligations.
- **Relay:** a connected network of independent anchors that passes load between teams rather than through one central operator.
- **Cairn mark:** an old inscription left by builders. It may state one condition or warning, but it can be incomplete, damaged, or written for a different era.
- **Load echo:** a phantom stress or sound in a reader's body after carrying a load. It is injury and diagnostic information, not a free power meter.
- **Fault scar:** physical or mental damage left when a load route fails near a reader. It can alter future work and should not be erased without consequence.

## Practical safety language

- **The Room Checks:** the five requirements for an occupied room: bearing, breath, drainage, escape, and change. The phrase is field shorthand, not a magical classification.
- **A safe refuge:** a room that passes the Room Checks for a stated occupancy and a stated time. No safe refuge is permanent or unlimited.
- **A route charter:** a legal and practical agreement that says who may use, maintain, close, or share a route. A charter cannot override a physical failure, but it can determine who is blamed for one.
- **Clearance seal:** a Cartwright Guild mark certifying a surveyed route. Its reliability depends on the survey date, occupancy, weather, and political pressure.
- **Salvage lien:** a legal claim to recovered materials or equipment. It does not by itself establish ownership of a route, a room, or the people who live there.
- **Route-custody order:** a temporary guild instrument that can lock a guild-controlled door, freeze guild-funded equipment, or restrict use of a licensed line while a dispute is heard. It cannot manufacture resident consent, air clearance, or public ownership.
- **A full-room test:** a safety exercise in which a room is treated as occupied at its normal maximum, not its empty inspection state.
- **Evacuation plan:** a route, destination, backup route, and decision-maker for moving people. “There is an exit” is not an evacuation plan.
- **Counter-support:** a physical or civic arrangement that receives the consequences of a design change. A person cannot serve as an invisible counter-support merely because they are willing to suffer.

## Factions and political terms

- **Cartwright Guilds:** commercial route, survey, salvage, and rescue organizations. They are not a single villain; their members have labor interests and conflicting incentives.
- **Ninefold Charter:** the dominant guild cartel that controls recognized clearance seals and route insurance.
- **Stonelight Compact:** the largest surface federation, responsible for several major Hollowmarks and the surface response to Lattice emergencies.
- **Wardens of the Lattice:** a custodian tradition with inherited records, rescue skills, and dangerous institutional memory.
- **Open Wardens:** faction arguing for public education, distributed anchors, and civilian stewardship of Lattice knowledge.
- **Seatwardens:** faction arguing that one trained custodian should hold final authority during recurring disasters.
- **Quiet Hands:** a sabotage network seeking to prevent a new centralized refuge by attacking public works. They are a recurring opposing faction, not a final enemy.
- **Cairn Directorate:** the ancient governing body that installed the One-Seat. It is a historical institution whose protocol remains active as a structure and an argument.
- **Morrowlight council:** the public, rotating civic body formed during the final volume. It is not a permanent ruler or a single elected hero.

## Faultform terms

A **faultform** is a physical hazard generated when a room's repeated function or unresolved load becomes a body. The term is more accurate than “monster” for planning, even though surface characters use the latter informally.

- **Returning stair:** repeats the first landing and prevents a group from choosing separate exits.
- **Doorhand:** grows around a door repeatedly used to lock or exclude occupied people.
- **Sump ox:** a pressure-supported form that occupies a stalled drainage route and worsens flooding.
- **Glass choir:** a cluster of remembering-glass panes that repeats an old crowd's path and can misdirect an evacuation.
- **Foldback:** a compact form that folds a room's previous doorway into the present, creating a false route; it is a severe hazard during retreat.

Do not create a faultform solely as a monster-of-the-week. Every named form needs a room function, load condition, ordinary residents or history, and a possible response that is more than “kill it.”

## Character and relationship shorthand

- **Marek's rule:** never call a space safe without naming its occupancy, time, and exit conditions.
- **Nera's rule:** a room that cannot breathe is not a shelter, however strong its walls.
- **Talla's rule:** a route that cannot be used by an ordinary resident is not a public route.
- **Iven's rule:** a brace that cannot be maintained by a tired worker is a temporary mistake waiting for an emergency.
- **Mero's rule:** a plan without a named injured person is not a plan; a plan with an acceptable casualty is not automatically acceptable.
- **Cassian's rule:** a decision without a responsible operator becomes an accident. The final volume tests the limits and the cost of that rule.
