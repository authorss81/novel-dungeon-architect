# Chapter 187 — A Line On Somebody Else's Paper

The second day of the thirty-sixth week is a Tuesday, and a shut Tuesday in this building is the day the packs came in, and the packs were still in a row along the anteroom bench, and nobody had moved them, which is a thing a room notices and does not comment on. The school did not run and lost nothing. The wall is on the other side of the room with five and a half shores on one side of it and a brace of a school's own beech on the other and a keeper's nine in the room behind that other side, and none of the three was being looked at.

Jo Verrick read the frame at the first bell before she said anything else, and the number was a finger and two-thirds, which is what it has been since the second day of the twenty-eighth week, and she entered it without comment and then went and read the party wall with a piece of chalk and a piece of string. The reading was a quarter of an inch. It has been a quarter of an inch for thirteen readings now and the string was where the string was, and a wall that agrees with itself thirteen times is a wall a person trusts for about four days.

She did not put the folding rule in the join. She never has, since the seventh day of the thirty-fourth week, and the reason she gave for it in a room is that a gap with air coming out of it is not a thing a rule belongs in, and a person who has been given nothing but walls for thirty-five weeks is not going to be the one who puts a rule into the first gap she is allowed near.

At the second bell Renn Vale came down the four steps with her slate and put her ear against the party wall on this side, and she was there for about the time it takes to say a sentence, four times over.

"**Nothing again,**" she said, coming back to the trestle. "**And I am going to be dull about it on purpose, because I said in this room on Saturday that I would say what I can hear out loud and would not know what it is about and would not pretend to, and the way to keep a promise of that shape is to be exactly as uninteresting as the truth usually is.**"

Then a road hand came up the nine steps with a wooden tally in her fist and stood on the anteroom floor and gave what she was before anybody asked, which is the only thing about her that has never varied.

"**Middle gate, and I have carried paper at that gate for six years, and I am not going to tell you a day, because I do not keep days and a woman told me on Saturday that nobody in this room is to be handed one.**"

Dalla Sen had her chalk down. "**Then give me the fact and not the day.**"

"**On the fourth day of last week a sheet came back onto a board at the middle gate. It is not the sheet that went down. It has the same wax head at the top corner and the same seal number in the bottom corner, and the seal number is the same number, and I checked it twice, because the number is the part a person forgets.**"

The pump made its two strokes and neither of them was for anybody, and a clerk who has spent a morning not asking a question is the last person in a room who would use them.

"**One line on it is filled in,**" the road hand said. "**And I could not read the heading of it from where I was standing and I am not going to guess at a heading. I will tell you what the line is, because I have watched that line get written four times in six years and I know what it is for. It is the line for the date it was observed.**"

"**Observed by whom,**" said Renn Vale.

"**That is the part I could not read,**" said the road hand. "**And I am a woman who carries paper and not a woman who reads offices, and if I stood at that board for a quarter of a day I would be a different kind of person and I would be doing it without a warrant.**"

Then she put the tally on the trestle, which she has never done, and kept her hand flat on it.

"**Here is the part I came up nine steps for, and it is a small part and it is the whole of it. That line is the only line on that form anybody is allowed to write wrong on purpose. I have watched four men fill it in and every one of them wrote a day that made their own afternoon easier, and I have never once in six years seen a man put a day on that line that was worse for him than the day he was writing on. Not one.**"

"**And what you are telling this room,**" said Bel Ordry from the step, "**is that the day on it is a day somebody chose.**"

"**I am telling this room that it is a day somebody chose,**" said the road hand, "**and I am telling you that I know it because I have watched the pen and not because I know what is on the sheet, and those are two different things and I would like the clerk to write them as two.**"

Dalla Sen wrote them as two, and then sat with the chalk off the slate for about the length of four strokes of the pump.

"**Entered,**" she said. "**That a sheet about this building is on a board four days up the middle passage with a seal number on it and a line for a date it was observed, and that the date on that line is a day somebody chose, and that the clerk does not know which way it was chosen and is not going to find out, and that this is the fourth such entry in six weeks, and I am not going to give you a figure of how many of them there have been.**"

"**You are not going to ask,**" said Nella Bray from the kitchen-line doorway, where she had been standing since the first bell.

"**I am not going to ask,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and I want the reason in the space where a reason goes, and the reason is not that it is right not to know. The reason is that the only road a fact like that travels is a piece of paper going up four days, and a piece of paper going up four days about a lie in a line is a piece of paper that arrives as a form, and this building has already sent one up this week and it has not come back.**"

"**Then you have made a decision this morning,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and nobody asked you for it and nobody could have stopped you, and I would like the room to hear what kind of decision it is. It is the first decision this boundary has made in thirty-five weeks that it cannot take back, and it is not a decision about a wall.**"

"**It is a decision about what we are willing to be able to correct,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I have made it, and I would make it again on Friday, and I want that entered in the same breath so that nobody reads the entry in a year as a clerk being careful.**"

"**The half against it is mine,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because I have been the man keeping a page on somebody else's anger for six weeks and this is the first morning I have had to keep a page on my own. A boundary that decides not to know what an office has written about it has made itself easy to live with. And the half against that is worse and I am the man who has to say it. Somebody in an office is going to read a column about this building, find it empty, and fill it in whichever way suits the hand holding the pen, and we have spent this morning finding out the shape of that hand.**"

Emrys Callow had been standing at the end of the bench with both hands on his own pack straps since the first bell, and he took one of them off.

"**Then I will put the crew's half against the whole morning,**" he said, "**and it is the same half again and I am not going to pretend to invent a new one. A crew works beside a room. A room that cannot tell you what is in it, and cannot put its own name in a line, and cannot take a line back off, is a room my five stand next to for four and a half hours on Friday. And in a year one of my five is under that wall, and there is nobody in this building who can say how many of us were under it, and who chose to be.**"

"**That is the best argument anybody has made in this room since I came up your steps,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**It is your own argument,**" said Callow, "**with a different man standing in it.**"

Then the room went quiet in a way it had not gone quiet all week, and the woman at the counter who does not carry came the length of the anteroom and stood on the trestle side of it, which she had not done since the day she gave her name.

"**I want to put a question in this room and I am not asking it of anybody,**" said Nella Bray. "**It is not for the clerk and it is not for the man on the step and it is not for the woman who keeps a corridor. I am putting it in the room because I do not know yet who to ask.**"

Nobody made a place for her either.

"**At a counter, a building has one person in it who answers for the air,**" she said. "**Not a name on a form. A person. And when that person is not in the building, the building has nobody, and a building with nobody in it about its air is a building somebody closes on a piece of paper. I have watched it happen to two rooms and one of them is a room people were living in.**"

She stopped there and put her hand flat on the trestle.

"**I am not asking whether she is going anywhere,**" she said. "**I have read that question on a form twice this year and I have never once been the person who got an answer to it. I am asking whether anybody else in this room does what she does. And if the answer is no, then a boundary in this Hearthhold has one person in it who can be stopped, and there is a sheet four days up this passage that says a district has already counted us once.**"

Nobody answered her.

Nera Oduya was on the third step and came down one of them and no more, and the dyed cloth was folded in her hand and did not get unfolded.

"**That is a question about a trade,**" she said, "**and I am not going to answer it in a room with a stranger in it, and I am not going to say no.**"

Dalla Sen entered the question in Nella Bray's own words, and entered that nobody answered it, and entered that she had noticed herself not asking a second question to fill the space. She did not give it a number. She had given that noticing a number on Saturday and had found that the number had started to be the thing she was keeping instead of the thing she was keeping a number of.

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle with his book shut under his forearm, and the sentence he had composed on Monday morning about a road and a pot was still in him, and it was a worse sentence now, because Monday's had at least been a coincidence somebody had refused and this one was a coincidence somebody had refused twice. So he let it go. What he watched instead was a woman at a counter refusing, in public, to give her office a shape, and understanding for the first time that the shape she was refusing was the same shape he had been refusing for thirty-five weeks with a chalk line and an unheaded column and a coat on a hook, and that neither of them had ever been able to say so to the other because they had met four days ago.

The anteroom's terms were said by a person and repeated by the clerk. The count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours and did not change. The outer service door was the one a body went out of, and the fifth of those six terms was in a room with both the people it names in it, and the pump was attended and stopped on a rising water line chalked on the frame that has still not moved.

At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's figures and did not read them. She read the count and she read nothing else, and the reason she entered and did not read was that she had spent the morning entering a thing she was not going to find out, and a clerk who reads a total out loud on a morning when the largest thing in her head is a blank does not want to hear what her own voice sounds like.

Two today and two hundred and sixty-two since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and seventy-nine and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen. Wen Pask is one hundred and twenty-two days and one hundred and twenty-two nights into a count that is his own. It is not going to be one hundred and twenty-three. Thirty-one weeks of the clerk promising a room she will go on writing the number down whatever the room decides to do with it, and thirty-one weeks of nobody thanking her for the promise, and she wrote it and turned the page.

The pot is four days, the seam gave one pot's worth on Monday and nothing this morning, and the four households at a second gate have nothing, and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody.

No sixth piece of work has been asked of anybody this week, and the reason is in the book in the space where a reason goes. The reason is that a clerk has spent her morning deciding not to know a thing, and a room that has done that is not a room that hands a second unfamiliar thing to five people before the bells are out. The seventy-one on the crew did not move.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a road hand had put a wax head and a seal number on a table and a man who reads numbers had not been in the room yet. A board is a column and a wax head is a column, and this building has had enough of those in one morning.

At the seventh bell the anteroom was a room with a road hand's tally still lying on the trestle because nobody had told her to take it, six packs along a bench, a hole in the far corner of the floor, and nine steps going up into a passage that is shut today and will be open on Wednesday.
