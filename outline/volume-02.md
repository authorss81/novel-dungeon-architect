# Volume 02 Outline — Reconciled After Delivery

## Volume identity

- **Title:** The Hearthhold Charter
- **Chapter range:** 51–100, delivered in five batches
- **Central promise:** a Hearthhold can keep its own air, water and exit through a winter, and the instrument that says so can be signed without putting a name against a route.
- **Ending type:** a public right that is real and bounded, held by a man, and not a system.
- **POV:** close third person on Marek Venn, with **one** marked viewpoint shift in the whole volume, in Chapter 58.

## Provenance — read this before using this file

**This file did not exist while Volume 02 was written.** Every one of Batches 0001–0005 was written against the **Volume 02 section of `outline/series.md`** and against `outline/ending.md`. The raw material for the reconciliation below is `state/current.md` (the commitment table at its head), `state/batch-summary.md` (Volume 02, Batch 0005, including its *Review and repairs* section), `state/continuity.md`, and `state/chapter-summaries.md` (Chapters 51–100).

**This file is therefore a volume outline and a reconciliation at once.** It records what was committed, what was delivered, in which chapters, and what is different. **Where the delivered prose and `outline/series.md` disagree, the delivered prose is canon.** Volume 01 and Volume 02 are both closed and this file cannot be used to reopen either.

It was written in the Volume 03 outline phase, after the volume had closed. It is owed to the repository as a record, not as a forward plan.

## Central pressure

The Hearthholds needed a permanent air, water and exit system before winter, and the Ninefold Charter wanted every route private. **The delivered volume kept that pressure in a smaller and harder form than the outline assumed.** The problem was never whether a network could be built. It was that a boundary has **no well, no tested seam, no timber, and a staffing question**, and that every instrument the guilds offered solved maintenance by putting a person's name against a route.

## Starting state (as delivered)

Marek Venn is a rescued outsider with **nine attempted readings in nineteen weeks and nothing usable**, a right hand permanently lost for fine control, a brass survey rule that has said nothing since early in the book, a public reputation he cannot control, and a crew under a maintenance obligation to a boundary he does not belong to. The boundary holds an air-gate anteroom rated **ten including one litter for no more than two hours, on a door that is packed and not repaired**; a kitchen pot falling week on week; six pails a day carried up a road; a pump with a scored bronze seat and no spare; a stair corded off with **a person under its lip**; and **a school running three days in seven with the corridor shut three**.

The series outline's starting state is accurate as far as it goes and is silent on the material ledger, the calendar, the lip and the pump, all four of which turned out to be the volume's actual engine.

## Starting relationships

- **Marek and Nera Oduya** begin at professional antagonism and deepen: she rejects his recorded air plan on first meeting, refuses to certify anything not found, tested and recorded in that order, and is wrong in public more than once.
- **Marek and the residents** begin as a crew that assumes a building is a rescue, and are corrected in Chapter 56 by a schoolteacher's question — *who keeps the Hearthhold rooms* — asked out loud and answered by nobody.
- **Marek and Iven Tallo** are in craft conflict, and by the volume's close have a private bench conversation recorded in a book of Marek's own that nobody else ever sees.
- **Marek and the guild office** are strangers who become, in Chapter 95, a competent counterparty, and are not made an enemy.

## Starting power level

**Unschooled eye, ending at the threshold of Reader.** Nine written nulls and nothing usable. No bearing mark, no route change, no binding, no relay, no brace, and a rule already in the room in Chapter 99 — *anybody who comes and asks him to read a boundary is going to be told no* — which the volume keeps.

## Major locations (as delivered)

- The Hearthhold air-gate **anteroom** and its lower level, the common landing and the stair head.
- The **corridor**: kitchen line, hearth corner, clinic door, the dip, **the waist at two feet eight inches**, the school door, the school corner, and the shut mouth in that corner.
- The **kitchen**, the clinic and the clinic return, the **wash rooms**, the **school store** under its shelf, and the lower level down nine steps.
- The stair's **service bend**, **fixed first landing** and **near landing**, the lip over **eleven feet** of shaft, and the four unwalked treads on the far side.
- The **flooded well** with the six-foot working depth, the one ladder, and a tiled floor under the silt in a pattern.
- Off the boundary: the **middle gate's store and counter** four days up the middle passage, the **second gate's water-lock** and four households, and the guild file forty days beyond the top of the passage.

## Major factions and people (as delivered)

- The **surviving crew** — Marek Venn, Leena Orr, Iven Tallo, Pell Roke, Tams Quill. Oren Sill died in Chapter 48 and is not recovered.
- The **boundary itself**: Sefu Aran (elected route keeper), Renn Vale (the slate, seven terms and an eighth), Dalla Sen (clerk), Jo Verrick (pump maintainer), Mikka Hale (cook, then the plan's second hand, then the charter's hand), Ilsa Norr (nine children), Isel Ferrow, Ruk Sella, Bel Ordry (keeper of the plan), the **woman from the wash rooms**, and **Wen Pask**, who finds the water.
- The **Cartwright office** as a series of competent bounded people rather than a villain: **Corva Nye**, **Sabra Kest**, **Harlan Merrow**, **Nell Ferrin**, **Corin Bexley**, **Hesk Ammery**.
- **Talla Roke**, in a courier's hand, who starts a route outside the guild system and is not in the building at the volume's close.

**Dael Harrow is named nowhere in Volume 02.** See *Divergences*, item 2.

## Escalation sequence (as delivered)

1. **Batch 0001 (51–60) — the boundary is half a boundary.** The pot falls, the office halves the water-carrying, a work is named for a shore under the lip, a courier's lawful order arrives and is withdrawn in the courier's own hand at a cost to four households, and a bar at the second bell hears a wooden knock from **eleven feet down**. A schoolteacher asks *who keeps the Hearthhold rooms* and the room does not answer.
2. **Batch 0002 (61–70) — the hand, the reply, and the thaw.** Tams Quill's right hand opens and stays a hand and not a working hand. The cold makes the shaft a sink and not a vent; the check hisses thin; Jo Verrick says *I cannot tell any more.* A reply arrives after forty days carrying Form 9-C with three boxes and no person to fill one. A thaw moves the waist a hand's breadth under nine children's feet.
3. **Batch 0003 (71–80) — the dependency, in a document.** A nine-row schedule on a dowel is described in a locked school store and is **not a drawing of a building.** The old page's conditions turn out to be the boundary's own rota. A twenty-two-year-old's bond goes with his master's. **The guild's fourth paragraph is read out and turns out to be the whole of the charter, on one line, in a notice about a boy.** The boundary's record becomes the only object in the Hearthhold a stranger can hold. **The midpoint reversal lands here.**
4. **Batch 0004 (81–90) — the plan, half-written, and the office's answer.** A release goes out on a road nobody can date. The public maintenance plan is begun in a kitchen and stops at its third column. The corridor's low end fills for eleven minutes and nobody is sent. The middle gate halves the water again. **The fourth column is offered, refused twice, and finally given a reason on the page instead of a heading.**
5. **Batch 0005 (91–100) — the water, the counter, the charter, and the man.** A lime seam behind the anteroom's own outer service door gives two jars a day and costs a door, a room's rating and a school day. A competent guild officer brings a six-head counter-draft and makes the best argument against the boundary's own position. Talla Roke starts a courier route outside the counters. **The charter is signed.** The air engineer is asked for a piece of work rather than consulted. **The Reader stage is granted once, and costs.** The obligation is discharged on a sentence, and the next volume's question is asked out loud and refused an answer.

## Midpoint reversal

**Committed:** *Marek's first public shelter saves a neighbourhood but creates a new dependency and a guild fee. The people he calls beneficiaries turn out to be the people who will have to maintain it.*

**Delivered in Batch 0003, Chapters 71–80 — and it arrived earlier, lower and as a different kind of object than the outline implied.** The series outline implied the reversal would arrive as a public-shelter event. What Volume 02 actually delivered was that **there is no public shelter to save**: the anteroom is rated ten, the corridor is shut three days in seven, and the room with the highest count on the page had an empty third column for most of the volume. The reversal was delivered as **a document and a dependency**:

- **Ch 71** — a school store, nine register boxes, and the condition that *a document you have not been in a room with is a document you can still argue with.*
- **Ch 72** — **nine rows, three columns, six of nine lines empty in the third**, and the finding that *a schedule is not a map, and it shows you that somebody expected to be here.*
- **Ch 73** — the old page's *wet* condition and Renn Vale's wet-seam days turn out to be **the same four days in seven**, and she cannot tell which of the two wrote the other one down. **A condition has stopped meaning the weather and started meaning a rota.**
- **Ch 74** — the woman from the wash rooms is asked for a piece of wood, says no, and gives the reason that the wood is standing up for a person lifting covers every second bell and every fifth.
- **Ch 75** — **the guild's fourth paragraph is read out and turns out to be the whole of the charter, on one line, in a notice about a twenty-two-year-old's suspended bond**, and the only route in the Hearthhold that could be entered against is the one the boundary has spent five weeks getting out of every file.
- **Ch 76** — Dalla Sen says the thing nobody had said: *this is the only object in this Hearthhold that a person forty days away can hold.* Nineteen weeks of care becomes the boundary's most exportable asset.
- **Ch 78** — Tams Quill's finding, which is the volume's thesis in one line: **that number has never been a measure of us; it is a measure of whether this boundary needs us.**
- **Ch 80** — the keeper puts the price on it out loud, and puts the stair outside every work in front of the room.

**The fee is delivered: the fourth paragraph's annual supervision fee, refused three times and never once paid.** The dependency is delivered: the boundary's own record. The people who will maintain it are delivered and they are named.

**Divergence.** The reversal did not arrive as a saved neighbourhood. It arrived as **a page that could leave the building** and **a condition that had quietly become a rota.** The Volume 03 outline is written on the assumption that the reversal was a document.

## Volume climax

**Committed:** *Marek, Nera, Talla and Iven build a public maintenance plan and force the Hearthholds to accept a charter that keeps a route open to residents.*

**Delivered, and finished, in Chapters 91–100, with its centre in Chapter 97.**

- The **plan is finished in Chapter 94** — eight of nine lines of the third column filled in four hands, one line empty which the keeper will not name, and a fourth column carrying a reason in two hands after Nera Oduya struck the modal out of Renn Vale's sentence and put it in her own.
- The **guild's counter-draft is met in Chapter 95** by **Harlan Merrow**: competent, sincere and bounded, conceding every objection and then making the best argument against the boundary, and naming the head of the six he did not write.
- The **charter is signed in Chapter 97** on butcher's paper, an office's number at the top, **no name anywhere**, eight clauses argued out loud before the chalk, five rewritten in the objector's hand, **Iven Tallo refusing in four words and not being argued with, Marek Venn refusing in nine seconds.** Seven signatures. It keeps the way up the middle passage open, will not be shut for a delivery, and nobody may be charged for it; **it puts no holder of a route in this Hearthhold into a register kept by the office, and the reason why not is written in the document**; and clause nine says the ninth room, the four treads, the wash rooms, the mouth and the blank line are **not in it, and that is deliberate.**
- **Talla Roke contributes the demand for an answer a person at a counter can be given**, in Chapter 96, and the boundary's contribution to her route is **two sentences with no name and no signature on them.**

**Divergence.** The outline said four people would build the plan and force the Hearthholds to accept a charter. What was delivered is that **four people did not build it.** It was built in a schoolteacher's hand and a cook's hand, kept by a man the room has ruled is not a holder of a route, and **the two people the outline names as its builders refused to sign it** — Iven in four words, Marek in nine seconds. The Hearthholds did not have to be forced: they had been arguing for it since Chapter 56. The resistance that had to be beaten was **the document's own shape**, not the residents.

## Concrete resolution

**Committed:** *the Hearthholds secure a legal and practical right to their essential infrastructure, and the Cartwright Guilds are not defeated; they begin competing for control of the new network.*

**Delivered.** The right is real, bounded, and is **the boundary's own public instrument**: enforceable by the boundary and against nobody else. **The office can neither grant it nor refuse it.** What the office does with it is receive it, file it, cite it, and take clause four to four other Hearthholds.

**The guilds are not defeated and are not wicked, and the competition is visible inside the last batch rather than promised to the next one:**

- **Ch 95** — Harlan Merrow states in a room that he wrote heads one, two, three, five and six and **did not write four**; that he will recommend the charter and does not expect it to be granted; and that both of those are true.
- **Ch 100** — a **halfpenny sheet** at the middle gate's store, a hundred copies, timber in the month after next, the fourth line verbatim, and **Nell Ferrin's true sentence on the back of it**: the office printed a hundred and counted them, the office is doing this because the first one said no, and she has watched a thing like this twice and both times it worked.
- **The same draft goes to four other Hearthholds. Three are expected to accept. Three will have timber. This one will be cited.**

**Divergence.** The outline implied the guilds would begin competing for *control of the new network*. What was delivered is that they began competing for **the boundary's neighbours** — the draft, the halfpenny, the timber in the month after next, the fourth line. Nobody in this volume tries to seize this boundary's route, because the charter does not give them a thing to seize. **The threat is a better offer a few days up the road.**

## Required relationship and power changes

**Power — the Reader stage, granted once, in Chapter 99, and nowhere else in the volume.** It identifies **a likely failure in one room** — the anteroom — out of the plan and the charter, with a **headache behind the left eye with a floor to it, a left hand that clamped and shook and did not close that night, the anteroom's count arriving as a sound under the pump's check and unable to be separated from it, and the count still audible at the seventh bell in a room he was not in.** The risk of treating a first impression as fact is the scene's centre: he says it in a room as a fact, the air engineer makes him say how he knows, he says *because the fourth row is empty*, she says that is a hole in a document and not a reason, and he cannot say it twice. **Tams Quill enters it as a claim and not a reading, with the condition that it may not be built on until somebody can do it again.**

**It saved nothing, and the volume says so out loud: *it did not save anybody, it was worth an afternoon, and my left hand closed properly this morning and it does not close tonight.*** A full-room drill found the frame a finger and a bit out of true; the shoring was done by two other people; Marek Venn was not asked into it and was not in it.

**There is no bearing mark, no route change, no binding, no relay and no brace in Volume 02. The right hand's fine control is not recovered. The brass rule still says nothing after ten weeks.**

**Relationships, as delivered:**

- **Nera Oduya became a collaborator and not a dependent, in three scenes and not one.** She was asked for a **piece of work** rather than consulted for a decision; the room ran four hours without her visibly and said so in front of the reader; she entered the arrangement and **stated its price**; she was **wrong** about the school corner's air and about the anteroom's danger, was wrong in public, and was not consoled; she **refused to sign the charter and gave a structural reason**; she **struck the woman's covers out of a legal instrument because nobody had asked the woman**; she **refused to give the air board to a man** and put it on a page; and she **left the thirteenth line unwritten with the reason on the page.** No intimacy, no recognition scene, no acceleration.
- **Talla Roke began organising a courier route outside the guild system, and it is hers** — a sheet in her own hand, three named people under hers, addressed to nobody in this building, and a boundary contribution of **two sentences with no name and no signature.** She is not in the building at the volume's close. **The spring is owed by nobody and was not paid off as a gift.**
- **Iven Tallo refused the guild's draft and the boundary's own charter in the same week**, gave the real reason half an hour later on a bench to one man, and **it went into a book of Marek Venn's own and nowhere else.** He told the boy the hard thing: *you will be paid and you will not be a carpenter, and both of those are true.* He is not a defector, not a martyr, not a saint, and not redeemed.
- **Sefu Aran discharged the crew's obligation himself, out loud, in the room, with the reason**, and refused to use his own condition because *a condition you can satisfy is not a condition, it is a date with a number on it,* and his was satisfied by luck. Nobody left and nobody was asked to.

## Required world reveals (as delivered)

- A Hearthhold is inhabited, self-maintained, and has **no well, no tested seam and no second pair of hands.**
- **Air authority and civic authority are separate, and neither creates the other's consent.**
- A **guild instrument is a door with a year's price on it, and the price is a person's name.**
- The oldest schedule in the building has **kitchens, classrooms and beds on it and no treasuries and no air at all**, and it is a schedule of keeping a place to live in.
- **The Lattice's rules are not shared:** a condition on a nine-hundred-year-old page and a condition on a landing in the same building turned out to be the same four days in seven.
- A **room's function persists in its shape**, and a room repeating an unresolved function is a **faultform.**
- **A document that names a holder names a person**, and a person in a file is a person forty days away on a road.

## Next-volume question

**Asked out loud in Chapter 100 and not answered.**

Nera Oduya asks two things and explains neither. **One:** a halfpenny sheet, a clay packing that shrinks, a word that has to be said in a room, and a frame that goes a finger a week **is how a door becomes a toll gate** — not a gate with a man at it, but a gate with a *rule* at it that a man forty days away can be handed. **Two:** she has had a theory since the fourth day of the eleventh week about why the oldest book in this building has kitchens in it and classrooms in it and beds in it and no treasuries; **it is worse than not knowing, and she will not say it in a cold month to a room that writes things down.**

**The condition on both is *when this boundary is keeping itself*, and it is the fourth thing in the book bound to it.** Nobody asked her in a room that day. The second half — *what happens when a door becomes a toll gate* — is **answered for this boundary by the charter and answered for the world by nothing.**

## Final chapter image

A room at the sixth bell with six things read out and nobody filling the space after the last one; a plan in a coat with a date in the top corner and an empty fourth column carrying a reason in two hands; a charter on a scrub board with a number and no name; a halfpenny sheet in a courier's bag; a boy thirty-five days and thirty-five nights into a count in a column with nothing over the top of it; a man's hinge in a bag for seven weeks; a brass survey rule on a hook in a coat that has said nothing for ten weeks; and **a person still under a lip.**

The volume's last character beat is Marek Venn telling the keeper to his face that **the open road is not worth a line, because a road is a road and nobody can point at the thing that built it** — and then refusing to let that answer run together with the eighth line, which **is** worth having, and which is a page in a coat worth having exactly as much as the man in the coat.

# Divergences, recorded honestly

**1. The volume's antagonist pressure was never a person.** The series outline names Dael Harrow and a distant Cassian rescue charter among the volume's major factions. **Volume 02's pressure came from four things, and none of them is a face: the guild office, the six-head counter-draft, the fourth paragraph, and the office's halfpenny sheet.** That is a better antagonist for this book and it is the one the next volume inherits — a competitor with a document, not a villain with a grudge.

**2. Dael Harrow is named nowhere in Volume 02, and he must not be smuggled into this reconciliation.** He is a Volume 01 figure. The antagonist pressure of Volume 02 was the guild office itself: a six-head counter-draft read out line by line in Chapter 95, a fourth paragraph refused three times in Chapters 85, 96 and 97, a store order and a halfpenny sheet, and **Harlan Merrow**, who is competent, sincere and bounded and is not an antagonist. `state/continuity.md` records the *Inherited Volume 01 defect* — the phrase about *the forty-eighth* in `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0045.md` — **separately, and it stays separate. It is not a Volume 02 divergence and it is not repaired here.** Sabra Kest, Corva Nye, Nell Ferrin and Harlan Merrow all appear and none of them is made into an enemy.

**3. The locations became interior.** The market chamber, the route office and the public work yard were written off in favour of one building and its corridor. The off-boundary world reached the reader only through paper and through people who came down four days of road, which is why the road itself is a subject in this volume and the market is not. **The market route and the route office are Volume 03's debt to this outline**, and Volume 03 pays it (see `outline/volume-03.md`).

**4. There is no Hearthhold assembly and no Cassian charter in this volume.** The first assembly is argued in the anteroom at the fifth bell in Chapters 96, 97 and 100 and is never given a name, a quorum or a minute book, because the room's argument was that an assembly would become a register. Cassian Rook is not named anywhere in Volume 02 and does not appear. The first Hearthhold assembly as an institution is Volume 04's problem, and it is on the road that Talla Roke's sheet is carrying.

**5. The midpoint reversal was delivered in Batch 0003, not where the series outline implied.** The outline's phrase *first public shelter* does not describe anything in the delivered volume, because the volume never had a public shelter to save. What Chapter 75 delivers is the whole charter on one line of a notice about a boy, and what Chapter 76 delivers is that the boundary's own record is the only object in the building a stranger can carry. **The reversal was a document, a dependency and a fee, and it happened at Chapter 75–80, twenty-five chapters before the volume's end.** Any later volume that assumes a public shelter was saved in Volume 02 is wrong.

**6. The climax's builders refused the climax's document.** The outline named four people as the plan's authors and the charter's movers. **Two of them did not sign the charter** — Iven Tallo in four words and Marek Venn in nine seconds — and the plan was written by a schoolteacher and a cook and kept by a man the room ruled is not a holder of a route. The Volume 03 outline is written on the assumption that **Marek Venn's not being a signatory is a standing condition of his presence**, and that any document Volume 03 needs him to sign will have to be a document that does not put his name on a route.

**7. The guilds compete for neighbours, not for the route.** Nothing in Volume 02 attempts to take this boundary's route, because the charter does not give the office a holder to press against. The competition is a halfpenny sheet, a hundred copies, timber in the month after next, and **three Hearthholds that will be able to shut a route when a clerk at a counter says so and will think they are being careful.** The unresolved claim is a Cartwright clerk's page and a guild salvage lien. **This is the pressure Volume 03 inherits and must not soften.**

**8. The volume's own final answer was written by a character, not by an instrument.** The last thing said in the anteroom is that **a page in a coat is not a system, that is a man.** The series outline did not commit to that sentence. It is the volume's most important divergence from its own plan, it is the sentence Nera Oduya actually said in Chapter 100, and **Volume 03 must be built on the fact that the claim is still open in exactly the form the volume argued for.**

**9. Oren Sill is not named in Chapters 91–100, and that is correct handling.** He is not named anywhere in Chapters 81–90 or 91–100 either; he appears in Volume 02 only as **a line in the column with no heading that was refused in the fourteenth week and has stayed refused**, and that is a refusal of a line and not a recovery. **The volume inherits his packing method three times and never repeats the one defective sentence in `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0045.md`** — *the forty-eighth*, which can only mean Chapter 48 and which reads as a day number above the locked maximum of forty-six. The method was read out once in the book, in Chapter 62, and not again. **That inherited sentence was not edited, because Volume 02 may not rewrite Chapters 1–50**, and the defect remains recorded in `state/continuity.md` as a Volume 01 matter to be settled by a Volume 01 decision.

**10. The next-volume question was asked out loud and not answered, on a condition.** In Chapter 100, Nera Oduya states both halves of it, says she has had one half since the fourth day of the eleventh week, that it is worse than not knowing, and that she will not say it in a cold month to a room that writes things down. **The condition on both halves is *when this boundary is keeping itself*, and it is the fourth thing in the book bound to it.** Nobody asked her in a room that day. **Volume 03 inherits the question and inherits the condition, and no room may do the asking.**

# What Volume 02 hands forward, as canon

**The volume closed with these things true, and Volume 03 may build on them and may not quietly re-derive them:**

- **The claim is still open in the form the volume argued for: a page in a coat is not a system, that is a man.** Bel Ordry has asked to be suspicious of in a month and nobody has been suspicious of him.
- **The charter** is on butcher's paper with an office's number and no name, and the office can neither grant nor refuse it.
- **The plan** is one document in four parts in a coat, nine rows, eight of the third column filled and one empty which the keeper will not name, a fourth column empty and unheaded with a reason in two hands, no name anywhere and a date in the top corner.
- **The four households at the second gate still have nothing**, and the keeper has promised to say so in person at the counter before the first of the month.
- **The anteroom's frame is a finger and a bit out of true, holding and not fixed**, and the anteroom's rating depends on a word said to a page and not to a doorframe.
- **The guilds are competing, not defeated.** A halfpenny sheet, a hundred copies, timber in the month after next, four other Hearthholds, three expected to accept, and this one cited.
- **A person is still under a lip**, packed and not held, and the post under it has never been built.
