# Chapter 168 — The Frame Comes Into True

**Open for the corridor on the third day of the thirty-third week, a Wednesday, and the school ran for the twenty-ninth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, with a brace of a school's own beech going into the wall that room leans on while nine children were in it, and the corridor was open and the pump was attended and nothing went through that frame all morning except a clerk's word.**

**Four people put their hands on it and each of them said out loud what they were holding and what stops it. A man with a page in his coat said that what stops it is a word. The frame came into true, which means it moved, and a woman with a folding rule took a figure off it before anybody said anything else.**

At the fourth bell the twenty-ninth morning began. Nine went in and one of them went in sitting down, and the strip of crate board under the ninth hearth stone was holding, and the two flags had not moved. Jo Verrick said the standing term out loud in the doorway and Mero Senn said it out of his own hand against hers, and the schoolteacher said yes to it. Mikka Hale asked to be asked about the fire for the ninth time, refused a ninth time, and asked for the children to be cold in the record in the same place as the refusal.

They were in for the morning. They would be in for the whole of it, and the anteroom was at the other end of the same party wall, and when the frame came into true on the second day the wall was going to tell them.

The agreement came first. Dalla Sen read it out of the terms, because it was the last of the six sentences and the room had not heard it said in a row since it was written.

"**Every person whose hands are on this thing agrees out loud, in a room, to what they are holding and what stops it, and the one who does not agree does not hold.**"

"**That is the fifth of the six,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, "**and I am the one who said it, and I am not going to be in the room when it is done, and I want that entered, because I have said that I cannot be one of the hands that put it in and a woman who says that and then stands in the room is a woman who has changed her mind and has not admitted it.**"

"**Say the four, then, in the order they go on,**" said Bel Ordry.

They said it at the anvil end of the trestle, in a corner of the anteroom where the lamplight reached and the pump was loud enough that a man had to lean in a little to be heard, which is where you do this.

"**Iven Tallo. I am holding the head end. What I am holding is the part of this thing that touches the stone, and what stops it is a shim I cut on Friday that is going to crush at its corners in about two years, and when it does the head settles and the frame goes a twelfth of an inch out of true again.**"

"**And who finds that out,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**A person. I do not know who, and I am not going to be the man who guesses.**"

"**Mikka Hale. I am holding a corner at about the height of my chest, and what stops it is a term in a clerk's book and two women, and if it starts to go I am letting go and stepping back.**"

"**Say the last of it, because a room is going to remember it longer than the rest,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**And I am not going to be the one holding it on.**"

"**Bel Ordry. I am holding the end that goes in.**" The man with the plan in his coat had both hands on the beech, which is a thing nobody in that room had seen him do, and his face was not the face of a man doing a thing well. "**What stops it is the word.**"

"**Say it again, and say which word, because a room that says the word by mistake has had a practice and not a rating.**"

"**Door.**" He did not take his hands off. "**The anteroom's count is ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and that count is restored when the word is said in a room and the clerk repeats it, and if the word is not said the count is not restored, and the count is the only thing standing between this room and being a room in a corridor. That is what stops it. Not the terms. The word.**"

"**Then say the half against that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because it is the fourth one, I have asked the other three, and this one does not go past without it.**"

"**Against it: a word is not a wall. I know that. I have said in this room for thirty-two weeks that a page in a coat is not a system and that I am the only person who has to say it, and this morning I have held a piece of beech with two hands for the first time since I came into this building and discovered that the thing I have been protecting is the word, and not the wood.**"

Then Marek Venn, who had not been holding anything, and who had the piece of chalk in his left hand and the right one open at his side.

"**Say yours, Marek, and do not describe the joint instead of saying what stops it.**"
"**I am not holding it. I cut it. I am the one who cut a splay by hand in green beech over two days and I cut it coarse, and the coarse cut is a wider face, and the wider face is carrying four tons, and that is arithmetic and not a virtue.**"

"**Then what stops it.**"

"**The four. That is the honest answer and it is a bad one. I cut it and I am one of the four who put it in, and I have known all along that a brace is sound and the shelter is not, and the shelter is the other side of that wall, and I am the person who is least able to see it, and I have known that since the second day of the thirtieth week and I have said it out loud in this room twice.**"

"**Enter all four in their own words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and enter that the fourth of them is a man who was not asked to hold anything and gave an answer about a word, and enter that the answer is the only one of the four that is not about a piece of wood.**"

Then they lifted it, and it went up.

Four people and a length of a school's own beech, and the head went under the lintel of a corner that had been shut and unused since the twenty-second week, and the shim went into a hollow worn in nine-hundred-year-old stone, and it took about forty seconds and nobody spoke during the forty seconds, and what was audible was the beech going against dressed stone and four people breathing at the rate of people who are working.

Then the wedges, which are two sound beech wedges that came out of a school door on the Wednesday of the week before last, and which were driven by a carpenter with a hand that does not close, and which the terms of the fifth asking did not allow the five to touch and which the boundary had allowed because the boundary is not one of the five, and which Renn Vale had said in a room was a door.

Then it stood, and the room looked at it, and Jo Verrick went to the frame before anybody else had said anything at all.

For about half a minute the four of them did not let go. That was not in the terms and nobody had said it, and Dalla Sen entered it afterwards without being asked, and entered that she had watched four people in a room stand holding a thing they had just put in, and that she had been in this building thirty-three weeks and had not known that was a thing people did.

Marek Venn had his left hand flat on the beech at about the height of his knee and his right one open at his side, and he found that he was not thinking about the joint.

He was thinking about a year from now. There would be a person in this building who had not seen this happen, and that person would have a book, or a slate, or a mouth, and would go to a wall and look at a piece of beech over a door and have to decide whether it was still doing its job. He would not be that person, and this was the first work he had ever put up in his life that he had a real chance of not being.

That was not a thought he said out loud. It came out as a figure, which is what a figure is for.

"**Say what you are doing,**" said Marek Venn, "**because a room has just watched four people put a thing in and the first words out of anybody should be the ones that tell us whether it did anything.**"

"**I am taking a figure off the frame,**" she said, "**and I am doing it before I say anything about it, and I would like that entered, because the last time I had a figure I said what it meant before I had said what it was.**"

She had the folding rule out of her apron and she was on her knees on the anteroom floor with the lamp on the sill, and it took her about two minutes, and nobody in that room said anything for two minutes, which was the longest quiet in thirty-three weeks.

"**Say the number,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**A twelfth of an inch back.**" Jo Verrick did not stand up. "**That is the frame. It was a finger and three-quarters and it is a finger and two-thirds, and that is the first time in thirty-three weeks that anybody in this building has measured that frame and got a better number, and I did not do anything clever and neither did anybody else and I am not going to let this room turn it into a story.**"

"**Say what it means,**" said Marek Venn, "**and say what it does not mean, because that is what a figure is in this building and I taught it to a room of people who did not know it.**"
"**It means the frame has moved back toward true by a twelfth of an inch, and I would take the same number again tomorrow and get a different one.**"

"**It does not mean the frame is fixed, and it does not mean the anteroom can hold one more person, and it does not mean anything at all about the wall on the other side.**"

"**Then say that last one again, louder, and say it to the cook, because the cook asked for the anteroom's number out loud in a room on the second day of this week and I am not going to have the number changed underneath the person who asked for it.**"

Mikka Hale had come to the doorway at the number and had not come further.

"**Ten including one litter, for no more than two hours,**" said Jo Verrick. "**That is the same as it was on Monday and the same as it has been since the nineteenth week. This brace has not made this room hold one more person and it was never going to, and a brace that makes a room safe by making it hold more people is the failure, and there is a line about that somewhere in a book in this building and I would like it read out at a sixth bell one day and not today.**"

The anteroom's terms were then said out loud, and the clerk repeated the word, and the man who kept the page said it first, because the practice says a person says it and a clerk repeats it, and it had been the other way round in a hundred mornings in this building and Bel Ordry wanted it done properly for the first time since a woman from another office read a plan out loud and got no copy of it.

"**Door,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Door,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Say which one,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, and she was not smiling and she was not not smiling, and nobody in the anteroom could have told you afterwards.

"**The outer service door,**" said Bel Ordry. "**The one a body goes out of. It is still the one a body goes out of, and a piece of beech over its head has not changed what it is for.**"

"**Then that is in the book,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the objection is still in the book, and I am entering both, and I am entering that the word was said twice and the clerk repeated it and the count is ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and that is the whole of the anteroom's rating on the third day of the thirty-third week.**"

"**Say the other half of the anteroom out loud today,**" said Renn Vale, "**because there is a brace over that door now and I would like a room to hear what the second way out of that room is, in full, with nothing left out of it for tidiness.**"

"**The last one out of a room decides when that room is empty,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the anteroom's second way out is a word said to a page, and a word is not a way out, and I have said that in a room in this building and I am saying it again because a piece of beech has gone over the door and not because anybody asked me to.**"

"**Then say what a body does,**" said Iven Tallo, from under the thing he had built, "**because I have been in this building thirty-three weeks and a word has been the second way out of that room since before I came, and this morning the word is under a piece of wood, and a person does not go out through a word, and everybody in this room knows that and has been going along with it for a month.**"

"**Then say the sentence that is worse,**" said Mero Senn, "**and then somebody tell me what the answer is, because I have been in this building eight weeks and two days and I would like to have asked it once in a room.**"

Nobody answered him, and the pump got four strokes in, and a clerk in an anteroom wrote a sentence in a book and did not read it out.

The twenty-ninth morning ended at about two hours in, because a school is a school. Nine children came out of a room at the kitchen-line end into a corridor that was open, and they came out the ordinary way, past the anteroom, where four people had been holding a piece of wood in a shape since the first bell. One of them was a woman with a board under her arm and one of them was a man who kept a page, and neither of them had anything in their hands.

The girl with the crate board and the bone in her foot went last, and stopped in the anteroom doorway on the way past. She looked up at the brace across the head of the door for about as long as a person looks at a thing they have decided is not about them, and then went down the nine steps.

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the board was not walked, and the count was read at one hundred and three, and the figure on the frame was a twelfth of an inch better than it had been on Monday and the anteroom's count was ten including one litter and did not change.**

"**Four today and two hundred and sixteen since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and thirty-three and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for sixteen weeks. The crew are at seventy-one.**"

"**I entered those and did not read them, and the reason is that a frame moved this morning for the first time in thirty-three weeks and a room asked a woman for a number, and I am not going to read a total out loud in the same breath as a figure that is the first good news this clerk has entered since the twenty-ninth week.**"

"**The pot is nine, and the seam gave two this morning.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and three days and one hundred and three nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and four, and I have said in fourteen weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and nobody has thanked me and a man read nine figures off a slate in this room on Monday without thanking me either.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is that this morning a board's own subject was put into a wall, and I would rather go a week without a column than be the clerk who walks a board on the day a thing gets built.**"

---

**Nine children were taught for the twenty-ninth morning in a room with a brace of their own school's beech going into the wall they lean on, and they came out the ordinary way, and the last one of them stopped in a doorway and looked up at it and went on down the nine steps.**

**Four people said out loud what they were holding and what stops it, and a man who keeps a page said the word, and the clerk repeated it, and the anteroom holds ten including one litter for no more than two hours, exactly as it did on Monday, and a brace that made a room safe by making it hold more people would have been the failure.**

**The frame is a twelfth of an inch closer to true than it was, and the wall on the other side has not been looked at since Friday morning, and a physician who wrote a refusal to certify it is still standing in the anteroom with his bag against the wall.**
