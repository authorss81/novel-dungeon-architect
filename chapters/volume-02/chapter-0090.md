# Chapter 90 — The Fourth Column

On the fifth day of the seventeenth week the corridor was open and nine children went through it at the first bell in twos at a walk, and Ilsa Norr walked behind them, and nobody said anything about it in that anteroom because it was a day that had been asked for and given and it did not need a speech.

At the second bell the boundary asked eleven people whether any of them would look after a piece of paper, and nine of them said no, and each of them said it for a different reason, and one of them said nothing at all and the room waited on him, and Dalla Sen wrote all of it down, and it is the shortest and the most useful page in this book.

**Dalla Sen** said no first, in her own words, without softening it: *my book is the one object in this building a stranger can pick up, and this has to be the one they cannot, and if I keep it then the only document anybody here has written about how this place keeps itself goes in a satchel. I would rather be the woman who writes down that it is not mine.*

**Renn Vale** said no and said it from the landing: *a page with a rota on it is a rota, and I have watched this building learn that in three days off a page nine hundred years old, and I will not keep the second copy of anything that can become a boot.*

"No," said Mikka Hale, with a cloth in her hand, and she did not stop. "I have one already. I did not mean to and I filled a column in three days and it came out as my own boots, and I am not going to do that again in a colder month than the last one. **Put it in the book as a no and not as a maybe, because a maybe from me is how a rota got in here in the first place.**"

**Iven Tallo** said no in two words, which were *not a trade*, and then explained them anyway, because he could not help it: *a page is not a bond and a man with a page is not a man with a trade. If I keep it, the boy in the school store keeps a page one day, and there is a man here with a page and no wage, and that is the exact shape of a holder. I have spent six weeks refusing to be one of those and I am not going to arrive at it sideways.*

**Wen Pask** said no and gave the reason that made the room go quiet, and he said it standing up with his weight on his good leg in the doorway of a school store he had slept in for twenty-three nights: *Twenty-three days and nobody has asked me to keep anything at all. If I keep a page I am the first person in this building to hold a thing, and I am the person nobody can pay, and a holder with no bond is a man an office can use. I have thought about that for four days and I would rather be nothing here than be that.*

**Nera Oduya** said no from three steps and gave the reason that she had been holding since the Sunday: *I am leaving in the spring, and a page kept by a person who is leaving is a page with an end date on it, and this boundary watched a yes turn into a nothing in a month because a piece of paper came back up a road. I will not be the person who holds the second one.*

"No," said Leena Orr, from the litter.

"Somebody asked me why not just now," she said, "and I am not going to give a reason in a room, and that is the no. Write *no reason given* and do not come back to it, because I will give the same no."

"Entered with the words *no reason given*," said Dalla Sen, "and I am not going to come back to it either, and that is a promise from the hand that holds this book and I would like the room to notice that the clerk and the woman on the litter have just agreed on something in public."

**Jo Verrick** said no because she was going to be the first person in a fire.

**Bel Ordry** said, "Then I will," and the anteroom stopped, and he had to start again.

---

"You have to understand what you are being given," he said. "I have held nothing for fifteen weeks and I have been *good* at it, and I explained why to the air engineer round a turn on Wednesday morning, and I am not going to explain it again in a room. So I am going to say the terms and then leave and let the room argue about them, and if the room likes them the page is mine and if it does not then I have said them and it is nobody's fault but mine.

"**One.** The first column is a count. **A number, and no name, and not an initial, and not a surname, and not *and two more* either.** The reason is not modesty and it is not a principle. A name in the first column is a holder, and a holder is what the fourth paragraph of that notice wants, and I have read that paragraph on nineteen days out of twenty-three, and it is for a man to be entered against a route so that a boy can be paid, and **I will not be the name in the first column of a page in this building while that paragraph exists.**

"**Two.** The second column is a condition. *Wet. Dark. Cold. First. Last.* And the *when* column is a word and never a day, and never *Monday*, and never the four days a person works, and I want it on the page itself and not only in the book, because a page is a thing a stranger holds and the stranger will not have the book. **A day of the week in a column is a rota. A rota is a register with the words *person* left out of it, and a cook found that out in three days and the clerk in eight and the air engineer in a fortnight, and I am not going to be the one who puts it back in.**

"**Three.** The third column is what is done. And I am not going to fill it in where the work is not yet known, and I want that in my own words and read out: **a column you fill in before you know is a promise you have not made yet.** A woman in this kitchen filled one in in three days and it came out as her own boots, and she said so out loud, and I am not going to be the second person in this building to do it.

"**Four.** The fourth column." He put both hands flat on the table, which he had not done in the anteroom in fifteen weeks. "**It stays empty and I am not going to head it.** And a man with a board has refused to head a column in this building for five weeks, and I would like everybody in this room to go and look at him while I say it, because I am not doing it for the reason he is doing it, and the room is going to want them to be the same reason, and they are not.

"He is not heading it because a heading makes a thing a unit and a unit is a thing a room can be praised in. **I am not heading it because a column of refusals with a name on the top of it is a list of things this boundary owes somebody, and I have been handed a page about the sixth minutes and the low end of a corridor by a man who was owed nothing and refused, and I would like this column to be the one place in this building where nobody is owed anything and nothing is promised.**"

Tams Quill, from the bench, with his right hand open in his lap, said: "That is a different reason and it is a better one, and I am not going to tell you that in front of a room."

"I would rather you did."

"No," said Tams Quill.

---

"Then the keeper has to rule," said Bel Ordry, "and he has been waiting two days for somebody to make him, and here it is.

"**A keeper of a page is not a holder of a route.** Those are not the same office. A holder of a route is what the guild's fourth paragraph means and what a man entered in a register is, and every one of those can be sent to a room and told to answer. A keeper of a page is a person who has agreed to look at a piece of paper and can be told *stop looking at it* and nothing else, and that is a smaller power and the only power this building has got left.

"Entered in the keeper's words, in his own hand, read out again, and on the same page as the water-carrying, because it is a term of the water-carrying and not a rule of the building."

Dalla Sen wrote it, and read it out, and read it out again, and then Sefu Aran said the thing that was under all of it, without coming off the wheel.

"And I will say the bad reason for it, because a room that cannot hear the bad reason has not got a good one, and I have held a door shut for thirteen weeks by never saying what was behind it.

"**I am writing that sentence in a book in order to keep a person out of a file.** That is the first thing I have ever put in this book on purpose to stop something happening, and for thirteen weeks everything I have written in it has been a record of something that had already happened, and I have been proud of that, and I have also been using it. In four years a reader in a guild file will find that sentence and read it as a boundary defending itself, and **it is not that. It is a fence round a man who volunteered, so that when the office comes — and it will come, and the notice is already on a road — the office will find that there is no holder here to enter.**

"And I have written that down in a book, and the clerk can read it out, and if that is a keeper keeping a file then I am keeping one, and I would rather be a keeper who keeps a file on purpose and knows it than a keeper who has never noticed he is holding one."

"Entered in the keeper's words and read out," said Dalla Sen, "and I am adding in the clerk's hand that the keeper has said out loud that he is doing the thing he spent a fortnight being frightened of and that the room did not stop him. In a year the fright will look like the virtue and the fence will look like the wall, and they are not the same thing, and only one of them is a keeper."

---

The page has nine rows. Three lines of the third column are filled and six are empty, and the other four rows went in during the sixteenth week in three hands and were read out and argued over, and nobody argued about them, which is the finding. and Dalla Sen read the whole of it out at the fifth bell with the anteroom over its number and six people down the nine steps listening.

Jo Verrick gave the first, in her own words, and it is the pump: **run attended, two people, from the second bell to the fifth, and stopped on a chalk line that has not moved since a woman with a slinged shoulder put it there; the check is a hiss on one note and the two-note vocabulary is withdrawn; twenty-six strokes, six minutes off, nineteen; the housing shut; five cleanings paid; the estimate is one, in my own hand, and I have not written zero; and the pump is run by two people, because a frame with one person on it and a lever in it is a pump that a man has said yes to.**

Renn Vale gave the second, and it was not hers either: **and on the morning the slab under that lip turns, the work stops, and the lever is not touched by anybody, and nobody is to be sent anywhere to find out whether it has turned.**

Bel Ordry read the third one out himself, twice, and first he checked three things out loud on the landing in front of the room and made Dalla Sen read her notes on them.

"No name on it, on this page or any other. It is not a day. It is not a rota and nobody is to make it one, and if it becomes one she will stop and she has said so. And it sends nobody anywhere to make it true, because it cannot: there is no person in it to send, no hour in it, and a page cannot walk down a corridor." He looked up. "**Those are her three, in my reading of them, and I have read them out loud and the clerk has read them back, and I have not put one word of mine in the line. Somebody else wrote it and I am only reading it.**"

The clerk read it, and it is in the book in her hand with no name against it, because it is the only line on the page that belongs to a person who is not in the room:

*I will not stand at that low end six times a week. I will come, any day, any hour, the moment that sound changes, and I will come once and I will look and I will shout if there is anything to shout about, and then it is over and I go back up to my covers.*

"That is hers," said Dalla Sen, "and it is in my hand because a hand is the only thing this building has, and there is no name against it, and I am not going to put one there."

Then she stopped, because the last thing on the page needed a name and could not have one, and the room had been avoiding it for nine days.

Renn Vale said, "**The wash rooms. A row, and the third column empty, and I am going to say why in my own words, and it is her sentence, and the clerk has it in the book in full, word for word, from a door in the fifteenth week, and I am not going to improve it and I am not going to shorten it here either.**

"Kept by the person who lifts the covers, and by nobody else. No work in them. Nobody to be sent down that corridor to do anything, ever. The floor refused and staying refused. The kitchen's book and not the route's. And no name against them, by anybody, including by accident, in four years.

"So the row is here, and the third column is empty, and the empty is not an omission and it is not a mystery: **it is a room kept by somebody who is not on this page, and that is the only kind of keeping in this building that nobody can turn into a rota, and it is the oldest answer anybody in this Hearthhold has, and we found it nine days ago at a door.**"

"And the ninth one," said Bel Ordry. "Where is the ninth one."

"It is not on the page," said Renn Vale, "and I am not going to let anybody put it on, and I said that in this room on Thursday and I am saying it again with a page in front of me, which is a different kind of saying and I would like the clerk to read both.

"And the reason is Nera's and it is the best reason anybody in this room has given for anything: **a blank row in an old document is a mystery, and a blank row in a new document is an omission, and a stranger reads an omission as the worst thing they can think of, and about that room the worst thing anybody can think of is that we have posted somebody there and are not paying them, and a fourteen-year-old said that sentence in this room and it is the most obvious reading there is.**

"So we do not publish the accusation before anybody has made it. And on Thursday the air engineer said the same thing to a schoolteacher, and the two of us have been saying the same thing in different words for four days, and **that is the first time in seventeen weeks that two women in this building have arrived at the same refusal from two different directions, and I would like it noticed by somebody who is not me.**"

---

Ilsa Norr asked the question in the anteroom at the sixth bell, and she had asked it once before, in the fifteenth week, in front of nine people, and nobody had answered it then either.

"Three of them now," she said. "The oldest book in this building: nine rooms, not one name, cannot be dated, and five women have been in a room with it. The clerk's book: a name against almost everything including a name against a refusal, a month old, and a stranger can pick it up. **And this one**, on a butcher's sheet, in my hand, three columns and a fourth with nothing in it and nobody's name on the top of it.

"And in the fifteenth week I said I could not tell you which of the two was safer, and the air engineer said nobody can, and asked for it to be read at the fifth bell so that in a year she could read it out and be believed. **It has been read out every day since. Now there are three. I am asking again, and I have earned the right to ask twice.**"

Bel Ordry looked at it for a while, and the room let him.

"I can tell you which of mine is safest," he said, "and it is not going to be liked, and I am not going to be thanked for it, so you can put it in the book however you like.

"**The one with the most empty in it.** A page you can pick up is a page somebody can carry out of the room it is about. The reason *this* one is different is that the man holding it sleeps upstairs of the clinic return, and there is no road out of this building except up nine steps and a gate. If he wrote it out he would have a page about this boundary, and he would not have this page, because of the fourth column, and he could not possibly guess what goes in it, and neither can I."

"That is a good reason," said Nera Oduya, from three steps, "and it will not survive this boundary's first year, and I would like somebody to be suspicious of it in about a month."

"I would like a person in this room to be suspicious of me in about a month," said Bel Ordry, "because I have been good at holding nothing for a very long time and I do not know what I am like holding something and neither does the room."

---

Tams Quill read the board at the sixth bell on the Friday, on the day the clerk had named on the Tuesday, and Dalla Sen made a point of saying so out loud before anybody said anything about it.

"I entered the day on the page on the Tuesday and I read it out this morning and I am telling the room before anybody notices," she said, "because it is one promise kept to the letter in fifteen weeks and **I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would like the room to notice the rate.** And I am also entering the other half: the count told me on the Tuesday that he would not quote a figure he had not walked and I said I would walk it for him, and I did not walk it either, because I did not want to be the one holding a number in a room over a weekend. So we both sat on it, and neither of us has mentioned it until now, and that is the shape of most of the things in this book."

"**Forty-eight and a half**," said Tams Quill. "And the other one, which I have now walked twice. **The last figure anybody in this room heard out loud was ninety-five and a quarter, on the last day of the fourteenth week, and it is a hundred and three and a quarter now, and eight has gone on since, and here are the eight, because eight is short and because it is the only list that matters.**"

"**Ilsa Norr, one.** A school on one day in seven, the plan, and a corridor she asked for back. **Mikka Hale, one and a half.** The pot, the book, a frame, and a number that went from thirteen to twelve on a day she can name. **Jo Verrick, one.** A stoppage before a cycle and the arithmetic of six pails. **Renn Vale, one.** A door, a slate, a column of refusals. **Dalla Sen, one and a half.** A page going out of a room, a page coming into one, a woman at a counter, and the fifth bell twice a day. **Bel Ordry, one.** A bar for a seventh morning and *nothing*, a valve asked and not answered, and a page. **Isel Ferrow, half.** A fill instead of a door. **Nera Oduya, half.** A draught on four treads and a hand on a stone.

"That is the list, and the list adds, and I have done it twice. **Eight on ninety-five and a quarter is a hundred and three and a quarter.** And Wen Pask's twenty-three days and twenty-three nights are in the column with no heading and in nothing else, and a page is in there too, and a line that was refused and is still not there, and it has still got nothing over the top of it, and that is twice now that two men in this room have refused to head a column, and this is not a coincidence and it is not a fashion. **It is a building learning that the things it has decided not to commit to are the things it is keeping.**"

---

There was a row for the anteroom, and the count in it was the highest on the page, and the condition said *wet*, and the third column was empty, and Bel Ordry read it out and then said the reason, and the reason was his own and he had not been asked.

"It is empty because the anteroom's work is a knee," he said, "and the knee is Jo Verrick's, and the seventh term already says what happens on the morning the slab turns, and the seventh term is in the third column, and the anteroom's row is empty anyway.

"**And the day somebody fills that row in with the word *person*, this page is finished and it is wrong, and I want that in my own hand and read out, and I want whoever does it to have to be in the room.**

"And I am not going to pretend there is a post under that lip. There has never been a post under that lip. It is packed and not held, and the packing is a knee, and the knee will be Jo Verrick's if the slab turns while she is on the lever, and she knows it, and it is read out at the fifth bell every day, and nobody has built anything to change it, and nobody is going to this month. I am saying that out loud in the seventeenth week so that it is a thing a person heard said and not a thing somebody found out."

---

The plan has no name on it, and it has a date.

Dalla Sen asked the room for a name for it at the sixth bell and got nothing, and not one person made a suggestion, and that is in the book. **A page with no name on it is a hole of a kind nobody had thought of until a woman from a counter four days up a road explained what was missing from it.**

"The dowel cannot be dated," said Nera Oduya, "and ours can, and that is the whole of the difference between the two objects, and it is not a large advantage, and I will take it. So: no name, and a date, in Bel Ordry's hand, in the top corner, being the day he took it, and I am not going to pretend he chose that day and I am not going to ask him to."

Bel Ordry wrote the date, and no name, gave the page back to himself, and put it inside his coat.

At the sixth bell the anteroom read six things on one line, as it has every day for three weeks, with Renn Vale's seventh term under the lip's range and the two read together, and the six were: **the lip's range, which was a finger and a half to a finger and three-quarters and worse on a cold morning; and the seventh term; and the ninth line; and the pot, which was twelve days; and the blank on the review page with the two sentences on it instead of a name; and the air valve, asked and not answered, and the spring.**

She read one more, because Renn Vale had asked her on the Wednesday to read it immediately after Bel Ordry's line, every fifth bell, from then on, and the two of them had to be read together, and the room had to hear both in one breath.

On the shelf above the bench there was a knotted bundle of work cloth with a thing wrapped inside it that had not been identified and had not been opened and was not going to be, and in the pocket of the coat on the hook a brass survey rule had said nothing for eight weeks. The crew were bounded maintainers on an obligation that had not been discharged, with a count's refusal on one side of a page and a keeper's *not yet* on the other, and a condition on the head of it that no page in this building could yet meet. The water was two thirds of what it had been on an open day. The pot was twelve days and had never once gone up. The corridor was shut three days in seven again and the school ran three, which is what it is supposed to be, and it took two closures and a lost week to get back to it. A courier had been asked for a spring and had not been promised one. A lock four days up a road was dry, and a man there had asked in five words who was going to tell the four houses, and this boundary has not answered him and has a month to try.

And a person was still under that lip.
