# Chapter 170 — A Term This Building Wrote

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the thirty-third week, a Friday, and the school ran for the thirtieth morning. A brace went back into the same wall a foot and a half lower down than it had been on Wednesday. A woman with a wooden tally was in the anteroom, and a clerk read four sentences this building wrote for itself three weeks ago against the original and compared the two, and the comparison is the whole of what happened in the last hour of the morning.**

**The school door at the far end of nine steps has no wedge in it and is not going to get one back this week. A board has eighteen entries on it and nine of them are under a column with nothing over the top of it. The count is at one hundred and five and is not going to be one hundred and six.**

At the fourth bell the thirtieth morning began, and the brace went back in at the second bell before the nine went in, because a corridor that is open on a Friday is the only kind of day left in this building this week and everybody in that anteroom knew it and nobody wasted it on a speech.

On Thursday night the carpenter had it figured out with a burned stick on a board offcut, and the figure was a foot and a half. The bad repair in that wall is at a height, and the push comes out at the worst of the wall, and the worst of the wall is the repair, and the repair has been carrying it for nine hundred years by accident rather than by anybody's design, which is the whole reason nobody ever looked.

"**You move the foot down,**" he said, "**and you put another length under it, and the push comes out below the repair, in the stone.**"

"**Say where that is on the other side,**" said Marek Venn, "**because the other side is a room with nine hearth stones in it and I am not going to have a figure and a direction and no place.**"

"**Below the sill.**" Iven Tallo put his own hand on the wall at about knee height on the anteroom side. "**Below the sill on that side is stone and the back of the stone, and nothing in that room is sitting on it. I have been in that room twice and I have looked, and I am telling you what is under the sill and not what is under the ninth stone, because those are two different questions and I have answered the one I was asked.**"

"**Then the ninth stone is out of it,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, and it was not a question.

"**The ninth stone is out of it,**" said Iven Tallo. "**And I am going to say the thing nobody wants said on a Friday morning, which is that the ninth stone was never going to be in it. A strip of crate board and a hand and a half is not a bed. It is a fortnight that became a month that became ten weeks, and everybody in this room has known that since the twenty-fourth week, including the two people who sat on it.**"

"**Then the ninth stone gets a wedge,**" said Marek Venn, "**which is a thing this building has got five of, and a beech wedge driven under a stone bed by a carpenter is not a repair and is not a brace and is not a mark, and it is the only thing in this anteroom today that costs nothing and is honest.**"

Marek Venn had watched the bed come up by half an inch on Thursday and had said nothing about it in the anteroom, because there was nothing to say in a room that was not the room it had happened in.

What he had done instead was get up on Friday morning before the second bell and walk the length of the party wall with his own book open on his forearm, both sides, which is a thing he has done four times in thirty-four weeks and has never once done out loud. He wrote nothing. The wall gave him nothing to write that Jo Verrick had not already said better in front of a room, and the private book in his coat had two walls in it and had had them for a month, and this week one of the two had been taken out of a wall by the person it was hurting, and neither of them was his to put back.

"**Say the number of wedges, because there were seven in this building in the eleventh week and a room is going to count them,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Five sound and one dead stick, and the dead stick has been missing since the twenty-sixth week,**" said Renn Vale. "**Five sound. Two of them are in that brace going in now. One of the five is going under a hearth stone. That leaves two, and one of the two is in the school corner's door and one of the two is in the basket, and the school door has none, and nothing in this room has solved that and nothing in this room is going to today.**"

"**Say the school door out loud, Dalla, because it is the only thing in that anteroom that has cost this building something, and I would like it said by the clerk and not by me.**"

Dalla Sen entered it without looking up.

"**A door at the school end of this corridor has no wedge in it. Two sound wedges are going into a brace and one into a hearth stone, and there were five sound wedges on the second day of the thirty-second week and there are five sound wedges on the fifth day of the thirty-third, and three of the five are spoken for and two are not, and the school door is not one of the two.**"

"**And a door with no wedge in it is a door that has to be watched, and the answer is a person, and the person is the woman who keeps it, and she has said so. And nobody in this room has been sent anywhere, and the page at the low end is still blank, and no name has gone on it.**"

They put it in at about half past the first bell. The foot went lower, which meant a second length under the head end, which meant four people again, and the four were the same four, and what stopped each of it was what it had been on Wednesday, and the fourth of the four said the word again, and the clerk repeated it, and the anteroom's count was ten including one litter and did not change.

Then the shim went into the hollow for the second time, and a beech wedge went under the ninth hearth stone's bed at the kitchen-line end, and the carpenter drove it with a mallet in four taps and stopped, and the schoolteacher watched him do it from about a foot away and did not say anything at all, which Dalla Sen entered.

Jo Verrick took the figure off the frame at the second bell and read it out before she said anything about it, and the number was a finger and two-thirds, and it was a finger and two-thirds again on the third reading four minutes later, and she gave both, and then gave the half of it.

"**A finger and two-thirds, twice, four minutes apart, and I have not got a reading from before the second reading, and a number that has only been the same twice in four minutes is not a number anybody in this room should put in a column.**"

"**Then put it in a book,**" said Dalla Sen, "**which is what a book is, and not in a column, and not on a page, and not on the anteroom's row, and I have written it down and the two readings are in it and the fact that there were only two is in it.**"

Then Talla Roke came up the nine steps, and said what she was before she said anything else, and she had her three behind her and her hands loose.

"**I am not here about a boy, and I have got a thing to say that is about a piece of paper and not about a road, and I want it said while four people are in this room who wrote it.**"

"**Say it, Talla,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Three weeks ago this building wrote down four sentences for five people, and one of the four was *you are not to open anything*.**" She looked at Bel Ordry and then at the clerk. "**A brace has been opened. A brace has been moved. Two wedges have gone out of a door. A name has been entered in this clerk's book and a page is going to be blank at the bottom of a piece of paper with a carpenter's record on it.**"

"**And I have been on roads for two years, and there is a guild paragraph that says a person may work under a named holder entered against a named route, and every office that has ever written one of those has said *this is the arrangement and you agreed to it*, and the reason those paragraphs work is that nobody can point at the day they stopped being true.**"

"**So say the thing you came to say,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**Say what you are going to do about a term you wrote yourself and cannot now keep,**" said Talla Roke. "**Because you are not the guild and I am not asking you to be the guild, and the difference between this boundary and a guild notice is not the content. It is that this boundary can be asked in a room by a person who is standing in it.**"

Then Dalla Sen read the four terms out of her own book, word for word, and Bel Ordry read them a second time against the original, and Iven Tallo read them a third time against what two people had said in a room on the second day of the thirtieth week, and all three were the same.

"**Then say what they say, and say it as terms and not as a story,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and then somebody who is not me has to decide what this building does about them, and I am the clerk and I am not going to decide it and I am going to hand it to the room and go and stand at the wall.**"

She did go and stand at the wall, with the book held against her chest, and gave the room the whole of the anteroom, which is a thing she has done four times in thirty-three weeks and not once before this week.

"**It is not a repeal,**" said Renn Vale, from the fourth step, before anybody else. "**Do not strike it out. A strike is a lie. It says we wrote it and we meant it on the second day of the thirtieth week and we were wrong, and it was not wrong, and it is not the reason a brace came out of a wall on Thursday.**"

"**Then it cannot be left either,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because a term that has been kept in the letter and broken in the fact, and left on the page after anybody has noticed, is a term a person four days up a road can pick up and read, and the office that reads it will read it as this boundary's practice and not as this boundary's accident, and the difference between those two is the only thing this building has that a guild notice has not got.**"

"**So say the third thing,**" said Ilsa Norr, from the doorway, "**which is the thing that is actually true, and I would like a room to hear it in one sentence and then I would like the morning to go on.**"

Bel Ordry said it, standing, with his hand on the stair rail.

"**The four terms bound a work and not a building. They bound five people going to look at a pile of timber four days up a passage. A building outlasts the terms of a work, and the next asking this boundary makes will have terms written for the thing and not for the job, and I am going to write the six sentences and I am not going to sign them, and Dalla is going to read them out and I am going to compare them.**"

Dalla Sen entered it from the wall without turning round, and her voice carried across nine feet of stone floor the way it does.

"**The four terms of the fourth asking were read out three times today against the original, and all three were the same.**"

"**They are not struck out, and they are not left, and they bound a work and not a building, and the reason a brace was opened on Wednesday and closed on Thursday is not in them and was never going to be in them.**"

"**The terms that applied to a pair of hands have now been extended to a piece of wood by a room and not by a clerk, and that is the first time in thirty-three weeks this boundary has changed what a set of terms is for.**"

"**And enter that I stood at the wall. I gave the room away for about four minutes, because the thing in front of me was not a fact and the two people in it were not numbers, and a clerk holding a book in a room like that is holding a thing that has stopped being hers.**"

Then the board, and the eighteenth thing on it, entered by the man who reads it, with the chalk in his left hand and the right one flat and open in his lap, and no day named.

"**Eighteen. Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace. Nine in the ordinary column and nine in the one with nothing over the top of it.**"

"**Say why the brace goes in the unheaded column, and say it as a reason and not as a joke, and do not tell me the week got away from you.**"

"**Because a brace is a support, and a support is a thing somebody is going to have to keep, and that is the volume's whole question standing on a wall, and I have put it on this board because a board that cannot hold a brace cannot hold anything.**"

He put the chalk down and left it there, which he has not done in thirty-three weeks.

"**And it goes in the unheaded column because the word over that column would be *keeper*, and there is no such word in this building, and there is not going to be one, and the five people who held it on Wednesday are not going to be standing here in ten years, and I will not be either.**"

"**Say the thing you have not said,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and then stop.**"

"**I am giving no eleventh reason, and not because the week got away from me.**" He did not pick the chalk up.

"**I gave a tenth on Thursday and it cost me something, and I have not worked out yet what it cost, and a man who has spent his life reading numbers is not going to stand in a room and guess at one.**"

"**So I am going to carry it and not put it on a page. That is the eleventh thing and it is not a reason, and it is the first time in eleven batches that I have had nothing, and I would like a person in a year to know that I had nothing and did not manufacture something.**"

Nobody in that anteroom gave him a reason to have one, and the clerk did not ask.

At the fourth bell the anteroom's terms were said. So was the word. The clerk repeated it. The count is ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and the brace is over that door, and the frame is a finger and two-thirds out of true. It has to be watched for as long as this Hearthhold is here, by people who are not going to be named on anything, and one of the names that could be on it is in a stiff cover four and a half days up a road, in a clerk's book, in a hand that has not decided anything yet.

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and the count was read at one hundred and five, and the board was walked at eighteen entries with nine under a column that has no heading over it for the eleventh time running, and the brass survey rule in the pocket of the coat on the hook had said nothing for twenty-four weeks and the wrapped plate on the shelf above the same bench had been unopened for thirty-three.**

"**Three today and two hundred and twenty-one since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and thirty-eight and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one, and have not moved since the second day of this week, and the fifth asking is in the book with six sentences and a split in it.**"

"**The pot is nine. The jars are two. The seam is four threads and was eight in the nineteenth week and has not been measured in between, and there is no number in this building for how fast that is going, and I have said in four weeks running that the number is not mine to make, and I am going to keep saying it until somebody else says it first.**"

"**The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and five days and one hundred and five nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and six. I have said in fourteen weeks running that I am going to keep writing it and I am writing it, and a woman with a wooden tally has been in this room and has not thanked me, and I am glad.**"

"**And entered, in the fourth column of nothing and on a page in a coat, is the fact that this boundary's record says a man in this building recommended shutting a room in the twenty-fourth week.**"

"**Nobody has read that out loud in a room in a month, and it is still true, and nothing that has happened with a piece of beech over a door this week makes it less true. I am entering it on the last day of a week in which something went right, on purpose, because a clerk who enters that only in a bad week is managing a record.**"

---

**On the fifth day of the thirty-third week a brace of a school's own beech is over the head of the anteroom's outer service door, a foot and a half lower than it was on Wednesday, and its shim is in a hollow cut nine hundred years ago, and its head is going to crush at its corners in about two years, and the term that says so is in a clerk's book in four hands.**

**The school door at the far end of nine steps has no wedge in it, and there are two sound wedges left in this building, and a woman who keeps that door is one person, and the page where a person would go is blank, and nobody was sent.**

**A board has eighteen entries on it and nine of them are under a column with nothing over the top of it, and a man who reads it gave the tenth reason for not heading that column a fortnight ago and cost himself something doing it, and today he had nothing and manufactured nothing, and the clerk did not ask.**

**And a count in that column is at one hundred and five, and it is not going to be one hundred and six, and it is not a register, and it has never dated anything, and nobody in this building is going to put a mark beside it.**

**And under a lip on the far side of this corridor there is still a person, packed and not held, with two timber shores on a common landing above it and a post that has never been built. The landing carries no number on any page in this building.**

**A man in a stiff cover four and a half days up a road has not decided anything. And a man who is nobody's to send is standing on an anteroom step in a building where a piece of beech over a door is going to need watching for as long as the building is here.**
