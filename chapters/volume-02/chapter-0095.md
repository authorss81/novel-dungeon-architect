# Chapter 95 — The Man With The Counter

On the fifth day of the eighteenth week a man came down the middle passage carrying a counter-draft in a flat case and a store's order in the other hand, and he introduced himself in the anteroom before anybody asked, which nobody in this building has ever done, and the clerk entered it and then had to enter the second thing, which is that he said his own name in the same breath as his office and both of them in the same breath as the age.

"**Harlan Merrow. Bond assessor, Cartwright Guilds, thirty-eight, twenty-one years in the office. I sign the fourth paragraph.**

"I know that is a sentence nobody wants to hear in a room, so I have said it first and I am not going to explain it, because if I explain it I am making an argument and I have come up four days of road with a draft and not with an argument."

Dalla Sen wrote his name and his office and his age and then stopped with the chalk up and said, "**You have also told me you sign the fourth paragraph and I do not know where to put that, and I am going to ask you instead of guessing.**"

"Put it in the book," said Harlan Merrow. "**Put all of it in. If I get anything in this room wrong it will be because somebody decided I was all right.** That is the whole reason I have said it out loud, and the reason is that I have read a file in my career where a man did not say it, and the man was honest, and the honest man lost a boy a year of his trade, and I would rather be refused than be the reason a clerk cannot write a page.**

"And nobody in this building can say how long I was on the road. I would like that entered too, because your notice had no date on it and I want you to know that the road did not have one either and I have thought about that for a fortnight."

---

Four hundred paces of seasoned hardwood at the middle gate's store, in the office's own hand. Bel Ordry read it out in the room and then said the thing that made the anteroom laugh for the first time in a fortnight, which was that **the office's habit is to send a store's order with a draft, and the office is not sorry about it, and this boundary released that timber in writing and the release stands.**

"It stands and I am not going to argue it," said Harlan Merrow, "and I would like it said that the man who signed the draft I am carrying also signed that order, and that I have read the release, and that I have brought the order anyway because a man who brings only the paper he agrees with is not a counterparty, he is a **supplicant**, and I have watched one of those take eleven years to be useful to anybody."

Then he opened the flat case on the anteroom table, which is Mikka Hale's table, and Mikka Hale made him move it two feet to the left because there is a place on that table where the bread goes and nobody has ever been allowed to put paper on it, and he moved it without arguing, and Dalla Sen entered that.

---

"**The office's answer to a boundary's notice that asked for nothing,**" he said, "is this. It is six heads and I will read all six, and the fourth one is the one you already know and it is the one that matters, and I am not going to pretend the other five are decoration because they are not, and I am not going to pretend the fourth one is mine, because it is not.

"**One.** The Cartwright Guilds will maintain the water-carrying, the air, the corridor and the stair's near side as a maintained work, at a price to be set by the office and reviewed at the first of the month.

"**Two.** Timber will be supplied against that work from the middle gate's store, and the supply is continuous, and a boundary expecting a plank from a store is a boundary with a plan and a boundary without one is a boundary with a queue.

"**Three.** Every work will be attended, and attendance will be recorded, and the record will be shown to the office at the first of the month, and a boundary that cannot produce its record at the first of the month loses the work.

"**Four.** *An apprentice under a suspended master may continue in the trade for a period not exceeding one year under the supervision of a named holder entered against a named route, in a register kept by the office, on payment of the annual supervision fee.*"

"**And five,**" he said, "**is that the boundary may withdraw at the first of the month on giving one term's notice, and six is that the office accepts no withdrawal that is not given in a room with a person in it.**

"That is the draft. It is the fourth paragraph with a network wrapped round it, and I wrote the wrapper, and I will tell you which heads I wrote. I wrote one, two, three, five and six, and I did not write four, and I have read four out loud because it is the head the office will not move and I am not the office."

---

"**Then here is the objection and it is written out loud before anybody picks up the chalk,**" said Bel Ordry, "and I have been a man with no argument in a room for eighteen weeks and I have learned how to do this from a woman with a board, and I am going to do it in order, and the first one is mine and it is about the page and not the men.

"**A named holder entered against a named route in a register kept by the office is a person the office can be sent to.** That is all it is. It is not a fee and it is not a trade and it is not a claim. It is a sentence that puts a man in a file against a piece of ground, and the file is forty days on from the top of this passage, and when the thing under the man goes, somebody far away will send a letter to the man, and the man will be alone, and the man will be on a stair he cannot get off, and the office will have kept itself extremely clean."

"That is true," said Harlan Merrow.

"**It is true and it is also the least of it,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps, "and mine is the second and it is a technical objection and I will make it the way I would make it to a client.

"**This network cannot be maintained by a holder, because there is nothing in it a holder can hold.** It is a valve on a seam. It is a lip with a knee packing it. It is a water-lock four days up a road with no carpenter in it. It is a stair with a slab under a lip and a person under that slab, and I have not certified its air in eighteen weeks and I am not going to, and no man in a file is going to certify it either.

"**You are offering to maintain a set of things that are kept by a room, and the only one of them that can be entered against a name is the one that must not be.** And I would put it at the second head and not the fourth if I were you, because head two is the timber and the timber is the only honest sentence in the draft."

"That is also true," said Harlan Merrow, "**and I did not write two either. I wrote the wrapper and I am telling you I am standing here watching a clerk discover it in real time and I would like it recorded that I knew it before I came.**"

---

Renn Vale gave the third from the landing, and hers was about a person and not about a paper, and the room went quiet before she had finished the first clause.

"**Head three says a boundary that cannot produce its record at the first of the month loses the work.**

"I have kept a room for eighteen weeks with seven terms and an eighth, in chalk, in a book, on a slate, and the eighth term and a two-hour work at a rail are in this building in two hands at the same time and are not in agreement, and I put my name on that and I named myself as the woman who decided it.

"**Under head three, at the first of this month, a woman in my position loses the stair, and the loss is not a room. It is a woman with a slate, on a landing, being told by a man with a file that her own record is not a record because it does not have a person who can be sent the consequences of it.** And that is exactly what I have been refusing to let anybody build, and I will not have it built by a form because the form is polite and came with timber."

"That is the strongest of the three," said Harlan Merrow, "**and I am not going to argue it, and I am going to argue with head six in about a minute, and I would like somebody to stop me when I do, because I have been walking four days to do this and it is not polite of me to do it to a woman on a landing.**"

---

"**Then here is what the office has against all three,**" he said, "and it is not a bad answer, and I am sorry.

"**A holder is not a threat. A holder is a name, and what the office does with the name is refuse to send anybody.**

"A person in the office's register is a person the office has promised not to use. I know how that sounds. I have been on the other side of it. But the fourth paragraph is not a leash; it is the *only* thing in this file that ever produced a refusal — a man in a register can be suspended out of a route by a clerk who has read his work, and I have seen it stop a bad arrangement, and I have also seen it fail to stop one, and I am not going to tell this room it has never failed.

"And to the woman on the landing: **a room with no name on it is a room with nobody to answer for it.** If your stair turns on a cold morning and the packing goes and a man is under that slab, this room will have kept a beautiful honest record in chalk of the fact that it was always going to. And who does the office send? **Nobody. Because there is no holder. And a document that cannot be sued is a document that cannot be blamed, and in my experience a thing nobody can be blamed for is a thing nobody has to fix.**"

Nobody said anything for a while, and the check hissed on its one note, and the man at the wheel did not come off it.

Marek Venn said, from the bench, "**That is the whole of the guild's case and it is a good one and I am not going to pretend it is not, because I have spent eighteen weeks learning to say out loud the thing that is true against me and I am going to use it.**

"And I am the man who is most frightened of it, and I want that in the room in my own words, and I am not going to write it down, so somebody please write it down."

**She said she would.**

"**The first false refuge in my life failed a full-room test with nobody in it,** Marek Venn said, "and I have a hand that will not close because of it, and I have spent eighteen weeks in a building where the answer to *who answers for this* has been *nobody, and that is on purpose.* And this man has walked four days to tell me that *nobody, on purpose* is how a boy is left under eleven feet of dressed stone.

"And I do not have a better answer. **I want that entered. I have not got one.** I have got a plan with two empty lines and a fourth column nobody can head and a jar of water out of a wall, and none of that is an answer to a man under a lip, and I would be lying in this room if I stood up here and pretended it was."

"Then we are two," said Harlan Merrow, "**and I have been four days on a road with a flat case and I would like to go home.**

"I am not going to take the draft back up that road as a refusal, because I have written the wrapper and I know what the wrapper is worth and I know what the fourth head is worth, and the office is going to send somebody with a stamp and this will be a different conversation with a worse man.

"So I will tell you the truth about the office, since nobody up there will.

"**The office will put this exact draft to four other Hearthholds in the next two months, and it will be accepted in three of them, and there will be timber at three of them by the first of the month after, and the three will work. And the four of you here will be the only one in the file that refused, and you will be cited.**

"I am telling you that so that nobody in this room decides it is a matter of a room standing together. **It is not that. It is that you have all four counters in this building and we have a form, and if we were honest about our own arithmetic we would know which of us is going to be standing here in a year.**"

---

Iven Tallo came in at the fourth bell and had not been in the room, and Harlan Merrow gave him the fourth head again, and Iven Tallo read it out himself, in a school store in Ilsa Norr's hearing, with a boy standing in the doorway, and then he said the thing that has been standing in this book since the fifth day of the sixteenth week.

"**I will not sign it.**

"And I am not going to give the argument, because the argument holds and I have said so three times and I have refused to be forgiven for it once and I am not going to spend it again in a room with the man who signs it.

"**The reason is that a signature is a bond.** I have one suspended. I have not read the bond in eight months because I was told not to, and I do not know what I swore, and a man who cannot say what he swore in a room with a counter in front of him has no business putting his name at the bottom of anything, and the man in front of me is a man who signs this paragraph for a living and has been honest with us for a day and a half, and I am not going to be the reason his last week of work was a lie."

Wen Pask said, from the doorway, "**And I will not either, and I said so in the sixteenth week and I have not changed my mind and I would like it entered that I said it before he did, because he has been in this Hearthhold four months and I have been in it thirty days, and both of those are the same amount.**"

Iven Tallo turned round and looked at him and said, "**You did not say it before me.**

"You said it in the sixteenth week, which is a fortnight ago, and this is the fifth day of the eighteenth week, and I have been in this Hearthhold since the first week, and one of us is wrong about a fortnight and I do not know which and I am not going to stand in a school store and be told the order of things by a boy who is correcting me on a matter of memory to make himself sound first."

"I said it before you," said Wen Pask, "**and I do not know how I know that, and I would like the clerk to enter that a man of twenty-two has claimed a thing about a fortnight that the man of thirty-six has contradicted, and that I can produce nothing.**"

"Then the clerk writes nothing," said Dalla Sen, "**and I am going to say why, and it is not neutrality. The boy's own words on this are in a book in his own order and they are four sentences and they were said on a staircase. And a man does not have to be believed twice. I have believed him once and I am not going to be asked to believe him in an order.**"

Tams Quill declined to read the board on the Friday, having named no day, and said that the figure on it was **fifty-two and a half**, that it had gone up on the second day and again on the fourth and again that morning, and that he would read it when he had walked it, and the clerk entered that he named no day and the keeper said the clerk's habit of entering things nobody asked for was the only functioning part of the government of this Hearthhold.
