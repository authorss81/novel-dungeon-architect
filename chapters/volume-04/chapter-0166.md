# Chapter 166 — The Air On The Other Side

**Shut for the corridor on the first day of the thirty-third week, a Monday, and a man of forty-nine went up the middle passage at the first bell with three of his own and the fir from a store, and the beech stayed. A woman who keeps the air asked a man for a second work in a room with the terms out loud, and she was wrong in public before the second bell and said so herself before anybody could be kind to her about it.**

**The brace is sound. The room on the other side of the wall it pushes on is not, and nine children are taught in it, and a physician named the new failure point in this anteroom before anything has been stood under anything, and gave it a name that is in a clerk's book and not on a page.**

The fact of the store going up the passage came down with a road hand who did not come in, because a road hand does not come in. She said it out loud on her way past and nobody asked her to repeat it, and it was in the anteroom by the second bell.

A Monday in that room has a different sound from the other six days in it, and Marek Venn had noticed it in his second week without ever having said so. The corridor is shut, so nothing comes down nine steps from above, and what is left is the pump, which runs on the second bell, and the beam under the table, which has a crack in it that the first bell sets off and the second bell lets go of. The anteroom on a shut morning is a room with one engine in it and no traffic, and everybody in it hears their own feet. The five cut pieces of beech and the two wedges were still on the bench where they had been since Wednesday, because nobody had been allowed to move anything under the fifth asking's terms, and a bench of wood that has not been moved in three days begins to look like an exhibit.

"**A man of forty-nine went up at the first bell with three of his own and the fir, and the terms stand, and the beech stays, and he said he would read the list again on any morning anybody asked him,**" said Dalla Sen. "**Entered, and entered that he was not in this room and that nobody in this building asked him anything before he went, and that the three of his own are not in this room either and are not named here.**"

"**Enter that he did not ask for anything on his way out,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step, "**and enter that I have been asked to be suspicious of him in a month and it is a month, and I am going to do it in a room and out loud and with a reason, and I am going to do it now, in an empty room, because he is four days up that passage with a load.**"

"**Say the reason before anybody argues with you, and say who arranged the timing, because I did and I am not going to have it done behind a man's back in a building with a book.**"

"**You arranged it. You asked me on Friday afternoon whether I had been to the store and I said no and you said the man is going up on Monday and that is when you will do it.**" Bel Ordry put his hand flat against the coat where the plan was. "**That is a dirty thing to have arranged. I would have said the same thing with the man standing in this room, and I want that entered, and I am going to say it now anyway.**"

"**Then say the suspicion, in your own words, and say what it is about, because a room does not carry a shape that a man has not described.**"

"**It is about the beech, and it is not about the man, and I have thought about it for two days and I would rather be wrong out loud than right in a coat.**" He came the two steps into the room. "**Forty-one pieces of beech, eleven of them better than the rest, kept eleven years, given to a boundary that will not put his name on a paper, and carried down four days of passage on four men's backs because a cart could not come. And nothing in this building can pay him for it, and nothing in this building can be found by it, and a store's keeper who has been given a favour by a place he is not in has less room to say no the second time.**"

"**Say the part of it that is against your own suspicion, because you have never once in this building left that out.**"

"**The part against it is that a man with eleven years of beech in a store, going to be a keeper of nothing in about a week, came down four days with nothing in his hands in order to be asked. That is a fact and it is in your book in his words and I have read it. And I am suspicious of him anyway, and a man who has said a thing out loud that argues against his own suspicion is the only kind of suspicion this building has ever had.**"

Then Nera Oduya came down off the step, which she has not done on a working morning since the twenty-seventh week, and put the valve board on the anteroom table flat, and squared it, and stood with her hands behind her back.

"**I am going to ask a man in this room for a work, and I am going to do it in the practice, and the practice is the one I have taken off people four times and offered once, and I am not going to explain why I am offering it twice.**"

"**Say the terms, Nera, and do them properly, because a room that skipped them last month did not get a better answer, it got a slower one.**"

"**Out loud, in a room, the whole of it: that somebody is named who can be told no, and that somebody is Renn Vale, who is on the landing and can hear me. And the three who are not going to be asked, named with the reason for each, before I ask anybody anything.**"

"**Mikka Hale. She said yes on Friday to holding a corner of a piece of beech and she said what stops it before anybody asked her to. A person who is holding the thing cannot be asked whether the thing ought to be there, and I am not going to put a cook in a position of having an opinion about a brace she is a hand in.**"

"**Jo Verrick. She reads that wall at a first bell every morning, and the air and the wall are two ends of the same question this week, and I am asking about the air. I am not going to have the woman who takes the first reading of a wall hold the same end of it as the woman who decides what the air is doing.**"

"**Iven Tallo. He cut it and he is in this room and I am going to ask him questions, because I have to, and I am not asking him for a work, and if those two things get confused in this building I will stop asking anybody anything.**"

Then the work, and it was one sentence.

"**Does the brace change the air in that room, and in the anteroom, and if it does, what is the limit, and I want the limit with no number and no day on it because a limit with a number on it is a thing I will be held to at a date instead of at a wall.**"

"**Four tons,**" said Mero Senn, from the doorway, "**or thereabouts, and your clerk has the or thereabouts written down beside it, and I am going to be in this room for the rest of it.**"

"**You are welcome and you are not the air authority.**"

"**No. And here is the part I came for.**"

He came in off the threshold and set his bag down against the wall, which he had not done in the eight weeks he had been in this building.

"**A brace is sound. That is the thing a room wants to say at the moment a thing goes up, and it is true today. And the shelter is not, and the shelter is the room nine children are taught in, and I want the whole of that sentence read out in a room where somebody is responsible for it.**"

"**Say the rule, Mero, and then say what you are asking for.**"

"**The rule is that a plan with a named injured person in it is not a plan, and I have said it, and the fifth time is not going to be in a shape that is easier to hear. I am asking for the failure point of this brace to be named out loud, today, before a person is stood under it, and I am asking that it be named in words that a person who is not a builder can carry.**"

Dalla Sen put the chalk down and did not pick it up again until she had asked her own question.

"**Say why it has to be named at all, because a man on a step has spent a fortnight saying that a name is a handle, and he is right, and I am the clerk who is supposed to know that better than anybody in this room.**"

"**Because it has one,**" said Mero Senn. "**Every brace has one and most of them find it out on a bad afternoon in a room with nobody in it. If it is going to be found out on a bad afternoon then I want it found out this morning instead, in a room, by a person, and I want a person who is going to be here in ten years to have heard the name of it from somebody who was frightened of it.**"

"**Then say the name, and say it in the plainest words this building owns, and no stone is going to be cut for it.**"

Mero Senn looked at the wall where the chalk line and the string were, at the height of a man's shoulder, with the two inches and a bit that a pump maintainer had given a room before anybody asked her.

"**The place where it comes out,**" he said. "**That is the name. It is not on the wall, and it is not on a stone, and it is not the ninth hearth stone's bed, because I do not know that, and I will not give a room a name for a thing I have not looked at. It is where four tons leaves this brace, and it is in a room nine children are in three mornings in seven, and on the fourth day of a week it is a classroom with a strip of crate board under a stone and a sill packed in clay.**"

"**Then read it back,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and read it against what he said, and if they are not the same I am going to have to ask him to say it again, and he is a physician and it is going to cost him something to be asked twice, and I am asking anyway.**"

Jo Verrick read it back, word for word, off the slate where Dalla Sen had written it, and compared it against what the man had said, and the two were the same.

Two steps behind the anteroom's back wall, on the other side of four hundred years of dressed stone, nine children were not in a room. It was a shut Monday and the school does not run on a shut Monday, and nothing had moved in that room since the Friday morning. Mero Senn had named the place where the push would arrive, in a room, out loud, with a room full of people, on a day when the room was empty, which is the cheapest kind of day to say a frightening thing on, and he had chosen it anyway.

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that it is a failure point and not a mark, and that no mark has been cut in the stone to hold it, and that the name is *the place where it comes out* and that it is a phrase and not a term, and that a phrase can be said out loud in a room by anybody and a term can only be read by the person who wrote it.**"

"**And I would like a boy to read the numbers,**" she said, and looked down the nine steps.

Pell Roke was on the bottom step with his ear against the stone where he had been for an hour, and he came up on the crutch as far as the anteroom floor and no further, and Dalla Sen gave him the slate and stood where he could see the wall and not the slate.

"**Say what the numbers are before you say them,**" she said, "**because you read numbers and the numbers are not counts of anything this time, they are a wall.**"

"**They are a wall,**" he said. "**They are the two ends of a piece of string on a wall and they are read at a first bell by a woman and written in a book, and I am going to read what the book says and not what the wall says today, because a book is what a person reads out.**"

"**Then read it.**"

"**First bell, second day of the thirty-second week. A quarter of an inch. First bell, third day. A quarter of an inch. First bell, today. Nothing.**"

The room did not fill in after it, which is what this room does, and then it did.

Marek Venn felt the whole thing arrive at once, in the part of him that has been standing in rooms in this building for thirty-two weeks waiting for a wall to say something, and what arrived was not a fact. It was a want. He wanted the eight readings of a quarter of an inch to have been caused by a thing he was about to do, and he wanted it badly enough that for about two seconds he was standing in a room with nine people in it doing arithmetic on a hope, and he caught himself at it in the time it takes to move a forearm off a knee.

He wrote nothing in his own book then, because there was nothing to write that was not a wish, and he had been thirty-two weeks in this building learning the difference, and the difference had cost him two burns and a channel and a knee that does not bend past about half, and he was not going to pay for it again on a Monday morning over a number.

"**That is nine readings,**" he said. "**And eight of them are a quarter of an inch and one of them is nothing, and the one that is nothing is the morning after the third day. Which day was the third day.**"

"**The third day was Wednesday,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and the school ran. And nothing on the fourth day and nothing on the fifth day, and the fifth day is today.**"

"**Say what you think that is,**" said Renn Vale, from the landing, "**and say it as a man who is allowed to think out loud in this room, and not as a finding.**"

"**I think the wall has stopped moving, and I have got no reason, and I am not going to give a room a reason I do not have, and I am going to be honest about what I would prefer, which is that it is because of the fourth hand.**"

"**Which is not up yet,**" said Iven Tallo.

"**Which is not up yet,**" said Renn Vale. "**And I am going to be more honest than that. I would like it to be because of the fourth hand, and I am one of the two people whose agreement holds that thing up, and so I am the last person in this building who should be allowed to be glad about a wall at the second bell of a Monday.**"

Then Nera Oduya said the thing she had been holding since the fourth day of the last week, and she said it before anybody could have got to it, and she said it flat.

"**I was wrong on Friday and I have had two days to think about how wrong, and here it is.**"

"**Say it in the first person, because a room has been told *say how you know* by you for thirty-two weeks and I would like the sentence to have an I in it.**"

"**I said the anteroom's air is the pump's business and not the brace's. That was wrong. A frame that has been moving a finger and three-quarters in a month and a frame that has been held for four days do not leak the same, and this corridor is the only thing that moves air between that anteroom and that schoolroom on a day the mouth is shut, and the pump runs twenty-six strokes and the crossing is four minutes, and I did not put those four things together in a month of looking at that end of this building.**"

"**Say the half of it that is against the answer you are about to give,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because you are about to give an answer and it is going to be a good one and it is going to sit in a room next to a bad one.**"

"**The half against is that the wall has stopped moving on a Wednesday and two days after a Wednesday, and a Wednesday is a day the school runs, and nine children is a load, and I am not going to stand in a room and say that nine children are not a load because I would like the wall to have stopped for a reason I can live with.**"

"**Enter that in her words, both halves, in that order,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And enter that she said the first half before anybody could have got to it, and that the clerk has not consoled her and is not going to, and that I would have consoled her last week and noticed myself nearly doing it this week and stopped, and that is a thing I have changed and not a thing anybody in this room should congratulate me on.**"

"**Say the limit now,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and say it in the practice, with no number and no day, and say what it stops.**"

"**The air in that room stays what it is now, and if it does not, then the brace comes out before the air is argued about, and I am going to say that as a condition and not as a hope, and I am not one of the two women who wrote the condition on the brace, because I am not one of the two people it can hurt, and I will not be able to see what they see.**"

"**Say the last of it, because you have left the hard part in the room and everybody knows it.**"

"**The hard part is that I have been the only person in this building who may turn that wheel for thirty-two weeks, and I have never once been asked, and I said on Thursday that I was not going to be grateful about it afterwards. So I am not going to be. I am going to be right about the air and I am going to be the only person in this room who is, and that is what being an authority is, and there is no part of this that anybody here should find comfortable.**"

**At the sixth bell the clerk entered the day's number and the total and read neither of them out, and the board was not walked, and the count was read at one hundred and one, and a boy read nine figures off a slate and said what they were before he read them.**

"**Two today and two hundred and nine since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and twenty-six and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile. The crew are at seventy-one.**"

"**I entered those and did not read them, and the reason is that the air authority of this building said two wrong things in a room today before anybody else found them, and a clerk who reads a number out loud in the same hour as that is a clerk who thinks the two kinds of thing belong together.**"

"**The wall read nothing on the fourth day of last week and nothing again today, after eight readings of a quarter of an inch.**"

"**And the plan has no row for that wall, and the figure is in a book and not on a page, and that refusal was argued in three hands in the twenty-seventh week and it stands.**"

"**A clerk has now read nine of those figures out loud through a boy of fourteen, and this is the first time in thirty-two weeks that column has been said in a room, and I am making no finding out of it, and I am not refusing to make one either.**"

"**The pot is nine. The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and one days and one hundred and one nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and two, and I have said in eleven weeks running that I am going to keep writing it.**"

"**And I did not walk the board, and the reason is that a failure point has been named in a room and it is on nothing, and if I walk a board on the day a thing gets a name I am going to find out what the board is for.**"

---

**A man of forty-nine went up the middle passage on the first day of the thirty-third week with three of his own and the fir from a store, and the beech stayed, and a keeper of a page said out loud in an empty room that he is suspicious of the man, and gave the half of the suspicion that argues against it, and said a clerk arranged the timing and that it was a dirty thing to have arranged.**

**The air authority of this building was wrong in a room on Monday and said so herself before anybody could be kind to her about it, and set a limit with no number and no day on it, and said that the only thing she is going to be in this building is right, and that there is no part of that anybody should find comfortable.**

**A physician named the brace's failure point *the place where it comes out*, and a clerk entered that it is a phrase and not a term, and that a phrase can be said out loud in a room by anybody, and that no mark has been cut in the stone to hold it.**

**Nine readings of a wall have now been said out loud in a room by a boy of fourteen, and eight of them were a quarter of an inch, and the wall read nothing twice, and a woman on a landing said she would like it to be because of a hand that is not in yet, and that she is the last person in the building who should be glad about it.**
