# Chapter 220 — Two Names And A Number

The third day of the forty-second week is a Wednesday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the fiftieth morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door, and somewhere about eleven feet below that room there is a chamber with water in it, and this morning somebody in the anteroom worked out how much.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the fortieth reading, and the same as thirty-nine, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, in the same words as Sunday and Tuesday, and the clerk entered it and entered that it was the same sentence for the fourth time and that the woman had said in advance that it would be.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is twenty-five.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it was right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and on the morning a room refused a figure this boundary had worked out about itself the count of people it may hold at one time did not move by a quarter of an inch, and the clerk entered that because she wanted it on the page next to the refusal. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing, and he had given a reason that morning about himself for the first time in five volumes, and the clerk entered both of those and joined them to nothing. The sixth was the pump, and it ran.

Then the cook came out of the kitchen-line doorway with the cloth over her arm and did not go to her own door, and stopped in the middle of the floor.

"**Say the thing you have been holding since the fifth bell yesterday,**" said Mikka Hale, "**or I will say it, and I would rather you said it because I have got a reason and you have got four.**"

"**I have got one,**" said Nera Oduya. "**And I am going to say it in the shape it comes in, which is that I can give you a figure of pails and I am not going to, and the reason is not mine and I am going to hand it to the person it belongs to and I would like her to have it in her own mouth and not in mine.**"

"**Then say the shape of the thing first,**" said Bel Ordry, "**so that the room knows what it is refusing.**"

"**A dressed chamber, a floor that does not dip, a water line at the same height on four walls, and a course of stone round the room at the height of a woman's knee, and a lamp in my hand on Tuesday and another one on Wednesday,**" said Nera Oduya. "**Those are four measured things and a wall. Put them together and they give a quantity of water. I have it. I worked it out on the stair yesterday and I have checked it twice and I am not going to say it out loud.**"

Mero Senn had not moved from the frame of the far door. "**Then this room is refusing a number, and I would like somebody who is competent to say whether it is right to refuse one, and I am going to say now that the competence is not mine, because a quantity of water in a room is not a medical fact and I have been in this room for four minutes and I have learned that the two people in it who would have an opinion about it have had one for a fortnight and have not said it.**"

"**Then say the reason,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say whose it is, and do not improve it.**"

Mikka Hale put the cloth over the other arm, which is what she does when she is about to be uncomfortable and not when she is about to be tidy.

"**It is my reason and it is not a new one and it is not the same one either, and I want both of those things said,**" she said. "**I have refused this book twice. Both times it was about my number. Four pails a day is mine and I hold it and I have kept it out of this book because the day a number of mine is in this book somebody divides it by something and gets a figure of how many people sleep under this floor. That is the first refusal and the second refusal and they are the same sentence and I have said so out loud in this room twice.**"

"**Go on,**" said Renn Vale.

"**This one is not the same sentence,**" said Mikka Hale. "**Because this number is not mine. An air engineer worked it out of a room with a lamp in it, and it is hers, and I have no claim on it and no reason of my own about it at all. And I am standing here refusing it anyway, and the reason I am refusing it is that a figure of pails becomes a figure of days the moment anybody in this building thinks about a day, and four pails is what this boundary uses, and a number of pails divided by four is a number of days. That is a division. That is the exact division I have been refusing for a month, and I have been refusing it in my own voice about my own number, and this morning it turns up in somebody else's voice about somebody else's number and it is the same division and I am not going to let it in the door just because it came in with a different key.**"

"**Then say the rest of it,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because you have not said the part that frightens you and everybody at that table can see that you have not said it.**"

Mikka Hale did not say it for about as long as the pump takes to make four strokes.

"**A number of days is a promise about people,**" she said. "**Four hundred people can drink that for so many days. That is a sentence. And a sentence about people in this district is not a thing a chamber gives you and it is not a thing a lamp gives you and it is not a thing a fall gives you, and the two people in this room who have found out how much water there is have found out how much water there is and have not found out one single thing about who drinks it. There are four households at the bottom of four sets of steps who have had nothing since the cold closed, and there are six people at my table who are not this building's, and there are nine children in the room at the end of that corridor four mornings in seven, and that is everything I know and it is not a number of days. If a figure of that water goes into a book then in a year a person divides it and gets days and says them out loud at a gate, and that is a promise, and this boundary has not been asked to make one and I am not going to hand a clerk the arithmetic that lets it.**"

"**Entered, in the cook's words, and entered that she was not asked, and entered that this is the third refusal and the clerk has said before and after it whether it is the same refusal and both times she has said no,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Then say what you make of that,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I do not know what to make of it and I have been asked twice now and I am going to say that I do not know in the space where a reason goes instead of in the space where a finding goes, and that I have not got a word for it, for the first time in forty weeks,**" said Dalla Sen. "**The first two times she refused, she was protecting herself. She was protecting a number out of her own head from a book she does not trust. This time she is protecting a room from a number she did not make and does not own. Those are not the same act. The first two are a person refusing to be used. This one is a person refusing to be used *on somebody else's behalf*, and I do not have a word for that and I have been a clerk for forty weeks.**"

"**Then the room decides,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because a thing that is refused by one person in a room is a thing a person has refused. This is going to be asked in a room or it is going to be entered anyway, and I am asking it in a room.**"

"**Entered, that it was asked in a room,**" said Dalla Sen, and had her hand near the book.

"**Does the figure go in,**" said Bel Ordry.

They did not all answer at once, which is a thing that has happened about three times in five volumes in this anteroom.

Emrys Callow said no first, and gave a reason, and the reason was that a crew is going to be asked to work on that water in a week and a man who is holding a figure of how much there is will be holding a figure of what is at the bottom of it, and the two of those are not the same and a foreman cannot work on both.

Tams Quill said no second, and gave no reason at all, and nobody asked him for one, and Dalla Sen entered that he had been asked nothing and had offered nothing and that this is his practice and not a refusal of the board.

Nella Bray said no third, and gave a reason, and the reason was that a figure of pails is a figure of a day to anybody who has ever been at a counter, and she has written the words *sufficient* and *inadequate* in front of a great many people, and neither of those words is true about water, and a book that says how many pails are in a room is a book that somebody at a counter will one day write *sufficient* next to.

"**Then the man at the end of the table,**" said Dalla Sen, and she turned the book round, and she did it in front of the room, which she has now done twice in a volume.

"**Are you going to refuse it,**" she said.

Marek Venn had his hand flat on the shut book, which is what he does.

"**No,**" he said, and the room waited for the second half, and it came, and it was not the second half anybody in that anteroom had been hearing from him for ten weeks.

"**I said on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week with my hand on a book that saying it in a room is making a claim, and that a claim in this building is a drawing, and that a drawing is a plan, and that a plan has a person in it. I still believe every word of it. I have believed it for ten weeks and I have used it four times in this room and three of those times it was the reason for a decision I was pleased with.**"

He took his hand off the book.

"**I am not going to use it this morning, because this morning it would be a hiding place, and I can tell the difference, and I would like the clerk to enter that I can tell the difference, and I would like her to enter that a man who can tell the difference and still wants somewhere to put it is not a man who has grown.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he refused, and entered that he gave a reason, and entered that the reason is not the reason he gave on the fourth day of the thirty-ninth week and that the clerk noticed the change inside a second and has not written down what she noticed,**" said Dalla Sen.

What he had actually done, sitting there, was turn over a sentence he had been carrying for ten weeks and put it down.

It was a good sentence. He had built it on the fourth day of the fortieth week with his hand flat on a book and it had been true then and it was true now: saying a thing in a room is making a claim, and a claim in this building is a drawing, and a drawing is a plan, and a plan has a person in it. Ten weeks. It had got him out of four rooms that wanted a figure off him. It had got him out of a conversation about a ninth hearth stone and a conversation about a head of water and two conversations about a man at a counter. Every one of those times, somebody in that anteroom had been about to turn a thing into a shape, and he had put the sentence in the way, and it had worked, and he had gone home — he had not gone home, he slept in a room off a corridor, but the verb was the verb — feeling like a man who had held a door.

And this morning it had been a door with nothing behind it. He had got as far as the first four words of it and had heard, in his own mouth, the exact intonation of the five weeks in which he had used it, and he had stopped, and what he had found at the bottom of the stopping was that he did not want to refuse the number. He wanted to not have had the conversation. Those are different and he had been living on the difference for ten weeks without once looking at it, and a cook in a doorway had done it in about ninety seconds with a cloth over her arm.

So he had a new reason, and the new reason was that he could tell the difference, and the difference was not that he had grown. It was that he had got good at the sentence. Ten weeks of being the man who says the true thing and is believed had worn a groove in him exactly the shape of that one sentence, and a groove is a thing that gets deeper every time water goes past it, and a woman who reads walls had told a room on the ninth day of this fortnight that a chamber built to be filled is not a cave, and the sentence in his mouth was about four hundred years old and belonged to somebody who had chosen a rate on purpose.

He looked at the shut book under his forearm, and then he did the thing he had been not doing all week, which was to put his hand on it without any conclusion in his hand at all, and leave it there, and find out later what it was for.

"**Then it is refused,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am going to say the half against, and the half against is that a room which refuses a figure it has worked out is a room that has decided to be poorer at understanding than it could be, and that is a real cost and it is not a virtue, and I am not going to stand here and let this morning be remembered as a morning about principle.**"

"**Then say what it costs,**" said Renn Vale.

"**It costs us the ability to argue,**" said Bel Ordry. "**In three weeks somebody in this room is going to want a drain, and a drain wants a figure, and this morning we have put the figure in a drawer. In a month somebody is going to want to know whether the water is coming or going, and a figure would have told us, and we will have to find that out by looking. And on the day we find it out by looking, somebody is going to be standing in that water with a lamp, and it is not going to be me and it is not going to be the man at the end of this table and I do not know who it is going to be, and I have said that out loud in a room and I am not going to take it back and turn it into a reason.**"

"**Then enter the cost and not the virtue,**" said Dalla Sen, and she wrote for about ten seconds and then stopped and looked at what she had written and did not read it back.

"**Entered, in the words of the man who said it, and entered that the room has chosen not to know how much water it is standing on, and entered that the choice can be changed by anybody in this room who is willing to be the person who changed it, and entered that this is the first entry in this clerk's book that says a room may do something differently tomorrow and mean it.**"

"**And enter this,**" said Dalla Sen, and she did not write for a while. "**The refusal is entered with nothing beside it. There is no reason in the space where a reason goes. I have done that three times in a fortnight and twice it was because I did not know the reason and once it was because I did know it and it was the wrong shape. This is the first time I have entered an act of the room and left the reason out on purpose, and I am doing it because the reasons are all available. There are five people in this room who have given a reason this morning and every one of the five would do. And any one of the five would be picked up by whoever finds this page first and used, and a used reason is a claim, and I have entered the refusal and the room has given me five reasons and I am not going to write one of them down.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything about that, and the clerk entered that nobody had.

Then the room got to the thing it had been carrying all morning, which is that a thing with two names in it does not become one thing.

"**There are two names for the same water in this building,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am going to say both of them out loud and I am not going to improve either, and then I am going to say the third one and the third one is not mine.**"

"**Say them,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The first is on a piece of paper on this trestle with a seal on it and a line of chalk through the fourth of its six lines: water, timber, metal, salt-crust, and anything a person can take out and carry. That is the guild's name for it and it has been the guild's name for nine hundred years of other people's chambers and it is not a stupid name. It is a name for what comes out of a room.**"

"**And ours,**" said Nella Bray.

"**Ours has been four names in a fortnight,**" said Bel Ordry. "**It was a gallery, on the fifth day of the fortieth week, in a clerk's book with the word guess beside it. Then it was a chamber, on the ninth day, when a woman who reads walls said a sentence in a room. Then it was a room with shelves in it, on the tenth. Then it was a cistern on Tuesday morning, in the mouth of the person who found the fall, and cistern is wrong, because a cistern is the thing at the top and the thing at the top is nine hundred feet away and she called it a cistern because it is a word she knows. So this boundary has had four names for one body of water in a week and a half and no two of the four of us agree about which one is right.**"

"**The third name is not yours,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The third name is water,**" said Bel Ordry. "**It is the name a woman from a second gate used when she stood at the end of this table and said that water is not a thing anybody keeps, it is a thing that goes somewhere, and the question is where it goes when it goes. That is in this clerk's book in three mouths including hers. And it is the only one of the four names that was said by somebody who has to drink it.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" said Mikka Hale.

"**The half against is that *water* is the least useful of the four names and the most true,**" said Bel Ordry. "**A guild can work with salvage. A boundary can work with a gallery. Nobody in this building can work with *water*, because water is a thing that goes somewhere, and this boundary does not know where it goes yet, and the woman who has used that name in this room twice has been told nothing this week and is not going to be told this week.**"

"**Then say what the plan is doing about it,**" said Renn Vale, "**because a plan is in this room with a door in it and the door has not been shut and everybody in here knows what happens to that plan if nobody goes down that passage.**"

"**Nothing,**" said Nera Oduya. "**The plan is sitting exactly where I put it on Friday, and the door is exactly where it was, and the four shut things and the two open things are in my head and not in a book, and the length of time the door has to be shut is still a length of time I cannot name, and I have had three days to find out and I have not asked anybody and I am not going to ask anybody this week.**"

"**Then say the thing that is true at the end of the week,**" said Dalla Sen, and she put the book the right way up and did not close it.

"**This is true: a chamber four days away holds water that nobody has measured and that this boundary has decided not to measure. A fall nine hundred years old comes out of a cistern and goes through a gallery and finishes under this pump. A pump has been running against that fall for nineteen weeks. A plan exists in this building that protects four rooms and needs a door shut, and the door has not been shut, and the length of time it has to be shut for is a number that is nobody's. Nine people are asleep at the end of a cook's room over that chamber. A guild's claim over the whole of it is on this trestle and is neither granted nor refused. A woman at a second gate has had nothing for two volumes and has been told to her face that she will go on having it, and she came up nine steps twice to say a thing about water and has been given a sentence in answer and not a pail. And a woman who reads walls has refused a figure of it in a room this morning with her hand not on anything.**"

She looked at the room.

"**None of those are joined to each other,**" she said, "**and I am not going to join them, and I have been asked three times in a month to and I have said no three times and I am saying it a fourth time in front of the whole room because the fourth is the one that costs, and the cost is that if I put them in order somebody in a year will read them in order and think the order means something.**"

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a room which has spent a morning refusing a number it wanted is owed three numbers it did not want at all, read out loud, by a person who has read them out loud for fifty-four weeks.

"**Six today and three hundred and seventy-two since the keeper signed. The boundary is at four hundred and eighty-nine and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and sixty-five days and one hundred and sixty-five nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and sixty-six, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for fifty-four weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in fifty-four weeks, and it is the last morning of my week with this room and I am not going to make anything of that either.**"

The board was walked, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven that are not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine. The second of them has nothing over the top of it, and the clerk entered that it had nothing over the top of it, and did not write anything above it, and did not say out loud how many times it had been walked, because that is a thing a clerk has never once done with that column and is not going to start.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman has four and a half hours and four open days to spend them on and has not said which and a man who reads walls has asked him for a reason for a sentence before he spends them.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for twenty-six weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and this boundary has just worked out how much water is underneath a room where nine children sleep and has put the figure in a drawer, and the four households are still the four households and the pot is still three days and the fire is still out and the clerk is still not going to say why.

And at the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a book with four unjoined things in it and an empty space beside a refusal, and a pump standing over the low point of a fall, and a woman in a doorway with a cloth over her arm who has refused three times now and is going to go and light a fire if anybody will let her, and two names for the same water in a room that has not managed to pick one.
