## The four craft rules, and whether they held

1. **No epigraph block and no closing recap block. The only bold in a chapter is a person speaking.** **Held: zero fully-bold paragraphs in five chapters, against 43 as Batch 0004 was delivered and 36 which Chapters 171–175 still carry.** Those 36 are already-delivered prose and are a recorded divergence, not a licence. A chapter opens on the room and closes on the room: 181 opens on nine steps and a pump and a floor that is colder at one end; 182 opens on nine children crossing a corridor twice; 183 opens on a man learning that a quiet can become a hiding place; 184 opens on nine children in a room on purpose; 185 opens on a Saturday and a wooden tally.
2. **The request-to-speak tag is retired.** **Held: zero formulaic request openers and two bare *Say* imperatives in five chapters, in two different mouths** — Dalla Sen on a foreman's own name, Talla Roke on the cost of a message. A room asks in about twenty shapes and several answers arrive with nobody having asked. **The half against is in all five chapters and in five different mouths, and the literal four-word opener appears twice and both times are in 181, where the room has not yet learned the phrase and the woman who uses it most says it twice on purpose.** The clerk's recitations are carried in narration by default; three of them are speech, and each of the three is an act rather than bookkeeping.
3. **Quoted share between roughly forty-five and sixty-five.** **Held: 47.5, 47.9, 56.1, 54.9, 59.5, weighted average 53.7, none above seventy and none below forty-five.** The two lowest are the two chapters in which a keeper and a foreman are in a room and the room carries itself; the highest is the chapter with fifty-two spoken paragraphs and a courier in it. **Narration carries the room, the objects, the weather of a working morning and the interior life, and it does it in 142 paragraphs across the batch against 169 spoken.**
4. **At least three of five turn on somebody deciding something, a document may arrive but may not be the cause, and the pressure types must not all run the same way.** **Held and exceeded: all five turn on a decision, and no chapter's turn is set running by a delivery.** 181 turns on Bel Ordry deciding not to defend his own page and a foreman deciding to say he has no answer; 182 on a keeper deciding nine children go back into a room; 183 on the air authority deciding to propose the cheapest thing in the room and the clerk deciding not to write the word *refused*; 184 on a keeper deciding and a foreman deciding to take a piece of timber out; 185 on a woman deciding to take a seventh thing and a courier deciding to come down nine steps. **The two facts that arrive by document — a counter clerk's account of a schedule on Wednesday, a road hand's account of a board on Saturday — are interruptions to scenes already under way and neither is a cause.** The pressure types are: a meeting about trust; a keeper and nine children; a political instrument; a piece of engineering on a wall; and an aftermath with a question asked in it.
