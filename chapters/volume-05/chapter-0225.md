# Chapter 225 — The Name In The Book

The first day of the forty-third week is a Monday, the corridor is shut, and the school did not run and lost nothing, and at about the second bell four people in this anteroom took the ends of a bar and put their shoulders under a stone drum at the bottom of a wet stair and wound a nine-hundred-year-old door back the way it had come, and it took about four minutes, and it took about four minutes in both directions, and the man at the end of the trestle was right about the fourth minute both times.

Jo Verrick had the frame at the first bell, a finger and two-thirds, and the party wall was a quarter of an inch, the forty-fifth reading, and the same as forty-four, and the string was where the string was. She put her thumb under it and the thumb came out of it right, and she did not give the four refusals, and she gave the reason instead, in one sentence, in the same words for the ninth time, and the clerk entered it and entered that it was the same sentence and that it has now been the same sentence for nine consecutive mornings and that a person in a year can have the argument.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is thirty.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it is right. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change, and on the morning that door was wound open again for about two hours to bring nine people out of a wet room, the four things that were closed on Friday stayed closed, and the clerk entered that a clerk had asked a keeper whether the four should be opened too and that the keeper had said no and given the reason that opening them would be a different plan and this building does not have one, and that the clerk had accepted the reason.

Then the door was open and two people in this room and a man with grey at the side of his head went down the wet stair, and about fifty minutes later nine people came up it, one at a time, in the order they had gone down, and every one of them was on his own feet, and the seventh one up was the fourth one down, and nobody in that anteroom said a single word while that was happening except a woman on the second of three steps who said *he is limping* and then said nothing else, and the seventh one up was indeed limping.

"**Nobody is hurt,**" said Seth Wray, at the trestle, and his voice was not steady and he did not pretend that it was, "**and I am going to say the word *hurt* once and then I am going to take it off the table, because a doctor in this room is going to pick it up and I would rather it came out of my mouth than out of his, and I would like the clerk to enter that I said it before he was asked and that I would rather be the one who says it.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he was not asked,**", and she did not look up.

"**The seventh one up is limping,**", with his bag against the wall where it goes.

"**The seventh one up is limping because he has been in water to his chest since Thursday at the fifth bell and he is forty-one years old,**" said Seth Wray, "**and I know which one he is and I know what he did in the water and I am not going to say it in this room because it is his and not mine, and I have never once in twenty-three years told a room what one of my men did in a room I was not in.**"

"**Then say the medical thing and stop,**", from the top of the nine steps.

"**Nobody is hurt,**" Mero Senn said, "**that is the physician's finding and it is the correct condition for this room to have a plan in, and I want it entered that I have found no injured person, that I have had a day and a night to find one and have not found one, and that the reason I have not is that the whole of that day and that night were spent in a room full of people saying what they would do and none of them was a doctor.**"

"**Then the room has two answers in it and one day of them is gone,**" said Bel Ordry, from the third of the nine steps, "**and I am not going to improve them, because a physician gave them on Friday to the man at the end of that table and not to the room, and the second one is still exactly what it was.**"

"**Then somebody is going to say the second one out loud in this room today,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I am going to ask for it, and I am asking because I am the clerk and what happened is what a clerk's business is, and I am asking out loud so that the person who says it is not a person who has been asked quietly in a doorway, which is how a room ends up accepting things.**"

Nobody in the anteroom said anything for about as long as a pump takes to make four strokes.

"**I am not going to be able to say it,**", and he did not put the slate down. "**I have had it in my mouth since Friday night and it is not the sentence I thought it was going to be. What I have got is not *we accept a controlled loss*. That is a thing an office says. What I have got is a pump.**"

He put one hand on the trestle, next to where the slate had been.

"**There is a pump on the far side of that door that has not been turned over in nine years, and nine men came four days up a middle passage to turn it over, and they have been in water since Thursday at the fifth bell and they came up it this morning on a door that a boundary wound open for them, and they did not turn it over. That is the whole of the loss. Not a room, not air, not a life. A turn-over that did not happen, in a room this building cannot reach, on a line this building has no authority on, and every one of those four clauses is a true sentence and none of them is mine.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**The half against is that a room full of people is going to hear a pump in a sentence and think it is small,**" said Seth Wray, "**and it is not small, and I would like a person in this room to notice that I have made it smaller on purpose, because the other half of it is nine men and I am one of them and I would like to say the nine half on my own and not have the room hold it for me.**"

"**Say the nine half,**", and he did not look up from the stair.

"**Nine men went down on my word on Thursday and I told this room about it before I did it, and that is the first decent thing I have done in this building and I would like that entered in the same breath as the rest of it, because a man who has been decent once and a half is not a better man than a man who has not, and a clerk who writes the decent thing down on its own line is a clerk who is making a list of the good parts of people.**" He looked round the room. "**And I have come here to say that I accept the loss. That is my word and it is the only one I have got for it, and I am saying it in the first person and not in the third, and I am saying it out loud in a room with eleven people in it, and I am not going to be a person who said it in a corridor first.**"

"**Then the clerk is going to do a thing and I am going to do it slowly,**" said Dalla Sen, and she did not have the chalk out.

"**Say what you are going to do before you do it,**", without coming down.

"**I am going to write a name in this book,**" said Dalla Sen, "**against a decision, and it is going to be the first time in forty-two weeks that I have written a person's name in this book against something they did not decide. In forty-two weeks the people whose names are in here are in here because they did a thing, and the clerk's rule is that a name goes in when a person has acted, and that rule has been the reason this book is worth reading. And this morning a man has accepted a loss in his own words and the loss is his, and if I put it in the way I put the other forty names in there then it becomes a thing he did, and it is not a thing he did. It is a thing that happened to a man who came four days for it.**"

"**Then say what shape it is in,**", and he put the page back in his coat.

"**It is a name on a line and a decision on the same line and the name is on the line and not the decision,**" Dalla Sen said, "**and there is no rule beside it, and there is no column over it, and it is not on the board, and I am going to say the last part out loud because somebody is going to ask me about it before this morning is over and I would rather answer it now. The board is a list of what this building found. That is not what happened this morning. This building was handed a loss by a man and it had no way to refuse it and it did not, and that is not a finding and it is not a claim and it is not a mark and it is not a row and it is not a plan and it is not a condition and it is not a store and it is not a brace, and it is not a person, because the person is on the line and the person is not the entry.**"

Nobody in that anteroom looked at Tams Quill, and the clerk entered that nobody had looked and entered that she had looked.

"**And I am going to write it now,**" she said, "**and I am going to read it back, and I have not read a line back in this book in a fortnight, and I am going to read this one back because a name is in it.**"

She wrote for about as long as it takes to say a sentence, and then she read it.

"**Seth Wray. First day of the forty-third week, at about the second bell. Accepted a controlled loss, in his own words, in a room. The loss is a pump turn-over that did not happen, on a line this boundary has no authority on, in a room this boundary cannot reach, and the boundary was given it and did not refuse it. He did not decide it. He would have carried it if the room had picked the other one, and this is entered in his words and not improved.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**And nobody in this room thanked him,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and I have looked for that and I have not found it, and I have looked for about as long as it takes to say a sentence, and I am entering that I looked.**"

Then the door went shut again at about the fourth bell, and the four things that had been closed on Friday stayed closed, and the air in the anteroom and the air in the room at the kitchen-line end went back to what it had been on Friday morning, and the woman on the second of three steps went up her steps and sat down and did not say anything about that either, which was the first thing in eight days she had not said something about.

"**Then the second answer has been taken and the first one has not,**" said Bel Ordry, "**and I am going to say the whole of the cost out loud in this room because nobody is going to be able to claim in a month that nobody said it, and the cost is this. Four rooms in this building were being served by a chamber four days away from about the second bell on Friday until about the fourth bell this morning, and they are not being served now, and a woman who can certify air says the two she has been in are fit to breathe and will not say where the air came from, and the plan is not a plan any more. It was a plan for two days. It protected four rooms for two days and then this building chose a pump.**"

"**Say the half against that,**", from the middle of the floor.

"**The half against is that nobody in this room chose a pump,**" Renn Vale said, "**and that the sentence I have just heard has a pump in it and a choice in it and this building made neither, and the reason this room got a pump is that a man came four days with a piece of paper and a woman had a plan and the two of them were put in a room on a Thursday and the room did not know it was choosing until the door was shut.**"

"**Entered, in the keeper's words,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that this is the second time in a fortnight that a woman in this room has named a thing this room was doing without knowing it was doing it, and entered that the first of those was on the sixth day of the forty-first week about a plan, and that neither of the two was entered beside the other, and that I am not going to put them next to each other.**"

"**Then somebody had better say what is on that slate,**" said Mikka Hale, from the kitchen-line doorway, with the cloth over her arm, and she had not been in the anteroom since the first bell and nobody had asked her to come.

The anteroom was quiet for about as long as a pump takes to make three strokes.

"**I gave those five things to a man at the kitchen-line end on the fourth night of the forty-first week,**" she said, "**and the fifth of them was in this boundary's own hand and it said that this boundary did not send them and did not know. That was written in a clerk's hand in a woman's voice at a counter in this building. It has been true in one half since it was written and this morning it stopped being true in the other half, and I am telling you which half, and the half that went is the half about *did not know*, and the half that is still true is the half about *did not send*, and both of those were said by people in this room and neither of them is mine.**"

She put the cloth over the other arm.

"**And I am going to say the sixth thing and I am not writing it on a slate and I am not writing it in that book, and I want it in a room. Those nine men have been fed at that table since the fourth day of the forty-first week and they are owed *fed*, and the pot is at three days, and the seam has given nothing for thirteen mornings, and I have not written how much because I will not have it written, and that has not changed this morning, and I am telling you that the sixth thing exists and that I have not written it down because the woman who holds that number has refused three times and I am not going to make it four by handing it to a different instrument.**"

"**Entered, that a cook told a room there is a sixth thing and did not write it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that she was not asked, and entered that the six of them is not a list and is not on the slate and is not in this book, and that I am not going to make it a seventh.**"

Then, at about the fifth bell, a man at the anvil end put down a piece of iron he had been holding for an hour and said the thing he had been not saying since the ninth day of the forty-first week, and he said it in his own words and the room was not ready for it and did not stop him.

"**A foreman who hands a man the first thing in three volumes that is worth having has handed him a post,**", and he did not sit down. "**And there is a man in this room who has been owed a work since the fourth day of the forty-first week because of that, and I said the reason out loud on the fourth day of the forty-first week and I meant it then and I mean it now, and I have had four and a half hours outstanding every morning this week and I have not asked for them again, and the reason I have not asked is that this morning nine men came up a wet stair who were not mine and whose work is not mine and whose loss is not mine either until a clerk wrote my name next to it, and I would like to say that I have not been angry and that would be a lie, and I have been angry about it every morning and I have kept the anger out of the room because the anger is mine and the work is not and a foreman who mixes them is a foreman who has handed somebody a post by accident.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I have just said the reason out loud in a room for the second time in a fortnight and that the second time has cost me nothing and the first time cost me a fortnight of not asking,**" Emrys Callow said, "**and I do not know which of those two facts is about the room and which is about me, and I would like the clerk to enter that I do not know.**"

"**Entered, in the foreman's words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that a carpenter at the anvil has not been thanked for anything in this volume and is not going to be,**" said Dalla Sen, and nobody said anything about that, and the clerk entered that nobody had.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's figure and the total out loud, and read the count, and gave the reason, and the reason was that a room which has just written a person's name into a book against a decision that person did not make is owed a total it can verify, and that a clerk who has not read a line back in a fortnight has read one this morning and is going to be perfectly ordinary about a number for the rest of the afternoon.

"**Five today and three hundred and ninety-six since the keeper signed. The boundary is at five hundred and thirteen and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and seventy days and one hundred and seventy nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and seventy-one, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for fifty-nine weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in fifty-nine weeks.**"

She wrote it down and read it and did not look up, and four people in that anteroom could have said something about a figure with a zero in it, and none of them did, and she did not offer it, and the clerk entered that nobody had and that she had not and that the two of those facts were the same fact.

The board was not walked, and the reason was entered, and the reason was that a board is a list of what this building found, and this morning this building was given something, and the two of those are not the same instrument and a clerk is not walking one this afternoon.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and it is the reason a foreman gave out loud in a room at the fifth bell and entered in his own words, and the clerk entered it under his words and entered that the column has not moved for a fortnight and that she has stopped expecting it to.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning for the thirteenth morning running, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for thirty-one weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and a cook has told this room that there is a sixth thing owed to nine men that is not on a slate and not in a book, and nobody in this building has any idea how to answer it.

Marek Venn sat at the end of the trestle for the whole of it and did the thing he does about four mornings in ten, and this time what it turned up was not a thing anybody else in the room had been carrying.

It was that he could have made the loss somebody else's.

Not by doing anything. By saying one sentence in the anteroom on Friday afternoon, in the voice he has, about how a loss that nobody has named is a loss that lands on whoever is nearest, and every person in that room would have understood it and two of them would have gone home and thought about it and on Monday morning the second answer would have been the first answer, because the first answer costs a person and every person in that room has spent five volumes refusing to be that.

He had not said it. And the reason he had not said it was the reason he had given in the room on the Wednesday of the forty-second week, the one about an offer being what a room hears instead of a cost, and the one a doctor had said about a plan with nobody named in it, and the one he had renounced once and then found his hand going straight back to the book.

But this time when he turned it over and looked at the bottom of it, what was down there was not a habit and it was not a groove. It was a thing he could name, and the name was this: he had been letting other people pay for his sentences. Not deliberately, and not for his own benefit, but because a man at the end of a trestle who says a true thing and then says nothing is the most comfortable man in any room in the world, and comfort of that kind is not a virtue and it is not a decision and it is somebody else's money.

He looked at the entry in the book, which he could not read from where he was sitting and did not try to, and it was four lines long and one of them had a name in it that was not his.

At the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a book with a new line in it, and a stone drum at the bottom of a wet stair that had gone across and come back in four minutes twice in one morning, and four closed vents that this room is going to have to open again before the week is out, because leaving them shut is not a decision either, and nine men at the far end of a cook's room eating what a pot at three days could produce, one of them limping, and a cook in a doorway who has a sixth thing she is not going to write down, and a foreman at the anvil who has been angry every morning for a fortnight and kept it out of a room, and a physician who has not found an injured person in a day and a night and is going to have to name one before the week is out, and a clerk who wrote a man's name against a decision he did not make and read it back out loud, and nobody in that room thanked anybody, and the clerk entered that nobody had.
