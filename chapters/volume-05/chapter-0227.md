# Chapter 227 — A Stalled Path With A Body On It

The third day of the forty-third week is a Wednesday, the corridor is open, and the school ran for the fifty-third morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, and the nine went up the nine steps at the first bell and came down them at the fourth and went out at the far door, and at about the second bell a woman went down the wet stair with a lamp and a folding rule and no cord, and the clerk entered that nobody had asked her, and entered that nobody had sent her, and entered that a clerk has put a thing of her own in a book nine times in forty-two weeks and has never once written beside it the word *why* when the reason was not hers.

Jo Verrick was not in the anteroom at the first bell. She was not in it at the second bell either, and Renn Vale did the frame herself at about a quarter past, because a keeper can read a string, and she did it in her own words and got it right, and the clerk entered that the woman who reads walls was thirty feet below this room and that the reading taken in her absence is the first in fifty mornings that anybody else has taken it, and that she will not be asked about it and did not ask.

The forty-seventh reading is a quarter of an inch and it is the same as forty-six, and the string was where the string was. The frame is a finger and two-thirds.

Renn Vale put her ear to the wall at the first bell, four times over, and reported what she could hear, which was nothing, four times, and the run of mornings out of mornings is thirty-two.

The terms were said at the first bell, all six, and the third of them was said without anybody being asked whether it is right, and the cook was in the room and did not leave it, which is the second morning running and the clerk entered that it is a run and not a decision and that a run of two is not yet a practice. Ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and it did not change. The outer service door is the one a body went out of. The fifth of the six is in a room with both of the people it names in it, and he named it and read nothing, and on this morning both of the people it names were in the room and one of them was a floor below. The sixth was the pump, and it ran, and there is a second line under the chalk on that frame and nobody in this room can read either of them.

The pump was started, two people turning it, and about an hour and a half after that a woman came up the wet stair with her boots full and a folding rule in her hand that had not been in her belt in fifty mornings, and she came to the trestle and put the rule down flat and did not pick it up.

"**I have got three things,**" said Jo Verrick, "**and I am going to give them in the order I found them, and the order is not the order of how much they matter, and I am going to say that out loud because I have watched this room for forty-two weeks take things in the order of how much they matter and then not do anything about them.**"

"**Say the first,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The run is clear for about a third of its length and then it is not.**" She put her thumb on the trestle, about four feet out from the join, which is where the noise comes up. "**Clear at the cistern end, the same way she said it was. Clear where it comes out through the dressed wall at the base of that chamber, the same way she said it was. And then somewhere between that and the point where it comes in under this floor it stops being a channel and starts being a hole, and I could not see the place where it stops, and I am not going to put a figure on where it stops, and I want that said before anybody asks me for one.**"

"**Entered, in the words of the woman who reads it, and entered that no figure was given and none was asked for,**", and she did not look up.

"**The second thing,**" said Jo Verrick, "**is that I have been in eleven flooded rooms in nineteen years and I have never once seen a thing lie in the bottom of one of them and stay.**"

She looked round the room and nobody had followed her, which is correct, and she went on.

"**Water in a cut channel does not lie in it. Water in a cut channel goes through it. I have put my hand down in eleven places in eleven rooms and the hand went into the channel and the channel took the water away from under the hand, every time, and that is the whole of what a channel is for. And in the third of it, about a chain's length above this building, there is something in the water that does not go away from under your hand.**"

"**Then say what it is,**" Dalla Sen said, and her voice was not different at all.

"**I have not got a word for it and I have got four facts,**", and she did not take her hand off the wall. "**One. It is not stone, because stone in water does not do what it did. Two. It is heavy, because I put a lamp on the bed of that channel and set my shoulder against it and it did not go anywhere at all, and I am not a strong woman and I did not expect it to. Three. It is slow, because I watched it for as long as I could stand there, which was not long, and in all that time it moved about as far as a hand moves on a table. And four. It is not standing in the water.**"

"**Then say the fourth one again,**" said Nera Oduya, from the second of her three steps.

"**It is not standing in the water,**" Jo Verrick said, "**and that is the one that matters, and I have had my hand on it and I want the room to have it. There is more water on the far side of it than there is on this side. I could feel the difference with the back of my hand. And when the water on the far side moved, it moved, and when the water on this side moved, it did not.**"

Nobody in that anteroom moved, and the pump made a stroke and then made another.

"**Then say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I have got four facts and no name for the thing and I have been in this room for forty-two weeks refusing four things a morning about a wall, and the reason I gave for it was that a wall is not mine, and a person who has spent fifty mornings not measuring a join on the grounds that a wall is not hers is not a person anybody ought to be sending to the bottom of a flooded channel with a lamp, and I am saying that about myself in a room on the morning I did it anyway,**" Jo Verrick said.

"**Entered, in her words, and entered that she was not asked, and entered that the reason she gave for having done it is the same reason she gave for fifty mornings of not doing it, and that I entered both of those and joined them to each other and to nothing else,**", and her voice was not different at all.

Then the man at the anvil end put down a piece of iron and gave it the name, and he gave it the way a man gives a word he has used in a trade and not the way a man gives a word he has made up.

"**Sump ox,**", and he did not sit down.

The anteroom was quiet for about as long as a pump takes to make three strokes.

"**That is a shoring word,**" he said, "**and it is not a salvage word and it is not a word any of you have ever had off a form, and I am going to say where it comes from and then I am going to stop. A sump is a low place water collects in. An ox is a heavy animal that stands where it is put and does not go. My grandfather cut drains in a wet ground in a different district and they called a thing that lay in a stalled cut a sump ox, and they did not call it that because it was clever and they did not cut it open, because everybody who has ever cut one open says the same two things afterwards, and neither of them is that the water went away.**"

"**Say them,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**I am not going to, because they are other men's sentences and I have not got the right to them,**" Emrys Callow said. "**I have got one of my own and it is this. I have been in nine years of shoring and I have taken iron to nine hundred places, and I have never once seen a heavy thing in a wet cut carry itself. Everything heavy in a wet cut is being carried by something. If a thing is standing on the bed you can shore it. If a thing is lying in the water and the water is holding it up, then shoring it is propping a bucket, and the bucket is full, and the water is not coming out through the bucket, and I would like the clerk to enter that as a shoring fact and not as a finding.**"

"**Entered, in a foreman's words, in the space where a finding goes, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that a man with nine years of his own has given this room its first name for a thing in it and that the name is a trade word and not a description,**", with her hand near the book.

"**And I am going to say the part that is mine and the part that is not,**" Emrys Callow said. "**A name goes on a form. A word a foreman used in a wet ground goes into a file four days up a road and then a man at a counter writes *ox* in a column, and a column in that office has a heading over it, and a guild's claim in this room has four lines and the fourth one is struck through and the third one says water, timber, metal, salt-crust and anything a person can take out and carry, and a body is not any of those four, and the office is going to have to invent a fifth, and I would like it entered that this room was asked for a name before anybody asked whether it wanted one.**"

"**Entered, and entered that the name is on no form and is in no book and is on no board, and that a guild's claim with three places on it is squared up on a trestle in this room and is neither granted nor refused, and that neither of those two facts is going beside the name,**" said Dalla Sen, and she wrote it on facing pages and did not join them, which is the third time in a fortnight she has done that and the first time anybody in the room has known she was doing it.

"**Then a woman at a counter is going to say the half against this,**" said Nella Bray, from the anvil end, and she had her slate out from under her arm and she had had it out since the first bell. "**A guild's claim covers water, timber, metal, salt-crust, and anything a person can take out and carry. That third line is a list of what a thing in a room is made of, and it is a good line, and I have filled in four thousand of them, and it works because everything a room has ever had in it that anybody wanted was made of one of four things.**"

"**And a body is not one of the four,**", from the top of the nine steps.

"**A body is not one of the four,**" Nella Bray said, "**and that is the interesting part and not the difficult part, and I am going to say the difficult part. The office four days away is going to have to put it somewhere, because the form does not have a place for it, and the place they put it will be the third line, and a man in a counter is going to write the word of a shoring foreman's mouth in a line about salvage, and that is how a thing gets a wrong name in a file and stays wrong for twenty years, and there is nobody in this building who can stop it and there never will be.**"

"**Then say the thing I can do,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**I can be asked at a counter and I can say that the word is not the office's word and that it came out of a room and not off a form, and I will say that the next time somebody asks me, and that is all I can do, and it is not nothing, and it is not enough, and both of those are true and I have said both out loud in this room before you asked me for either.**"

"**Entered, in a counter clerk's words, and entered that she was not asked, and entered that a name given to a room in a morning is a thing that leaves the building without anybody carrying it, and that this is the fourth time a clerk has entered that and that the clerk has not joined any of the four,**", and she had the book shut.

"**Then I am going to say one thing about my own trade and then I am going to sit down,**" said Emrys Callow, "**because nine years of shoring is a thing I have and a room is going to want to do something with it, and I would like it in the book before the room gets an opinion. In nine years I have taken iron to heavy things in wet cuts four times. Twice it was stone and the iron held it. Once it was a fallen brace and the iron held it. And once it was not stone and the iron did not hold it, and I have never told that story in a room because there was never a room. I am telling it now. The iron held about as long as a man can hold a thing and then the water moved on the other side of it and the iron went with the water and what was left was in a heap, and the water did not go anywhere. It was in the heap. It was in the heap for a month and then the water went down on its own and it had nothing to do with the heap.**"

"**Then say the half against that,**" Dalla Sen said.

"**The half against is that I have just told this room that a body in a wet cut cannot be shored and that I have not told it what can be done instead, because I do not know what can be done instead, and a foreman who says *I do not know* in a room where five other people are saying things is a foreman who has handed the room a hole,**" Emrys Callow said. "**And I would rather hand it the hole than hand it a plan I have not stood behind.**"

Then Nera Oduya came down all three of her steps, which she does perhaps twice in five volumes, and she put her chalk on the trestle and did not pick it up, and she gave the room the thing that four people had been carrying since the second day of the fortieth week and that a man in this room had written the sum of.

"**A stalled path with a body carried on it,**" she said. "**That is all of it, and I have been waiting nine days to say it in that shape, and I am not going to soften it into something that sounds like a discovery. The water in that channel is not doing what somebody cut it to do. It has been going down at the same rate for nine hundred years and it has gone on doing it until the part of it that this building is standing on stopped taking it, and where it stopped taking it, it made a place where the water can sit, and the place has got a body in it, because a place where water sits and does not go is a thing the structure does instead of letting the water go somewhere.**"

"**Say the half against that,**" Bel Ordry said.

"**The half against is that I did not see it and I am describing a mechanism from a chain of three readings and a lamp held by another person,**" Nera Oduya said, "**and that a mechanism described at three removes is a sentence and not a thing, and that the sentence I have just said is exactly as good as a groove nine hundred years old, and that I have spent nine weeks telling this room that a groove does not look like an instruction, and that I have now put a mechanism in a room that this building cannot reach and cannot test, and I am not going to pretend that is different from a man at a counter four days away filling in a fifth line correctly.**"

Nobody in the anteroom improved on that, and the clerk entered it in the air engineer's words, and entered that the words *groove*, *instruction*, *mechanism* and *fifth line* are all in this morning's entry, and entered that she had not put them next to each other on purpose.

"**Then I am going to say the thing nobody has said yet,**" said Mero Senn, from the frame of the far door, "**which is that this building has had nine days of a rule in it and has not learned one new thing since the woman read it off a wall, and that a rule is not a thing you do anything with until you know what is on the line, and that the room which finds a stalled path in the tenth day and recognises it as a path and does not learn anything is a room that has already been told what a path is, and that somebody in this building has been told and did not use it, and I would like to know who and I would like to know it now.**"

No one in that room took it up., and Dalla Sen entered it in the physician's words, and entered that he was not asked, and entered that the words *stalled path* and *rule* are in this morning's entry on the same line and not joined to anything, and that she entered the join as a question and not as an answer.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the count out loud and did not read the day's figure and the total, and gave the reason for both, and the reason was that a room has just been given a name and a mechanism and a body's position in a body of water by three people who were not asked, and that the two figures in this book belong to this room and those three things belong to a channel four hundred yards up a passage, and that a clerk who reads a total in the middle of a morning like that is telling the room which of the two she thinks is going to matter.

Six today and four hundred and five since the keeper signed. The boundary is at five hundred and twenty-two and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, both columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen. The day's figure and the total were read out loud and the count was read out loud and the reason was given, and the reason was that a room which has just been handed a name and a mechanism and a body's position in a body of water is owed three numbers it can verify, and that a physician in that room had asked out loud who had been told what a path is and had got no answer, and that the three numbers are the only things in this building that nobody can argue with.

"**Wen Pask is one hundred and seventy-two days and one hundred and seventy-two nights, and it is not going to be one hundred and seventy-three, and it is a number of days and not a number of anything, and I have been promising this room for sixty-one weeks that I would go on writing it down, and nobody has thanked me in sixty-one weeks, and this morning three people told a room that there is a heavy thing in a flooded channel that is being held up by the water and cannot be taken out, and that is not a number of days and it is not a number of anything else, and I have written it down and I am not going to say it out loud again.**"

The board was walked, and it is at eighteen entries, eleven of them nulls and seven that are not, and the seven are a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace, and the two columns are nine and nine, and the second of them has nothing over the top of it. Tams Quill said, in four words, that a name is not on it, and the clerk entered that, and entered that she had not asked him, and entered that a man at the anvil had put a trade word into a room and a board that is a list of what this building found had not taken it and did not need to, and that the difference between a word a man said out loud and a word on that board is a name, and that the difference between a name and a name in a column is a heading.

The crew are at eighty and did not move, and the reason for the day is in the book, and the reason is that a foreman has four and a half hours outstanding and has this morning named the thing a crew of his would have been sent at, and the clerk entered that she wrote the reason down twice and crossed one of the two out and entered that she crossed one out and did not enter which one.

The pot is three days, and the seam gave nothing this morning for the fifteenth morning running, and it is at four threads, and nobody measured it and nobody explained it, and the fire in the room at the kitchen-line end has now not been lit for thirty-three weeks, and the four households at a second gate have nothing and no figure is attached to that gate by anybody, and there is a heavy thing in a channel in a passage off the second gate that this building has now named and cannot reach and cannot shift and has not decided what to do about, and the name is in a clerk's book on a line with nothing over the top of it.

Marek Venn had been at the end of the trestle for the whole of the three hours she was gone and had done the thing he does about four mornings in ten, and this time what came up was the oldest thing in the book and the thing he has been refusing longest.

He had wanted, for five volumes, to be the person who found out. He had given a shape away in the second week of this volume so that two other people could find out and be wrong in a room with no name on it, and it had cost him a fortnight of being the man who found out and gained him nothing except the right to sit at a trestle. This morning a woman who had spent fifty mornings refusing to measure a join put a folding rule that has been in a belt for fifty mornings on a trestle in the middle of this anteroom, and the finding was hers, and the name was a foreman's, and the mechanism was an air engineer's, and the one he would have given away if anybody had asked him for it was that a bed that does not dip and a fall that goes down at the same rate for nine hundred years are the same sentence, and that he worked that out in the second week of this volume and has not said it since and was not asked for it.

He had wanted to be the person who found out, and the finding had gone to three people who were not him, and two of them had found things in it that he would not have found, and one of them had found it because he had spent fifty mornings refusing to look at a wall, and the reason she had refused was true, and the reason had made her free.

He put his hand flat on the shut book. It had been there all morning. He had a name in his head for a body in a water channel that he had not said, and he was not going to say it, and the reason he was not going to say it was not the reason he had given on the sixth day of the forty-first week about a room hearing an offer instead of a cost. It was simpler and worse. It was that a woman had come up a wet stair with her boots full and put a rule on his table and said *there is more water on the far side of it than there is on this side*, and if he had said his sentence then two people in that room would have looked at the two women and stopped listening to them, and they would have been right to, and the clerk would have entered it in the space where a finding goes, and there would have been a name in that book in a fortnight with a heading over it.

So he said nothing, and the room had four facts and a name and a mechanism and no plan, and a heavy thing lay in nine hundred years of water in a passage off the second gate and did not move, and the water on the far side of it moved.

At the seventh bell the anteroom was nine steps and a trestle and a folding rule lying flat on the trestle with mud on it that had not been out of a belt in fifty mornings, and a room that had found a rule nine days ago and had learned one thing this morning and not another, and a man at the anvil who had given them a word he had from a wet ground in another district, and a woman on the second of her three steps who had said the whole of it in eight words and had then said that the eight words were a sentence and not a thing, and a clerk who had put three of them on facing pages and had joined none of them, and a physician who had asked out loud who in this building had been told what a path is and had not used it, and nobody in that room answered him.
