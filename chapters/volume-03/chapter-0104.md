# Chapter 104 — The Cheapest Answer

The fourth day of the twentieth week was shut for the corridor, which was a Thursday, and the school was at the kitchen-line end in a room with a low ceiling, and the school corner stood empty at the school-door end with nine children's worth of wear in its flags and a wet band across the low side that had not moved and had not dried, and at the second bell Renn Vale said the thing that every person in this building had been thinking of since the first bell on the first day, and she said it properly, and it took four minutes, and it was correct.

"**Take it out of use as a room and shut it,**" she said.

---

Nobody argued with her for about eleven seconds, and the eleven seconds were the finding.

She put it the way she puts everything, which is on her slate and in her own hand and with the terms in it before anybody has agreed to hear them. She wrote it at the second bell standing on the common landing with the slate on her hip and Dalla Sen on the fourth step with the book, and she read it out standing, and it is in the book in a clerk's hand with the slate beside it.

"**The school corner is taken out of use as a room and shut. The school does not go back into it. The floor is wet at the low side and the room is empty and both of those are the reason and neither of them is the whole of the reason.**

"**The condition is: while the floor is wet and the room is empty of anybody. If either of those two stops, the room comes back into use on a person telling this room and not on a date. And the person who can stop this closure is me, and there is one of me, and I am not going to be asked twice.**"

Then she said the rest, and the rest is why it passed.

"**It is a door and a wedge and nothing bought. The door is the school door and the wedge is one of the three that are already at that door's head, so nothing is moved and nothing is bought and nothing is taken off a wall that is doing a job.**

"**And here is the whole of it, and I want the room to hear it before anybody votes. This is a room and not a route. It closes no day. The corridor is shut on the first, second and fourth days and open on the third, fifth and sixth and that does not change. The school runs on the three open days and it does not change. The water-carrying is not touched and costs nothing. The anteroom's second way out is not touched. And there is nobody behind the door, because the school is at the kitchen-line end and the school is not coming back.**

"**It is the cheapest thing anybody in this building has done in five months and it is the right thing and I am going to vote for it and I would like the room to understand what it costs, which is that it costs nothing, and that is not the same as costing nothing.**"

---

Marek Venn went looking for the objection for about a quarter of an hour and did not find one, and the not finding it is the reason the thing passed.

He checked it the way he checks everything, which is by trying to break it. He asked whether the bed joint under the floor runs anywhere that a shut door changes. It does not: **the joint runs to the haunch behind the anteroom's outer service door, and a door in a school corner is not a door in a wall, and shutting one of them does not shut the other.** He asked whether there is a mouth in the room. There is, and it was shut before this and it is shut now, and it was shut when the room had nine children in it, and nobody has opened it in twenty weeks. He asked whether shutting the room makes the corridor colder, because that is his habit, and the answer was that the corridor's air does not come out of a shut door in a room that has been shut to the corridor all week, and Nera Oduya confirmed that from three steps in nine words and went back to her valve.

He asked Jo Verrick what a shut school door does to the water, and she told him, on one knee, with a rule, in about a minute, that a shut door in a room whose floor is dished toward that door makes the dish a very slightly better place to be wet in than a room with a door open and a draught in it, and that in eight weeks the corner will be the driest part of that room and the only part of it anybody will stand on, and that a room shut for a wet corner in a building where the air is a subject is not a neutral act.

"**It is not neutral,**" she said, "**and it is not a room with a bad floor any more. It is a room with a known bad corner. That is a different object and it is a better one and I have got no argument against it and I would like the clerk to enter that I looked for one.**"

"Entered," said Dalla Sen, "**that the carpenter was asked for an objection by a man and gave one that is not an objection, and entered that the room has now had the thing it has wanted for five months, which is somebody looking for a reason not to do the cheap thing, and finding none.**"

---

The vote was not a vote. It was a condition, and the condition was passed, and a room did a thing it has not done in five months, which is to act on a Tuesday-Thursday-Monday and record it in a slate and a book and neither of them is a charter.

Bel Ordry said the one thing about it that had to be said and said it in a sentence.

"**It goes on the slate and not on the plan, and I want that written as my reason and not as an objection. The plan's second column already carries a condition with no number and no day on it, and a room does not need the same instrument twice, and if I put this on the page in four parts in my coat then I have got a page with nine rows on it and I do not know which nine.**"

"**Accepted,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the keeper of the page did not want the closure on the page, and entered the reason, and entered that a plan is not a place to put everything a boundary does.**

"And the room's answer to that," she said, "is **no**, and the room is wrong, and the keeper is right, and I am the clerk and I have written the room's answer down so that it is on the record that it was said."

"Nobody is going to read that in a year," said Bel Ordry.

"**That is the second time this week a thing has been put next to another thing in a book so that a reader in a year cannot make it into a finding,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and both times it was a person who was afraid of a coincidence, and the second one is in a book I keep and I have decided that is a good way to be afraid.**"

Then the thing the room does at the end of every business, which is that the person who wrote it stands up and says yes and the person who can stop it says her name.

Renn Vale said: "**The condition stands. The person who can stop it is me. I am not going to say when I will and I am not going to be asked, and anybody who wants it argued is going to have to argue it with me and not with a room.**"

"Entered," said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the condition has a person on it who can stop it, and entered that there is a name against it, which is the first name in five months that has been entered against anything in this building except a charter, and that the person it is entered against is the person who entered it, and that this building has learned that shape from a guild and does not like it and has not got a better one.**"

---

Jo Verrick shut the school door at the fourth bell with a sound wedge that was already at that door's head, and it took ninety seconds, and nothing was bought.

And then the finding, which was said out loud by two people in the same half hour and by nobody before them, and which is the whole of what this volume is going to be about.

Renn Vale said it first, from the landing, with the slate under her arm, in the voice she uses for a road:

"**Nothing that anybody in this building does every day is stopped by that door.** The pump runs. The water comes up in two crossings, four and then two, at a walk. The air valve is where it is. The anteroom is at its number. The cook's ten days is ten days. The two jars come out of the haunch in the afternoon. The review page has the same blank on it it has had since the eleventh week. The bar goes at the second bell. **Nine children are off a floor that nine children made a dish in, and they are dry, and the room is shut, and I have got nothing to do about any of it.**"

And then Ilsa Norr said the other half, from the top of the nine steps, and it was not about the room and she said so before she said it.

"**I am not defending the room,**" she said. "**The room can stay shut and I will fight anybody who opens it, and I have got a rope round the wet corner and a wedge in the door and I am not going to be sad about it.**

"**What we have just stopped being able to do is be wrong in a room with children in it.**

"**And I have only just worked out how much of what this boundary has done for five months was easier because nine people were going to be in the room. Every argument we have had for three weeks — the charter, the door, the word *door*, the fourth column, the pot, the six pails, the two jars, my own line on the page — every single one of them was had in a room with nine children sitting in it, and it is not that nine children make a room clever. It is that nine children in a room means that everybody arguing is arguing about a thing somebody is standing in.**

"**And the school corner was the worst room in this building and it was the only room in this building that nine people were ever going to be in, and now there is not one, and I do not know what this boundary's arguments sound like without one and neither does anybody else in here.**"

The room did not fill the space after that, and Dalla Sen entered that the schoolteacher had said it and had said it about a building and not about a floor, and that the clerk did not know what to do with that and had written it down in the third column place and was going to leave it there.

---

Marek Venn was on the bench at the top of the nine steps, and he was the only person in this Hearthhold who could see what had happened, and he had been able to see it since the fourth bell on the second day, and being the only one had been a thing he had wanted for five months.

It had stopped being a thing he wanted somewhere in the middle of Renn Vale's four minutes and it had not come back.

**A room kept safe by making it a room nobody is in.** That was the shape of it and he could not get round it and he could not find a person in the building who was going to say it out loud except himself, and he was not going to say it out loud either, because there were nine children dry at the other end of this corridor and a cook with ten days of food and a pump window and a door on four rusted pins and if he stood up in a room and said *this is the first of a series* he would be spending the one afternoon he had on the only good thing this boundary has done since the charter went on the board.

So he sat on a bench and did what he has done four times in five months, which is to write it in a book of his own and show it to nobody, and what he wrote was not a plan.

*They have learned how to keep a room by not being in it. It cost nothing. It was correct. Everybody in the room voted for it and I voted for it and the person who voted for it hardest is the woman who found the water and the person who voted against it is the man who cannot read anything any more. The trouble is not that it is expensive. The trouble is that it is cheap and it works and it will be cheaper next time and it will work then.*

*I have wanted for nineteen weeks to be the only one who could see a thing. It is the first time in five months I have had it and I would like to give it back and I cannot find anybody to hand it to, because the only other person in this building who sees it is a man with a page in his coat who asked to be suspicious of in a month and nobody has been.*

He read it back and did not tear it out and put the book inside his coat, which is not the coat the plan is in, because the plan is in Bel Ordry's and his has nothing in it that anybody can be handed.

---

At the sixth bell the six things were read, and the six were the same six with three more on them, and the three more were a shut room and a corridor arrangement that had not changed and a condition with a name against it in a woman's hand, and Mikka Hale read the pot out first, at ten days, and said it had not gone up, and said it in the words she uses and not in any other.

The corridor was shut on the first, second and fourth days and open on the third, fifth and sixth and the seventh day was not a crossing day, and the school ran on the three open days, and the school corner was shut as a room and not as a route and it changed no day of that at all, and the school was dry at the kitchen-line end in the worst room in the building past a fill that has moved three times, and the anteroom held ten including one litter on a frame a finger and a bit out of true with the water from a wall now all in one place against it, and a person was still under a lip.

And on the surface, four hundred feet above a line of chalked stations that nobody in this building knew was there, a woman with a permit had finished walking her round and had written the word *route* four times in a book and was coming down a shaft that this boundary's charter said nothing about.
