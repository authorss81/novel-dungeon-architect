# Chapter 175 — Terms For A Piece Of Wood

**Open for the corridor on the third day of the thirty-fourth week, a Wednesday, and the school ran for the thirty-first morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, which is the room a printed sheet has in its second column and has not struck. The fire was asked about for the tenth time and refused for the tenth time, and a physician asked a room out loud whether it was a good idea that the terms that governed a pair of hands had been extended to a piece of wood, and did not get an easy answer.**

**The anteroom's word was said by a person and repeated by a clerk, and for the first time in seven weeks there were two people in that room who could stop it.**

**A man who reads a board was asked whether a returning keeper goes on it, and gave the eleventh reason of his life, and the board is still at eighteen.**

At the fourth bell the thirty-first morning began, and nine went into the room at the kitchen-line end. The anteroom at the other end of the same party wall had a piece of a school's own beech going across the head of its outer service door, the frame was a finger and two-thirds out of true, and the shim was in its hollow. The cord at the joint had a little slack in the middle, which nobody had written down, because a carpenter had said it out loud twice and had been ignored on paper twice.

Mikka Hale asked to be asked about the fire and refused for the tenth time, in one sentence, and asked for the children to be cold in the record in the same place as the refusal, and then she put her board down on the sill by the doorway, which is where it goes when she is going to do arithmetic instead of cooking.

"**Say it out loud, and then say the rest of it, because you have said the number twice this month and both times it went down,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The pot is nine days. It has been nine or ten for thirty-four weeks and it has not gone up once. The seam gave one on Friday and two on Wednesday and it gave eight threads in the nineteenth week and gives four, and nobody has measured it in between, and I have asked for it to be measured five times now.**" She did not pick the board up. "**And I am going to say the arithmetic and I am not going to say the reason, and the reason is not going in a column and it is not going beside the number either. A crew of five for two days, a brace, a school, an elected keeper eating in this building since Saturday, and a printed sheet on the anteroom table for two days that has cost four people their appetite and one road-hand her appetite as well. This is eight by the middle of next week and there is nothing in this Hearthhold that fixes it.**"

"**Entered the arithmetic, in her words, and entered that she would not say the reason, and entered that I have asked twice this week to be given a reason and have been refused twice, and that the four households at a second gate have nothing and that no figure is attached to that gate in this book and there is not going to be one.**"

Then the anteroom's terms were said at the fourth bell, and the word was said by a person and repeated by the clerk, and the count was ten including one litter for no more than two hours, and the pump was attended, and the outer service door was the one a body went out of.

"**Say the fifth,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it with both names in it, and then let a person in this room say whether it is still the same sentence.**"

"**The air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it.**" Bel Ordry looked at the two of them. "**Same sentence. And I am going to say the half against it, because there is one and I have had it since Saturday. For seven weeks that term could not be kept, and nobody in this building knew, and the reason nobody knew is that one clause of six did not get read, and the reason it did not get read is that this room has never had a practice of reading them.**"

"**And the reason it has not had that practice,**" said Renn Vale, from the fourth step, "**is that for thirty-four weeks the two people who could stop this room have been able to stop it, and a room that can be stopped does not get asked whether its own words are true. You do not audit a handle you are holding.**"

"**Then say what the handle was this morning,**" said Nera Oduya, "**and say it in the first person, and a room has been taught that by me.**"

"**It was mine and it was yours, and mine was on a valve and yours was on a wheel, and neither of us was in the anteroom at seven in the morning because neither of us has to be, and that is the arrangement, and it works, and no room this morning is going to pretend it was a near thing.**"

Then Mero Senn asked the question, and he asked it standing up, and he had his bag against the wall and had not sat down all morning, and Dalla Sen wrote nothing while he said it and wrote it all down afterwards.

"**Six days ago a room found out that four sentences a clerk had written to govern a pair of hands had been applied to a piece of wood. Not by me and not by any of you. A room did it, in a room, and the clerk stood at the wall and gave the room away for four minutes, and the finding was that the four sentences had bound a work and not a building, and a building outlasts the terms of a work.**"

"**Say your question, Mero, and say it as a person with a question and not as an objection, because a room that hears an objection to a thing it did two weeks ago is going to hear it as an accusation.**"

"**Is it a good idea?**" He put his hand flat on the trestle. "**That is the whole of my question and I have not got a better shape for it. Four sentences were written on a named morning by a named person for five named people. A piece of beech was moved into a wall and the four sentences went with it, because the room wanted them to go with it, and nobody voted. And now there is a brace in this building that is governed by sentences that were written for a pair of hands, and the sentences are the only thing holding it.**"

"**The woman who can take it out is one of the five the sentences were written for. The man who is the other one of us who can take it out is not one of the five and has never been one.**"

"**Say the half against your own question,**" said Renn Vale, "**because you have not and I will, and then somebody has to answer you, and nobody is going to let this stand as a question for a month.**"

"**The half against it is that those four sentences are the only instrument in this building that four people agreed to out loud in a room, and a piece of beech that is not governed by anything is a piece of beech that somebody with a knife can take out on a Tuesday.**"

"**And say what a room should do with a question,**" said Marek Venn, "**because a physician has put a question on a table and there is a piece of beech over a door and a woman who can take it out and a clerk with a book, and all four of those things are in this room. The room has been extremely good at noticing things and not very good at saying what should happen.**"

"**Then say it, and say it as the man who cut the splay, because nobody else in this room is going to say it as badly as I will,**" said Marek Venn.

The book had been open on his forearm since the first bell and he had put nothing in it for three days.

He had been waiting since Saturday for a morning when the answer to something was going to be useful, and he had told himself twice this week that he would not wait for one.

"**I do not know,**" he said. "**And I want to say the three things I know before I say that, because a man in this room has been saying *I do not know* out loud for five weeks and every time he has said it somebody has improved on it for him, and I am not going to leave it bare.**"

"**Say them, and say them in the order you know them.**"

"**One: those four sentences are the only thing in this building that four people said out loud. Two: a brace that is not governed by anything is a piece of wood. Three: I cut the head of it and I am the one person in this room who is least able to see whether it is working.**" He shut the book. "**And the answer to your question is that I do not know, and I am going to find out the way I find out everything, which is by being wrong in front of people who will tell me.**"

"**I have been in this building thirty-four weeks. I have had a standing for a fortnight and I still do not have a post. I have stopped waiting for a morning when I will be the man who is asked.**"

Nobody improved on it, and the pump got four strokes in, and Dalla Sen entered it in the space where a reason goes.

"**Entered, and entered that a man said *I do not know* three times in a room this week and put three facts under it, and that this is the first time in thirty-four weeks he has done that without being asked twice. And entered that the difference goes in as a fact and not as a virtue, because he has been asked twice this week and I have counted.**"

Then the board, and the eighteenth thing on it, and the man who read it.

"**Eighteen,**" said Tams Quill, with the chalk in his left hand and the right one flat and open in his lap, and no day named. "**Eleven nulls, one claim, one mark, one row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace. Nine in the ordinary column and nine in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim's condition has not been met and the mark is not coming off the board.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it in the room and not to me, because you have got a question hanging in this room and it has been hanging since Saturday.**"

"**The question is whether a keeper goes on it.**" He put the chalk down on the anvil end of the trestle. "**And I have had it since Saturday and I have not said a word about it for three days, and I have been in a bad month for twelve weeks, and this morning I have decided that I have something, and I am going to say it, and it is the eleventh, and it is not about a heading.**"

"**Say it,**" said Bel Ordry, from the step.

"**I gave a tenth reason a fortnight ago and it cost me something I have not worked out. The eleventh is not about a word over a column. It is this.**" He did not pick the chalk up. "**A brace is on that board and a brace is a thing somebody is going to have to keep, and that is why it is on there. A keeper is a person, and a person on a board is a thing on a board, and the day a returning man is a nineteenth entry on a piece of slate then he is a thing that gets kept. And there is a man standing in this room who says he does not know what this building is for, and he is me, and I have been eleven weeks not knowing it, and I am not going to find out by putting a person on a list of things.**"

"**Say the other half of it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and then say whether the number can be asked about, because that is the part somebody is going to want.**"

"**The half is that if a keeper is not on that board then nobody in this building is, and that is correct, and I have been getting that for eleven weeks without knowing I was getting it.**" He picked the chalk up at last. "**And the number can be asked. Eighteen. I have just said it, and you did not send anybody anywhere to hear it, and I would like that entered in those words, because a person who has to be found before a number can be read is a person being kept and I have just refused to do it twice in one morning.**"

"**Entered, in his words, that the board is at eighteen and that no nineteenth entry has gone on it, and that the word over the unheaded column would be *keeper* and there is now a keeper in this building and the word still does not go on, and entered that the number can be asked without anybody being sent for.**"

Then the clerk read the two things back to the man who had said them on Saturday, which is the practice, and the practice is that somebody says a thing in a room and a second person reads it against what was said and asks him whether it is the same.

"**Read them,**" said Dalla Sen, "**both of them, word for word, and I am going to ask him whether it is what he said. I have never once asked a man that about a thing he said in this room on a Saturday, and Wednesday with nine children in the next room is not the day I start.**"

She read the first one.

"**One. I am not taking anything back. Every decision this boundary made without me stands, and I will not overturn one of them.**"

"**And I will not stand in this room and say I would have done it differently, because I do not know that and I have not earned the right to say it.**"

"**That is what I said,**" said Sefu Aran.

"**Then the second.**"

"**Two. A room that has been getting on without a keeper has still had a keeper's work done in it every single day.**"

"**And the somebody who did it was not elected, and nobody is going to be found out this week, and it will not be done in this room, and it will not be done with a piece of paper.**"

"**That is what I said, and I am going to say the half that has been sitting on me since Saturday, and it is in the space where a reason goes and not in a column.**" He looked at Renn Vale. "**On Sunday you said *I did some of it* and I have thought about that for two days and here it is. You keep a shore, a road, the lead terms, a corridor, a landing, a door with no wedge in it, and since Saturday a brace and a condition. That is a keeper's work and it was not elected, and it will not be done with a piece of paper, and it will be done in a room, and the room is this one, and not this week.**"

"**Entered, in his words,**" said Dalla Sen. "**And entered that I have been asked four times this month to put a person in a column and have said no four times, and that this is the fifth refusal, and that it is the first one where the person refusing did not want a space left for somebody else.**"

Renn Vale said, from the fourth step: "**You will get an answer on the sixth day of a week and it will not be the answer you expect, and that is not a threat, it is a schedule, and I am aware of what I have just said.**"

Then, at the kitchen-line doorway, with nine children behind her in a room that a printed sheet has in its second column, a woman who keeps the air stood still for about ten seconds and did not come in and did not go away.

"**Say the half against your own error,**" said Ilsa Norr, and the woman at the doorway said it.

The room heard a woman who certifies air admit in a doorway that the number she has been carrying for a fortnight is the number for a room as it is and not for a room as it will be. And heard her say that she was not going to be told it was wrong by a room that wanted to be comfortable, and that she had said it out loud on purpose, on a morning when nine children were in the next room.

At the sixth bell the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and it was the first time in two days that she had read either of them, and on Tuesday the reason was a printed sheet that had been open on that table since the second bell with a column on it headed for a person. The count was read at one hundred and ten, and the board was walked at eighteen entries for the last time this week.

"**Two today and two hundred and thirty-two since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and forty-nine and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and the counter is at seventeen.**"

"**I read those out because I have entered a finding this week that is not a number, and a person in a year is going to come to this book and want to know what the week cost, and the cost is in the two columns and not in the finding.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one, and have not moved all week, and the sixth asking has not been made, and the reason is that a room has had four things come into it in four days and not one of them is a work anybody can be asked five people for. A clerk who asks because the week feels like it ought to be asking is a clerk making a rota out of a mood.**"

"**The pot is nine. The seam gave one on Friday. The count. Wen Pask is one hundred and ten days and one hundred and ten nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and eleven, and I have said in nineteen weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and I am writing it. It is in the column with nothing over the top of it and in nothing else. It dates nothing and it is not a register. I would like it entered that I read it out five times this week and made a face on none of them.**"

"**And I walked the board, because a man gave me a reason for the first time in twelve weeks and a board does not get walked on the morning a man finds a reason, and a board does not get skipped on it either, because that would be making the board a punishment.**"

The brass survey rule in the pocket of the coat on the hook had said nothing for twenty-five weeks, and the wrapped plate on the shelf above the same bench had been unopened for thirty-four. Neither of them was in a room and neither of them moved.

The anteroom's frame is a finger and two-thirds out of true. The count is at one hundred and ten and is not going to be one hundred and eleven. The school door at the far end of nine steps is shut with nothing wedged in it, and three of the five sound wedges in this building are spoken for and two are not. The page where a person's hours would go is blank, and it has three conditions on it, and the third of them says that nobody is to be sent anywhere to make it true.

**On the third day of the thirty-fourth week the school ran for the thirty-first morning in the room a printed sheet has in its second column, and a cook was asked about the fire for the tenth time and refused for the tenth time.**

**The pot is nine days and going to eight, and four households at a second gate have nothing.**

**A physician asked a room whether it was a good idea that the terms that governed a pair of hands had been extended to a piece of wood, and gave the half against it himself, and no carpenter was in the room to give one.**

**A man who cut the splay said he did not know, and put three facts underneath it for the first time in thirty-four weeks without being asked twice.**

**And a man who reads a board said no to a nineteenth entry, and gave the eleventh reason of his life, and it was not about a heading. A person on a board is a thing on a board, and the day a returning man is an entry on a piece of slate then he is a thing that gets kept.**

**The number is at eighteen, and it can be asked without anybody being sent for.**
