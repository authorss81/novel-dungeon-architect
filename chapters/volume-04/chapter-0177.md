# Chapter 177 — A Permission Or A Count

**Open for the corridor on the fifth day of the thirty-fourth week, a Friday, and the school ran for the thirty-second morning in the room at the kitchen-line end, which is the room a printed sheet has a figure for and has not struck. The fire was asked about for the eleventh time and refused for the eleventh time, and a woman who certifies air stood in that room at the fourth bell with nine children in it for the first time in eight weeks and said nothing about a sentence she wrote in a room a fortnight ago.**

**The two documents went on the anteroom table side by side and a second person read one against the other, and the numbers agreed, and a clerk entered that this boundary has been printing a clearance figure in its own public column for thirty-four weeks on purpose.**

**And a man was told out loud by a woman on a landing that the thing he has been offered is a reason to go and look at things, and he did not want it.**

Jo Verrick read the frame at the first bell and the number was a finger and two-thirds, and she entered it without comment, and the school came in at the first bell and went up nine steps and round a party wall into a room with nine hearth stones in it, and the corridor crossing made twice by nine people and a woman with a slate.

Nera Oduya went into that room at the first bell, which she had not done on a morning the school was running in eight weeks, and she stood at the back with her dyed cloth out and the valve board against her hip, and she stayed there through the whole of the reading and the whole of the writing and most of the morning.

Mikka Hale asked to be asked about the fire and refused for the eleventh time, in one sentence, and asked for the eleven refusals to be counted in the book, and the clerk counted them.

"**Say it in the same place as the last ten,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**On the fire line, in her words, and the count of refusals beside it, and not in a column,**" said Mikka Hale. "**And I am saying the count out loud because in about a month somebody is going to ask this room why there is a room in it that nine children are taught in and a woman in it who will not light a fire, and I want the answer to be *because she was asked eleven times*, and I do not want the answer to be a sentence.**"

Nobody improved on that either, and at the second bell the leaf came back onto the anteroom table and stayed there all morning, and Bel Ordry took a coat off a hook.

He did not take the page out of the coat. He undid the four folds of the outer sheet and set the coat on the back of the bench with the page still in it, and then he took the page out of the coat and laid it on the table face up, which is the first time in thirty-four weeks that page has been on a table in a room with another document on it.

"**Say what I am doing,**" he said, "**because I have never done it and there are six people in this room who have never seen me do it, and one of them is going to write down that it happened and I would rather it was said first.**"

"**You are going to put a page in a coat on a table beside somebody else's paper,**" said Renn Vale.

"**I am going to do that, and I have thought about it for a day, and the reason is that a page in a coat is the only instrument in this building with a column for a person in it, and that column is empty, and I have said in this room for thirty-four weeks that the empty column is the whole of what I have.**" He squared it with the edge of the trestle. "**And I am about to find out whether the empty column is a virtue or a hiding place.**"

"**Say the half against your own question,**" said Dalla Sen, "**because you have not and a room is going to hear it as a speech.**"

"**The half against it is that I have kept that page in a coat for thirty-four weeks and it has never been in a room with another document, and a page only ever read by itself is a page that can be wrong in a direction nobody notices.**"

"**Then read them,**" said Dalla Sen. "**You read the sheet and somebody who is not you reads the page, one row at a time, and says which figures are the same and which have nothing on the other side of them, and nobody in this room is to stop you when a row has nothing.**"

She said it to Renn Vale, and Renn Vale came down four steps, and read the printed figure for each of the six district rooms out loud, and then read the first column of the sixteenth-row page aloud against it, and said what was on the other side of each one.

"**Two of the six match. The anteroom, ten, and the room at the kitchen-line end, ten, and both of ours are the same figure.**" She put her finger on the next line and left it there. "**Four of the six have nothing on the other side of them, and not because they are wrong. This boundary has sixteen rows and the district has more rooms than that, and the difference is not a mistake. It is a different size of claim.**"

"**And now say the sentence you have not said,**" said Dalla Sen.

Renn Vale did not move her finger.

"**Say it, because I have read the same two documents and I have got to it by a different road and I would like to know whether it is the same sentence,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The first column of our page is a permitted occupancy and has never held a count of anybody, and we have known that since the second week and we have said it out loud in rooms and it is written in the reason for the fourth column in two hands.**" Renn Vale took her hand off the paper. "**And the figure in the first column of a clearance record is a permitted occupancy. And ours is a permitted occupancy. And the two documents on this table have the same kind of number in them, made in the same shape, for the same reason, and the reason ours is there is a reason and not an accident.**"

"**Say the reason,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**A boundary that wrote down how many people sleep under its roof would be handing a stranger a number it could not take back, and this boundary has had a stranger on its doorstep with a sheet since the third week of the twenty-ninth week and has been refusing to fill in two lines ever since.**" He put his hand flat on the page in the coat. "**And the reason is not a good one. It is a frightened one wearing a coat.**"

"**Entered in his words, and entered that he called it that himself, and entered that I have not improved it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Enter the two figures and the four with nothing on the other side, and enter the finding, and enter it in this form:**" She wrote for a while.

"**Entered that a page in a coat and a printed leaf from a cleared-risk office were read against each other in a room by two people and by a third, and that two of six district rooms have a counterpart figure on our page and that both figures are the same, and that four of six have no counterpart and that this is not a fault in either document.**" She read the line back. "**Entered that our first column is a permission and that a clearance figure is a permission and that they are the same kind of number, and that ours has been one for thirty-four weeks, and that it was written that way on purpose, and that the purpose was correct, and that a correct purpose is not a defence and does not get entered as one.**"

Then the woman who certifies air said a thing from the back of a room nine children were in, and she said it to the wall and not to anybody.

"**I have certified the air in eleven rooms in this district.**" Nera Oduya did not turn round. "**Eleven. I have a number behind each one and the number is a rate and the rate is right, and I have never once counted the people in any of them, because counting the people in a room is a different job and it belongs to a different person and I do not have that job.**"

The room went quiet in a way that is not the same as a pump stopping.

"**Say the half against that,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The half against it is that eleven rooms in this district have a number in them that somebody certified and not one of the eleven has ever had a figure for the bodies in it, and that includes every one of the eleven I did, and I have been proud of that for eight years and this morning it is the sentence I would hand to the man who printed the leaf.**" She turned round at last. "**And I am not going to be told by a room that wanted to be comforted that the two things are the same, because they are not the same instrument and this room has spent three weeks teaching me the difference between two instruments.**"

"**Nobody was going to tell you that,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and entered, in her words, both halves in that order, and entered that the woman at the back of that room was standing in a room with nine children in it on a morning the school ran, which she had not done in eight weeks, and that I noticed it at the fourth bell and I am entering the noticing and not the reason.**"

Nobody asked her about the sentence she wrote in a room a fortnight ago. Renn Vale looked at her once, from the fourth step, and did not ask, and Dalla Sen entered that she had not asked and had noticed herself not asking, which is the second time.

Iven Tallo had the chalk in his left hand and the right one open and flat on his knee and had been listening to all of it without moving, and he got up off the anvil end and went and stood at the head of the nine steps and looked down them, and came back.

"**Say the thing you are not going to say until somebody asks you,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Four things about a hole, and I am a carpenter and this is the trade view and it is not the engineer's view and he is standing there and he can tell you where mine stops.**" He put the chisel down on the anvil. "**One. A hole is not a route. Two. A hole is a thing you cut and a route is a thing you keep, and this boundary has spent thirty-four weeks learning that the keeping is the whole of it. Three. There is a difference between a hole into a void and a hole into a room, and a void is not a room, and a hole into a void is a hole with the bottom taken off it.**"

"**And four,**" said Bel Ordry, "**because you have said three and stopped.**"

"**Four is that a room that repeats what it has been doing is not a room anybody should cut a hole into on a Friday.**"

"**Why a Friday,**" said Marek Venn.

"**Because I have been in this building thirty-four weeks and I have watched this room find out about things on the days it is closed, and I do not know one single thing about the party wall, and the party wall is the wall the brace pushes on, and the wall the brace pushes on is the wall nine children lean against.**" He looked at the corner of the room where two walls met. "**And a thing that is being pushed on does not get quieter. I do not know that it gets louder. I know that I would not be asleep on the seventh day with a lamp in a void I have not seen.**"

Marek Venn found that the objection had gone straight through the middle of the thing he had been drawing for three days, and that the carpenter had put it there in nine seconds without looking at the page, and that the worst of it was that he had known it and had not written it down, which meant he had been drawing for three days in order to be able to write it down.

That was the whole of his want and it took him about four seconds to see it, and he had two burns and a channel and a knee and thirty-four weeks of practice at seeing a want after it has had three days.

"**Say it,**" said Dalla Sen, "**and say it as the man who drew it, because nobody in this room is going to say it as badly as you will.**"

"**I have been drawing a hole in a wall I have never been inside, for three days, so that I would have something to be wrong about.**" He put the book on the table and left it there. "**That is the whole of it and it took a carpenter nine seconds. And I am not going to cut it, and I am not going to throw the page away either, because the page is a correct answer to a question I have not finished asking, and I would rather be holding a correct answer to a wrong question for a fortnight than a wrong answer to a right one.**"

"**Entered, in his words, and entered that he left the drawing on the table and did not burn it and did not fold it away, and entered that a room has now twice this week watched a man put a want of his own on a table in front of people instead of taking it into a book,**" said Dalla Sen.

Renn Vale came down the four steps, all of them, and stopped on the anteroom floor, which she does not do for anybody.

"**Then I am going to say the thing that is coming, and I am saying it now so that it is in a room before it happens and not after.**" She had her slate under her arm. "**You are the only person in this Hearthhold that nobody's to send. You are not on the rota, you have no post, you cannot stand a watch, and there is nobody above you to be asked whether you may. A store four days up the passage needs somebody to stand in it. A void behind a party wall needs somebody to stand in it. A road four days up the middle passage will need somebody to stand in it, and soon, and the middle passage is four days and a man is not a courier.**"

"**Now say the whole of it,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The rest of it is that I have watched you go quiet twice this week and I know exactly what it is, because I have been going quiet in this building for thirty-four weeks and the reason is the same both times and it is never a good one.**" She put the slate down on the trestle, which she has not done in that room. "**Somebody is going to offer you a reason to go and look at things, and you are going to hear it as an answer, and a reason to go and look at things is the cheapest standing anybody has ever handed you. It is not a post. It is the thing a person is given instead of a post.**"

"**Entered, in her words, and entered that she is the second person in three days to say a version of it to him, and entered that the first was her and the second was him and that I am putting them next to each other and am not joining them,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**I have been asked nothing and I am going to answer it anyway, and then I am going to close my book, and then somebody is going to ask me for a number at the sixth bell.**" Marek Venn took the book off the table and shut it and did not put it on his forearm. "**I do not want it. I want that written down in the space where a reason goes, because in about a year somebody is going to read that I was asked and think I was glad, and I was not glad, and the not being glad is the part of it that is true.**"

Nobody said anything to that, and the pump got four strokes in, and the woman at the back of the room with nine children in it turned the dyed cloth over in her fingers and put it away.

The sixth bell came round and the clerk read the day's number and the total out loud, both of them, and went down the board at eighteen entries, and the count was read at one hundred and twelve.

"**Three today and two hundred and thirty-seven since the keeper signed. The boundary is at three hundred and fifty-four and a quarter, it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile. The counter is at seventeen and I am carrying it.**"

"**I read those out because this morning a room found out that its own page and a stranger's page carry the same kind of number, and a room that has just found that out deserves to hear where the money went.**"

"**The crew are at seventy-one and have not moved since the second day of the thirty-second week, and there has been no sixth asking. The reason is that a man in this room was offered a reason to go and look at things and said out loud that he did not want it. A clerk who asks five people for a piece of work on the back of that is turning a man's honesty into a rota.**"

"**The pot is nine days. The seam gave one on Thursday. And the count. Wen Pask is one hundred and twelve days and one hundred and twelve nights into a count that is his own, and it is not going to be one hundred and thirteen, and I have said in twenty-one weeks running that I am going to keep writing it, and nobody has thanked me and I have stopped keeping track of who does not.**"

"**And I walked the board, and the reason is that a page came out of a coat and lay on a table with another document, and a board is a column with a heading on it, and a room that has just said out loud that it does not trust its own headings needs a man to read eighteen entries out loud in front of it.**"

Tams Quill had the chalk in his left hand and the right one flat and open on his knee, and named no day, and gave eighteen in the order they were in.

"**Eleven nulls. Then a claim, a mark, a row, a plan, a condition, a store and a brace. Nine in the ordinary column and nine in the one with nothing over the top of it. The claim's condition has not been met and the mark is not coming off.**"

"**Say the other thing, and then stop,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**The column is not headed, and it is not headed on a morning when a word was read out of a wax head on a printed sheet, because a wax head is exactly the thing I have been refusing to put over that column for twelve weeks, and I have nothing else to say about it and nobody has asked me.**" He put the chalk down. "**And a map is not going on that board. I am not giving a reason for that either. I have had two days.**"

"**Entered that he gave no reason and that I did not ask, and entered that a map was not put on the board and that the man who reads the board gave no reason, and entered that this is the second morning running that a thing has come into this building on somebody else's paper and has not gone onto a board in this building, and that I have noticed the shape of those two mornings and I am not writing the shape down.**"

The brass rule had been in that coat since the ninth week of this building and had said nothing for twenty-five weeks, and the plate above the same bench had been wrapped for thirty-four. Neither had been in a room this week. A brace of a school's own beech stood across the head of the outer service door a foot and a half below the frame, and the frame was a finger and two-thirds out of true, and the wall on the other side of it had ten readings on it and two of them were nothings.

**The school ran for the thirty-second morning in a room that a printed leaf has a figure for, and a woman who certifies air stood at the back of it with nine children in it for the first time in eight weeks, and said that she has certified eleven rooms in this district and has never once counted the people in any of them, and gave the half against her own sentence before anybody asked.**

**A page came out of a coat and lay on a table beside a stranger's paper, and two people read one against the other, and two of six district rooms have a counterpart figure on our page and both figures are the same, and four of six have nothing on the other side of them and that is not a fault in either document.**

**A clerk entered that this boundary has been printing a clearance figure in its own public column for thirty-four weeks on purpose, and that the purpose was correct, and that a correct purpose is not a defence and does not get entered as one.**

**A carpenter said a room that repeats what it has been doing is not a room anybody should cut a hole into on a Friday, and a man who had been drawing one for three days said out loud that he had drawn it so that he would have something to be wrong about.**

**And a woman on a landing told him that the cheapest standing anybody has ever handed a man is a reason to go and look at things, and he said out loud that he did not want it, and asked for the not-wanting to go in the space where a reason goes.**
