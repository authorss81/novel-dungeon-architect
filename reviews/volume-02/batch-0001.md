# Novel Review — Volume 02, Batch 0001

## Scope and provenance

Chapters 51–60 were checked against `AGENTS.md`, `NOVEL_SPEC.md`, `outline/series.md` (the Volume 02 section), `outline/ending.md`, `outline/volume-01.md`, the bible, the Batch 0002 prompt, the post-batch state files, and the delivered Chapters 43, 45, 48, 49 and 50 as the immediate continuity and tone baseline.

**`outline/volume-02.md` did not exist when this batch was written.** The batch was written against the Volume 02 section of `outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md`, and the provenance is recorded in `state/current.md`. That is a planning-phase gap, not a prose defect, and it is called out in the Volume 02 outline phase.

An independent `novel-reviewer` pass was run over the delivered prose and returned **thirty-four blocking findings and twenty-eight craft findings**. The reviewer also independently re-derived the project's own repetition counts and found that `reviews/volume-01/batch-0005.md` had recorded *read back* as 37 where the measured figure is about 47, and that this batch's own state files had recorded 13 where the measured figure was 47. **Every blocking finding is repaired. Most craft findings are applied; the ones that are not are recorded below as author decisions with reasons, and the measured baseline is now in `state/batch-summary.md` so the next batch is not planned against a false number.**

Baseline confirmed sound before any repair: all ten chapters exist as finished prose, the five-survivor party intact, **Oren Sill never named as a living man**, the plate never identified, activated, or used, no crew member on the rota or holding a watch, the anteroom rating unaltered, the two-foot-eight figure unaltered, Nera's air authority never folded into a resident's consent, Sefu's veto never folded into an air decision, Tams Quill never two-handed, **no power stage, bearing mark, repeatable reading, route change, binding, relay, or automatic solution anywhere**, no second death, and no meta language inside the fiction.

## Blocking repairs applied

**Geometry and ledger**
- **B1.** Three pieces of three feet is nine feet, not twelve. Tams Quill's arithmetic in Chapter 51 was wrong in the mouth of the character whose existence is to catch that: now three pieces of three feet, a scarf taking a foot out of every joint, honestly four pieces and twelve foot, and twelve foot is not in this building either.
- **B2.** The parapet slab was **eleven feet** in three places and **twelve** in two, in the same scene, with nobody correcting it in a room whose method is correction. All five are eleven feet.
- **B3.** The cost of Talla Roke's withdrawal moved between chapters — *the second gate*, *the fourth gate*, *four gate families*, *four households*. All are **the second gate, four households**.
- **B4.** Chapter 60's ledger charged the **ninth-week** closure's half-day of shutter route to the **tenth-week** closure as well. The two are now separate entries, and the clerk states in her own hand that she made the error in the direction of the ledger sounding more careful than it is.
- **B5.** The *spent* column listed **the screw jack twice** inside a single read-aloud tally, unremarked, in front of Dalla Sen. One entry, with the best-thing-we-ever-owned line folded into it, and Tams says why he will not put it in twice.
- **B6.** Chapter 54 called fifty-one seconds *the best figure in the record* when thirty-eight is in the record. It is now *the best figure in the record for a party with a head of timber in its hands, and the best on the stair is still the thirty-eight.*
- **B7.** The anteroom's clearing figure in Chapter 57 sat beside the ninth week without distinguishing *the outer step* from *the kitchen line*. Both are now distinguished on the page.

**The calendar**
- **B8.** Five in-batch events were dated to a register value the locked calendar forbids: the Chapter 52 death in the **seventh** week (now the **ninth**), the Chapter 53 death **four days** after a seventh-week draw (now a fortnight, and a week of thinking, which is not long enough), the Chapter 57 woman saying she has had **five weeks** to think about the Thursday (now a week), Chapter 55's *the fourth week of the tenth* (now *on a morning in the tenth week, in the anteroom, in front of eleven people*), and Chapter 51's *the fourth of the month* (now *the week the shore went in*). All five are gone, and the week's measure is reconciled once, early, in Chapter 51.
- **B9.** Chapter 53 had a rope standing in *six weeks of water for seven weeks*, which is not a duration. It has been in the water six weeks for seven weeks.
- **B10.** The **eleventh item** on the unresolved list was claimed by three different things. The post is now consistently the **tenth** and the seat-and-check the **eleventh**, in the prose and in every state file.
- **B11.** The **knock** exists twice — the one Nera heard at the lip and the one Bel Ordry's bar found — and `state/open-threads.md` had merged them, describing a knock Bel heard as the origin of a thread that began with Nera. Both are now recorded separately, in separate hands, and Bel Ordry says so out loud in Chapter 60.

**Locked continuity**
- **B12.** Chapter 52 had **Sefu Aran holding the sixth watch at the waist**, which is Renn Vale's, four days in seven, and had him holding two watches at once in a book where *a person doing two things is doing neither* is standing rule. The waist is now Renn's, with the distinction said out loud.
- **B13.** Chapter 52 had Ruk Sella, **repeating the terms back**, invert the standing rule — *the waist is a post that morning because the corridor is shut*. It is the exact opposite, and the whole function of the repeat-the-terms beat is to reproduce the rule accurately. It now says *not a post*, which is the sentence four other chapters in this batch and three in Volume 01 give it.
- **B14.** Chapter 56 said **six strangers**. Oren Sill died in Chapter 48, Tams corrected this number in Chapter 50 in front of a room, and the review of Volume 01 Batch 0005 recorded that correction as a blocking repair. It is **five strangers**, and Tams did not need to say it a third time.
- **B15.** Chapter 51 invented a new counting rule — *children are not on the count and should not be* — in order to dismiss a number, in a book whose own narrator has said *a building does not announce its own population*. The number is now dismissed by that sentence instead.
- **B16.** Chapter 54 had Iven Tallo restating Renn Vale's school-door-head wedge refusal as a general refusal of wedges, which it is not, with Renn on the day's board and not correcting him and Tams stating her refusal correctly twenty lines later. Iven now has her actual refusal and states that he is **extending** it himself, on purpose, so that somebody in the room can tell him he is wrong.

**Chapter 58, the viewpoint shift**
- **B17.** The marker sentence contained **the four terms on the terms of this boundary**, which is not English, in the one place the reader decides whether to trust the narration. Fixed.
- **B18.** The plate was in Marek's **coat pocket** in one line while Marek put his hands on the bench **on either side of an object that was not there** in the next. The bundle is on the shelf above the bench, where Chapter 50 and the state files put it, and he puts his hands on the bench either side of where the shelf is. The incoherent version had already propagated into `state/continuity.md`; the state was corrected with the prose.
- **B19.** The board behind the valve was delivered as **four bare `On it:` items in a row**, with nobody reading any of it aloud and nobody objecting, twelve lines after Nera's most careful speech about certifying by standing in the room. Cut to two, inside her head, where the chapter's viewpoint permits.
- **B20.** The marker paragraph's *What follows is the part of this week that belongs to the air engineer* is the narrator describing the book's construction, which is the defect `reviews/volume-01/batch-0005.md` recorded as C5. Cut to the image.

**Chapter 60, the hand-over**
- **B21.** **The largest defect in the batch.** Chapter 60 lifted Chapter 50's hand-over almost verbatim — *the plate went with them, because evidence goes with the people who found it* — and in doing so **asserted a departure that has not happened**, because the crew end this batch as bounded maintainers on an unclosed obligation. A second paragraph was a near-verbatim copy of Chapter 50's bare-place paragraph. Both are cut. The plate now **did not go anywhere, because nobody went anywhere**: it is on the shelf, unopened, and its entry is the shortest in the book, and the brass rule is named in the same paragraph, still silent, and Marek still does not put his hand on it.
- **B22.** The **no-bare-specification rule failed outright** in Chapter 60: about a thousand words of italic ledger block, the objection **asserted** rather than shown, no reader objecting to a specific line and no hand correcting it — at the batch's most ceremonial moment, in a document whose whole subject is not getting a record wrong. The block is cut by roughly half, the restatement of the ninth week's stores is gone, and the entries that remain are **dramatised**: Talla Roke asks for the whole ledger and the clerk refuses and argues, Dalla Sen makes a correction in her own hand because a half-day has been read out as belonging to two closures instead of one, and **Jo Verrick stops the entry** and makes her rule and the air engineer's finding be recorded as two people's sentences in two hands.
- **B23.** The closure entry said **a man from above** went onto the fixed first landing with a hand on the head of the timber while a woman packed it with her knee. No such man was there; Jo Verrick carried the head. That sentence is the one that produces the chapter's entire point, and it was factually wrong about the one event it described. Corrected, and the elapsed time settled at *a fortnight ago* against *a week ago* in the next paragraph.
- **B24.** Marek's account of why the obligation has not ended named **a man** who will not stand on a stair (it is Renn Vale, who is a woman), refused to be on **the belt** (there is no belt in sixty chapters), and then called two named people three. It now names two of the four who could say it and says that Marek is the third.
- **B25.** The unresolved list gave a clerk's margin to **five people**; it has one author and a copy in Dalla's hand. Corrected, and the six/five pairing — the standing corrected error in this manuscript — removed from the same sentence.
- **B26.** The review line carried the seat in the future conditional although the hiss had already gone thin in Chapter 57 and Jo had already said the seat is going. The line now says the hiss has been thin since the Thursday and **the seat is going now and not in the cold.**

**Numbers the narrator got wrong**
- **B27.** *Four words* appeared three times and was wrong every time: a five-word question, an eight-word speech, and a three-hundred-word speech. The one worth keeping — the schoolteacher's question — now counts correctly; the other two are fixed or dropped.
- **B28.** Chapter 51's closing summary of Marek's silence read like a chapter log — *and the shutting took about two seconds and it was the first honest thing he had done all week*. Rewritten from inside him, and two further interior passages added (see below).
- **B29.** Chapter 52 stated the hand's trajectory backwards: the travel got *smaller*, which means the hand got worse, one line before Jo says it is going the right way. The **gap** is what is getting smaller.
- **B30.** Chapter 55 gave Nera Oduya a *girl on a bench* and *a bed to cut*, neither of which exists, in the middle of her most exact speech. Replaced with things that are on the page.
- **B31.** Chapter 54's version of Sefu Aran's bell sentence read **the moment you have pulled it you are not the man on the bell, you are the man on the bell** — the word *mark* had become *bell* in the first clause. This is the most-quoted line in the book, delivered four times, and it is the justification for Bel Ordry taking the bell at all. Restored.
- **B32.** Chapter 57 gave **the bearer** the word, in a manuscript whose reader has learned that the plinth reader's word is a warning and not a stop. The woman on the string has it, as in Chapter 54.
- **B33.** Chapter 55 had Corva Nye **come down the nine steps and have been standing in the anteroom the whole time**. She had been standing at the bottom of them, out of the light.
- **B34.** Chapter 59 said the plate **came up the stairs**, which is the one place in the batch where the plate is given a history, and the history is false. It came up a shaft.

## Craft repairs applied

- **C1 — Marek has an interior life now, three times.** `grep` over the first draft returned sixteen hits for his name and **one** paragraph of interiority, delivered by a narrator who was otherwise not inside him. That is the batch's largest defect, because Volume 02's arc is a man learning to accept shared responsibility and a reader cannot watch that from outside a room he is being talked about in. Three passages were written: what it is like to be handed a tool with a handle shaped so his hand cannot get round it (Ch 51); **what it is like to be stopped in four words by a woman he has worked beside for eleven weeks, and to be glad of it** (Ch 55); and the two-o'clock plan as thought rather than as confession (Ch 59). The narrator's chapter-log summary of his silence is cut.
- **C2 — the net paragraph-opening monotony was worse, not better.** The *Nobody …* opener halved from 26 to 12, which is a real improvement and is now the house default. The definite-article opener rose to fill the gap. Forty-six remain; Chapters 53 and 57 were re-opened at ten and eleven respectively and are now six and five.
- **C3 — *read back* is down by a third, and the recorded figure was wrong.** The measured count is 32 against Volume 01 Batch 0005's measured 47. The project's own records said 13 for this batch and 37 for the last, and the next batch would have been planned against a number that was out by fifteen. The honest figures are now in `state/batch-summary.md` and in the Batch 0002 prompt.
- **C4 — the ledger is read five times.** The second full reading of the stores in Chapter 56 is now two lines and a refusal to repeat, in Tams's voice, and Chapter 59 reports the reading rather than staging it.
- **C5 — six chapters closed on the record.** Chapters 52, 55, 56 and 60 now close on a person or a physical fact: the four cards hanging in a row with one word on each, a courier asking her brother a question, a schoolteacher's key flat on the end of the bench, and a man writing down *nothing* and going to his own work.
- **C6 — Chapter 59 opened by resolving its own tension**, and the withdrawal was then explained four times. It now opens on the ink and the creases and a woman who has decided twice on the road and once in a school store, and the decision arrives in the first paragraph.
- **C7 — Iven's guild assessment was a thesis statement with nobody against it**, alongside three others in the same chapter. Cut to four sentences. His position survives intact in `state/continuity.md`.
- **C8 — narrator editorialising removed** in four places, including the ranking of Isel Ferrow's not-moving as *the best thing anybody has done on that landing*, and the assertion that Pell *would not have wanted to* hear his sister.
- **C9 — doubled phrases removed**: *heard the wheel, and heard it*; *got nothing, and got nothing*.
- **C10 — bold on a person's name removed** from running prose in Chapter 54, where Volume 01 uses italics.

## Watch items carried forward, not defects

- **W1 — the batch's frame is uniform.** Nine of ten chapters are the same room, the same bench, the same hour, with the same physical action: a page is read, one person objects, a second corrects, a third asks for a time. The **pressure types** vary — character conflict, institutional argument, action and discovery, craft, political pressure, reckoning, action and recovery, mystery, record — but type variety in a uniform frame is not pacing, and Volume 01's review predicted exactly this and said it would get worse before it got better. **Chapters 53 and 54 are the exceptions and are the best two.** The next batch should give at least three chapters a location and a job.
- **W2 — Corva Nye is thin.** A function with a costume and a professional ethic. She has one want beyond the form and never costs anything. She must not be turned into an antagonist, so the next batch should give her an interest that is not the form.
- **W3 — Iven Tallo is the thinnest of the working cast** in this batch. He is the explainer four times and has no want of his own. Volume 02 needs the guild to become a pressure on *him* and not on the boundary.
- **W4 — the ledger's staging is now correct but the ledger is still a large object.** Chapter 60 runs about 1,900 words of record-keeping even after the cut, and it is the batch's closing movement. A shorter closing instrument — a slate, a margin, a form, a page held up — would carry the same work.
- **W5 — the two knocks, the two four minutes, and the two doors.** Three deliberate echoes now sit close together (the wash-rooms' *eleven words*, the route's four minutes and the litter's four minutes six, the door that made the sound and the door in a plan). Each is doing real work. Left as they stand, and recorded so a second reader knows it was a decision.
- **W6 — the 51-second figure.** Kept, and now scoped to *a party with a head of timber in its hands*, so that the record's fastest figure is still the thirty-eight and nobody can build a plan on the new one.

## Handoff state after repair

The route is open for people and air, four at a time, in the pump's window, with **a person under the near landing's lip**, kept by the people whose rooms it is under, closed by any of four of them or by the cord or by the cloth, and not for a cart and not a charter. It now has **two shores** and a **seventh term** and a keeper's name and a date. It is not a fix: the shore took the lip from a range of *a finger and three-quarters to two inches* to **a finger and a half**, has **no wedge in it**, and the post under it is the **tenth** item on the unresolved list and is not built.

**The check is gone and the seat is going now.** Five cleanings paid for, two or fewer on the estimate, unmoved in eleven weeks, the housing never opened, the check a flat hiss read by ear with a two-note vocabulary, the hiss thin since the tenth-week Thursday, and no spare and no bronze.

**Corva Nye's page is in a file forty days of walking away**, and Iven Tallo's recorded answer is *nothing*, and the guild's claim is unresolved. **Talla Roke's order is withdrawn in her own hand** and four households in the second gate are carrying water up four sets of steps this winter. **A schoolteacher asked out loud who keeps the Hearthhold rooms and the room did not answer**, and a clerk has written in her own margin that her form has a line for a holder and no line for a keeper. **A door was heard eleven feet down twice, by two different people, on two different mornings, and nobody has been down there.**

Still open, named, and deliberately unresolved: the post; the seat; the cold; a route closure that cuts off water; the four treads; the register boxes and the plan on a dowel, whose key turns in three weeks with the crew not in the room; the band; the stair's history; the two springs; Form 7-B; Iven's bond; the kitchen's nineteen days; the sealed litre; the plate, unexplained and travelling; and the two questions the page carries without answering.
