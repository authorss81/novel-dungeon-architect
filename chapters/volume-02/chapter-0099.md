# Chapter 99 — One Room

He had been asked for a reading ten times in nineteen weeks and had given nothing ten times, and on the fourth day of the nineteenth week he was not asked, and that is the only reason it happened.

In four parts on the anteroom table, weighted at the corners with two pails and a jar stopper, the plan lay out of Bel Ordry's coat because a stranger was going to be asked to come and stand in a room and look at it. The corridor was shut and the school was not running and the anteroom was at its number with the door packed behind stone and clay.

Marek Venn put his left hand flat on the middle part, on the fourth row, and he did it without deciding to and he noticed afterwards that he had not decided to.

Then, instead of nothing, there was a place.

---

Not a force. He had spent nineteen weeks learning the difference between a thing he could use and a thing he could only feel, and what arrived was not a pull and not a direction and had no size to it. **It was a place.** The anteroom. It came up the way a name comes to you when somebody says four others and waits, and it was so much more definite than anything he had ever got off a wall that for about a second and a half he was frightened of it in a way he had not been frightened of anything since a dry service tunnel.

Then it cost him.

He took his hand off the page and it would not go straight; the left hand, which has done every piece of honest work in this building for four months, clamped and shook for about six seconds and he put it flat on the table to stop it and it went on shaking against the wood, and the headache came up out of the back of his skull in about four seconds and it was not the headache he had got at the bottom of the stair, it was behind his left eye and it had a floor to it.

**Then the count came in.**

Ten, the anteroom holds, and Renn Vale says the number out loud at the fifth bell and says it every time anybody comes through, and Marek Venn had heard that number four hundred times in nineteen weeks without it meaning anything. Now it arrived in the middle of the headache and it arrived as a **sound**, the actual sound of ten people breathing in a room with one door open, and it was under the check on the pump, which was hissing on its one note two steps away, and the two noises were one noise. He could not get them apart. He put his fingers on the table and the table was moving, or the room was, and he was not sure, and he sat down, which he had not planned to do.

"Marek," said Dalla Sen, from the fourth step, which is the first time in nineteen weeks that the clerk has come down a step for anybody, "**you have gone the colour of the wall and you are about to go over, and I am not going to catch you, because a woman on a litter caught me once in the stair and I did not know until I was in the air.**

"**Say what you are doing.**"

---

"I read the page," he said.

He said it in that tone, flat, before he had decided anything, and it went out into a room with ten people in it and it was received as a fact, and that is where the trouble started, and it took the room about ninety seconds to notice and him about ninety seconds more.

"**He read the page,**" said Nera Oduya, from three steps. "Marek Venn. You have a hand on a piece of butcher's paper."

"I read the page."

"You have given me nothing off a wall in nineteen weeks. I have watched you give it. I was on the landing when you gave the ninth and I have not forgotten the noise you made about it. **What did you read.**"

His mouth opened to say it, and the thing he wanted to say was *the anteroom, and it goes there first*, and it was true, and he had it, and it was in his mouth.

**Then he noticed,** because Nera Oduya had stopped and was waiting and had not said a word, and a woman who is waiting for the rest of a sentence is not waiting politely.

"The anteroom," he said. "It is the anteroom and it goes there first."

The room made the sound a room makes.

---

"**How do you know that,**" said Nera Oduya.

"Because the fourth row is empty."

"That is not a reason. That is a **hole in a document.** An empty cell is a thing a man is frightened of and I have spent two weeks saying so to a schoolteacher in about a different hole in a different document. What did your hand do."

"**Nothing I can say twice.**"

"Then say what you did."

Marek Venn said it, and it took him a while, and it is in the book because Tams Quill wrote it and read it out and did not improve it.

"**I put my hand on the middle part of the page, on the fourth row, and I did not choose the row, and I have never in nineteen weeks been able to choose anything, and something came up and it was the anteroom and not the pump and not the school and not the stair and not the throat. And I have got a headache and my hand is not working and the number ten is where the hiss is.**

"And I cannot say what it was. And I do not know whether I would have got it standing in the room. **And I was about to tell this room that a room is going to fail, in front of ten people, off a piece of paper, with no way of showing anybody where I looked.**"

Dalla Sen asked him, with the chalk up, and she asked it the way she asks everything now: "**Is it a reading?**"

"No," said Tams Quill, before Marek answered, and the count had been on his feet for some time.

"**It is a claim. It goes in as a claim.** A null has a room and a temperature and a wall and nothing at the end of it, and this has a plan and a hand and a man who is bleeding out of one eye and something at the end of it, and neither of those is a reading, and this is the first thing anybody in this building has had that is not a null and I am not going to call it a reading in a room where a plan is going to a stranger.

"So: **a claim, in his words, with the date, and the condition on it, and the condition is the same one I have put on the other ten and it is that it may not be built on until somebody can do it again.** And he cannot do it again, because his hand is not working and he is sat down, and so be it."

"**Eleven,**" said Marek Venn, from the bench.

"It is eleven," said Tams Quill, "and it is the eleventh, and it is in the ordinary column, and **I am not moving it.** The other three went in my column because you went and found them on a cold wall in the dark on your own. This one you were asked for and it came and it is a claim and it goes where claims go, in the ordinary column, in your own hand, and I am not going to touch it."

---

He tried again at the third bell, and nobody asked him to, and he put his hand on the middle part of the page for eleven seconds and got nothing at all, and the headache got worse and his balance went, and Jo Verrick caught the bench and did not catch him and said afterwards that she had not caught him because she had both hands full and had chosen the bench, and Dalla Sen entered that in the clerk's hand because it is the first honest thing anybody has entered about an accident in this building in three weeks.

**The counting noise did not go away when he stood up,** and it followed him up the nine steps and it was still there at the sixth bell in a room he was not in, and at the seventh bell it was still there and he said so out loud, and the clerk wrote down that on the fourth day of the nineteenth week a man reported hearing the number of people in a room he had left, and entered, in her own hand, that she did not know whether that was a fault and would not guess in a book.

At the sixth bell the clerk took the brass survey rule out of the pocket of the coat on the hook, because his right hand could not get the pocket open and because she had been asked to and he had said yes, and she put it on the table beside the four parts of the plan and it lay there, flat, cold, saying nothing at all, and it had said nothing for ten weeks, and nobody in the room looked at it for more than a second and nobody said a word about it, and it went back in the pocket at the seventh bell.

---

At the fourth bell the anteroom ran its own drill, at its declared number, and it was run the way it has to be run, which is full and not empty, and the charter had been signed two days before and the room had a rule in it about a word and a doorframe.

They filled it to ten and held it two hours and the outer service door on its packed frame was the second way out and the pump was attended and the wheel was on the keeper and nobody said the word.

At the end of the second hour Jo Verrick got down on one knee with a three-foot folding rule she kept in her apron, and put it against the frame and then against the stone beside it, and looked for a while, and then said one word.

"**Finger.**"

"A finger," said Bel Ordry.

"**A finger and a bit, at the head of the frame, and it is the packing, and the packing has set.**" She did not get up off the knee to say it. "I packed it with stone and clay on the sixth bell on the third day of the eighteenth week, and I said it holds and it is not fixed, and both of those are still true, and here is the other half: **clay shrinks as it dries and stone in clay carries nothing that stone carries alone. The frame was bearing on the frame, and now the frame is bearing on me.**

"And I have no timber to hold that frame with and I have said so for eleven weeks, and there is no timber coming, and the office's draft was offering it to three Hearthholds this month and not to this one."

"How long," said Nera Oduya.

"**I do not know and I am not going to guess in a room. It held a finger when it was packed and it has got a finger and a bit out in eleven days and there is nothing in this world to tell me how it goes from there.** And it will not be a sudden thing. It is a door that gets harder to open and then it is a wall with a door in it and everybody inside has been told the second way out is open.

"And the air engineer's line on the page says the word has to be said to a page and not to a door, and she is right, and it is the rule I would have given, and **the rule does not help, because the rule is about the word and this is about the stone.**"

"Entered that in the carpenter's own words," said Dalla Sen, "**and entered that the air engineer's line on the plan is the right rule and is the wrong room's rule, and I am putting that next to each other because in six weeks somebody is going to read one of the two on its own.**"

---

They shored it, because shoring a frame is stone against stone and lime and a wedge and two people and forty minutes, and they had seven sound wedges and two dead sticks, and the third wedge at the waist is up a quarter of an inch and a bit and is not the same wedge and is not in this. It took an hour and a half. It is not a repair and Jo Verrick said so twice, in the same words, and it holds.

**And the shoring held, and the anteroom was emptied at its declared time, and the rating was read out again with the word *door* said to a page and not to a doorframe, and the word worked. The frame is a finger and a bit out of true and holding. Those two facts are on the same page and neither of them cancels the other.**

Marek Venn was not asked to be in it and was not in it, and he sat on the bench with his hand in a cloth and said one thing at the end, out loud, in front of the room, and the clerk wrote it down without being asked, which is the only compliment this building gives.

"**It did not save anybody.** A drill would have found that frame in a month, and the frame goes a finger every week in the cold, and it was going to be found, and I want that in the book in the worst words I can find, because the man who says his hand told him something is going to be asked in a year whether it was worth anything, and the answer is that it was worth an afternoon, and the afternoon was spent sat on this bench with a cloth on my hand listening to a number that is not mine, and the frame would have been found in a month anyway.

"And I am not going to say it was nothing, because it was not nothing, and I have got a headache, and **my left hand closed properly this morning and it does not close tonight**, and I can hear ten people breathing in a room I am not standing in. **I am going to say it was one room. It is one room. There are eight others on that page and I do not know one of them and I am not going to be asked to, and anybody who comes and asks me to read a boundary is going to be told no, and I am saying that now while I can still say it with the hand I have left.**"

**Ten days on the pot, the corridor shut, a man of twenty-two thirty-four days** and thirty-four nights into a count in a column with nothing over the top of it, and the plan lay on the anteroom table in four parts with two pails and a jar stopper on it, and the eighth line of its third column was in a departing woman's hand, and its fourth column was empty and had a reason on it in two hands, and a person was still under a lip that had not moved and had not been looked at and was packed and not held.
