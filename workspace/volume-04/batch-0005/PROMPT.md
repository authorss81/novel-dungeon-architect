## Craft — these four rules are new, and they come out of the Batch 0004 review

**An independent review of Batch 0004 was run after it was delivered and its findings have been repaired in the five delivered chapters. The four rules below are the result. They are craft rules, not plot, and they do not touch `outline/volume-04.md` or the ending.**

1. **No epigraph block and no closing recap block.** A chapter opens on the room and closes on the room. Batch 0004's five chapters each carried three bold paragraphs at the top that gave away the turn and four to six bold paragraphs at the bottom that restated it beat for beat. All of that is gone from 176–180 and it is not to come back. **The only bold in a chapter is a person speaking.** If a paragraph of narration is bold, it is an error.
2. **The request-to-speak tag is dead as a device.** Batch 0004 had fifty-two spoken paragraphs that opened by demanding speech — *Say the covering*, *Say the half against*, *Say it back to me* — and twenty-six clerk recitations opening *Entered, in his words, and entered that*. Repaired: **no formulaic request-to-speak opener anywhere in 176–180, and two bare *Say* imperatives in five chapters, each in a different mouth.** The half against itself is canon and stays; **what is retired is asking for it in the same four words.** A room asks in whatever shape the speaker has: *The word.* / *Of what,* / *All of it.* / *Order.* / *From the top.* / *You have not said the other half.* / a look / a silence / an action on the document / no request at all, and the answer arrives anyway. **The clerk's recitations are to be carried in plain narration by default, not spoken.** Roughly a third of the batch's spoken paragraphs were the clerk repeating a room back to itself.
3. **Quoted share is a real constraint again.** Measured as every word inside matched quotation marks over total words: **Volume 01 ran 25–40%, Volume 03's late chapters ran 60–75%, and repaired Batch 0004 runs 47–59% at a batch mean of 52.4%.** A chapter in this batch should land between roughly forty-five and sixty-five, and **a chapter above seventy is a chapter with no scene left in it.** Narration must carry the room, the objects, the weather of a working morning and the interior life; speech carries what people say.
4. **Pressure must come from a decision, not from a delivery.** The reviewer's finding was that two of Batch 0004's five turns were set running by a schedule: a courier arriving, and a repair letting go at a bell. Both are canon and both stay, and both were softened by moving the chapter's real weight onto a person's choice — Chapter 176 now opens with Marek three days into hunting a lie and takes the leaf as an interruption, and Chapter 179 now opens on Jo Verrick's thumb coming out of the string wrong with the lime already gone in the night. **For 181–185, at least three of the five chapters must turn on somebody deciding something.** A document may arrive; it may not be the cause. Also alternate the pressure type: this batch has two crews meeting, a concession demanded, a fact from the party wall, and an aftermath, and that is only a shape if the chapters inside it do not all run on the same kind of pressure.

**One repair made in Chapter 179 is a fact and not a rule:** the deleted epigraph said the lime repair came away at the third bell while the body had Jo Verrick finding the gap at the first bell. The prose now says the lime let go in the night, before the first bell, which is the only timeline in the text and agrees with the state layer. **The next phase inherits the corrected timeline, not the epigraph's.**
