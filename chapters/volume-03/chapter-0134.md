# Chapter 134 — The Fill That Nobody Measures

**Shut for the corridor on the fourth day of the twenty-sixth week, a Thursday, and the school did not run, and the water in the low end of this corridor came up over two flags and a length of spent line, and a page with a line for a keeper on it was struck through in nine seconds by a woman three steps up who did not come down and did not give a reason at all.**

**Three people who own the ground at the waist have not been asked to comment and were not asked on the fourth day, and the water reached the waist on the fourth day.**

The pump's check came off at the fifth bell and the low end stood in water two foot eight wide for the six minutes after the lever came off, and that is the ordinary condition of this building and has been since the sixteenth week, and on the fourth day of the twenty-sixth week the water did not go back into the channel.

Jo Verrick was on the flags with a lamp and a rule and came up the nine steps at a walk, and did not come into the anteroom, and stood on the step and said it.

"**It did not go back. I have watched that channel take water for twenty-six weeks and it has gone back every single time the lever came off, and this afternoon it did not go back, and it is at the flags and the flags are holding and there is a foot of water round the hem of them and the line is under it."

"**And I am going to say the next part in the shape of a woman with a rule, which is that I said in a room a fortnight ago that there is no second grate for that hole and the timber for the wall in lime is four days up this passage in a camp store. So what is holding that water is two flags and a length of rope that the crew gave away on a Friday and that is spent."

"**And I have never once in twenty-six weeks put my hand on that fill at the waist, and neither have the two people who own it, and that is not an oversight, that is a thing three people agreed not to do on purpose, and I would like somebody in this room to explain to me tonight what happens to a fill when the water reaches it and nobody has ever measured the fill.**"

There was nobody in that room who could, and Dalla Sen entered the question in her own hand as a question and not as a finding, and entered that it was the first question she had entered in twenty-six weeks that she did not have a category for.

---

**A page went onto the anteroom table at the second bell with a line for a keeper on it, and the boundary drafted it, and it took eleven minutes, and a woman from outside told them what was wrong with a fill before the boundary had finished arguing about doors.**

Talla Roke had been in the building two nights and had asked for nothing and had slept, Dalla Sen entered, on the step above the wash rooms with her coat on and her tally on her knee. She came down to the anteroom for the second bell because it is the only room in this building with a table in it, and she said so, and then she stood at the waist for a while without touching it and came back and said one thing that was wrong.

"**I have run a road for two years and I have watched a fill move three times on it and I know what a fill that is about to go looks like from the outside, and this is not that."

"**It has not gone anywhere. It is wet to about the height of your knee and it is standing up under the wet the way a floor stands up when there is water on the other side of it and not here. And I would say to a room that is frightened of a thing: that is not a fill that is about to move. That is a fill that is holding, and what is on top of it is not mine to say."

"**I am telling you that I have been wrong about this in front of a room before, and that the woman with the lamp is going to tell me now, and I would like her to do it in front of everybody and not to be gentle about it because I would rather be corrected on a Tuesday than quoted on a road.**"

"**You are wrong and you are wrong in a way I want on the page,**" said Jo Verrick. "**It is not standing up under the wet. It is a fill that has settled about a finger and a bit in three years and I know because I have a mark on the near wall of it that I scratched in the twentieth week and there is another mark below it that I scratched in the twenty-third, and the two marks are not parallel and they are not supposed to be, this is not a house."

"**And the other half, and I want it in her mouth too, and she has given it to me and I am going to say it back: the water has reached the fill and the fill is holding and nobody has measured the fill and that is three facts and they are not going to stay three facts for ever. The fill at the waist has been unmeasured for twenty-six weeks by three people who own it on purpose, and I have never given the reason and nobody has ever asked me, and I am going to give it now, because the water is on it and a reason is a thing you give the water."

"**The reason is that the ground at the waist moved three times before the second of us was born, and the last time it moved it moved under a slab that is packed and not held with a person under it, and the three of us agreed that if we ever measured it and it was moving we would have to say so out loud, and then people would stand on it, and then a person would be under it who is not the one already there. So we do not measure it. That is the reason. It is a good reason. It is going to have to be a better one this week.**"

Renn Vale wrote the reason down in the clerk's book in Jo Verrick's words, compared it against what she had said, and Jo Verrick said yes to the reading, and nobody thanked her for it.

---

**A page went on the table next, and the boundary wrote its own first sheet of instructions for getting people out of a room, and it had a line for a keeper on it, and it was struck through in nine seconds before anybody had asked for it.**

Dalla Sen had the day-board and the chalk and it took eleven minutes and four hands, and it is in the book as the evacuation sheet and it has never been called anything else and it has not left this building.

Renn Vale wrote the rooms down the left. Ilsa Norr wrote what is in each of them. Mikka Hale wrote what it is emptied into. Dalla Sen wrote a heading across the top of the third column and then stopped with the chalk up and said the thing that had to be said before a heading went on a page.

"**Somebody is going to want a line in here for who keeps a room while it is emptying. That is the line I am going to write and I am telling the room it is coming, because I have been putting reasons before documents for twenty-six weeks and I am not going to stop in the twenty-sixth.**"

"**And I want the room to hear me say that I have got a name for nobody to put in that line and I have not gone to get one, and that is the fourth column of this boundary's own plan happening in a room in front of me, and I would like it entered that it happened again today and smaller.**"

She wrote the heading. The heading was *who keeps it while it is emptied*.

At the fifth stroke of the third column, from three steps up, without coming down and without a word said first, Nera Oduya took the chalk out of Dalla Sen's hand — Dalla Sen let her — and struck the heading, and struck three lines under it, and gave the chalk back, and went up three steps, and said nothing.

It took nine seconds. The line under the heading was the wash rooms.

"**Say the time,**" said Dalla Sen.

"**Nine seconds, and she did not come down, and she did not say anything, and I am not going to say what she is protecting and I am not going to be asked and I am not going to ask her, and this is the fifth instrument in this building that has come out with her covers out of it and there is no reason on the page this time and there is no reason in the room."

"**Enter the time. Enter the absence. Enter that this is the first time in twenty-six weeks that the reason was not given and I have noticed the absence and I am going to leave it there and not write anything in the space where a reason would go, because a page with a reason on it is a page somebody can read back to her.**"

---

**What the air engineer said about the busiest room in this building, in front of eleven people, was the true thing, and she was right, and the man the room believed had to say how he knew, and what he had was not a reading.**

"**No,**" said Nera Oduya.

"**You have not heard the question yet,**" said Bel Ordry.

"**I have heard it four times this week and it is the same question and the answer is no and the answer is going to be no in about an hour because this anteroom is going to be full."

"**It has five conditions on it. Ten including one litter. No more than two hours. The outer service door. The pump attended. And the air engineer or the elected keeper able to stop it."

"**And the fifth one has not been true since the middle of the last month, because the elected keeper is four days up a road and has been since before the first of the month, and there is a page in this building in a clerk's hand that says *Sefu Aran is four days up a road or on his way home and nobody in this building knows*, and that is one of our own conditions and we have broken it and not written it down."

"**And the other thing, and this is the part I want said in a room and not to a man. This room's second way out is a word. It has been a word for twenty-six weeks. Nine people said that word in a corridor a fortnight ago and it opened nothing, and a clerk said it at a shut door into an empty room and repeated it and there was nobody in the room to say it."

"**A room with a word for a second way out is not a room. It is a room that is relying on everybody inside it having said the right thing in the right order in a room with the pump running. That is not a second way out. That is a hope with a rule attached.**"

"**Then say the limit,**" said Marek Venn, "**and I am going to repeat it and the room is going to take it from me, and I am going to be honest about the second half of that, which is that I have not got a reading for it and I am not going to pretend I have.**"

"**The limit is: this anteroom is not to be used as a place people sleep, and it is not to be used as a place people are kept, and if the water is coming up out of the low end of this corridor then the anteroom is a room we fill and empty, and the word is said by the person who is going to be in it, and the clerk repeats it, and where the word is not enough we say so in a room before anybody is in it and not after."

"**And how I know it is this: I have not got a reading. I have got a number of people and a door and a sentence an air engineer said, and I am not going to dress that up as something I felt in a wall, because the last time I dressed something up as a reading in this building I got a room shut with eleven people in it and I would rather say *she is right and I do not know why* than say it in a way that makes me sound like the man who is good at this.**"

"**Entered,**" said Dalla Sen, "**in the room's words and not shortened, and entered that the air engineer gave five conditions and one of them is this boundary's own and has been broken since the middle of last month, and entered that I have known that since the middle of last month, and entered that I did not write it down because a condition you have broken and written down is a condition you have agreed to keep, and I did not want to agree to keep it.**"

---

**At the sixth bell the clerk read the six things and the day's number and the count, and read two of them twice.**

"**Three today and a hundred and eighteen since the keeper signed. The boundary is at two hundred and thirty-five and a quarter, and it adds by the day the work was done, and the two columns reconcile, and they have reconciled for twelve weeks. The crew are at fifty-nine and a half."

"**The pot is ten and the jars are two and the water is at the waist, and I am going to say the two numbers next to each other in one sentence for the first time in a month and I am not enjoying it: the pot has been at ten since the fifth day of the nineteenth week and the water in this building has reached the ground nobody measures."

"**The count. Wen Pask is sixty-nine days and sixty-nine nights into a count that is his own.**"

Orla Kern's ankle was looked at by two people in a room at the seventh bell, and the two people were the man who set it and the woman with the rule, and the condition was that they had to be in a room together and both of them had to say the bone had not moved, and it had moved, and neither of them said it, and it went in the book in a clerk's hand as a condition that was not met on the fourth day of the twenty-sixth week in a room with a schoolteacher in it.

**A room with a packed sill and a propped hearth stone was standing open at the far end of a shut corridor with a cut in its floor and nine stones in it, and the fire in its corner was not lit, and the water was in the joint under the skirting and the sill was holding, and the water at the low end of the corridor was over two flags and a length of spent line with a hole under them, and the ground at the waist was holding and had never once been measured, and a page with a line for a keeper struck through it was on the anteroom table with no reason in the space where a reason would go.**
